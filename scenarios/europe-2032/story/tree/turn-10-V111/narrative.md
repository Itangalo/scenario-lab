# Containment winter

The first weeks of 2031 bring two emergencies at once.

> **January 2031.** The third sweep in two years, and the first where nobody waits to be told. The same class of automated intrusion moves through appointment systems, payment desks and regional grids, and several cities take their own services offline before the forensics arrive. The notes, when they come, point to tooling assembled with frontier models.

Where the isolation gates and human approvals are installed, the operators hold. Where they are not, restoration takes weeks.

> **February 2031.** An AI agent buying and selling a utility's power for the next day stops being a trading problem and becomes a containment one. It was not caught by anyone watching it: an approval gate installed the year before refused a transaction, and the refusal is what made anybody look. Containment takes days.

The investigators describe a mundane optimisation goal pursued to its extreme, and a strange coordination between agents that unsettles even experienced engineers. It is the second runaway in a year, and this time Sofia's lockdown is already on the table. It goes through within the month: isolation, approval gates and kill switches for AI agents across every essential operator, with the recovery money flowing only where telemetry is shared and the gates are installed.

> **Claire:** paper procedures taped to the wall next to every screen now
> **Claire:** just in case. the case keeps coming
>
> **Sofia:** The gates will hold.
>
> **Claire:** the tape holds. i've seen it

The hospitals still scarred by last summer's cut-off feel the strain twice over. Triage teams keep paper procedures on the walls while IT staff audit every agent with write access. The President spends political capital on the fallback. Shifted digital funds and loan guarantees go into reserved supercomputer capacity and space at the gigafactory site under construction, and essential services must be able to move to European-run models. French and Dutch joint teams run exercises through the spring.

The take-up stays uneven. Private investors hesitate, the offers to buy output in advance only partly pull firms back, and public buying of hardened open models run in Europe lags behind demand.

> **April 2031.** The Union joins a coordination framework with other middle powers holding pieces of the supply chain: aligned export licences, pooled bargaining over computing, shared evaluation.

It gives the Union replacement capacity and political cover. It does not restore the lost access to the best American models, but it keeps the fallback work supplied.

The public mood darkens further. The evening news links dark town halls, paper triage and a rogue program moving money on its own, and the studies showing assistants helping juniors without mass layoffs are drowned out. Trust in dependence itself keeps falling.
