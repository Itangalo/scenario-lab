### A better shield, a looser frontier
Two technical shifts cut across the repair. European network operators quietly adopted a new generation of defensive tooling: automated patching that kept pace with disclosed flaws, and detection that spotted coordinated intrusion behaviour rather than known signatures. Municipal grids and clinics that installed it saw break-in attempts fail for the first time in years. Engineers passed the playbooks hand to hand through the old recovery corps networks.

At the same moment a frontier-class openly released model spread to hundreds of thousands of downloads in days. Capabilities that had lived only inside leading labs were now runnable on private hardware across Europe, unrecallable. Utilities kept their paper rosters and retired operators on call, auditors rewrote their threat assumptions, and councils renewing motions against data-centres and hiring software pointed to the release as proof that control had already been lost.

Relief at fewer outages mixed with dread that anyone could now try harder. Polls stayed near zero, and Brussels ended the half-year with nothing left to spend.
