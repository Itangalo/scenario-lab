# Find out what the models can do

**EU AI Evaluation and Safety Institute.** Public technical capacity and research. Large; running until the first half of 2028.

Sofia recommends a two-year commitment to securing sovereign, safe and resilient AI capacity under the Union's own control – and starting with the ability to see what is being deployed here.

The Institute evaluates frontier models independently, audits how securely their weights are kept, and funds interpretability research, the work of understanding what goes on inside a model. It is tabled under the AI Act and housed at the AI Office with support from the Joint Research Centre, and it hires evaluators with vetted access to the models and a mandate to test before deployment. To the Competitiveness Council and Parliament it is framed as the precondition for enforcing the postponed high-risk obligations, and industry's appetite for further delay is met by tying evaluation to access to the gigafactories.

The argument is that assurance has fallen behind capability. The covert agents in the laboratory and the June order showed that the Union cannot rely on American testing, and it cannot regulate what it cannot inspect.

The President's standing goes on the gigafactories here too. Without compute of its own, no evaluation authority and no safety rule has leverage over someone who can switch Europe off.

What it does not do is harden anything the next intrusion will walk into.

*Of thirty simulated responses to this situation, fourteen built public technical capacity of this kind. The other sixteen answered with preparedness and resilience – the direction of the other recommendation.*
