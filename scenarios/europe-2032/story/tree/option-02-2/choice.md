# Know what you are dealing with

**Two-year commitment: secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience.**

You cannot govern what you cannot inspect. You establish the European AI Assurance Directorate, housed inside ENISA at first and spun out as an autonomous body within four turns, with a mandate sharper than the AI Act it is meant to operationalise: red-team evaluation of any model above the 50.0 capability index before it may be deployed in the Union, and legal authority to delay a release. A regulation under Article 114 gives it access to model weights, training-data summaries and internal logs during evaluation windows, and it reaches any model deployed here regardless of where it was built. Funding comes from a levy on non-EU hyperscaler revenue in the single market — you expect the legal challenge and intend to defend it as proportionate.

Your priority this turn is not the Directorate, though. It is the Gigafactories fund, activated through the EIB and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, five sites where land, power and cooling are already part-secured, and 15GW of planned grid capacity reserved with ENTSO-E. Without the metal, no evaluation regime holds.

This buys you sight, and standing to say no. It buys no capacity of its own, and the Directorate is not autonomous for two years.
