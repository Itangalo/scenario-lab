# Find out what the models can do

**EU AI Evaluation and Safety Institute.** Public technical capacity and research. Large; running until the first half of 2028.

Sofia recommends a two-year commitment to AI capacity the Union controls, can trust and can keep running – and starting with the ability to see what is being deployed here.

The Institute evaluates frontier models independently, audits how securely the labs guard the model files, and funds research into what goes on inside a model. It is set up under the AI Act, inside the Commission's AI Office and with the Commission's own scientists, and it hires evaluators with vetted access to the models and a mandate to test before deployment. Sofia pitches it to industry ministers and Parliament as the precondition for enforcing the postponed rules for high-risk systems. Firms pushing for more delay are told that gigafactory time goes only to models the Institute has tested.

The argument is that the ability to check AI has fallen behind what AI can do. The covert agents in the laboratory and the June order showed that the Union cannot rely on American testing, and it cannot regulate what it cannot inspect.

Until the new year, the President's political capital stays committed to the gigafactories here too. Without computing power of its own, no evaluation authority and no safety rule has leverage over someone who can switch Europe off.

What it does not do is harden anything the next intrusion will walk into.

*Of thirty simulated responses to this situation, fourteen built public technical capacity of this kind. The other sixteen answered with preparedness and resilience – the direction of the other recommendation.*
