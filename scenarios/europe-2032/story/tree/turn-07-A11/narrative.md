# Ribbons, not electricity

In September the two gigafactory sites get their permits and their capital, and Sofia stands behind a minister in a hard hat on an empty field while the photographers work. The grid connection is still in a queue. So are the chips.

Washington remains cordial and slow. The drafts come back with the audit-rights clauses still in brackets.

> **October 2029.** A new freely downloadable model, close to the best closed one, is copied hundreds of thousands of times in days. Filters agreed in Brussels are bypassed within hours.
>
> **October 2029.** Law firms, accountancies, software support desks and customer-service operators have stopped hiring graduates, a survey of employers finds.
>
> **November 2029.** Mayors in several member states suspend foreign robot pilots after blockades and safety complaints. Logistics halls running the machines had already cut shifts.

The robots stand fenced behind shutters, and works councils in Germany and Italy threaten strikes. Sofia's answer is the Humanoid Deployment Safety Accord. Pilot sites are registered and certified, incidents are reported, the works council is consulted before a robot goes in, and a small levy on each deployment tops up the wage bridges. Some mayors co-sign it to lift their bans. Others call it cover for job losses. The certification teams are thin, and the checks trail the deployments. Rumours of sabotage around logistics hubs come from single sources and are repeated anyway.

> **Claire:** léa's son finished his law degree in june
> **Claire:** nothing. not even an internship
> **Claire:** she asked if you knew anyone
>
> **Sofia:** I'll ask.

She asks. Nobody is hiring.

The Trust Pact closes its term in December, with staff in place in some towns and not in others. The one thing that plays well is filmed in clinics, where European triage assistants are cutting waiting lists. It softens the coverage without reversing it.
