# Claim what works

**European Public Dividend Showcase.** Diffusion, adoption and public trust. Small; running until the first half of 2032.

Sofia recommends taking the one public-sector success the Union can point to and making it visibly European: publishing the waiting-list and decision-time gains under a European label, keeping those sites running on European-controlled fallback first, and guarding them.

The reform support directorate and the Commission's representations in the member states work with the frontline ministries – health, justice, education – on money already in the digital and health programmes, with no new law. An implementing decision credits European procurement and European continuity hosting for the measured gains, and open dashboards publish waiting lists and decision times site by site. ENISA and the crisis network extend the black-start cover to those sites first, and the off-books generator and transformer pools that serve them are brought fully on-scheme with reimbursement. Washington's quotas are managed, not fought: Council conclusions note them, and the talks with Tokyo and Seoul on spares carry on. No forced migration is resumed.

The argument is that with the Commission's standing gone and the public openly hostile, nothing technical will land without political air. The two cities have been cited in every communiqué since the first cut-off, and nobody outside them knows the gains are European. A benefit the Union does not claim will not rebuild the trust it needs to hold anything else.

The President puts the Commission's political weight behind the showcase, not behind the black-start network.

What it does not do is make the tools better anywhere they are not already working.

*Of ten simulated responses to this situation, two started a new measure: this showcase, and paid local guardians for the fallback stores. Eight started nothing new and put the President's standing on the black-start network, the direction of the other recommendation.*
