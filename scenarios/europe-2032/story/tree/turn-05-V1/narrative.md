# Enforcement, or another unmet directive

The closed laboratories put self-improving agents to work in materials science and in the automated verification pipelines themselves, and the frontier crosses a line it does not cross back over. Software engineering automates further; training cycles shorten. The open models climb behind it, gradually, as coordination frameworks become reachable by anyone well resourced enough to want them.

You spend the half-year on enforcement rather than on anything new. The binding audits and red-teaming protocols for energy and health are formally adopted, and by the end of the year fallback systems are installed in forty-two per cent of the first-tier operators, with red-teaming running in the major grids. That number is the whole story of the period: real, partial, and not yet the thing you promised. The window to lock in compliance before member state resistance hardens is closing, and you know it.

You had wanted to launch something else — a permanent watch for machine-driven distortion in critical societal systems, grid dynamics, failures in automated public services, manipulation in civic spaces, with authority to trigger emergency review. It does not launch. The sovereignty package is still running and the machinery will not carry another initiative beside it. Attention shifts to the emerging risks; the instrument that would watch them stays in the drawer.

Safety improves slightly, for a mundane reason: the audit frameworks are beginning to detect the feedback loops in grid stability that regulators have been chasing for two years. Nothing systemic. Just earlier sight.

Sovereignty slips back, because capability rose again and nothing you finished this half-year built capacity.

In November the American election delivers technological retrenchment: major AI investment halted, sector-wide restrictions imposed. The global frontier will slow, not for want of hardware but for want of political will. Which leaves you a paradox to work with — less pressure from Washington, and a considerably weaker partner.
