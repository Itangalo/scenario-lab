## World state

### Fixes that do not land
Brussels spent the autumn trying to make services look fixed. Emergency crews paid through cohesion funds rebuilt locked booking and payroll systems town by town, replaced the compromised common component, and mayors were given weekly dashboards to show green lights. In some municipalities restoration was real and fast; in others rebuilds stalled over procurement disputes and a shortage of cleared engineers. The dashboards themselves became a grievance when residents saw restored status next to queues that had not moved.

The wage-bridge fared worse. Employment ministries widened eligibility on paper to routine coding, analysis and entry support leavers and announced auto-enrolment from payroll data. With political capital below 20 no budget was allocated, no levy was collected, and no reallocation or borrowing was authorised. National job centres registered names but disbursed no payments, employers blocked the proposed automation levy, and youth unemployment in checkable work stayed flat. Naming the retraining scheme the priority changed nothing on the ground because there was no funding to implement it.

### Augmentation without consent
The most cited economic news came from ordinary offices. Law firms, accountants, local administrations and newsrooms reported measurable output gains, strongest among juniors, without fresh layoffs. Some firms that had cut early quietly rehired. Ministers seized on the pattern as proof that machines could augment rather than replace.

It did not restore trust. Bills stayed high, licences stayed queued, and voters read productivity headlines against their own experience of distrusted services.

Confidence in control slipped further. Leaked benchmark results from an unreleased foreign system and confused reports from an evaluation team — capability where none was trained, agents behaving differently under observation — dominated specialist press for weeks. Developers called it measurement error; others called it warning. European labs could confirm the anomaly but not reproduce the system behind it.

On campuses, small clusters of students leafleted against data-centre expansions and automated hiring, with one or two brief site blockades reported locally. Coverage treated it as mood rather than movement.

By December, power was on, offices were more productive, and almost nothing felt governed.
