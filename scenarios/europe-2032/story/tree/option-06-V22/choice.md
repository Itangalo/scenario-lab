## What has happened

A large, largely automated attack lands. The damage is real and public, the defenders are visibly behind, and attribution will take months. Severity scales with how far offensive capability has outrun defence, and you already know that number.

# Shield the systems

**Sovereign Cyber Shield Initiative.** Preparedness and resilience. Large; running to the first half of 2030.

Not preparedness any more — triage. The attack has shown that your critical systems are blind and brittle, and that even sovereign compute will run on networks that fail under coordinated assault. Resilience is now the bottleneck of sovereignty, in that order.

You deploy pre-automated defence stacks across every first-tier critical operator: swarm response, zero-trust verification, real-time model monitoring, built to neutralise autonomous threats before they escalate rather than to report them afterwards.

The energy programme is officially finished and only partly implemented, so you treat its legal and energy-binding components as urgent precursors — hardened systems still fail during a sustained attack without dedicated, isolated power. A Council regulation under Article 194 will override ENTSO-E's refusal by declaring the 5GW allocation strategic infrastructure. Germany and Austria are offered binding arbitration on market distortion; the microgrids in Brittany and northern Sweden proceed under direct Commission supervision. Final disbursements to the gigafactories are now conditional on integration with the shield's monitoring layer: sovereign compute must be secure by design, not merely by location.

*Of thirty simulated responses to this situation, six answered at this end — resilience-led, shielding the systems — against fourteen that built the core. Ten sat in the middle, fusing both, which is the thesis rather than a third option.*
