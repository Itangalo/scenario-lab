## World state

### The accord announced elsewhere
In February, Washington and Beijing announced a limited risk pact — weights security, autonomous escalation channels, restraints on certain biological design tools — with verification described as thin but real. Brussels learned the outlines from press wires and a late briefing. European labs were not signatories, European regulators not verifiers.

The Commission reframed quickly: seek entry as third verifier, offer supply-chain commitments and clinical incident data, ask in return for re-licensed therapy models and a seat on the escalation hotline.

### Containment holds, clinics do not
The bio containment surge formally closed this half-year. Isolation wards, transfer corridors and stockpiled treatments stayed open; no regional cascade followed the modified-pathogen winter. Emergency planners counted it a success.

Wards told a different story. The overtime pool that had staffed containment was now asked to also deploy the sovereign medical channel. A handful of hospitals in the two hardest-hit states ran tailored treatments on ring-fenced European compute; most lists did not move. Licensed stand-ins remained slower and narrower than the cut-off foreign system.

Talk of coordinated walkouts hardened. Union bulletins spoke of synchronized stoppages, local papers of sick-outs and closed outpatient mornings. Nothing yet carried a national strike notice, but rosters thinned and appointments slipped further.

The large member state's separate supply arrangement stayed outside joint procurement, and its ministers offered only vague language about folding it into a common position later.

American retrenchment continued to slow frontier advance, and the new pact slowed it a little more. That bought time. It did not restore access, and with audit of opaque systems still conceded as impossible, no frontier law was tabled. Europe held its wards open while the cures stayed behind someone else's door.
