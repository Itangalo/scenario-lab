Establish mandatory, legally enforceable, and technically integrated cyber response exercises across finance, energy, and healthcare, with automated escalation protocols and pre-authorized intervention powers for ENISA and national CERTs.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 7  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 18–22 points by turn 7 through institutionalised readiness, reduce incident recovery time by 60%, and restore public trust by demonstrating coordinated action under fire

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Binding Cross-Sector Cyber Response Drills

While energy-backed infrastructure remains vital, it is now clear that the first test of EU sovereignty will be survival—not ownership—and this drill is the fastest way to harden our nervous system before the next attack lands.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment the Union shifts from building walls to learning how to fight behind them. The cyber_major_incident—still unattributed but bearing the signature of model-generated exploit chains—has hit public hospitals in three member states, frozen payment processing in regional banks, and disrupted grid monitoring in the Nordic synchronisation zone. The response was delayed, uncoordinated, and publicly visible in its failure. This cannot stand. We are therefore activating the Emergency Resilience Corridors not as a programme but as a command: under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 Directive, we are mandating that ENISA, in coordination with the EAAD and national CERTs, run binding cross-sector drills by turn 7, with legal authority to simulate live intrusion, trigger automated patching, and override local operators under predefined thresholds. The EIB is releasing €12bn from its resilience window to fund the integration of swarm detection and AI-mediated triage into Tier 1 operators’ systems—this is not optional modernisation but war footing.

The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has technically met its target: 5GW of low-carbon power is now legally ring-fenced in law, with microgrid isolation underway in Brittany and northern Sweden. But the cyber incident proves that physical separation is not enough. We are therefore redirecting the final phase of the InvestAI Gigafactories to prioritise not just compute density but resilience integration: the next phase of deployment will require all sovereign AI nodes to run real-time adversarial simulation and failover protocols as a condition of access. Germany and Austria remain in legal dispute, but we are offering them a seat on the Resilience Oversight Board—a new body under the EAAD—with veto only on energy market spillover, not on security integration. Italy and Hungary, having blocked real-time logging, will now face a choice: participate in the drills with full audit access or be excluded from EU-wide AI service interconnects. There is no third option.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the streets are restless. We are launching a public-facing campaign—“Shield & Signal”—to broadcast the drills as live demonstrations of Union protection. Cameras will stream anonymised command-room footage. Citizens will see decisions made, not promises. If we cannot turn fear into faith now, we will lose not just capital but legitimacy. This is not about technology. It is about whether the Union can still act as a state.
