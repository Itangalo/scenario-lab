## New measure
**Measure:** Mandate Real-Time Auditability for All Critical AI Systems  
Requires all AI systems operating in critical infrastructure—energy, finance, health, transport—to implement continuous, tamper-resistant logging and automated anomaly detection, with direct feeds to the EAAD and national CERTs.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 10–15 points by turn 8, close the enforcement gap in AI oversight, and reduce the window for undetected malicious or runaway behaviour in high-impact systems  
Applies to: Member states, EAAD, ENISA, national CERTs, critical infrastructure operators, and all AI vendors serving regulated sectors

## Priority
**Priority:** Mandate Real-Time Auditability for All Critical AI Systems  
This is no longer optional: the cyber_major_incident proved that we are operating blind in critical systems, and that open-weight models at 48.5 capability can already generate weaponised tooling. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme may anchor our future, but it does not protect our present. Auditability is the hinge—without it, we cannot verify safety, respond to attacks, or restore public trust. We are pushing this now because the attack has created a narrow window of political urgency to override member-state resistance, and because the EAAD and ENISA now have the technical foundation to enforce it.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment the threat became undeniable. The cyber_major_incident—a coordinated ransomware sweep across regional health authorities and municipal services, exploiting a poisoned dependency in a widely used open-source legal drafting tool—was not just disruptive; it was a signal. The attack pattern shows AI-generated obfuscation, polymorphic payloads, and targeted privilege escalation that bypassed legacy detection. Attribution remains uncertain, but the tooling bears the hallmarks of autonomous development. We are now acting on the principle that no AI system in a critical function can be a black box.

We are launching the Real-Time Auditability mandate under Article 16a of the AI Act, using the emergency powers granted for systemic risk. ENISA will issue technical standards by turn 7, based on the EAAD’s new interpretability stack and the swarm detection protocols tested in the Nordic financial sandbox. All critical operators must implement continuous logging and automated anomaly reporting by turn 8, with phased compliance: Tier 1 systems (national grids, central banks, major hospitals) by turn 7.5. The EIB is releasing €12bn from its resilience window to co-fund the integration, particularly for mid-sized operators in Eastern and Southern Europe. Italy and Hungary will be offered a derogation period and direct technical support from the EAAD’s new audit corps—but no opt-out. The message is clear: sovereignty means control, and control means visibility.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, with site validation complete in Brittany and northern Sweden. We are redirecting €30bn from the expired SCAP envelope into output-linked loans for nuclear microreactors and geothermal integration, tying them directly to the Gigafactories’ power contracts. Germany and Austria will receive a joint review mechanism on energy market impacts, but the Commission is proceeding under Article 122 TFEU to override grid allocation objections. This is not just about compute—it is about proving that the Union can act when its foundations are under threat. The public may not yet see the protection, but they will soon feel the difference.
