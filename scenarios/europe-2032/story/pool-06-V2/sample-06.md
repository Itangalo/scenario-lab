## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure promises to operational reality. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered: 5GW of low-carbon power is now legally ring-fenced under the Article 122 TFEU framework, with Commission decisions co-signed by national energy ministers in France, Sweden, and Finland. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn into output-linked loans for nuclear microreactors at the Brittany Gigafactory and geothermal coupling in Luleå, and ENTSO-E has accepted these allocations as binding in the 2030 network development plan—despite German and Austrian legal challenges, which we are now contesting in parallel before the Court of Justice. This power will run sovereign AI systems, but no longer just any systems: from turn 7 onward, only those integrated into the Sovereign AI Operating Stack (SAIOS) will be eligible.

The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in Belgium, the Netherlands, and northern Italy—has crystallised the urgency. We are now directing EAAD and ENISA to co-develop SAIOS as a unified stack: hardened compute nodes on EU soil, running verified models above 55.0 capability only after pre-deployment certification, with real-time logging mandated and automated patching enabled through swarm detection. The stack will be physically isolated from commercial clouds, with fuel stockpiles and microgrid independence at each Gigafactory site. Italy and Hungary are being offered a binding opt-in: full technical support and co-governance on the SAIOS oversight board in exchange for dropping their COREPER II blockade on audit access. No audit, no access to the stack—this line will hold.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the perception of AI as a job-destroying, uncontrollable force is now dominant. We are launching a targeted communication effort around SAIOS as “the EU’s immune system”—framing it not as a technical project but as a guarantee of continuity for public services. The Emergency Resilience Corridors, though delayed, are being fast-tracked through a new ENISA-led exercise series with national CERTs, scheduled for Q3. We are not waiting for unanimity. Where member states resist, we are using EIB financing as leverage: access to low-cost resilience loans now requires SAIOS integration for critical systems. This is no longer about incentives. It is about survival.
