## New measure
**Measure:** Accelerate Sovereign Audit Infrastructure  
Deploy hardened, EU-operated monitoring nodes inside all Tier 1 AI systems used in critical infrastructure, enabling real-time anomaly detection, model reasoning traceability, and automatic shutdown upon violation of assurance thresholds.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 12–18 points by turn 8, close enforcement gaps left by stalled logging mandates, and restore credibility in EAAD’s oversight through demonstrable technical reach into operational systems

## Priority
**Priority:** Accelerate Sovereign Audit Infrastructure  
Because without enforceable visibility into how AI systems behave in operation—especially those approaching or exceeding 60.0 capability—we cannot prevent harm, cannot verify compliance, and cannot justify public trust; and because the failure of legal mandates in COREPER II has proven that regulation without technical teeth is ceremonial, not sovereign.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the hinge between symbolic authority and material control. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded, but its promise remains unfulfilled: only 2.1GW of the 5GW target has been physically ring-fenced, and no site operates under full EU control. The InvestAI Gigafactories continue, but their timelines slip without guaranteed power delivery. We are now shifting focus from infrastructure promises to operational assurance—because even if we had the compute, we still could not see what it does.

The cyber_major_incident this turn—a cascading ransomware sweep exploiting an AI-generated exploit chain across municipal health registries and regional tax platforms—landed precisely where our defences were weakest: in systems using foreign-hosted models with opaque logic and no audit trail. Attribution will take months. Damage is contained, but confidence is shattered. This proves that resilience cannot be built on protocols alone; it must be rooted in technical presence inside the machines themselves.

Accordingly, we are launching the Accelerate Sovereign Audit Infrastructure measure as a Category 5 effort under joint EAAD-ENISA leadership. Using €18bn redirected from the expired Tech sovereignty package and SCAP remnants, we are commissioning the design and deployment of sovereign monitoring stacks—hardware-rooted, zero-trust inspection nodes—that will be embedded directly into AI systems managing essential services. These will extract reasoning traces, detect goal drift, and trigger automatic fallbacks when anomalies exceed safety bounds. Deployment begins with French and Dutch healthcare AI pilots by turn 7, expanding to energy grid optimisers and financial supervisors by turn 8. Italy and Hungary will be offered co-development roles and early access to threat intelligence in exchange for dropping resistance to mandatory integration. Legal basis will be established via revised AI Act delegated acts under Article 25a, invoked for systems above 55.0 capability operating in critical domains.

We are also initiating a quiet review of the two_year_commitment. It ends next turn. While sovereign infrastructure remains unfinished, the deeper failure has been one of enforcement: we can mandate auditability, but not achieve it. The pivot to technical sovereignty—where the Union does not just regulate but *inhabits* the stack—must now become explicit. If that requires redefining what "sovereignty" means in practice, we will do so—but only after seeing whether this measure can break the deadlock.
