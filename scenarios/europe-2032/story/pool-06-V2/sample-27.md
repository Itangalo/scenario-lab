## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Core Programme  
This is the decisive pivot from dependency to agency: with private investment collapsed, legal gridlock paralyzing energy ring-fencing, and the US poised to restrict access, we can no longer rely on market-led or permission-based access to capability. The only way to ensure the EU can determine its own future is to build and operate our own stack—physically, legally, and operationally within our jurisdiction—and this measure begins that at scale.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the end of emergency reaction and the beginning of sovereign execution. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded—its goal of 5GW ring-fenced power was not fully delivered due to ENTSO-E’s refusal and member-state legal challenges, and the Tech sovereignty package has also finished with negligible private capital mobilised. These failures confirm that market incentives and emergency powers alone cannot secure autonomy. We are now redirecting €45bn in unspent SCAP and EIB resilience funds into the new Sovereign AI Core Programme, co-developed with France, Finland, and the Netherlands, to build a fully sovereign AI stack: compute at the InvestAI Gigafactories in Brittany and northern Sweden will be reprofiled to host a closed, high-assurance training run under EAAD oversight, using geothermal and nuclear-backed power and isolated microgrids. This system will not be connected to commercial clouds or foreign supply chains; its weights will never leave EU-controlled hardware, and its operation will be legally anchored under a new Council Regulation on Strategic AI Infrastructure, proposed under Article 114 TFEU with a national security rider.

The EAAD is now operational and has certified its first public-sector models, but its authority remains contested. We are reinforcing it by mandating that all systems above 55.0 capability used in national security, critical infrastructure, or cross-border services must be trained and run within the Sovereign AI Core. Real-time logging is being reintroduced through a backdoor: the new Regulation will require it as a condition of access to sovereign compute, bypassing COREPER II objections by making it a technical prerequisite rather than a standalone directive. ENISA is integrating swarm detection and automated patching into Tier 1 operators ahead of the cyber incident’s fallout, but we know now that defence without sovereign capability is temporary. Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the protests are growing—we will not win back trust through promises, but through proof. This Core will be the first system the public can point to and say: *This runs on our power, our rules, our land.* If we do not build it now, we will not have the chance later.
