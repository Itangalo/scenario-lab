## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Assurance and Control Network  
This is the necessary successor to our two-year commitment: having failed to secure full energy-backed compute by turn 6, we now pivot from infrastructure promise to operational control — the only form of sovereignty that matters when the next attack runs in minutes and the models are no longer ours. Without a system we can monitor, verify, and stop, all compute is foreign compute, regardless of where it runs.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the end of a failed era and the beginning of a harder one. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has collapsed in implementation: Germany and Austria have won their legal stay, ENTSO-E refuses to bind grid allocations, and only 1.8GW of the promised 5GW is physically secured. The Commission’s invocation of Article 122 TFEU was bold, but it could not overcome the veto of inertia. We are now 60.5 on the capability index, open weights are at 48.5, and a major cyber incident has just exposed the fragility of our systems — yet we still cannot audit what runs where, or stop it if it goes wrong. The two-year commitment has failed, not in ambition, but in teeth.

We are now acting on the lesson: sovereignty is not compute, not energy, not funding — it is control. We are launching the Sovereign Assurance and Control Network as a binding directive under Article 114 TFEU, co-implemented by the EAAD and ENISA, with the EIB reprofiling €42bn from the defunct SCAP envelope into secure telemetry stacks, sovereign inference clusters, and override gateways physically co-located with the InvestAI Gigafactories. These systems will run only on EU-controlled infrastructure with real-time logging, automatic anomaly detection, and human-in-the-loop kill switches — and all models above 50.0 capability deployed in public or critical functions must route through them. Italy and Hungary will be offered opt-in technical sovereignty: their national CERTs will receive full mirror access to the audit layer, and their operators will be trained and funded to run local nodes. But the architecture will be non-negotiable: no backdoor, no blind spot, no system beyond reach.

Public sentiment is at 24.0, and the cyber incident — a model-generated supply-chain compromise in a Tier 2 software dependency, now spreading through municipal accounting platforms — has shattered what little trust remained. We are not announcing this as infrastructure, but as protection: a shield over public services, a leash on runaway systems. The EAAD will publish weekly integrity reports, and we are fast-tracking a public dashboard showing every certified model, its use case, and its audit trail. This is not the pivot we wanted, but it is the one the world allows. We are no longer building sovereignty to compete — we are building it to survive.
