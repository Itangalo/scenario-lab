## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Assurance & Audit Infrastructure (SAAI)  
Stand up a physically secured, EU-operated evaluation environment with real-time monitoring, model interrogation, and red-teaming capabilities for all systems above 55.0 capability deployed in critical functions.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 12–15 points by turn 8, close the gap between certification and operational control, and restore credibility in EAAD by demonstrating sovereign technical capacity to verify what it certifies  
`Applies to:` EAAD, ENISA, national intelligence and cyber agencies, critical infrastructure operators, and EU-funded research institutes

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Assurance & Audit Infrastructure  
This is the necessary next step: the EAAD now exists, but its authority rests on tools and access it does not control. Certification without sovereign verification is symbolic. With ai_capability at 60.5 and openweight at 48.5, we are already behind the curve—our evaluators must be able to run models in our own secure environment, under our own conditions, with our own interpretability stacks. This measure anchors the credibility of our entire assurance regime and makes real the promise of auditability we staked ourselves on.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from symbolic authority to operational sovereignty. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered: 5GW of low-carbon power is now legally ring-fenced for EU-controlled AI infrastructure, with microgrid isolation protocols activated in Brittany and northern Sweden. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn from the defunct SCAP envelope into long-term loans for nuclear microreactors and geothermal integration, directly tied to the InvestAI Gigafactories. ENTSO-E remains resistant, but we are invoking Article 122 TFEU to treat these allocations as binding under the network development plan—Germany and Austria will receive a joint oversight mechanism on market impacts, but the directive proceeds. This is no longer about permission; it is about survival.

The EAAD is now live, and its first certification cycle is underway for models above 50.0 capability. We have mandated automatic verification in public-sector contract and clinical coding systems, leveraging the interpretability tools developed under the pre-deployment research programme. But we know the limits: without real-time logging, enforcement is blind. Italy and Hungary continue to resist audit access in COREPER II, and we are offering them technical assistance from ENISA and a seat on the EAAD advisory board—but we will not compromise on the red line. No audit, no access to EU-wide services.

Public sentiment is at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident has shattered confidence. A ransomware sweep, model-generated and highly adaptive, crippled municipal services in three member states. Attribution is ongoing, but the tooling was clearly AI-amplified. We are accelerating pre-exercised cyber response protocols through ENISA, but the real answer lies in restoring trust through capability, not communication. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Assurance & Audit Infrastructure: a secure, EU-operated environment where models above 55.0 can be interrogated, monitored, and red-teamed in real time. This is not oversight—it is ownership. The facility will be hosted across hardened sites in Finland and Austria (under EU legal jurisdiction), with staffing drawn from the EAAD, ENISA, and vetted national experts. Funding will come from the EIB’s resilience window and redirected from the now-completed Tech Sovereignty Package. If we cannot verify control, we do not have it—and right now, we do not. This measure begins to close that gap.
