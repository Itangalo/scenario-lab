## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign Assurance and Audit Backbone (SAAB)  
Establish a fully EU-operated, real-time monitoring and control layer for sovereign AI systems, integrating EAAD-certified models into air-gapped, energy-backed compute clusters with mandatory interpretability hooks and live audit feeds to ENISA and national oversight bodies.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Assurance and Audit Backbone  
This is the necessary next step beyond certification and energy-backed compute: without a sovereign technical stack that we operate, monitor, and audit in real time, our assurance is symbolic and our sovereignty contingent. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves that capability without control is vulnerability; this measure ensures that the models we certify are not only safe at release but remain under our authority in operation.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure promises to operational control. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded, but its 5GW allocation remains only partially implemented: Germany and Austria continue to challenge the ring-fencing of nuclear and geothermal power, and ENTSO-E’s grid integration plans are still non-binding. We are now shifting from securing energy to securing the systems that will run on it. The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track, but their output cannot be trusted unless it is sovereign not just in location but in function. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Assurance and Audit Backbone (SAAB): a public technical stack, built and operated by the Union, that integrates EAAD-certified models into air-gapped clusters powered by the Gigafactories’ energy-backed sites.

The SAAB will mandate live interpretability feeds, continuous anomaly detection, and direct audit access for ENISA and designated national bodies. No model above 50.0 capability will be allowed to operate in critical public functions unless it runs through this backbone. We are directing the EIB to allocate €45bn from the residual SCAP envelope to fund the first two clusters—Brittany and northern Sweden—with completion tied to Gigafactory output. Legal authority will be drawn from Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 directive, with a new implementing act under the AI Act to close the loophole that allows certified models to be deployed outside auditable environments. Italy and Hungary will be offered co-governance roles in the audit framework, but participation in SAAB will be a condition for access to EU-wide AI services.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident has deepened fear. We are reframing the SAAB not as another technical fix, but as the Union’s guarantee: if AI is to displace workers and disrupt services, then it must do so under our eyes and under our control. The message is simple: we will not outsource sovereignty to code we cannot see. This is the first measure that truly unites our two mandates—keeping the EU capable of determining its own future, and preventing lasting harm—by making control the foundation of both.
