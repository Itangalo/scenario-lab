## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Integrated Sovereign AI Stack Pilots  
Deploy and stress-test fully sovereign AI systems—hardware, energy, software, and audit—from EU-controlled stack components in three critical domains: emergency response coordination, medical diagnostics, and financial market monitoring.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–18 points by turn 8, demonstrate end-to-end control, and generate real-world assurance data to feed EAAD certification; indirectly raise public_sentiment by showing operational capability  
Applies to: EAAD, ENISA, EIB, national health and finance agencies, selected EU research labs, and the InvestAI Gigafactory sites in Brittany and northern Sweden

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Integrated Sovereign AI Stack Pilots  
Because the cyber_major_incident has shattered confidence in fragmented defences, and because our sovereignty remains untested in practice, we must now prove we can operate, not just fund or regulate. This measure forces integration across our stalled programmes—tying energy, compute, and assurance into live systems under EU control—and answers the public demand for visible capability, not just promises.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure planning to operational sovereignty. The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted emergency dispatch systems in three member states—has made clear that our current posture is not merely delayed, but dangerously disaggregated. Assurance without control, energy without deployment, resilience without integration—all are insufficient. We are now repurposing the end of the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme not as a conclusion, but as a foundation: the 5GW allocation, though only partially implemented, will now be legally bound to the Integrated Sovereign AI Stack Pilots. The EIB is redirecting €12bn from the expired Tech sovereignty package into co-investment with the InvestAI Gigafactories, conditioning further funding on full-stack integration: nuclear microreactors in Brittany will power a live medical AI pilot with real-time EAAD audit logging, while Sweden’s geothermal sites will host financial monitoring agents under ENISA supervision.

The EAAD is expanding its mandate: it will not only certify models but now oversee the full-stack pilots, requiring interpretability, weight provenance, and continuous logging as conditions of operation. We are invoking Article 122 TFEU again to bypass COREPER II gridlock, instructing ENTSO-E to treat the pilot sites as critical infrastructure with priority grid access and microgrid isolation. Italy and Hungary are being offered a joint audit role in the EAAD’s pilot oversight board—transparency in exchange for obstruction. ENISA is launching red-team exercises against the pilots by turn 7, with results public. This is not symbolic: we are building systems that must work, survive, and be seen to work. If we cannot demonstrate sovereign capability in crisis response, diagnostics, and finance—three domains where failure is public and catastrophic—then we have no claim to autonomy. The Union must not only survive AI; it must be seen to command it.
