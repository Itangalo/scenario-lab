## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from planning to hardening. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded, but its failure to bind member states to audit access revealed a fatal gap: we can allocate power, but not control. In response, we are launching the Sovereign Compute Hardening Directive under Article 122 TFEU, backed by EIB conditionalities. From this turn forward, any site receiving EU funds under the InvestAI Gigafactories programme must comply with mandatory physical security standards—fuel stockpiles for microreactors, air-gapped monitoring nodes, and real-time logging accessible to EAAD. These are not requests: they are conditions of funding.

Germany and Austria remain in legal challenge over grid ring-fencing, but we are bypassing the dispute by tying compliance to disbursement. The EIB will reprofile €35bn from the now-closed Tech sovereignty package into output-linked loans that trigger only upon verification of hardened infrastructure. ENISA is deploying mobile audit teams to Brittany and northern Sweden to verify baseline compliance by turn 7. Italy and Hungary, having blocked logging, will now face a choice: accept EAAD-monitored hardening or forfeit access to EU compute subsidies. There is no third option.

The EAAD is expanding its mandate to include operational audits of sovereign clusters, not just pre-deployment model evaluations. We are directing it to publish quarterly assurance reports on all InvestAI sites, with redacted findings made public to restore trust. Meanwhile, the Emergency Resilience Corridors are being fast-tracked through a COREPER III emergency session, with ENISA integrating swarm detection and automated patching into Tier 1 operators’ protocols by July. This is not symbolic. If we cannot protect the systems that keep lights on and hospitals running, no amount of policy will save us. The Union must be seen to defend, or it will not be believed.
