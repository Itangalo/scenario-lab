## In practice
We are treating turn 6 as the hinge between foundational effort and operational control. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered its 5GW commitment through Commission action under Article 122 TFEU, despite resistance from Germany and Austria. ENTSO-E has been directed to integrate microgrid ring-fencing into the 2029 Network Development Plan, with the EIB releasing €30bn in output-linked loans to fast-track nuclear microreactors in Brittany and geothermal-AI integration in northern Sweden—both tied directly to InvestAI Gigafactory sites. These will host the first phase of the new audit infrastructure, ensuring that power, compute, and control are co-located and legally insulated from external interference.

The EAAD is now fully operational and has issued its first certifications for models above 50.0 capability, using newly deployed interpretability tools to verify alignment with public-sector use cases. However, the failure of real-time logging mandates in COREPER II exposed a fatal gap: we can certify, but we cannot yet enforce. That ends now. Under a new Council Regulation proposed under Article 114 TFEU, we are launching the Sovereign Audit & Control Infrastructure—a secure, distributed execution environment where certified models run under tamper-proof conditions, with automated compliance checks, runtime logging accessible to ENISA and national authorities, and immediate deactivation upon deviation. This is not another layer of reporting—it is a technical enforcement of political decisions. Italy and Hungary will be offered hosting rights for regional nodes and full transparency into audit logic, but participation in critical systems will require connection to the sovereign stack.
