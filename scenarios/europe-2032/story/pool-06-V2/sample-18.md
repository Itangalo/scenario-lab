## New measure
**Measure:** Enforce Real-Time Auditability in Critical Infrastructure  
Mandate continuous, tamper-proof logging and external monitoring of all AI systems in Tier 1 critical sectors—energy, finance, healthcare, and transport—with automated anomaly detection and immediate reporting to ENISA and the EAAD.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 10–15 points by turn 8, close the enforcement gap in AI oversight, and restore credibility in the EAAD by ensuring all certified systems are continuously monitored  
`Applies to:` Member states, ENISA, EAAD, national regulators, critical infrastructure operators, and EU-wide cloud providers

## Priority
**Priority:** Enforce Real-Time Auditability in Critical Infrastructure  
This is no longer optional: the cyber_major_incident proved that capability without visibility is surrender. While energy-backed compute remains foundational, the attack exploited the very gap we failed to close in COREPER II—real-time logging. Without it, assurance is retrospective theatre. This measure forces the operational integrity our sovereignty depends on, and it must land before the next incident.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as a wake-up call: the Union was not resilient, and its defences were not sovereign. The attack—automated, model-generated, and targeting public services—exposed a fatal flaw: we have built systems we cannot see. The EAAD may certify, but without continuous access, it cannot protect. We are now invoking Article 122 TFEU again, this time to fast-track a Council Implementing Regulation under the NIS2 framework, mandating real-time logging for all AI systems in Tier 1 critical infrastructure. ENISA will deploy automated monitoring nodes within 90 days, funded by a €12bn reallocation from the EIB’s resilience window. The systems will use open, verifiable protocols to ensure no single vendor controls the audit trail.

Italy and Hungary’s resistance in COREPER II cannot stand. We are offering them co-leadership of a new ENISA-Audit Task Force, with technical support from the German BSI and French ANSSI, and a fast-track review mechanism to address implementation burdens. But the rule will be binding: no logging, no operation. We are also revising the AI Act’s conformity assessment to include ongoing compliance, not just pre-deployment checks. The EAAD will now have standing authority to demand live data feeds from any certified system, and to trigger emergency suspensions if access is denied.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the perception that AI is harming more than helping is deepening. This measure is not just technical—it is political. We are launching a public-facing dashboard, “AI Watch,” showing anonymised logging activity, incident response times, and EAAD interventions. Transparency is our only path to trust. If we cannot prove we are in control, we are not sovereign—and if we are not sovereign, we are not safe.
