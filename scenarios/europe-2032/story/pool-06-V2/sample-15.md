## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure to endurance. The cyber_major_incident—targeting public health records through a poisoned software dependency, with AI-generated obfuscation delaying detection by 72 hours—has shattered any illusion that sovereign compute alone protects us. The tooling was derived from open models at 48.5 capability, now in the wild and weaponised with speed and precision. ENISA confirms the attack exploited verification gaps in third-party libraries used by national health IT providers. We are now acting on two fronts: containment and transformation.

On containment, we are invoking the Emergency Cooperation Mechanism under Article 122a TFEU to coordinate cross-border health data recovery, funded by the EIB’s emergency window. ENISA is deploying automated patching swarms to all Tier 1 operators, prioritising healthcare and energy. The EAAD is expanding its audit mandate to include third-party dependencies in public-sector software supply chains, effective immediately. Italy and Hungary have been offered emergency technical integration teams to overcome their audit resistance—this is not a negotiation, but a lifeline.

On transformation, we are launching the Sovereign Resilience Integration Programme as the successor to the failed Emergency Resilience Corridors. This is not another communication campaign—it is a binding directive. We are establishing a Joint Operational Cell under ENISA and the EAAD, with legal authority to enforce real-time logging, automated failover, and pre-exercised response protocols across all InvestAI Gigafactory-linked systems. The EIB is reprofiling €40bn from the now-defunct SCAP and Tech Sovereignty Package envelopes into this effort, tying funding to resilience benchmarks. Germany and Austria will be consulted on grid integration, but the Cell will have override authority during declared incidents. Sovereignty is no longer a promise—it is a posture. And it must hold.
