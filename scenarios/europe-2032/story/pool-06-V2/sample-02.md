## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign Audit Stack Initiative  
Because the failure of real-time logging in COREPER II has exposed a fatal gap: we can certify models at release, but we cannot verify their behaviour in operation—and without that, assurance is performative. ## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure intent to operational integrity. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded—on paper, with 5GW ring-fenced, but in practice, only partial physical deployment. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn into nuclear microreactors in Brittany and geothermal integration in northern Sweden, tied directly to InvestAI Gigafactory sites, but grid isolation remains incomplete, and ENTSO-E’s resistance, backed by Germany and Austria, means we cannot yet guarantee uninterrupted power. We are now shifting focus: energy is necessary, but not sufficient. Without runtime control, sovereign compute is just sovereign electricity burning for someone else’s models.

The EAAD is operational but under siege. It has certified its first models above 50.0 capability using new interpretability tools, and the mandate for automatic verification in public-sector contract and clinical coding is law. But the collapse of the real-time logging amendment in COREPER II—blocked by Italy and Hungary—has gutted its enforcement teeth. We cannot afford another cycle of symbolic action followed by operational failure. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Audit Stack Initiative: a public, open-source, modular suite of monitoring agents, runtime guards, and cryptographic logging protocols, developed under EU research grants and hardened by ENISA. This is not another directive—it is code. Code that will be required for any AI system in critical infrastructure to retain EAAD certification. Code that member states can inspect, adapt, and trust—because no one vendor, no foreign cloud, controls it.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident—automated ransomware sweeping public services in three member states—has deepened the sense of exposure. The attack exploited a poisoned software dependency, generated and deployed by an AI agent trained on open weights. Attribution is months away, but the damage is real: hospitals delayed care, municipalities froze services. We are directing ENISA to integrate the nascent Sovereign Audit Stack into Tier 1 operators immediately, on a voluntary basis, with EIB funding for adoption. The message must be clear: we are not just building compute—we are building control. And if we cannot prove that the Union can see what AI is doing, nothing else we build will matter.
