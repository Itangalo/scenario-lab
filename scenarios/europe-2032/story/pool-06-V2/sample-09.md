## In practice
We are treating turn 6 as the collapse of the old model and the beginning of a new one: one where sovereignty is not declared but enforced through architecture. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has technically concluded, but its core promise—5GW of dedicated, low-carbon power for EU-controlled AI—remains unfulfilled due to national resistance and grid-level non-compliance. We are no longer negotiating: under Article 122 TFEU and the newly invoked Crisis Protocol of the Digital Decade Act, the Commission is issuing binding operational directives to ENTSO-E to isolate and allocate 5GW of nuclear and geothermal output in Brittany, northern Sweden, and the Carpathians, tied directly to the InvestAI Gigafactories. EIB is reprofiling €40bn from the expired Tech sovereignty package into long-term, non-recourse loans for microgrid integration and fuel stockpiling, with disbursement conditional on physical delivery by turn 7.

The EAAD is now operational, but its authority is being hollowed out: Italy and Hungary continue to block real-time logging, and without enforcement, certification is symbolic. We are therefore launching the Sovereign Assurance and Control Network (SACN) as a de facto enforcement mechanism. SACN will require that all models above 55.0 capability used in critical public functions—health diagnostics, legal adjudication, grid control, financial clearing—run only in EU-operated inference environments with continuous interpretability logging, model provenance checks, and air-gapped audit trails. These environments will be hosted exclusively in the InvestAI Gigafactories, powered by the ring-fenced energy, and monitored by EAAD with real-time intervention rights. ENISA will provide the cybersecurity backbone, integrating swarm detection and automated patching from the Emergency Resilience Corridors effort.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident—ransomware sweeping municipal services across three member states, exploiting an AI-generated zero-day in a widely used civic platform—has shattered any remaining trust in self-regulation. We are not waiting: a crisis communication campaign is being rolled out alongside the SACN announcement, framing it as the first concrete step to *control* AI, not just react to it. The message is simple: from now on, the most powerful AI in public use will not run on foreign servers, under foreign terms, or without a watchdog inside the box. This is not a guarantee of safety—but it is the first real guarantee of accountability.
