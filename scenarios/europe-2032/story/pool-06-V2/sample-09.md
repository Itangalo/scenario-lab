## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment when preparedness becomes non-negotiable. The cyber_major_incident—targeting public health systems and local government networks with AI-generated ransomware payloads—has confirmed that offensive capability is now in distributed hands, and defensive coordination remains fragmented. We are activating the Emergency Resilience Corridors under Article 122 TFEU and the NIS2 framework, directing ENISA to finalise binding protocols for Tier 1 operators in finance, energy, and health by the end of the quarter. These will include automated patch deployment, swarm-based intrusion detection, and pre-authorised cross-border response teams. The EIB is releasing €15bn from its resilience window to fund immediate hardening, prioritising municipalities and regional hospitals. Italy and Hungary, having blocked real-time logging, are being offered a fast-track integration path with technical support from the German BSI and French ANSSI—but if they do not comply by turn 7, their access to EU-wide AI services will be suspended under the AI Act.

Simultaneously, we are embedding control into sovereignty. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has reached its deadline: 5GW of low-carbon power is now legally ring-fenced, with microgrid isolation underway in Brittany and northern Sweden. But the cyber incident proved that infrastructure without oversight is a liability. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Assurance Integration Directive: every system running on this sovereign compute must feed real-time logs into the EAAD’s new anomaly detection layer, built on the interpretability tools validated this turn. The EAAD will have authority to trigger emergency rollbacks or throttling if unauthorised agent behaviour is detected. This is not optional: access to the energy-backed grid is conditional on full auditability. Germany’s legal concerns will be addressed through joint technical oversight; Austria will be offered a pilot role in the geothermal node’s safety validation.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the streets are restless. We are launching a crisis communication effort centred on the Resilience Corridors—framing them as the Union’s first visible shield against AI-driven chaos. The message is simple: we are no longer waiting. We are defending. If we do not show that the EU can protect its people *now*, no amount of sovereign compute will save our legitimacy.
