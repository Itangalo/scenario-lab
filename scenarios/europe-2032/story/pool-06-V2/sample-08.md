Require all EU-critical AI systems—public and private—to run on physically and legally sovereign infrastructure with auditable energy sourcing, real-time logging, and no dependency on foreign-controlled supply chains.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)

## Priority
**Priority:** Enact Binding Sovereign Compute Mandate  
This is the decisive follow-through: the failure of voluntary coordination and the partial implementation of energy-backed compute have shown that only a binding, enforceable mandate can prevent strategic drift. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves that capability without control is catastrophe in waiting—our sovereignty must be operational, not aspirational, and this measure forces the integration of energy, infrastructure, and audit into a single, enforceable standard.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from investment to enforcement. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally concluded, but its goal—5GW of low-carbon power for sovereign AI—remains only partially realised. Germany and Austria continue to challenge ring-fencing under state aid and internal market rules, while ENTSO-E refuses to bind grid allocations without unanimity. We can no longer accept fragmentation on strategic infrastructure. Therefore, we are launching a new directive under Article 114 TFEU, framed as a single market measure for digital resilience, to mandate that all AI systems above 50.0 capability operating in critical sectors—energy, finance, health, defence, and public administration—must run on compute infrastructure that is physically located in the EU, legally subject to EU jurisdiction, powered by audited low-carbon sources, and integrated with EAAD’s real-time logging and verification protocols.

This mandate will be enforced through the revised AI Act and linked to procurement rules: no access to EU public contracts, and no certification for deployment in critical systems, without compliance. We are instructing the Commission to draft the regulation immediately, with a vote scheduled in COREPER II by the end of the turn and a Council decision targeted for turn 7. To break the deadlock, we are offering Italy and Hungary a co-governance role in the EAAD’s audit committee and fast-tracked EIB funding for national resilience upgrades. For Germany and Austria, we are proposing a binding opt-out mechanism for non-strategic workloads, but the core 5GW will be ring-fenced under strategic energy reserve rules, invoking Article 122 TFEU as a last resort.

The cyber_major_incident—automated ransomware sweeping municipal services in three member states—has crystallised the cost of delay. We are directing ENISA, in coordination with national CERTs, to map all critical AI dependencies by turn 7 and identify foreign-controlled chokepoints. These will be the first targets of forced migration under the new mandate. The EIB is reprofiling €40bn from the expired Tech sovereignty package into low-interest, output-linked loans for sovereign stack integration—compute, energy, and software—at the InvestAI Gigafactories. This is not industrial policy; it is strategic survival. We will build the capacity, mandate its use, and enforce the boundary—because if we do not, no one else will.
