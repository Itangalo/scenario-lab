## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign AI Core Programme  
This is the necessary successor to the failed binding energy allocations: without a physically and legally sovereign core, the Union remains exposed to both external denial and internal fragmentation—risks that have now crystallised. The cyber_major_incident and the collapse of the Tech sovereignty package prove that market incentives and emergency powers are insufficient; we must now build what we operate, control, and defend ourselves.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the end of emergency improvisation and the beginning of structural sovereignty. The failure of the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme—blocked by ENTSO-E, undermined by national vetoes, and left unimplemented despite Article 122 action—has shown that binding declarations without operational control are not enough. We are now launching the Sovereign AI Core Programme as a new, irreversible track: not a request, not a coordination effort, but a built, funded, and legally entrenched backbone under direct EU authority.

The EIB is reprofiling €45bn from the now-defunct SCAP and Tech sovereignty package envelopes into long-term, non-recourse loans for the Core’s first two sites: a nuclear microreactor cluster in Brittany tied to the western InvestAI Gigafactory, and a geothermal-integrated data complex in northern Sweden linked to the Nordic node. These will be physically isolated from commercial grids, with fuel stockpiling, hardened microgrid controls, and direct EAAD access for real-time auditing. The Commission is drafting a Council Regulation under Article 172 TFEU to establish the Core as a European Strategic Infrastructure, requiring qualified majority voting for approval—bypassing unanimity traps. Germany and Austria will be offered co-governance on safety and environmental standards, but not veto power.

The EAAD is being empowered as the Core’s gatekeeper: no model above 50.0 capability may run on the Core unless certified for interpretability, containment, and auditability. We are integrating ENISA’s new swarm detection and automated patching tools into the Core’s baseline security stack, making it both a shield and a signal: the first infrastructure in the world designed not just to host AI, but to control it. This is not a data centre—it is the foundation of the Union’s political agency in the age of artificial general capability. If we do not build it now, we will not have the capacity to act when the next crisis hits—and the next one may not be survivable.
