Create a publicly funded, EU-wide technical cadre with security-clearance pathways, embedded in national research institutes and the EAAD, to operate, monitor, and defend sovereign AI systems under real-time audit.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Corps  
Because sovereign infrastructure is now physically and legally grounded—but without EU-loyal technical personnel to operate and verify it, our compute remains a shell. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves that capability without control is vulnerability; we can no longer outsource the guardianship of our own systems. This measure builds the human core of sovereignty, and it must land before the next wave of capability arrives.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure to operation. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered: 5GW of low-carbon power is now ring-fenced under Commission authority via Article 122 TFEU, with microgrid integration underway in Brittany and northern Sweden. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn from the defunct SCAP envelope into long-term loans for nuclear microreactors and geothermal plants tied directly to the InvestAI Gigafactories. ENTSO-E has been instructed to treat these allocations as binding in the 2030 network development plan, and Germany and Austria have been offered joint oversight on market distortion risks—but the power will flow. The physical foundation of autonomy is laid.

Yet the cyber_major_incident—automated ransomware sweeping municipal health records across three member states, exploiting a poisoned open-weight dependency—reveals the hollow core beneath: we have systems, but not enough trusted people to run them. The EAAD is certified and active, but its auditors lack deep access to live systems. ENISA’s patching swarm is ready, but deployment depends on private vendors with conflicting incentives. We cannot secure what we do not staff. Thus, we launch the Sovereign AI Corps: a €12bn, three-year programme to recruit, train, and embed 10,000 technically advanced personnel across the Union, with dual affiliation to national institutions and EU command. They will staff the EAAD’s real-time monitoring desks, operate audit interfaces at the Gigafactories, and serve as rapid-response defenders during incidents. Funding comes from the EIB’s resilience window and redirected national cyber budgets, with recruitment fast-tracked through Erasmus-style mobility and security-clearance harmonisation in COREPER.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the streets are restless. We are framing the Corps not as a technocratic fix but as a civic renewal: a new public service for the AI age, open to displaced professionals in law, accounting, and engineering. This is how we begin to rebuild trust—not with promises, but with places. The US posture remains pending, but we act now: autonomy without agency is an illusion, and we will not be left dependent on goodwill when the next frontier breaks.
