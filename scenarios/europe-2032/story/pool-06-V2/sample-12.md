## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign AI Corps  
Establish a dedicated EU civil service corps for AI operations, security, and assurance, drawing from displaced professionals, national cyber units, and frontier researchers, to staff sovereign infrastructure, operate EAAD audits, and maintain real-time control over critical systems.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points by turn 8, improve ai_safety by 8–10 points through embedded public-sector expertise, and modestly lift public_sentiment by offering visible career transitions for displaced workers  
Applies to: European Commission, EAAD, ENISA, member states’ digital ministries, InvestAI Gigafactory operators, and public-sector AI deployment units

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Corps  
Because the cyber_major_incident has exposed a fatal gap: we are building sovereign infrastructure and designing audit regimes, but we lack the people to run, defend, and verify them at scale — and the public sees no path for those displaced by AI to be part of the solution. This measure turns a liability into capacity, anchoring sovereignty not just in hardware and law, but in human skill and institutional memory.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure intent to operational control. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered: 5GW of low-carbon power is now legally ring-fenced for sovereign AI use, with microgrid isolation in Brittany and geothermal integration in northern Sweden progressing under Commission oversight via Article 122 TFEU. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn from the failed SCAP envelope into output-linked loans for nuclear microreactors tied to the InvestAI Gigafactories, and ENTSO-E has accepted these allocations as binding in the next network development plan — not through unanimity, but through legal compulsion under crisis powers. Germany and Austria remain opposed, but their legal challenges are now outpaced by physical deployment.

The EAAD is operational and has issued its first certifications for public-sector models above 50.0 capability, using new interpretability tools to verify reasoning paths in contract and clinical coding systems. However, real-time logging for critical infrastructure remains blocked in COREPER II. In response, we are conditioning access to EU-wide digital services on audit compliance: no logging, no interoperability. Italy and Hungary are being offered a fast-track to co-host an EAAD regional verification hub in Trieste, with dedicated funding and staffing — but the requirement stands. ENISA is integrating automated patching and swarm detection across Tier 1 operators, using the cyber defence breakthrough to harden defences ahead of the next wave.

Public sentiment is at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health systems in three member states — has deepened fear and distrust. We are reframing the response not as damage control but as recruitment: the Sovereign AI Corps will draw from unemployed legal, accounting, and administrative professionals, retraining them in model auditing, cyber defence, and AI operations. The EIB will fund 15,000 positions over two years, hosted by the EAAD, ENISA, and Gigafactory control centres. This is not just about capacity — it is about legitimacy. If the Union is to command AI, it must be seen to command it with its people, not against them.
