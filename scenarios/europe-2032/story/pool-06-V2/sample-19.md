## New measure

Establish a unified, EU-operated infrastructure for continuous model evaluation, runtime monitoring, and tamper-proof logging of high-capability AI systems, integrating EAAD-certified interpretability tools with ENISA-operated secure enclaves.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)

Finishes on turn: 8

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Assurance and Audit Backbone  
This is now the decisive edge of our sovereignty: without independent, continuous verification, even our own certified systems can drift or be subverted, and the collapse of real-time logging in COREPER II has left us blind at the worst possible moment. The cyber_major_incident proves that capability has outpaced control, and we cannot rely on goodwill or voluntary compliance when the tools to automate harm are already loose. SAAB delivers what the two-year commitment demanded — full operational control and real-time auditability — not through promises, but through infrastructure we own, operate, and trust.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from contested authority to operational sovereignty. The failure of the Tech sovereignty package and the partial implementation of Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty have shown that market incentives and emergency powers are not enough. We now build the nervous system of control: the Sovereign Assurance and Audit Backbone. This is not another layer of bureaucracy — it is a technical spine. We are directing the EIB to reprofile €18bn from the expired Tech sovereignty envelope into SAAB, funding secure enclaves co-located with the InvestAI Gigafactories in Brittany and northern Sweden. These will host runtime monitors that ingest EAAD-certified interpretability outputs and log decision traces in tamper-proof chains, accessible only to EU-authorised auditors. ENISA will operate the network, with design input from the EAAD’s new red-team unit, and we are mandating integration for all public-sector systems above 55.0 capability by turn 8.

The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states — has crystallised the urgency. Attribution remains unclear, but the attack chain used open-weight models fine-tuned for lateral movement and evasion, exploiting vulnerabilities discovered autonomously. We are treating this not as a one-off but as the first visible wave of a new threat regime. In response, we are fast-tracking integration of SAAB’s monitoring stack with national CERTs, starting with France, Finland, and the Netherlands, under the newly invoked Article 4(2) TEU coordination mechanism. Italy and Hungary, having blocked real-time logging, are being offered a transitional compliance window: they can either join the SAAB audit framework by turn 7 or face suspension of EU digital infrastructure funding. This is not coercion for its own sake — it is the price of participation in a Union that must now defend its cognitive sovereignty as fiercely as its borders.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the social cost of displacement is mounting. But we cannot buy trust with promises. We must show that the EU can act — decisively, technically, and independently. The SAAB is that demonstration. It answers the two mandates at once: it prevents lasting harm by closing the control gap, and it restores our capacity to determine our own future by ensuring that no AI system of consequence operates beyond our sovereign reach. This is not the end of the fight — but it is the first time we are fighting from strength.
