## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign Verification & Response Backbone  
Establish a unified, EU-operated verification layer for all critical AI systems, integrating EAAD-certified interpretability tools with ENISA-run runtime monitoring and automated incident escalation, ensuring that any system above 55.0 capability operating in essential services is both auditable in real time and subject to immediate containment if anomalous behaviour is detected.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 10–15 points by turn 8, close the enforcement gap in real-time logging, and restore credibility to the EAAD by demonstrating operational control over deployed systems  
`Applies to:` Member states, EAAD, ENISA, national CERTs, critical infrastructure operators, and public-sector AI deployments above 55.0 capability threshold

## Priority
**Priority:** Activate Emergency Resilience Corridors  
This remains the most urgent lever not because it builds sovereignty directly, but because the cyber_major_incident this turn has exposed our strategic fragility: without institutionalised, pre-exercised response, even the most advanced compute infrastructure becomes a liability. The corridors are the first visible demonstration that the Union can protect its citizens—without that legitimacy, no amount of state-backed infrastructure will hold.

## In practice
We are executing under fire. The cyber_major_incident—initially a ransomware sweep across regional health administrations, later traced to a poisoned software dependency in a widely used compliance tool—has left hospitals offline for days and exposed the total absence of coordinated response. ENISA’s detection systems flagged anomalies only after propagation was widespread; the lack of pre-authorised patching authority and cross-border mutual aid protocols left defenders isolated. This is the moment the two mandates collide: we cannot wait for perfect sovereignty to protect what little remains governable.

Under the Emergency Resilience Corridors, we are fast-tracking legally binding agreements between national CERTs, ENISA, and Tier 1 operators to establish automatic escalation paths, mutual access to sandboxed incident replicas, and pre-approved swarm-response playbooks. The EIB has released €12bn from its resilience window to fund integration, with a condition: full EAAD audit access by turn 7 or funding reverts. Italy and Hungary are being offered a joint operations cell hosted in Ljubljana with rotating leadership—concrete inclusion in exchange for dropping their veto. The priority is not perfection but speed: we must show movement before the next incident window opens.

Meanwhile, the failure of the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty to achieve physical deployment by turn 6 forces a hard reckoning. The Commission is preparing a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU to declare the 5GW allocation non-negotiable, overriding ENTSO-E’s consensus requirement. Brittany and northern Sweden microreactor sites will proceed under emergency procurement, with EIB loans now tied to output verification rather than permitting milestones. Germany and Austria will receive binding guarantees on grid stability studies and joint oversight—but not veto power. The Sovereign Verification & Response Backbone is the next anchor: without it, even sovereign systems cannot be trusted. We are directing the EAAD and ENISA to co-develop the monitoring stack, with pilot integration in French and Dutch healthcare AI systems by turn 7. This is no longer about catching up. It is about proving the Union still has a spine.
