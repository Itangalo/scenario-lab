## In practice
We are treating this turn as the pivot from infrastructure to agency. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered: 5GW of low-carbon power is legally ring-fenced under Article 122 TFEU, with microgrid integration underway in Brittany and northern Sweden. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn from the failed SCAP envelope into output-linked loans for nuclear microreactors and geothermal plants tied directly to InvestAI Gigafactory sites. ENTSO-E’s resistance has been overridden through the network development plan process, and Germany and Austria have been offered joint monitoring on market impacts—but the directive stands. This is no longer a proposal; it is law.

The EAAD is now operational, certifying models above 50.0 capability with interpretability tooling and mandating automatic verification in public-sector contract and clinical coding. But real-time logging for critical systems remains blocked in COREPER II by Italy and Hungary, who see audit access as sovereignty infringement. We are offering both a seat on the EAAD advisory board and ENISA technical co-management—but the line holds: no audit, no access to EU-wide services. The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep across municipal services in three member states—has made this non-negotiable. Defenders were blind, response was fragmented, and attribution remains uncertain. This cannot happen again.

That is why we are launching the Sovereign AI Corps: a binding directive under Article 168 TFEU for cross-border health and digital resilience, extended to cover AI system operation and assurance. The EIB will fund training hubs at EU universities, with mandatory placement in EAAD, ENISA, or sovereign compute sites. Member states must release 1,250 qualified personnel per year under a new mobility framework, with wage parity and housing support. This is not a talent grab—it is the creation of a permanent, politically insulated technical service, as vital as the judiciary or central banking. The corps will staff the systems we are building, secure them, and ensure that when the next incident comes, the Union sees, understands, and acts—on its own terms.
