## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Core Programme  
This is the necessary successor to the failed private-led model: with the Tech Sovereignty Package and Secure Energy-Backed Compute both collapsing at the hurdle of member-state fragmentation and legal resistance, we must now build what no coalition of interests will allow us to buy. Sovereignty is not access—it is ownership, operation, and unbreakable control. Without it, every audit, every resilience plan, every assurance claim depends on systems someone else can switch off.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the end of a failed experiment in market-led sovereignty and the beginning of a state-built one. The collapse of the Tech Sovereignty Package—despite its €200bn private capital target—and the partial, contested rollout of the Secure Energy-Backed Compute programme have confirmed that we cannot out-incentivize the fragmentation of our own governance. The cyber_major_incident this turn, exploiting model-generated exploits in a compromised software dependency across municipal health systems, landed precisely because our defences were reactive, our logging incomplete, and our infrastructure shared. We were not attacked by a superpower, but by a capability that is now loose, and we were unprepared.

The Sovereign AI Core Programme is our answer: a fully integrated, state-run stack, legally and physically insulated from external control. We are directing the EIB to reprofile €45bn from the expired Tech Sovereignty envelope into direct state ownership of compute at the InvestAI Gigafactories, with binding agreements that all power from the 5GW nuclear and geothermal sites in Brittany and northern Sweden flows exclusively to sovereign AI systems. These will run only models certified and continuously monitored by the EAAD, with real-time interpretability and automatic shutdown triggers if capability drift or unauthorised adaptation is detected. ENISA is being tasked with building the closed-loop control architecture by turn 7, using the verification widening from contract and clinical coding as a template.

We are not waiting for unanimity. The Commission will propose a Council Regulation under Article 122 TFEU to establish the Core Programme as a matter of strategic emergency, with qualified majority voting to bypass national vetoes. Germany and Austria will be offered a formal role in the oversight board and first right of audit on energy market impacts. Italy and Hungary will be offered co-location of a Tier 1 resilience node and direct access to the Core’s defensive AI tools—but participation is not optional for access to EU-wide critical services. This is not coercion; it is survival. The US anti-AI posture incoming next turn may slow their frontier, but it will not slow the models already loose. Our only path to safety is control—and control must be built, not bargained for.
