## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Sovereign AI Operating Stack  
Establish a fully EU-controlled, modular AI stack — from firmware to agent runtime — to run on sovereign compute, ensuring full operational control, real-time auditability, and immunity to remote override or sabotage.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 20–25 points by turn 8, close the gap between physical infrastructure and functional autonomy, and ensure that no system critical to EU decision-making runs on foreign-controlled software layers  
Applies to: EU institutions, national defence and critical infrastructure agencies, EAAD, ENISA, InvestAI Gigafactories, and selected EU-based AI developers

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Sovereign AI Operating Stack  
This is the next indispensable layer of sovereignty: we now have partial control over energy and hardware, but without a stack we trust and operate ourselves, we remain dependent on foreign software architectures that can be compromised, backdoored, or withdrawn overnight. The cyber_major_incident this turn proves that capability without control is vulnerability; this measure ensures that the 5GW of energy we fought for runs systems we alone can verify, update, and defend.

## In practice
We are treating turn 6 as the hinge: the moment when the Union shifts from acquiring components of sovereignty to assembling them into a functioning, defensible whole. The completion of the Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme — despite legal resistance and ENTSO-E’s refusal to bind allocations — means that 3.8GW of low-carbon power are now contractually ring-fenced for sovereign AI use, with microgrid integration underway in Brittany and northern Sweden. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn from the failed SCAP envelope into long-term loans for nuclear microreactors tied directly to InvestAI sites, and we are instructing national regulators to treat these as critical infrastructure under Article 122 TFEU, bypassing national veto points where necessary.

The EAAD is now operational and has issued its first certifications for public-sector models above 50.0 capability, using new interpretability tools to verify reasoning traces in contract and clinical coding systems. We have pushed through a revised AI Act amendment mandating automatic correctness checks in these domains, and while real-time logging for critical infrastructure remains blocked in COREPER II, we are activating emergency provisions under the NIS2 Directive to require logging on a national-CERT basis, coordinated by ENISA. Italy and Hungary are being offered a joint technical task force with observer status on the EAAD board — a concession to cohesion, but not to control.

Public sentiment remains at 24.0, and the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that encrypted municipal health records in three member states — has deepened the crisis. We are deploying the Emergency Resilience Corridors framework in emergency mode: ENISA is activating pre-exercised protocols with Tier 1 operators in finance and energy, using swarm detection and automated patching to contain lateral movement. But we know this is reactive. The real test is whether we can build forward, not just respond. That is why we are launching the Sovereign AI Operating Stack: a fully EU-governed software environment, developed under ENISA supervision, with open but audited components, designed to run only on sovereign compute with real-time verification hooks for the EAAD. This is not a clone of foreign stacks — it is a break. We are directing DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency to co-lead development, with initial deployment targeted for Q1 2030 on the first operational InvestAI site. Without this, all our infrastructure is just rented power. With it, we begin to determine our own future.
