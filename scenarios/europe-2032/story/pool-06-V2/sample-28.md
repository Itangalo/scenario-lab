## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Audit Stack Initiative  
Because the failure to enforce real-time logging in COREPER II has exposed a fatal flaw: we can certify models, but we cannot verify their behaviour once deployed. This is not a delay—it is a breach. The cyber_major_incident this turn confirmed that unmonitored systems are attack vectors, and without a sovereign, technically credible audit stack, our assurance regime is performative. This measure closes the loop between evaluation and enforcement, and it is the only way to salvage trust in the EAAD before public sentiment collapses further.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment the Union confronts the gap between authority and actual control. The Secure Energy-Backed Compute Sovereignty programme has formally delivered its 5GW commitment on paper, but physical deployment remains partial—delayed by legal resistance and grid coordination failures. The EIB has reprofiled €30bn into nuclear microreactors in Brittany and geothermal integration in northern Sweden, but without sovereign audit, these sites risk becoming compute islands with no verifiable integrity. We are now pivoting from infrastructure promises to operational credibility.

The cyber_major_incident—a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health administrations in three member states—has made clear that capability without oversight is catastrophe in waiting. ENISA confirms the attack exploited a chain of open-weight tools refined through automated red-teaming, and the absence of real-time logging delayed detection by 72 hours. Italy and Hungary still block audit access, but we are no longer negotiating on principle: we are offering them co-development rights on the Sovereign Audit Stack, with hosting of the primary node in Trieste, and a fast-track seat on the EAAD technical council. The stack will be built on open standards, with mandatory deployment for any AI system accessing EU critical infrastructure, and funded through the EIB’s resilience window at €8.5bn over two years.

Public sentiment is now at 24.0, and the streets are filling with displaced professionals who see no protection, only acceleration. We cannot restore trust with promises of future compute. We must show that the Union can see what AI is doing, right now. The Sovereign Audit Stack is not optional—it is the minimum technical foundation for democratic oversight in an age of autonomous systems. We are tasking the EAAD and ENISA with joint delivery, under a new Article 114 TFEU legal base for AI system integrity, and we are preparing a Communication to the European Council in June that frames this not as a technical upgrade, but as a constitutional necessity: if we cannot audit power, we cannot constrain it.
