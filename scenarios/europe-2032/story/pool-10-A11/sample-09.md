### Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event crystallizes what has been structurally true for two years: our sovereignty is not a function of regulation or aspiration, but of access and operational control. Being cut off from the frontier model without recourse — while hospitals, courts, and infrastructure built on it falter — proves that preventing harm now requires securing capability, and that determining our own future means holding the instruments of intelligence, not just governing their use. This identity still holds, but its meaning has shifted: it can no longer sustain a balance between restraint and empowerment when one side of the scale is empty.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will act before the picture is clear, but only where sovereign sensing provides a minimal ground truth; otherwise, we risk compounding blindness with motion  
- Grounds: With political capital at zero and public sentiment collapsing, every action now risks backlash or irrelevance. The failure of the Emergency Response Corps and the delayed Behavioural Observatory show that acting under uncertainty without *any* reliable sensing is indistinguishable from reacting in panic. We retain the imperative to move early, but must now condition it: movement without telemetry is drift. Our commitment evolves not to retreat from urgency, but to tether it to the only sources of clarity we control — our own sensors, our own compute, our own analysis.

- retire `two_year_commitment`  
- Trigger: the measure **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory** has been launched and will finish on turn 12; the prior two-year commitment cycle ends with this turn  
- Grounds: The goal — secure sovereign AI situational awareness — remains valid, but the commitment as stated has been overtaken by events. Awareness is no longer merely a precondition for governance; it is now a survival threshold. The denial of frontier access mid-turn, with no warning and no appeal, transforms the calculus: awareness without control is vulnerability. We cannot govern what we do not possess. The next phase must be defined not by observation, but by possession and resilience.

- add `secure_sovereign_ai_control` (commitment): We will ensure the EU possesses and operates at least one AI system at the global frontier, under irrevocable legal and physical jurisdiction, as the non-negotiable foundation of strategic autonomy  
- Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event is not a temporary outage — it is a demonstration of fundamental dependency. When the most capable systems are unreachable by nationality, contract, or policy, no regulatory framework suffices. To determine our own future, we must run the models ourselves. This commitment accepts the cost: fiscal, political, industrial. But without it, all other measures are exercises in managing decline.

## In practice

We are activating Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Regulation framework to designate AI infrastructure as essential to Union survival, unlocking emergency procurement and state aid flexibilities. Under this authority, the Commission is directing the EuroHPC JU to repurpose 40% of existing exascale capacity — currently shared with commercial partners — into a sovereign AI training enclave, beginning immediately at LUMI (Finland), JUWELS (Germany), and Leonardo (Italy). This is not a new data centre — it is a reclamation of what we already fund, now hardened under EU-only access protocols and integrated with the newly established AI Security Directorate within ENISA.

Funding will come from a €12 billion reallocation: €6 billion from unused REPowerEU allocations, €4 billion from the Digital Europe Programme’s dormant infrastructure lines, and €2 billion in emergency issuance backed by the Multiannual Financial Framework review, to be ratified in June. We are negotiating binding site agreements with host states to prohibit foreign jurisdictional claims on systems trained here, modeled on nuclear energy safeguards. In parallel, we are launching a targeted recruitment drive through the EU AI Talent Corridor, offering triple salaries and fast-track citizenship to 200 core engineers and researchers currently working at frontier labs outside the EU, particularly those in jurisdictions showing instability or alignment with adversarial powers.

Resistance is expected from two member states hosting shared cloud zones and from the European Parliament’s Budget Committee, which questions the legality of unilateral repurposing. We are countering with guarantees: first, that civilian climate and health workloads will be restored once new capacity comes online; second, that this cluster will eventually serve all member states under a unified access protocol. But we are not waiting for consensus. The denial of access was not negotiated — neither will our response be. This is not duplication. It is deterrence.
