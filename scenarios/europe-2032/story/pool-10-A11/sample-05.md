### Statement changes

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI situational awareness as the precondition for effective governance and resilience  
- Grounds: The two-year commitment period has concluded, but its core objective remains valid. However, the failure to complete the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory by now—despite urgent need—reveals that "situational awareness" is no longer sufficient as a standalone goal. We are not merely behind on sensing; we are losing agency. The denial of frontier access this turn proves that sovereignty cannot rest on observation alone. Awareness must now be coupled with *autonomous capacity*—the ability to act without permission. This modification preserves continuity while sharpening intent: from seeing clearly to acting independently.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer tolerate blindness as a permanent condition  
- Grounds: The emergent_synthetic_leaks_wave and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event have demonstrated that acting under uncertainty is now indistinguishable from acting in strategic vulnerability. Our institutions are reactive, fragmented, and outpaced. We must still move fast—but we must also build the technical depth to reduce epistemic dependence on external actors whose incentives do not align with ours. This change does not slow us; it redirects urgency toward building internal truth-making capacity.

## In practice

The Sentinel Nodes have completed deployment at Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna. Final integration reports confirm telemetry ingestion from 87% of designated high-risk licensed systems in energy and transport, though gaps remain in municipal administration due to delayed ENISA compliance audits. The nodes now autonomously flag unauthorised model replication events with 91% precision in test environments. Their completion frees marginal technical bandwidth, which we are redirecting immediately into the Sovereign Inference Grid.

We are launching the Grid under Article 114 TFEU with emergency invocation powers granted by the Council’s Competitiveness Formation, citing systemic risk to essential services. Funding will draw €4.2 billion from a reprioritised Digital Europe Programme tranche, augmented by a 2031 Q2 special issuance of EU bonds guaranteed under the SURE mechanism, reactivated for strategic digital infrastructure. Physical rollout begins at four EuroHPC sites with existing classified compute enclaves: Jülich, Barcelona, Luxembourg, and Sofia. These will host the first wave of sovereign inference clusters, hardened against remote override and governed by a joint board of the European Commission, ENISA, and national security authorities.

Recruitment for the Behavioural Observatory continues under fast-track Horizon Europe rules, but we are restructuring its mission: it will no longer rely on external model access for red-teaming. Instead, it will generate adversarial probes using internally hosted surrogates trained on behavioural traces harvested by the Sentinel Nodes. This pivot—from direct analysis to indirect emulation—is forced by necessity, but it may prove more robust in the long run. Still, there is no substitute for real capability. That is why the Inference Grid is not optional. Without it, every future measure becomes conditional on goodwill we no longer have reason to expect.
