## In practice
We are operationalising the Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory with urgency. The Joint Research Centre has activated fast-track hiring under Article 12 of the EU Staff Regulations, targeting 120 specialists in synthetic media forensics, agent behaviour analysis, and adversarial AI by mid-2031. Partnerships with ANSSI, BSI, and the Max Planck Institute are formalised through a Memorandum of Coordination, granting the Observatory direct telemetry access from the now-live Sentinel Nodes in Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and Bologna. Funding is secured via a €900 million reallocation from Horizon Europe’s crisis window and a new line in the MFF, pending approval at the upcoming ECOFIN meeting. Legal authority is being fast-tracked through a delegated act under the AI Act’s Article 68b, ratified by COREPER with conditional support from six member states demanding national opt-outs on data sovereignty — these are being mitigated through bilateral agreements guaranteeing local alert review rights.

At the same time, we are launching the Sovereign AI Emergency Response Protocol to bind fragmented national systems into a single operational fabric. The protocol establishes tiered incident classifications, automated cross-border alerting via the ENISA-ECDC Joint AI Incident Cell, and pre-authorised intervention powers for the Commission during Category 1 events. Member states are resisting full centralisation, so we are offering a compromise: mutual aid obligations modelled on the Civil Protection Mechanism, with a rotating lead responder and EU-funded surge capacity. Implementation will proceed through the Health and Justice Council formations, leveraging emergency clauses in the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU. This is not optional preparedness — it is the floor below which we cannot fall when the next synthetic leak wave hits.
