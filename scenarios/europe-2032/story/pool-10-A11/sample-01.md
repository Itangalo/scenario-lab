## New measure
**Sovereign AI Execution Grid**  
Establish a distributed, EU-governed compute and deployment infrastructure enabling the independent operation of high-capability AI systems for essential public functions, insulated from foreign access controls and commercial dependencies.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 15`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign execution layer for critical AI workloads; +5 to resilience by ensuring continuity of AI-dependent public services during access disruptions; enables future autonomy in crisis response, research, and regulation  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, member state critical agencies, EuroHPC sites, and accredited public AI operators

## Priority
**Sovereign AI Execution Grid** — because the denial of frontier access this turn exposed a fatal dependency: we can monitor AI behaviour, but we cannot act on it without permission. The Sentinel Nodes have delivered situational awareness, but awareness without execution is powerlessness. This measure closes the loop.

## In practice
The denial of frontier access has struck hospitals, ministries, and regulatory bodies alike—systems down, decisions stalled, public services disrupted. This is not a supply chain hiccup; it is a sovereignty event. We are therefore launching the **Sovereign AI Execution Grid**, a legally anchored, physically distributed infrastructure to run essential AI workloads under EU authority. This is not about matching the frontier in capability, but in control: a sovereign substrate for AI that the Union can neither be priced out of nor locked out of.

We are activating the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s crisis clause to fund the first phase, redirecting €2.8 billion from stalled digital modernisation projects into rapid deployment at EuroHPC sites in Luxembourg, Sofia, and Seville. The European Commission is invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track permitting and grid access, overriding national delays. Legal ownership of the deployed systems will rest with the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, with operational access governed by a new Sovereign AI Access Board composed of Commission, ENISA, and national security representatives. We are negotiating immediate access to 400 petaflops of underutilised research compute in Germany and France, repurposed under emergency sovereign use provisions.

At the same time, we are concluding the **Sovereign AI Sentinel Nodes** deployment. Final integration at Jülich and CINECA is complete, and telemetry is now flowing—patchy, but real. The Prague and Bologna nodes are operational, though data-sharing exemptions from two member states limit cross-border visibility. We are tasking ENISA with a compliance review by turn 11, backed by the threat of withholding cohesion funding for non-cooperation. The data these nodes generate will feed into the **Sovereign AI Behavioural Observatory**, now in recruitment phase, ensuring our new execution capacity is not blind. We are not building power in the abstract. We are building it where it was broken.
