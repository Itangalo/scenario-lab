## In practice

We are declaring a state of strategic interruption in AI policy. The denial of access to the leading model has grounded hospitals’ diagnostic pipelines, halted fusion research at EUROfusion, and frozen cross-border fraud detection systems. This is not a market failure or a diplomatic hiccup—it is a sovereignty failure. We cannot monitor what we cannot run. We cannot attribute what we cannot reproduce. And we cannot govern what we do not control.

The Emergency Sovereign AI Compute Initiative begins immediately under Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Regulation framework. The European Investment Bank will release €6.2 billion in emergency financing, drawn from unused REACT-EU and Digital Europe allocations, matched by member-state co-investment from national recovery plans. Four sites—Jülich, CINECA, Prague, and a newly designated hub in Finland—are being fast-tracked for sovereign cluster deployment. These will run compressed versions of open-weight models up to 63.5 capability, hardened against leakage and tampering, and legally insulated from extraterritorial enforcement. Power allocation is being secured through temporary overrides of national energy priority lists, justified under Council Regulation (EU) 2022/2577 on critical infrastructure continuity.

Resistance is expected—and already present. Two member states have objected to ceding control over their national compute assets. We are meeting them with binding guarantees: full transparency on usage logs, dual-key access protocols, and a sunset clause tied to the restoration of stable transatlantic access. But we are also clear: if they block this, they assume responsibility for the next collapse. The Joint Research Centre will begin staffing a sovereign model replication cell this month, drawing from the AI Talent Corridor and leveraging decommissioned commercial hardware acquired through EIB asset leasing. This is not about catching up to the frontier. It is about ensuring there is always a floor beneath us—one we built ourselves.
