## In practice
We are declaring an emergency exception under Article 122 of the TFEU, invoking the energy-security precedent to unlock immediate funding and bypass procurement delays for the Emergency Sovereignty Corridor. The European Commission, in coordination with the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking and DG CNECT, is re-tasking 40% of existing Tier-1 supercomputing capacity—currently underutilised due to the U.S. investment collapse—into a dedicated sovereign AI enclave. This includes JUWELS at Jülich and Leonardo at CINECA, now placed under direct operational control of the Joint Research Centre’s new AI Security Division. Legal anchoring is being secured through a Council Implementing Decision under the Single Market Emergency Instrument, with a sunset clause tied to the restoration of stable frontier access.

At the same time, we are launching an emergency tender for 50,000 next-generation AI accelerators under the newly activated Crisis Compute Procurement Framework, funded through a €3.8 billion reallocation from the Digital Europe Programme and the unused portion of the Innovation Union budget. The first shipments will be directed to Prague, Bologna, and a newly secured site near Helsinki, co-located with hardened energy and cooling infrastructure. National governments are being offered binding commitments: in exchange for waiving local permitting delays and data sovereignty objections, they will receive guaranteed access slots for national security and public health applications. ENISA is drafting the access control architecture to ensure compliance with EU fundamental rights standards, even under emergency conditions.

This is not a replacement for the Behavioural Observatory—it is its foundation. Without sovereign compute, we are analysing shadows cast by systems we do not control. Now, we begin building the light.
