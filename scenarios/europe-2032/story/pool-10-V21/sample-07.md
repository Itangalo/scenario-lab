## In practice  
The Sovereign AI Core is operational. Audit-verified compute clusters in Brittany and northern Sweden are running under dedicated energy grids, disconnected from foreign hyperscalers, and hosting EAAD’s real-time evaluations and MPAC’s shared sandbox. The final €30bn EIB tranche has cleared, securing 18 exaF-days under Union control. The Real-Time Logging Mandate flagged the hospital cyberattack within hours, and MPAC’s nascent alert channel enabled a coordinated response—proof that our infrastructure is not just built, but *alive*. The Commission invoked Article 122 TFEU to override German and Austrian objections, treating this as emergency-critical infrastructure; their technical teams are now embedded in the oversight board, and ENTSO-E has stabilised integration through dynamic load isolation.

With the MPAC framework now complete, we pivot to the next frontier: legitimacy. The emergent Supply Chain Pact—led by South Korea, the Netherlands, Finland, and Taiwan—confirms that the middle powers are aligning on safety-conditioned access to compute and components. The EU must now act as a core node in this network, not just a consumer of its benefits. But we cannot lead a coalition of responsible powers while our own citizens see AI as an engine of erasure. That is why this turn we launch the EU AI Labour Transition Accelerator: a €22bn programme co-funded by the ESF+ and national governments, targeting 500,000 displaced workers in law, accounting, and software over the next 18 months. It combines wage insurance for those in phased transitions, fast-track digital and green skills retraining through Erasmus Pro and national upskilling hubs, and employer incentives to redeploy rather than replace. The Council’s Employment formation will lead negotiations, with implementation tied to the Recovery and Resilience Facility disbursements—making AI adjustment a condition of funding, not an afterthought. Without this, the Core may be sovereign, but the Union will not be.
