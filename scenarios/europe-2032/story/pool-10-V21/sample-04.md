## In practice  
We are operationalising sovereignty. The Core is live, but its value turns on use: we are now mandating, through delegated acts under the AI Act’s Article 28b, that all AI systems supporting health, energy, and emergency response in the EU must run on sovereign stacks by turn 12. ENISA is drafting compliance thresholds, and the Commission is preparing infringement proceedings against any member state that routes critical workloads through external providers. Brittany and northern Sweden are now live nodes, but we are also activating secondary sites in Finland and Romania to ensure redundancy. The EIB’s bond release has secured compute, but the real test is whether we can enforce jurisdiction over it—so we are embedding EAAD auditors directly into core operations, with real-time logging now mandatory for any model above 60.0 capability running on EU soil.

Internationally, MPAC hangs in the balance. The Council is divided: France and the Nordic states push for strong mutual obligations, while Germany fears overreach and the Netherlands demands reciprocity on chip exports. We are negotiating through COREPER II this week, offering market access for compliance with joint safety baselines and threatening exclusion from future compute-sharing pacts for defection. Japan’s intent to join export controls is real, but only if we formalise by June. Norway’s Arctic co-location offer is contingent on joint command protocols, and South Korea’s mutual recognition hinges on EAAD accepting their eval standards. We are not building an alliance—we are assembling one, deal by deal, concession by concession. The cyberattack proved the threat is live, but the window to bind it closed is narrow. If we do not close MPAC this turn, the emergent_supply_chain_pact will become the default coalition, and we will be locked out.
