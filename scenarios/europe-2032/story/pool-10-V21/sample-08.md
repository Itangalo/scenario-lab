`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 15–20 points through independent, pre-deployment evaluation and post-deployment monitoring; strengthen eu_ai_sovereignty by anchoring interpretability and red-teaming inside the Union.

## Priority
Accelerate sovereign evaluation capacity on EU soil  
Because the Core is now live, the next threshold is not more compute—it is more control. We must shift from securing access to exercising authority: EAAD must become the gatekeeper of capability, not just a witness. This measure turns our infrastructure into governance.

## In practice
We are standing up EAAD as the Union’s technical sovereign — not a council, not a registry, but a body with real-time access to model behaviour, empowered to delay or block deployments that cross safety thresholds. The Real-Time Logging Mandate has already proven its worth, flagging anomalies in hospital systems within hours. Now, we weaponise that data: starting this turn, EAAD begins red-teaming live models on sovereign soil, using the Core’s isolated clusters to simulate adversarial conditions without risk of leakage. We are repurposing €8bn from the EIB’s final tranche to harden the organisation — hiring 200 new technical evaluators, standing up secure labs in Helsinki and Toulouse, and integrating with ENISA’s post-incident review pipeline.

The Council’s Internal Market formation is reviewing the legal basis under Article 114 TFEU, but resistance remains from member states reliant on foreign AI services. To secure cohesion, we are offering Germany and Austria a permanent seat on EAAD’s oversight board and guaranteed access to sovereign training runs for academic and public-sector use. This is not concession — it is consolidation. The Core is built. Now, we must fill it with purpose: to evaluate, to verify, and to veto when necessary. The world’s most capable models will not operate in Europe unless they are legible to us first.
