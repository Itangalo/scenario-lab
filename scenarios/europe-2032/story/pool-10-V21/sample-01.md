## In practice  
We are moving fast to convert the Core’s technical sovereignty into strategic weight. The Commission has convened an emergency JHA-INDU-COMP trio meeting under Article 218 TFEU to authorise accession talks with the Supply Chain Pact, treating it as a critical infrastructure alignment rather than a trade agreement—this avoids requiring unanimity, but still demands buy-in from Germany and Poland, who fear dependency on Taiwan. We are offering both a seat on the Pact’s technical steering board and guaranteed access to 3 exaF-days of EU sovereign compute for joint red-teaming in return for support. The European External Action Service is deploying envoys to Seoul, Helsinki, and Taipei this week, backed by a €2bn EIB liquidity window to co-finance early procurement under the new framework.

Domestically, EAAD is integrating the Pact’s safety baseline into its real-time logging engine, so that any model accessing EU markets—directly or via third parties—will be checked against shared interpretability and anomaly thresholds. This turns our internal safeguards into an external standard. The cyberattack on hospital systems proved that open-weight models are now vectors of strategic risk, and the Pact’s mutual licensing regime lets us block unsafe designs at the source. We are not waiting for consensus: the Core gives us leverage, and this turn we use it to shape the rules of the game, not just play by them.
