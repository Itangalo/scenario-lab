## New measure
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**  
A binding directive requiring all operators of essential services in energy, health, transport and digital infrastructure to implement AI-resistant intrusion detection, failover protocols, and red-teaming exercises modelled on known automated attack patterns.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +10 resilience by turn 7 through mandatory hardening of critical systems against AI-enabled cyber threats; reduces brittleness exposed by cyber_major_incident; begins to close the gap between offensive capability and defensive readiness  
`Applies to:` National regulators, ENISA, essential service providers across all member states, and EU-level incident coordination bodies

## Priority
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** — because the cyber_major_incident has revealed that our defences are structurally unprepared for automated, model-generated attacks, and without immediate action to compel hardening, every other investment — in sovereignty, evaluation, or industrial capacity — risks being rendered irrelevant by cascading systemic failure.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the operational launch of the European Evaluation Shield, delivering its first confidential findings to the Commission and national security authorities. Though the legal challenge looms, we proceed with the agency’s mandate under Article 114 TFEU and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, treating the pilot audits as urgent risk assessments rather than full enforcement actions. We secure limited data-sharing agreements with two developers under strict non-disclosure, focusing on agent persistence and memory leakage in long-horizon planning systems approaching 60.0 capability. The findings will inform a classified annex to the upcoming AI Safety Report, due before the end of the turn, which will assess whether current safeguards can meaningfully constrain autonomous systems.

Simultaneously, we advance the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield through the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO), backed by a rapid risk assessment from ENISA that maps exposure across 120 essential service operators in energy, health, and transport. Germany and Poland remain cautious, citing fiscal strain and subsidiarity concerns, so we offer conditional access to InvestAI Gigafactories co-funding and liability shielding under the AI Liability Directive for early adopters. We also commit €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to co-finance red-team tooling and detection R&D, with delivery milestones tied to ENISA’s publication of standardised attack scenarios by mid-turn. The directive will be negotiated under comitology with a fast-track clause, aiming for provisional application by turn 7 in anticipation of full adoption. This is not optional resilience — it is the baseline for sovereignty in a world where AI can weaponise interdependence faster than we can patch it.
