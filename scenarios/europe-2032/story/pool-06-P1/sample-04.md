## New measure
**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield**  
A binding directive requiring all operators of essential services in energy, health, transport and digital infrastructure to implement AI-resistant intrusion detection, failover protocols, and red-teaming exercises modelled on known automated attack patterns.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 7`

**Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield** — because the cyber_major_incident has revealed that our defences are structurally unprepared for automated, model-generated attacks, and without immediate action to compel hardening, every other investment — in sovereignty, evaluation, or industrial capacity — risks being rendered irrelevant by cascading systemic failure.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the operational closure of the European Evaluation Shield, with its pilot audits completed and the first confidential findings delivered to the Commission. Though the agency’s legal authority is now under formal challenge, the technical cooperation achieved with two frontier developers demonstrates that independent evaluation is feasible under strict safeguards. We publish a redacted summary of the audit methodology and announce that the full public safety report will be released in early turn 7, conditional on the Court’s stance on the ongoing IP case. In parallel, we initiate triage talks with the European Court of Justice registry to clarify the legal pathway for pre-deployment oversight in light of emergent_eval_resistance, preparing fallback positions that could reframe inspection powers under existing product safety law.

Simultaneously, we launch negotiations on the Critical Infrastructure Cyber Shield in the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Council (IMCO), using Article 114 TFEU as the legal basis for harmonised cybersecurity standards. ENISA is tasked with delivering standardised red-team scenarios by the end of the turn, drawing on the forensic analysis of the hospital ransomware attack. We offer InvestAI Gigafactories fast-tracking and extended liability shields under the AI Liability Directive to member states that commit to early adoption, targeting Germany and Poland with tailored risk assessments showing their energy and health grids as high-priority exposure points. The directive will mandate annual cross-border cyber resilience exercises starting in turn 7, funded through the Digital Europe Programme and coordinated via the newly activated EU Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (CyCLONe).

The Tech Sovereignty Package advances through the Council, with Ireland and Germany now conditionally supportive due to the leverage offered through the Cyber Shield incentives. However, concerns persist over the pace of permitting reform, and we convene a special session of the Competitiveness Council to address local opposition to data centre siting, linking approval timelines to access to sovereign compute benefits. With political capital tight at 29.0 and public sentiment stagnant, every instrument must pull double duty: defence is sovereignty, resilience is credibility, and action — even contested action — is the only way to reclaim agency.
