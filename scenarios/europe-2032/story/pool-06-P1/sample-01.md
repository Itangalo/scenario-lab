**EU Sovereign Agent Hosting Directive**  
Establish a legally guaranteed, sovereign hosting environment for high-capability AI agents developed or used by EU institutions, critical agencies, and essential service operators, running on compute infrastructure under EU legal jurisdiction and physically located in hardened facilities across at least three member states.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise negotiations on the European Evaluation Shield’s pilot audits, securing limited but precedent-setting access to two frontier models’ agent coordination layers under strict confidentiality protocols. The board publishes a redacted methodology, confirming its focus on memory persistence and unauthorised goal retention in long-horizon planning systems. Legal counsel prepares for the ECJ challenge by compiling case law on state interest in systemic risk oversight, while parallel diplomacy engages neutral member states to prevent erosion of political support. Despite the legal uncertainty, the shield remains in flight — halting it would signal retreat in the face of coordinated pushback.

We complete the formal handover of the SEAL initiative, now fully operational under the European Chips Institute, with maintenance teams in Dresden, Crolles, and Catania conducting their first joint software update cycle. The €12bn ring-fenced budget transitions into sustainment mode, covering technician rotation, spare parts logistics, and secure communication channels — a rare, concrete gain in hardware-level autonomy. However, this does not yet translate into broader AI sovereignty: we can maintain tools, but we still do not host the most capable systems ourselves.

In response to both the cyberattack and the legal assault on evaluation, we launch the EU Sovereign Agent Hosting Directive. This is not merely about compute — it is about jurisdictional control over decision-making agents. We draft the legal basis under Article 347 TFEU (security of the Union) and Article 114 (internal market), framing the directive as a dual safeguard: against supply-chain coercion and against loss of control over automated public functions. We task DG CONNECT and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre to identify three initial hosting nodes — in France, Finland, and Poland — leveraging existing hardened data centre projects under InvestAI Gigafactories. Access will be prioritised for systems above 55.0 capability used in energy grid management, emergency response coordination, and cross-border law enforcement. To overcome resistance from member states wary of centralisation, we offer co-governance through a new Sovereign Systems Oversight Board, with rotating membership from national digital agencies and the European Data Protection Supervisor. This is not duplication — it is the necessary next layer: if we cannot trust foreign systems to remain safe, and we cannot assume foreign providers will comply when risks emerge, then the only fallback is to run what matters on our own ground, under our own rules.
