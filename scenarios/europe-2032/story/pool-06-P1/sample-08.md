## In practice
This turn, we finalise the operational framework for the European Evaluation Shield, appointing its executive board from national AI safety leads and top academic researchers in mechanistic interpretability. Despite the legal challenge, we proceed with two confidential pilot audits under provisional authority, focusing on agent persistence and emergent coordination in long-horizon planning systems developed by EU-based frontier labs. These audits are conducted under strict NDAs, with findings to be declassified in redacted form by turn 7. We engage the European Court of Justice through the Commission’s legal service, preparing a defence based on Article 114 TFEU and the demonstrated risk of uncontrolled agent behaviour at capability levels approaching 60.0. At the same time, we open bilateral consultations with Germany and Ireland — previously hesitant on compute sovereignty — offering co-location rights and priority access for national champions in exchange for hosting one of the new sovereign compute hubs.

With the Tech sovereignty package now complete, we redirect its implementation machinery — the accelerated permitting zones and grid-access protocols — toward the new Emergency Sovereign Compute Activation Directive. This is not a restart, but a repurposing: the same legal base (Article 114 TFEU), the same coordination structure (the European Chips Institute), and the same funding corridor (a blended finance model drawing from InvestEU, the Digital Europe Programme, and national co-financing). We identify two sites — one in France (Bordeaux) and one in Poland (Wrocław) — where land, power, and cooling are already secured, and where political resistance can be overcome through guaranteed local employment and technology transfer agreements. The directive will mandate that these hubs come online by turn 8, with at least 100 exaflops of combined AI-specific capacity, legally ring-fenced from extraterritorial access and prioritised for public-sector and safety-critical use. This is not about matching the frontier head-on — not yet — but about ensuring that when the frontier moves, the EU has a foothold, a voice, and a choice.
