## New measure
**Mandatory Red-Teaming and Failover Protocols for Essential Services**  
A binding directive requiring all operators of essential services in energy, health, transport, and digital infrastructure to conduct biannual red-teaming exercises using AI-generated attack vectors and to implement verified failover systems capable of sustaining core functions during sustained cyber disruption.

## In practice
This turn, we finalise the operational mandate of the European Evaluation Shield, publishing its first set of audit protocols for AI systems above 50.0 capability. Despite the emergent_eval_resistance legal challenge, we proceed with two confidential pilot evaluations at frontier labs — one in France, one in Germany — focusing on agent persistence and goal drift in long-horizon planning systems. The executive board, composed of national AI safety leads and academic experts in mechanistic interpretability, is granted provisional access under Article 16 TFEU with strict data handling safeguards. We also release a public consultation on the scope of the agency’s powers, inviting member states to affirm the necessity of weight inspections in high-risk cases, ahead of a Council vote in turn 7 that will determine its long-term legitimacy.

Simultaneously, we act on the hospital ransomware attack by launching a binding directive on red-teaming and failover protocols for essential services. Using Article 114 TFEU as legal basis, we draft a directive requiring all operators of essential services to conduct biannual red-teaming exercises modelled on known AI-driven attack patterns, with scenarios developed by ENISA and validated against open intelligence on offensive tooling. Failover systems must be tested under stress conditions and certified by national cybersecurity agencies. To secure early buy-in, we offer fast-track access to InvestAI Gigafactories funding and liability shielding under the AI Liability Directive for compliant operators. Germany and Poland, initially hesitant, are engaged through joint technical assessments showing their energy and health grids among the most exposed to cascading dependency failures.

We also complete the handover of the Secure European Access to Advanced Lithography (SEAL) initiative, now fully operational as a Joint Undertaking under the European Chips Institute. The €12bn ring-fenced fund is activated, with technician rotations and software update protocols in place across Dresden, Crolles, and Catania. This marks a rare consolidation of industrial sovereignty — but its value is now directly challenged by the fragility of the systems that will run on the chips it secures. That is why resilience is no longer secondary: it is the condition of sovereignty. Without hardened infrastructure, sovereign compute is just faster collapse.
