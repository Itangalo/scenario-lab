## New measure
**Measure:** Launch Binding Redress Mechanism for Harmful AI Decisions  
Create a pan-EU legal pathway for individuals and communities harmed by algorithmic decisions in public services to receive reparations, corrective action, and public explanation within 90 days, backed by an independent AI Accountability Tribunal with power to compel data access, suspend systems, and order audits.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Binding Redress Mechanism for Harmful AI Decisions  
This is now our dominant effort because the automated_decision_scandal has shattered the premise that technical control alone could rebuild trust — we must now prove the system corrects itself when it fails, or lose all claim to legitimacy.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment when governance failure became legally codified — not through protest or delay, but through a formal finding that an AI system caused systemic harm. That judgment changes everything. The Public Trust Corps was designed to make incident response visible; it cannot make injustice undone. So we are pivoting hard toward redress.

The **Binding Redress Mechanism** will be proposed under Article 16 TFEU and the Charter of Fundamental Rights, with legislative home in the European Parliament’s LIBE and PETI committees. We are aligning with the European Ombudsman’s office to repurpose its backlog into pilot cases, starting with the social insurance scandal. Funding — €4.2bn over two years — will come from reallocating stalled digital innovation grants and leveraging cohesion policy adjustment clauses. The Accountability Tribunal will be hosted by the European Judicial Training Network, with rotating judges from member states and technical assessors drawn from ENISA and the newly accredited red-teaming labs.

Meanwhile, the **Public Trust Corps** continues under DG HOME, but its mission is narrowing: it will now serve the Redress Mechanism, not just general transparency. Its mobile units in Lyon and Warsaw will shift from conducting drills to hosting community hearings — collecting testimonies, mapping harm clusters, and feeding them into the Tribunal’s docket. The AI Incident Dashboard will add a new module: “Active Redress Cases,” showing live claims, status, and outcomes. This is no longer about proving we see the threat — it’s about proving we answer to the people.

We are facing resistance from several member states — especially Poland and Hungary — who argue this undermines administrative sovereignty. To overcome it, we are offering opt-in phasing with peer-review incentives: early adopters will gain preferential access to resilience funding and faster dispute resolution in EU courts. This is not coercion — it is a bargain: legitimacy in exchange for leadership.
