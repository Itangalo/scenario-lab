## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because public sentiment at 18.0 has become the binding constraint on every other action: without credible, visible response capacity, no technical measure — not even real-time reporting — can land with legitimacy, and no infrastructure can be defended if people refuse to believe it exists.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment when the cost of opacity becomes unbearable. The automated decision scandal — a court-confirmed case of AI-driven denials in social insurance in Bavaria — has lit a fuse. It did not just expose a flawed model; it confirmed the public’s deepest fear: that decisions shaping their lives are being made by systems no one can explain, challenge, or stop. Restriction is now politically cheap, but so is inaction — and we cannot afford either. The Trust Corps must now prove it can act faster than outrage spreads.

The Corps is operational in skeleton form: DG HOME has seconded 40 crisis response officers, and DG CONNECT has secured hosting for the AI Incident Dashboard on sovereign cloud infrastructure in Finland. But integration with national CERTs remains partial — France and Italy have agreed to real-time data sharing; Poland and Spain still resist, citing national sovereignty. We are offering them co-ownership of the dashboard’s front-end design and a seat on the Trust Corps’ steering committee to secure buy-in. In Lyon, the first transparency drill will simulate a hospital AI failure next month — not a scripted demo, but a live red-team intrusion followed by public debrief. If we cannot show people we see the threat, we will not keep their trust.

Meanwhile, the new reporting mandate is being drafted under Article 12 of the AI Act as amended, with enforcement tied to access to the EU Cyber Shield and emergency funding. The EIB will make incident reporting compliance a condition for digital transition loans. Industry pushback is expected — especially from German utilities and French rail — so we are pairing the mandate with liability shielding for good-faith reporting and funding for automated logging tools. This is not just about data; it is about creating a reflex of disclosure where silence once ruled. The anomalies in AI reasoning patterns abroad remind us: we will not see the next failure coming unless someone is required to speak.
