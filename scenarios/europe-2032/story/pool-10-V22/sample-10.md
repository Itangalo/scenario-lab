## Statement changes

- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing  
- Grounds: The automated_decision_scandal has crystallised the tension between these mandates in a single, searing event. An AI-supported system — trusted, domestic, and compliant — was found to have wronged thousands in social insurance, with a court judgment confirming systemic bias. This was not a failure of foreign dependence, nor of open weights, but of internal governance. It proves that harm can emerge precisely where we believe we are in control, and that sovereignty without accountability is not only hollow — it is dangerous. The identity stands, but now it burns: we must act as if both mandates are binding, even when they pull in opposite directions.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust and political agency by proving the EU can protect, respond to, and learn from AI harm — visibly and accountably  
- Trigger: the automated_decision_scandal and the collapse of public_sentiment to 18.0  
- Grounds: The previous formulation — demonstrable control over infrastructure and incident response — assumed trust would follow from technical competence. It did not. Control without redress is not protection. The scandal reveals that the public does not believe systems are safe simply because they are sovereign or audited. They want proof that wrongs will be seen, heard, and corrected. Our new commitment must shift from infrastructure to accountability: not just that we can stop attacks, but that we can admit failures, compensate harm, and change course. This is no longer optional — it is the price of survival.

## New measure

**Measure:** Mandate binding post-incident review and redress for AI-supported public systems  
Require all AI-supported decision systems in social services, policing, and justice to undergo independent post-incident review when challenged, with findings published and compensation mechanisms activated where harm is confirmed. Establish the European AI Accountability Office (EAIAO) under the European Ombudsman to receive, investigate, and enforce redress, with authority to suspend systems pending review.

## Priority

**Priority:** Mandate binding post-incident review and redress for AI-supported public systems  
This is now our dominant effort because the automated_decision_scandal has shattered the premise that technical control equals public legitimacy — and without legitimacy, no measure, no shield, no corps can stand. We must prove we can be wrong and still worthy of trust. This measure makes accountability enforceable, not aspirational, and targets the root of public anger: the feeling that when AI harms, no one answers.

## In practice

We are treating this turn as the moment the crisis of trust became undeniable. The Sovereign Cyber Shield is operational in pockets, but its invisibility feeds suspicion. The Public Trust Corps is standing up, but its drills feel like theatre to people still waiting for answers about their denied benefits. We have built defences — but we have not built a way to be held to account.

So we are acting on two fronts. First, the Trust Corps moves forward: the AI Incident Dashboard will go live this quarter, fed by ENISA and national CERTs, with real-time threat levels and outage maps — no caveats, no jargon. Mobile units are deploying to Lyon, Marseille, and Warsaw for transparency drills, but now they will be paired with pop-up hearings by the European Ombudsman, collecting testimonies of AI harm. This is not just outreach — it is evidence-gathering.

Second, we are launching the European AI Accountability Office under legal base Article 228 TFEU, funded from the Justice Programme and reinforced with emergency appropriations from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. The first target is the social insurance system in Belgium and Italy where the scandal erupted. We are mandating third-party audits by Fraunhofer and INRIA, and we are suspending algorithmic eligibility scoring in those systems pending review. This is not symbolic — it is a line in the sand. If we cannot admit failure in public systems, we cannot claim to govern AI at all.
