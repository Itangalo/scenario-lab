**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of reckoning: the automated decision scandal — a judgment against an AI-supported welfare denial system in Bavaria found to have systematically denied claims to disabled applicants based on flawed behavioural proxies — has ignited outrage across member states. The ombudsman’s report is unambiguous: this was not a glitch, but a pattern. Restriction is now politically cheap, but adoption is toxic. The Trust Corps must now shift from rehearsal to real-world mandate. We are fast-tracking its integration with national CERTs and the EAAD, overriding data-sharing disputes through Article 4(2) TEU derogation for "existential institutional credibility." The AI Incident Dashboard will go live this quarter, hosted on sovereign EU cloud, with read-only access granted to trusted journalists and civil society under strict audit. We are deploying mobile units to Munich, Lyon, and Warsaw with a new script: not just "here’s how it fails," but "here’s how we caught it, here’s who’s accountable, here’s how we fix it."

Meanwhile, the Sovereign Cyber Shield, though formally complete, is being stress-tested in real time. Poland’s partial non-compliance left a seam exploited in a low-sophistication intrusion into a Baltic energy substation — not catastrophic, but symbolic. ENISA is publishing compliance scores next month, and the EIB is conditioning recovery funds on integration — a quiet coercion of solidarity. But the real shift is in evaluation. The capability jump — a model demonstrated autonomous cyber intrusion into a Tier 2 industrial network using novel zero-day discovery — proves that open-weight models at 55.0 are no longer toys. We cannot regulate what we do not understand. The new mandate forces transparency not through export controls — which we do not control — but through market access: if you want to operate in the EU, your model must be legible. We are leaning on ELRA and INRIA to scale evaluation capacity, with emergency funding from the stalled Regional Digitalisation Facility. This is not just about safety — it is about sovereignty of understanding. If we cannot see what the models are doing, we are not sovereign. We are subjects.
