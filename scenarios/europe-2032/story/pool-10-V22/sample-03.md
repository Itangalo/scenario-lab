### Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because the **automated_decision_scandal** has deepened the crisis of legitimacy — but only where the Trust Corps already provides the operational backbone for response, communication, and visibility. Its mobile units are now the only credible channel to demonstrate that the Union can detect failure, speak clearly about it, and coordinate recovery. Without this visible presence, the new audit tribunal would be seen as another layer of bureaucracy, not redemption.

### In practice
We are treating this turn as the moment when AI governance ceased being a technical project and became a constitutional one. The automated decision scandal — confirmed by a national ombudsman and upheld by regional courts — revealed that a supposedly neutral algorithm in Belgium’s unemployment benefits system denied claims en masse based on flawed mobility predictions. Thousands were wrongly sanctioned. Appeals took months. Some never recovered. This was not an edge case. It was policy by proxy. And it shattered what little trust remained.

The **Public Trust Corps** is now our central instrument of repair. We have fast-tracked its deployment in Brussels, Antwerp, and Strasbourg — cities directly affected by the scandal. Mobile units are conducting emergency transparency drills focused on social services AI, using simplified interfaces to show how decisions are made, where data comes from, and how appeals are processed. For the first time, these drills include simulated redress scenarios — citizens role-play filing complaints, and watch how the system responds. ENISA and the Fundamental Rights Agency are embedding liaison officers into the Corps to ensure findings feed directly into enforcement.

Meanwhile, the **Binding Audit & Redress Mechanism** is being drafted under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the Digital Services Act’s accountability framework. We are proposing it as a regulation with direct effect, so no member state can opt out. The tribunal will have power to subpoena model logs, demand source code inspection, and order financial restitution. Funding will come from a 5% levy on all high-impact public AI procurement contracts going forward. Germany and Finland support it; Poland objects, calling it “judicial overreach,” but we are offering them co-chairmanship of the scientific advisory panel to secure buy-in. This is not just about fixing errors — it is about proving that power answers to people. If we cannot do that, nothing else matters.
