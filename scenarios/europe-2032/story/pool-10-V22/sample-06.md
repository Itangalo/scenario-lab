- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore political agency and public trust through demonstrable control over AI infrastructure, transparent incident response, and accountability in automated decision-making  
- Trigger: the automated_decision_scandal this turn  
- Grounds: The exposure of systemic harm caused by an AI-supported decision system — whether in social insurance, policing or the courts — has transformed the landscape of public legitimacy. What was once a deficit of visibility is now a crisis of justice. Trust cannot be rebuilt merely by proving we can defend or respond; it must also be earned by proving we can correct, compensate, and constrain. Our commitment must expand to include accountability for past harms and enforceable limits on future misuse, or risk being seen as managing oppression rather than ending it.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late, and now explicitly include redress when action causes harm  
- Grounds: The automated decision scandal confirms that acting under uncertainty carries not only strategic but moral risk. Our posture must evolve: anticipatory action remains necessary, but it cannot be unchecked. When we act early and wrongly, we must have mechanisms to acknowledge, repair, and reform — or lose legitimacy entirely. This addition does not weaken our resolve; it grounds it.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch EU-wide Audit and Redress Programme for High-Impact Automated Decisions  
Establish binding procedures to investigate, document, and compensate individuals harmed by AI-driven decisions in public services, beginning with social welfare, law enforcement, and judicial support systems, using findings from the automated_decision_scandal to set precedent.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 14  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by improving accountability, reduce public_sentiment backlash by demonstrating redress, and strengthen eu_political_capital by showing responsiveness to harm  
Applies to: Member states’ public administrations, European Ombudsman, EAAD, national data protection authorities

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish EU Public Trust Corps for AI Incident Response and Transparency  
This remains our dominant effort because while the audit and redress programme responds to a specific failure, the Trust Corps is the vessel through which we re-establish credibility at scale — making incident response visible, continuous, and participatory, which is essential if any new measure, including redress, is to be believed.

## In practice
We are treating this turn as one of reckoning: the convergence of the automated_decision_scandal, the capability_jump, and supply_chain_coercion has crystallized the cost of dependence without accountability. The Trust Corps, already in flight, is now being repurposed in its implementation to foreground redress. We are instructing DG HOME to integrate the first cases from the scandal into the Lyon transparency drill — not as hypotheticals, but as live simulations of investigation and compensation. The AI Incident Dashboard will include a dedicated stream for “harm events” starting this quarter, co-published with national ombudsmen.

The new Audit and Redress Programme will be funded through a reallocation of cohesion policy reserves and launched via a Council Regulation under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to set minimum standards for procedural fairness in automated governance. Resistance from member states citing administrative sovereignty — particularly Poland and Hungary — is expected. To counter it, we are offering conditional access to recovery and resilience funds tied to compliance, and deploying EAAD teams to assist national agencies in backlogged case reviews. ENISA will publish a standardised impact assessment framework by turn 11.

Meanwhile, the fallout from supply_chain_coercion is forcing a quiet recalibration of our industrial strategy. While no new compute initiative is launched this turn, we are directing the European Chips Act task force to assess the feasibility of reactivating legacy semiconductor lines for non-frontier but trusted AI inference hardware — a move that may later feed into category 4, but for now remains preparatory. The capability_jump, though narrow, has been confirmed in cyber and code generation domains; we are fast-tracking integration of anomaly detection modules into the Trust Corps’ monitoring stack, working with Aarhus red-team lab to simulate adversarial reasoning leaks. This is not a new measure, but a necessary adaptation within existing instruments.
