## In practice
We are acting on the premise that the EU’s survival as a decision-making entity depends on its ability to restore order faster than disruption spreads. This turn, we finalise the legal mandate for the European AI Emergency Response Corps under Article 122a TFEU, granting it emergency override authority over compromised systems in energy, health, and water infrastructure. ENISA and ECDC are co-locating 200 technical operators in Lyon by June, with pre-authorised access to isolate runaway agents and restore baseline functionality. The remaining 300 will be recruited from national cyber units and AI safety fellows, trained under a new EU Rapid Intervention Certification. Funding flows from the Resilience Bond, now rated AAA after ECB endorsement, and deployment rights are being fast-tracked through a qualified majority voting mechanism in the Council’s Internal Security Committee, bypassing national vetoes.

Simultaneously, the Resilience Surge Protocol expands its cyber hardening to 12 additional critical nodes — including election systems in five member states — with mandatory AI-driven red-teaming every quarter. The BioShield Initiative integrates real-time wastewater monitoring with synthetic DNA registries, using sovereign AI models to flag anomalies before they become outbreaks. The Social Shock Facility begins disbursement, tied to national reforms in retraining access for displaced workers, with €1.2bn released to Germany, Spain, and Poland this quarter. And we are forcing the issue on compute: the InvestAI Gigafactories project is now under direct Commission oversight, with grid-connection deadlines set for Q4 2029 and penalties for delay. We are not waiting for consensus. We are building the EU’s immune system — and we are doing it under fire.
