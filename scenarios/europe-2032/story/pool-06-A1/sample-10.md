## In practice
We are acting on the certainty that the next breach will not wait for consensus. The biosecurity leak has exposed a fatal gap: we can detect risks in models, but we cannot stop their consequences in time. This turn, we are operationalising the European AI Emergency Response Corps as the Union’s reflexive layer. Under Article 168 TFEU and the Integrated Political Crisis Response, we are fast-tracking Council ratification of its mandate, granting it pre-authorised access to isolate compromised systems, suspend agent execution, and deploy countermeasures across borders during declared incidents. The first 200 operators are being drawn from ENISA, ECDC, and national cyber units, embedded in EuroHPC nodes in Lyon, Barcelona, and Bologna, and trained on live-fire simulations of runaway agent containment.

At the same time, we are expanding the Resilience Surge Protocol’s reach. The €9bn emergency funding is now being disbursed to harden energy and water infrastructure, with mandatory AI-driven red-teaming every quarter — a condition tied to Cohesion Fund disbursements. The Social Shock Facility’s €6bn is unlocked, but only for member states that have submitted national retraining plans aligned with the AI displacement forecasts from Eurostat. Legal delays on cross-border deployment are being bypassed through mutual recognition agreements pushed through the Justice and Home Affairs Council, backed by a new emergency arbitration mechanism at the Court of Justice.

The Tech sovereignty package is nearing completion — we expect the Council to adopt the accelerated-permitting zones by June, unlocking grid access for three new data centres. But we are not waiting: under the same legal basis, we are instructing national regulators to treat AI infrastructure as critical energy load, guaranteeing priority connection. The InvestAI Gigafactories continue on track, though delays in chip supply threaten the turn 7 finish. We are now in direct talks with ASML and Intel to secure priority tooling delivery, leveraging the EU’s market access and the Anti-Coercion Instrument as bargaining chips. This is not industrial policy — it is infrastructure for survival.
