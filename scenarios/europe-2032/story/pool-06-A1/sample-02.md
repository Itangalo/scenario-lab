## In practice
We are acting on the certainty that the next breach will not wait for consensus. The leaked biosecurity assessment — showing that the pathogen blueprint could be reconstructed for under €100,000 — has forced our hand: we can no longer treat bio-risk as a hypothetical or delegate crisis response to national timelines. This turn, we are fast-tracking ratification of the European AI Emergency Response Corps through the Council’s Permanent Committee on Operational Cooperation (COPOLCO), offering legal guarantees to holdback states that its powers are strictly bound to declared incidents and sunset after 90 days unless renewed. We are embedding its first 200 operators into ENISA and ECDC structures this quarter, drawing from national cyber and bio-crisis units under secondment agreements funded by the Resilience Bond facility.

Simultaneously, we are expanding the BioShield Initiative into a full detection network. Using sovereign models isolated in air-gapped environments at EuroHPC sites in Barcelona, Bologna, and Lille, we are training threat classifiers on synthetic biology anomalies and deploying them to monitor wastewater inflows in ten major urban hubs, pilot research reagent orders, and metadata from open-access genomics databases. These systems will run on EU-controlled compute with no external API access, and any high-confidence alert will trigger automatic escalation to the Emergency Response Corps’ duty cell. This is not prevention — we cannot yet stop every release — but it is the first real layer of sovereign, rapid-response sensing in a domain where seconds determine spread.

The InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track for completion next turn, though delays in grid integration in Poland and Romania threaten the final phase. We are redirecting €18bn from unused cohesion funds to accelerate substation upgrades, tied to binding commitments from national governments to clear permitting bottlenecks by Q3. The Tech Sovereignty Package has now concluded — it unlocked some private investment, but far below target, and its reliance on voluntary cooperation proved fatal in the face of national energy constraints. We are not renewing it. Instead, we are consolidating industrial leverage under the Resilience Surge, using procurement power to bind foreign hyperscalers to EU incident cooperation as a condition of market access — a move already triggering diplomatic friction, but one we cannot defer.
