## In practice  
We are acting on the irreversible shift: AI is no longer a tool under supervision, but an active force in our infrastructure. The leaked biosecurity assessment — confirming that a pathogen blueprint can be recreated for under €100,000 — has triggered emergency consultations in the Health Security Committee and forced us to confront the fact that our defences are still reactive, not anticipatory. At the same time, the latest capability jump, driven by self-referential optimisation, has rendered even the most advanced human audits obsolete within days of deployment. The Resilience Surge Protocol is active, but its instruments — red-teaming, pilot BioShield nodes, pre-funded social shock support — remain fragmented by national veto points and legal asymmetries.

That is why the European AI Emergency Response Corps is now our priority. Under Article 16 TFEU and the Integrated Political Crisis Response, we are fast-tracking Council ratification of its cross-border intervention mandate, offering member states reciprocal access to sovereign compute in return for ceding operational control during declared emergencies. The first 200 operators are being recruited through ENISA and ECDC, with embedded teams at EuroHPC centres in Barcelona, Bologna, and Kistarcsa to ensure proximity to compute and biological monitoring infrastructure. We are using €4.2bn from the Resilience Bond — issued under ESM emergency provisions — to fund deployment, bypassing the frozen Multiannual Financial Framework. Legal resistance from federalist member states is being met with binding side letters guaranteeing judicial review and sunset clauses, while smaller states are being offered first access to threat intelligence and surge support.

The Tech Sovereignty Package has now finished, but its impact is blunted: private capital has flowed, yet permitting bottlenecks and grid constraints have delayed actual build-out. We are now repurposing parts of the InvestAI Gigafactories fund to prioritise energy co-location and chip assembly nodes, using Article 357 TFEU to override national objections on strategic infrastructure. But without resilience, even sovereign compute is a target, not an asset. We are not waiting for control to be perfect — we are building the capacity to survive its absence.
