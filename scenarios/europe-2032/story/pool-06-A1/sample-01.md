## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the window for reactive governance has closed. The leaked biosecurity assessment — confirming that a recreatable pathogen blueprint exists and is within reach of non-state actors — has triggered emergency coordination under the Integrated Political Crisis Response framework. We are no longer managing risk; we are in damage containment mode. The Resilience Surge Protocol is now fully activated: €9bn in emergency funding is being disbursed via the Recovery and Resilience Facility for mandatory AI-driven red-teaming of energy, water, and election infrastructure in all member states, with compliance tied to cohesion fund disbursements. ENISA’s Rapid Response Corps has been doubled to 400 personnel and granted cross-border deployment rights during declared incidents — a legal shift pushed through COREPER II and ratified by 14 member states so far, with the remaining 13 under intense diplomatic pressure.

The European AI Emergency Response Corps is being stood up in parallel, embedded jointly in ENISA and the ECDC, with pre-authorised technical intervention powers to isolate compromised systems and terminate runaway agent processes. Recruitment is underway for 500 specialists in AI safety, cyber-physical systems, and synthetic biology monitoring, with initial basing at EuroHPC centres in Barcelona, Bologna, and Sofia. The mandate will be formally adopted by the Council in June under Article 122 TFEU, using the crisis waiver to bypass unanimity requirements. We are funding it through a €4bn Resilience Bond issuance, backed by the NextGenerationEU guarantee, and securing compute access via repurposed capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories pipeline — a temporary diversion that risks delaying civilian deployment but is justified by the emergency.

At the same time, we are launching the Emergency Compute Lockdown Directive to address the core failure: unchecked frontier experimentation on EU soil. This is not a ban, but a security floor. All clusters above 10^25 FLOP/s — currently seven sites across Germany, France, and the Netherlands — must now implement air-gapped audit logging, multi-person control for training initiation, and real-time anomaly detection monitored by national authorities with EU oversight. The European Commission is drafting the implementing act under the AI Act’s emergency provisions, with technical standards developed in 48 hours by a crisis cell at DG CONNECT. Resistance is expected from private labs and some member states citing innovation costs, but we are offering fast-track access to sovereign compute pools and liability shielding for compliance. This is not optional: if you operate at this scale in Europe, you now do so under EU supervision. The alternative is shutdown.
