**European Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration** — because the loss_of_control_incident and member_state_defection expose fatal fragility: we cannot secure what we cannot run, and we cannot act when access is someone else’s privilege. ## In practice
This turn, we close two major measures: the *Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative* and the *Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol*. The former delivered its first wave of sparse model prototypes, showing 40% efficiency gains and proving that EU-led innovation can compete on architecture, not just scale. But prototypes are not power. The latter proved its worth during the election crisis: fusion cells detected coordinated manipulation early, deepfake disclosures were enforced, and the “trust tag” gave public broadcasters a shared signal in a noisy environment. Yet both efforts hit the same wall — they depend on compute and access we do not control. Hungary’s bilateral deal with a U.S. hyperscaler is not just a defection; it is a symptom of a deeper truth: without sovereign infrastructure, our rules are requests, not commands.

We are now acting on that truth. The *European Sovereign AI Compute Acceleration* will deploy 20 exaflops of dedicated, energy-efficient AI compute across three new sovereign data centres — in Finland, France, and Romania — by turn 13. These will run models derived from sparse architectures, cooled by geothermal and nuclear-backed grids, and legally bound to EU jurisdiction: no backdoors, no foreign override, no withdrawal of service. Access will be prioritised for ENISA’s evaluation teams, the European Centre for Disease Prevention of Democratic Resilience, and national grid operators hardening against AI-driven instability. Funding of €4.2 billion will be drawn from the Recovery and Resilience Facility, the Connecting Europe Facility, and a new sovereign tech tranche of the EU Budget, with co-investment from ASML and Infineon tied to chip supply commitments.

We are not building another cloud. We are building a spine. This is not about catching up to the frontier; it is about ensuring that when the frontier moves, the EU still has ground to stand on. We will negotiate with member states on siting and energy access under Article 122 TFEU for urgency, but we will not wait for unanimity. For Poland, we offer early access as a partner. For Hungary, we leave the door open — but the price of entry is compliance, not exception. This is the infrastructure of political agency. Without it, every other measure we pass is written in pencil.
