## In practice  
This turn, we close two major initiatives. The Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol is now operational: fusion cells between ENISA, national electoral commissions, and public broadcasters are active in six member states, with real-time deepfake detection and joint alert protocols tested during regional elections in Spain and Latvia. The “trust tag” system has been adopted by ARD, RAI, and France Télévisions, and early data shows a 17% increase in audience retention for tagged content. The credibility signal is landing — but only just. The annulment of the election remains a wound, not a lesson learned.

The Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative reaches its scheduled end. The sparse model prototypes from ASML and Infineon have delivered 40% efficiency gains in simulation, and we are now transitioning the work into operational trials: the Dutch grid operator Tennet will test a sparse model for real-time load balancing, while Poland’s electoral commission will pilot one for synthetic media detection. The European Institute for Sparse Intelligence is formally established under the Horizon Europe framework, hosted in Eindhoven, with a mandate to scale and secure these architectures. But Hungary remains outside the loop, relying on its U.S. hyperscaler deal — a growing liability as openweight_capability hits 54.0 and the risk of distributed misuse rises.

With both measures now complete, we pivot to consolidation. The adoption_success — a pilot in Finland that cut hospital wait times by 30% using an EU-developed scheduling agent — proves that public-sector AI can deliver tangible benefit *and* be seen as European. We seize this moment to launch the European Trust Layer: a binding standard for all EU-funded or member-state-deployed AI in public services. It requires open logging, human-contestable decisions, and a visible “made in the EU” certification. Funding of €350 million comes from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with compliance tied to Cohesion Fund access. The legal basis is Article 16 TFEU and the AI Act’s high-risk provisions, enforced by the European AI Office. We are offering early technical support and bonus funding to laggards — not to punish, but to unify. Because if we cannot build trust in what we *do*, we will lose not just capability, but consent.
