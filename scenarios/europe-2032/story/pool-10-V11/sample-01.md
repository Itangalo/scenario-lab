## In practice

This turn, we formally conclude the Algorithmic Sovereignty Initiative. The prototypes from ASML and Infineon have demonstrated 40% efficiency gains in closed trials for grid monitoring and election integrity systems — a breakthrough in making high-performance AI viable on sovereign, energy-constrained infrastructure. With this proof of concept secured, we transition to consolidation: launching the European Institute for Sparse Intelligence as a legal entity under Article 185 of the TFEU, co-funded by the Horizon Europe programme and the Innovation Fund, with €1.2 billion committed over three years. The Institute will be headquartered in Eindhoven, leveraging the existing semiconductor ecosystem, and will operate a secure compute cluster on EU soil, governed by a board with majority EU Commission and member-state representation.

We are already in talks with Germany, France, and the Netherlands to transfer the sparse model IP into the Institute’s stewardship, ensuring it remains under collective EU control. Poland has expressed interest in joining, provided early access to the Institute’s tools for its own grid security upgrades — we are treating this as a bridge back to cohesion. Hungary remains outside, but its bilateral deal with the U.S. hyperscaler is now under scrutiny by the European Court of Justice for violating the Digital Markets Act’s interoperability requirements — a legal lever we are prepared to pull. The loss_of_control_incident, while not yet fully attributed, has shifted the political ground: even sceptics now agree that we cannot outsource the core of our cognitive infrastructure.

Simultaneously, we are closing the Emergency Democratic Integrity Protocol as a crisis measure and folding its components — fusion cells, trust tags, deepfake disclosure — into a permanent European Democratic Resilience Framework, to be proposed in the next legislative cycle. The protocol worked: during the recent election cycle in Slovakia, synthetic media was detected within hours, and coordinated inauthentic behaviour was disrupted before it could spread. But crisis powers cannot be permanent. We are now building the durable institutions that can operate between crises — because the next one will not wait for us to get ready.
