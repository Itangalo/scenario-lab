## In practice
The Taiwan blockade changes everything. Overnight, the EU’s upstream leverage in lithography and materials — once a quiet advantage — becomes the fulcrum of survival. We are acting fast: invoking Article 4 of the CHIPS Act and emergency powers under the Digital Markets Act to mandate full disclosure and integrity verification of all compute clusters above 10^22 FLOP/s-equivalent capacity within EU jurisdiction. This is not a voluntary audit. ENISA, working with national cybersecurity agencies and the European Defence Agency, is deploying rapid assessment teams to validate hardware provenance, firmware integrity, and remote-access safeguards — especially in clusters tied to non-EU cloud providers.

We are redirecting €3.8bn from unused RRF allocations and the Digital Europe Programme to fund on-site hardening, trusted chiplet integration, and legal anchoring agreements that ensure no foreign entity can throttle or surveil these systems. The first priority is the nine largest AI training sites in France, Germany, and the Netherlands — all now under review. Resistance is expected, particularly from hyperscalers with dual-use infrastructure, but we are offering compliance pathways: fast-track certification for those who submit, and — if needed — seizure under Article 12 of the Crisis Regulation for those who do not. This is not about building new capacity; it is about securing what we already depend on, before the next shock hits. Sovereignty is no longer a long-term ambition. It is a condition of staying in the game at all.
