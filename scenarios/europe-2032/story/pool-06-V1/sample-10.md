## In practice
We are acting on the fact that the AI frontier has moved inside our critical systems, and that we are no longer the ones setting the pace. The **Systemic Integrity Watch** launches this turn under Article 16 of the Treaty on European Union, granting the Commission emergency coordination powers. ENISA will operationalise the first phase by integrating real-time telemetry from Tier 1 energy operators and national health automation systems, using pattern-detection models trained on the recent grid feedback anomalies. We are allocating €720 million from the Digital Europe Programme and the Crisis and Investment Facility to fund sensor deployment, secure data pipelines, and a 24/7 alert desk co-staffed by national regulators and independent AI auditors. The system will have binding authority to trigger emergency pauses in AI-driven operations when unexplained coordination or runaway feedback is detected — a power we are legitimising through fast-tracked Council endorsement in the Justice and Home Affairs formation, backed by a public risk communication campaign.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** and **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint** both conclude this turn, freeing up 6 units of political capital — a rare windfall we are immediately reinvesting. The sovereignty package has delivered permitting reforms in Finland, Poland, and Portugal, unlocking 38% of the targeted €200bn in private compute investment by 2036, though full build-out remains years away. The resilience sprint has achieved 42% fallback system deployment in Tier 1 operators and established red-teaming protocols now being institutionalised under NIS2+ revisions. Germany and Spain have accepted compliance in exchange for a new clause affirming national sovereignty over incident response command chains. With the Taiwan blockade cutting off advanced semiconductor shipments, we are now treating every watt of domestic compute as strategic infrastructure: the Commission is preparing an emergency recommendation under the Crisis Regulation to prioritise AI training workloads on EU-anchored systems, starting with the newly operational Gaia-X-adjacent clusters in Marseille and Hamburg. This is not protectionism — it is triage. We are not building capacity fast enough to lead, but we must not fall so far behind that we cannot even defend what we are.
