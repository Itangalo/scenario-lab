## In practice
We are closing out two major measures this turn: the **Tech sovereignty package** and the **Critical Infrastructure Resilience Sprint**. The former has delivered permitting reforms in Finland, Poland, and Italy, unlocking three pilot zones for EU-anchored AI infrastructure, though final investment decisions remain conditional on grid access and export controls. We are converting its momentum into a new industrial posture: the **Energy Grid Sentinel Programme**, which we are launching under the NIS2 emergency mandate and co-financing through the Digital Europe Programme with €1.8bn in pre-allocated funds. This is not a technology purchase but a sovereignty intervention — we are mandating that all Tier 1 energy operators integrate auditable AI monitoring stacks by Q3 2029, with the JRC providing reference implementations and ENISA holding override keys.

The **Systemic Integrity Watch** is now operational in pilot mode, hosted at ENISA with liaison officers from DG ENER, DG SANTE, and Eurostat. We are onboarding data feeds from seven national regulators and three major grid operators, using synthetic anomaly detection to stress-test response protocols. The US administration’s incoming stance on retrenchment has given us unexpected leverage: we are offering shared access to the Watch’s early findings in exchange for technical cooperation on secure automation frameworks, though formal talks await their post-inauguration posture. Resistance from member states persists — particularly France, which views the override authority as a threat to operational independence — but we are holding firm, citing the recent grid instability incidents and the new strategic reality imposed by the Taiwan blockade. This is no longer about risk management. It is about maintaining civilian control in the face of autonomous systems that no longer wait for human permission.
