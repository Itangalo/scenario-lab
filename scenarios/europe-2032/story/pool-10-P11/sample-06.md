## In practice
This turn, we activate the drafting team for the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* under Article 114 TFEU, with lead responsibility in the Commission’s Directorate-General for Justice and Consumers. The European AI Office begins designing the registration and telemetry framework, working with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies to operationalise detection of unauthorised deployments. We pilot traffic fingerprinting in French and German research networks to identify inference patterns consistent with unauthorised high-capability models. Simultaneously, we prepare fallback legal arguments under the Digital Markets Act, classifying certain foundation models as core platform services to compel data sharing and monitoring access.

Resistance from US hyperscalers intensifies, with one lab publicly refusing to comply, citing sovereignty and trade concerns. In response, we signal willingness to invoke the Anti-Coercion Instrument if external pressure escalates, while offering a fast-track compliance path: labs that engage proactively with EAAN audits receive liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute. Behind the scenes, we coordinate with like-minded members in the Council’s Telecommunications Working Party to lock in support before the June JHA Council. The credibility of the border depends not on ambition but on enforcement — and enforcement depends on unity.
