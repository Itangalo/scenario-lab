## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the initial drafting phase under Article 114 TFEU, with the European Commission’s AI Office leading inter-service consultation and legal drafting. The act will be presented to the Internal Market and Consumer Protection (IMCO) and Legal Affairs (JURI) committees in the European Parliament, and a trilogue preparatory group will convene under the Council Presidency to begin aligning member states. Crucially, we integrate the technical architecture of the European AI Assurance Network—already under construction in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich—into the enforcement logic: these nodes will serve as sovereign inspection enclaves where model weights and runtime telemetry must be submitted for certification before market access.

We are meeting resistance from US-based hyperscalers, who argue that real-time monitoring and kill switches compromise IP and national security commitments. To counter this, we are offering a dual-track compliance pathway: labs that proactively engage with EAAN audits and submit to limited, time-boxed red-teaming exercises will qualify for liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute through the upcoming Horizon AI programme. Simultaneously, we are working with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies to operationalise detection capabilities—traffic fingerprinting, inference-pattern recognition, and API watermarking—piloted in German and French networks, to identify unauthorised deployments that attempt to bypass the boundary. The message is clear: sovereignty does not mean exclusion, but it does require accountability. Without it, the EU cannot and will not serve as a testing ground for systems it cannot understand or stop.
