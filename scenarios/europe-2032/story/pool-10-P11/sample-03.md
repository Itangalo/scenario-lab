## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing.``
  - Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports and the Taiwan tension rise expose a new reality: capability is emerging unpredictably, and geopolitical fragility is compounding it. Our dual mandate is no longer a balance between two priorities — it is a single imperative. We cannot secure Europe’s future without confronting uncontrolled systems, and we cannot confront uncontrolled systems without strategic autonomy. The two mandates have fused.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late.``
  - Grounds: The anomaly reports are not proof — but they are not noise either. We now know that capability can emerge outside design intent, and that developers will not self-report it. Our commitment must evolve from accepting uncertainty to acting *against* opacity: we will impose observability as a condition of access, not a favour to be granted. This is no longer about timing — it is about power.

## New measure
**Sovereign Compute Corridor**  
We fast-track the construction of a secure, high-capacity AI compute corridor on EU soil, legally anchored and politically insulated, to ensure the EU can run, inspect, and control frontier models independently.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 16`  
`Targeted effect:` +15 to +20 eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 16; begins closing the gap between openweight_capability and EU-controlled capability; enables autonomous evaluation, red-teaming, and fallback operation under crisis conditions  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), and selected private partners with binding security commitments

## Priority
**EU Border for AI Systems** — because the eval_anomaly_reports confirm that capability is emerging outside developer control, and one US lab has already withdrawn from EU deployment, we must lock in our enforcement posture now: if we waver, we lose the leverage to demand observability, and the border becomes a fiction.

## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the European Parliament’s internal market committee, invoking Article 114 TFEU to justify harmonisation of essential requirements for AI systems as goods entering the internal market. We coordinate with the European AI Office to draft technical implementing acts, specifying telemetry formats, audit intervals, and kill-switch verification protocols. ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies begin operationalising detection: pilot systems in Germany and France now use traffic fingerprinting to identify unauthorised inference patterns, feeding into a central alerting layer managed by the EAAN.

At the same time, we initiate the **Sovereign Compute Corridor** by mobilising the Connecting Europe Facility and reallocating unused cohesion funds to accelerate permitting for three strategic sites: a repurposed nuclear facility in Belgium, a geothermally powered site in Iceland under EU jurisdiction, and a new EuroHPC JU node in Poland. We invoke the European Union Strategic Projects framework to override national bottlenecks, offering member states a 70% funding guarantee in exchange for fast-tracking grid connections and security clearances. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that, whatever happens beyond our borders, we retain the capacity to see, test, and act.
