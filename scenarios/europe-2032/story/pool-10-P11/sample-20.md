## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the early stages of co-legislation, initiating trilogue discussions with the European Parliament and Council under Article 114 TFEU. The legal text defines a clear threshold: any AI system assessed at or above 60.0 capability index operating in the EU must undergo sovereign evaluation by EAAN, implement runtime observability through certified telemetry nodes, and submit to verified kill-switch protocols tested annually by red teams. We designate Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich as the first three certification gateways, integrating them with ENISA’s ongoing work on inference-pattern detection and traffic fingerprinting to identify unauthorised deployments.

Resistance from US hyperscalers intensifies, with one lab publicly withdrawing from the AI Pact and another restricting API access to EU-based researchers. In response, we prepare contingency designations under the Digital Markets Act, considering certain foundation models as core platform services subject to interoperability and audit mandates. Simultaneously, we activate early channels under the Anti-Coercion Instrument, signaling readiness to respond to extraterritorial pressure. To build domestic cohesion, we launch a fast-track compliance pathway: labs that engage proactively with EAAN audits gain liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to the EuroHPC JU’s upcoming compute allocation. Framing remains critical—this is not a trade barrier but a condition of market access, no different than safety standards for vehicles or pharmaceuticals. With political capital low and public sentiment skeptical, we cannot afford to be seen as reckless; we must be seen as responsible.
