## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through drafting under Article 114 TFEU, coordinating with the Legal Service of the European Commission to ensure compatibility with both internal market principles and international obligations. The European AI Office, in close cooperation with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies, begins operationalising detection protocols for unauthorised deployments, scaling up traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition piloted in Germany and France. Certification gateways in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich are being staffed and equipped to conduct real-time audits and red-team evaluations, drawing on capabilities being built under the AI Safety Emergency Investment.

Resistance from US hyperscalers remains high, with one major provider maintaining its suspension of EU deployments. In response, we prepare fallback options under the Digital Markets Act, classifying certain foundation models as core platform services subject to interoperability and audit mandates. Simultaneously, we signal openness to fast-track compliance through liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute for labs that proactively engage with EAAN. Diplomatic channels remain open through the Trade and Technology Council, but we do not mistake dialogue for leverage: the border is not negotiable. We frame this as regulatory hygiene — a line drawn not against innovation, but against opacity, ensuring that no system too powerful to understand can operate too freely to control.
