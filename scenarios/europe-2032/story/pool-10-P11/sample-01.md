## In practice
This turn, we initiate emergency permitting procedures under the European Chips Act and the Critical Infrastructure Resilience Directive to fast-track the siting and grid connection of three new sovereign AI clusters — in Finland, France, and Italy — each designed to host at least 100,000 H100-equivalent GPUs under EU legal jurisdiction and physical control. The European Commission, in coordination with EuroHPC and national energy regulators, will designate these as strategic infrastructure, enabling state aid and streamlined environmental assessments. We allocate €12bn from the Marguerite II Fund and the Innovation Council Facility to de-risk private investment, with matching obligations: any recipient of EU compute subsidies must grant EAAN audit access and runtime telemetry for models above 60.0 capability.  

Simultaneously, we begin negotiations with the US Department of Commerce and leading hyperscalers to establish a mutual recognition framework for AI safety evaluations — not as a concession, but as leverage. We offer expedited border compliance for labs that submit to EAAN red-teaming and share model telemetry, in exchange for lifting deployment suspensions. But we prepare fallbacks: if access remains conditional on surrendering sovereign control, we will invoke Article 36 TFEU on grounds of public security and proceed with autonomous capability build-out. The message is clear: the EU does not reject foreign innovation — but it will not accept unaccountable power. The border is not a wall; it is a checkpoint. And we will staff it with our own people, on our own soil, using our own compute.
