## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through initial drafting under Article 114 TFEU, with the European Commission’s AI Office leading inter-service coordination. Legal teams in DG CONNECT and DG TRADE are reconciling the proposal with internal market obligations and external commitments, while the Council’s Working Party on Artificial Intelligence begins informal consultations. We circulate a draft directive that defines “operating within EU jurisdiction” to include inference serving, model-as-a-service APIs, and agent deployments with EU user-facing functionality — closing potential loopholes.

Simultaneously, we accelerate the technical track: EAAN nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich are now being provisioned with secure enclaves for weight inspection and runtime telemetry. ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies are integrating traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern detection tools piloted in Germany and France into a cross-border monitoring framework. We are negotiating with two EU-resident labs — one public, one private — to serve as early adopters, offering liability shielding and preferential compute access in exchange for full audit access. Resistance from US hyperscalers is expected; we prepare to invoke the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions and the Anti-Coercion Instrument if deployment restrictions are weaponised. The message is clear: sovereignty is not optional. If you want to operate here, you must be inspectable, controllable, and accountable.
