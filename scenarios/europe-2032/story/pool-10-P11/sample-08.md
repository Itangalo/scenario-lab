## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the European Parliament’s JURI and ITRE committees, anchoring it in Article 114 TFEU as a single market measure. The European AI Office begins drafting technical standards for runtime monitoring, requiring real-time telemetry from all models above 60.0 capability operating in the EU, with data routed to EAAN nodes in Brussels and Sophia Antipolis. ENISA activates pilot detection systems in German and French networks, using inference-pattern recognition to flag unregistered deployments. We coordinate with national cybersecurity agencies to simulate enforcement scenarios, including takedown of rogue agents and legal action against cloud platforms hosting non-compliant models.

Simultaneously, we launch the **Sovereign Capacity Initiative** in response to the erosion of eu_ai_sovereignty and the fragility of access. The Commission issues a call for urgent proposals under the Important Project of Common European Interest (IPCEI) framework, inviting consortia led by French and Finnish state-backed operators to bid for funding. We fast-track environmental and grid-connection permits through a new emergency clause in the EU Net-Zero Industry Act, and allocate €18bn from the Margins of the Multiannual Financial Framework to cover 70% of capital costs. The initiative is framed not as competition with US or Chinese labs, but as insurance: if the frontier becomes inaccessible or hostile, the EU must still be able to run, audit, and safeguard systems essential to its own governance and critical infrastructure. We offer access to this compute as a carrot to developers complying with the Border Act—proof that the EU is not only a regulator, but a partner capable of hosting capability.
