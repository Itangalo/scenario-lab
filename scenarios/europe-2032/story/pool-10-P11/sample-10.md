## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the European Parliament’s LIBE and IMCO committees, anchoring it in Article 114 TFEU to frame it as an internal market measure with cross-border implications. The draft regulation mandates that any AI system operating above 60.0 capability in the EU must register with the European AI Office, submit to real-time monitoring via EAAN nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich, and implement cryptographically verifiable kill switches tested by red teams under ENISA oversight. We integrate the act with the ongoing AI Safety Emergency Investment, directing €8bn of that programme to stand up three secure inspection enclaves with access to model weights and telemetry — a capability that must land before the border can be enforced.

Resistance from US hyperscalers remains high; one has paused deployment, while others warn of reciprocal barriers. We prepare contingency use of the Anti-Coercion Instrument, but also open a fast-track compliance channel: labs that engage proactively with EAAN audits gain liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute pools in Finland and Ireland. Coordination with national cybersecurity agencies intensifies, as Germany and France expand pilots using traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition to detect unauthorised deployments. Public messaging stresses regulatory parity — no car without brakes, no drug without trials — while low public sentiment forces us to avoid framing this as containment, instead calling it “digital product safety.” The line is not yet held, but it is being built.
