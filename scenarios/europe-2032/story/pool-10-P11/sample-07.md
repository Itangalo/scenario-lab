## In practice
This turn, we advance both the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* and the *Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative* in parallel, though only the former bears the weight of priority. Under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission tables the draft Act in the Council and Parliament, with the European AI Office tasked to operationalise certification gateways in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich by turn 12. We instruct ENISA to expand its pilot on inference-pattern detection into a standing monitoring regime, deploying traffic fingerprinting tools in national CERTs across six member states — Germany, France, Italy, Poland, Sweden, and Spain — funded through the Digital Europe Programme. To counter resistance from US-based labs, we prepare a legal annex under the Digital Markets Act reclassifying certain foundation models as core platform services, enabling ex-ante obligations, while quietly signalling readiness to activate the Anti-Coercion Instrument if external pressure escalates.

Simultaneously, we launch the Sovereign Compute Acceleration Initiative through an emergency Council meeting on strategic infrastructure. We propose binding targets: 30 exaflops of frontier-class compute on EU soil by turn 15, co-located with secure energy feeds and legally insulated from extraterritorial seizure. National governments are asked to designate “AI Infrastructure Priority Zones” with streamlined permitting, direct grid access, and tax incentives for chip fabrication and data centre build-out. We offer conditional access to EU-funded model training runs — once sovereign capability reaches threshold — as leverage to attract private capital. The initiative draws on unused allocations from the Recovery and Resilience Facility and redirects 15% of the Horizon Europe budget toward lithography R&D and talent retention. Resistance from member states wary of fiscal overreach is met with assurances of job creation and energy sovereignty, while public sentiment remains a constraint: no new data centres will be sited without local referenda and community benefit agreements. This is not just about power — it is about permanence.
