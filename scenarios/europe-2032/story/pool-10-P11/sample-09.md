## In practice
This turn, we are finalising the draft of the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* under Article 114 TFEU, with the European Commission’s AI Office leading inter-service consultation and legal scrutiny. The text mandates that any AI system operating above 60.0 capability in the EU must register with EAAN, submit to real-time telemetry, and implement verified kill switches. Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich are being equipped as certification gateways, with secure enclaves allowing EU auditors to inspect weights and monitor agent behaviour. We are coordinating with ENISA and national agencies to operationalise detection of unauthorised deployments using traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition, already piloted in German and French networks.

Resistance remains intense. US hyperscalers argue the Act violates sovereignty and intellectual property, and one has suspended deployment of its latest model in protest. In response, we are preparing to invoke the Digital Markets Act — classifying certain foundation models as core platform services — and the Anti-Coercion Instrument, should pressure escalate. At the same time, we are offering a fast-track compliance path: labs that proactively engage with EAAN audits will receive liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute. This is not protectionism — it is regulatory hygiene. No car enters without brakes, no drug without trials, no AI without controls. The border stands.
