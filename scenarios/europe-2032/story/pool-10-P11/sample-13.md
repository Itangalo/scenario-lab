This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the initial drafting phase under Article 114 TFEU, with the European Commission’s AI Office leading inter-service consultation and preparing the formal proposal for the June Justice and Home Affairs Council. The text mandates that any AI system operating above 60.0 capability in the EU must register with the EAAN, submit to real-time monitoring via secure telemetry nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich, and implement cryptographically verifiable kill switches tested by red teams under EU authority. We align this with the ongoing AI Safety Emergency Investment, directing EAAN to operationalise three secure inspection enclaves by Q3 2031 to receive model weights and runtime data from early-complying providers.

Resistance from US hyperscalers intensifies, with one lab publicly withdrawing from EU deployment and others lobbying through the US-EU Trade and Technology Council. In response, we prepare to invoke the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to classify certain foundation models as core platform services, subjecting them to interoperability and data access rules. We also signal readiness to use the Anti-Coercion Instrument if external pressure attempts to block the Act’s adoption. To build internal cohesion, we offer a fast-track compliance pathway: labs that engage proactively with EAAN audits will receive liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to the EU’s emerging sovereign compute grid. Framing remains critical — we position the Border not as a barrier to innovation but as the minimum condition for lawful operation, equivalent to safety certification in aviation or pharmaceuticals. With public sentiment stagnant and political capital at 19.0, we rely on technical precision and legal defensibility to hold the line.
