## In practice
This turn, we advance the *AI Deployment Boundary Act* through the initial drafting phase under Article 114 TFEU, with the European Commission’s AI Office leading inter-service consultation and legal drafting. The core requirement — sovereign evaluation, continuous monitoring, and verified kill switches for all systems above 60.0 capability — will be operationalised through the EAAN nodes in Brussels, Sophia Antipolis, and Munich, which are now being equipped with secure enclaves for weight inspection and runtime telemetry analysis. We coordinate with ENISA and national cybersecurity agencies to integrate detection capabilities into existing network monitoring, using traffic fingerprinting and inference-pattern recognition tools developed in the German and French pilot programmes to identify unauthorised deployments.

Resistance from US hyperscalers remains intense. One major lab has suspended deployment, and others warn of fragmentation. In response, we prepare to invoke the Digital Markets Act, classifying certain foundation models as core platform services subject to interoperability and audit rights, and stand ready to deploy the Anti-Coercion Instrument if external pressure escalates. At the same time, we offer a fast-track compliance pathway: labs that engage proactively with EAAN audits receive liability shielding under the revised AI Liability Directive and preferential access to EU-funded compute. This dual track — hard enforcement at the border, soft incentives for cooperation — is designed to split the opposition and lock in early adopters. We frame the measure not as a trade barrier but as regulatory hygiene: no car without brakes, no drug without trials, no AI without controls. The message is clear — the EU will not accept systems it cannot govern.
