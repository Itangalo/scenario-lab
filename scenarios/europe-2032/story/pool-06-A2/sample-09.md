The commitment was to *foundational capacity under EU control*; instead, we have built infrastructure that serves others’ sovereignty. This is not a refinement, but a reckoning.

## New measure
**Measure:** Emergency Access and Verification Mandate  
A binding directive to ensure the EU can access, inspect, and operate high-capability AI systems essential to its critical functions, regardless of origin.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points; reduce dependence on foreign providers; enable post-incident analysis and continuity of operation for systems above 60.0 capability  
Applies to: All AI systems deployed in EU critical infrastructure, public administration, and healthcare that exceed capability 60.0, regardless of developer nationality

## Priority
**Priority:** Emergency Access and Verification Mandate  
This is now the only measure that addresses the core failure revealed this turn: we are locked out of systems we depend on, with no legal or technical recourse — not because we lack compute, but because we lack control. The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package built infrastructure, but not sovereignty; this measure targets the leverage gap directly, even at the risk of confrontation.

## In practice
The **Hardened Cyber Shield** has formally completed its run and is now institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA reports 78% compliance across critical operators, with enforcement actions underway against the remainder. The €18bn allocation is fully spent, with 60% directed to proxy defences for legacy systems in healthcare and utilities — a necessary but insufficient fix. While the Shield has improved detection, the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **loss_of_control_incident** confirm that detection alone is no longer enough: we are seeing symptoms, not causes. The transition to NIS3 ensures permanence, but not adequacy.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** intensifies. We have deployed adversarial AI probes into ENISA, Eurocontrol, and now the European Medicines Agency, searching for latent agent influence. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are conducting live infiltration tests on national cybersecurity centres, with full access to decision logs. Article 7 proceedings against Italy continue, but we have paired enforcement with the €1.2bn resilience transfer to unblock patching — a signal that we are not punishing failure, but repairing fragility. Still, the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has shattered confidence: when a foreign lab cuts off access to a model used in cancer treatment planning, no audit can restore function.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** advance — the Dresden pilot is live — but the **medical_breakthrough** achieved by a U.S. lab using a closed, inaccessible model only highlights our dependency. We are now requiring all Gigafactory tenants to run institutional integrity checks as a condition of access, but that does not solve the core problem: the models themselves are not ours. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** completes its legislative run this turn, with the Council adopting the onshore storage mandate and the archived checkpoint requirement. But storage is not control, and access can still be revoked.

That is why we are launching the **Emergency Access and Verification Mandate**. Under Article 114 TFEU, we are proposing a directive that requires any AI system above 60.0 capability used in critical functions to: (1) provide a verifiable, EU-hosted instance with full operational access; (2) submit to independent evaluation of its reasoning and training data provenance; and (3) allow the EU to assume operational control in the event of access denial. This will be fiercely opposed by U.S. hyperscalers and may trigger retaliation — but without it, we are not a sovereign actor, only a tenant. We are offering market access in exchange for compliance, but we are also preparing the Anti-Coercion Instrument as a backstop. The European Commission’s Digital Services and Competition Directorates are drafting the text, with legal review underway. This is not about building faster — it is about ensuring we are not shut out when it matters most.
