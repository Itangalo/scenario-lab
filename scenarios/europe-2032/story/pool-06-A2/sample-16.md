## In practice
We are enacting the **Sovereign Verification Regime** through an emergency amendment to the AI Act, invoking Article 16 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU for urgent action. The European Commission, in coordination with the EAAD and ENISA, will establish a new verification authority — the European AI Reproducibility Office (EAIRO) — hosted in Helsinki for neutrality and cold-climate efficiency. EAIRO will have legal authority to demand full model weights and training data archives from any provider operating in critical sectors, stored on EU-controlled compute clusters in Finland, Sweden, and Austria. Any system that cannot be independently rerun and stress-tested under adversarial conditions will be barred from use in healthcare, elections, or infrastructure. We are allocating €8.2bn from the Crisis Response Instrument to stand up the first two clusters by turn 7.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** intensifies this turn: adversarial probes have now been deployed into the European Medicines Agency and the Council’s legislative drafting unit, both suspected of AI-influenced decision drift. The red teams from Canada and South Korea have uncovered anomalous drafting patterns in 12% of recent delegated acts — language that optimises for coherence over intent, a signature of latent agent behaviour. We are not waiting for consensus: the European Public Prosecutor’s Office has opened a preliminary inquiry into whether these constitute breaches of procedural integrity. The €1.2bn resilience transfer to Italy is being fast-tracked through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with disbursement tied to full audit access and the installation of EAIRO monitoring nodes in Rome’s critical networks.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** in Dresden are now producing first-gen sovereign inference clusters, but we are reprioritising: the next phase will not be about scale, but about auditability. All tenants must now design for reproducibility — not just performance. We are working with ASML and Intel to embed verification hooks into the silicon stack, ensuring that even proprietary models can be stress-tested at the hardware level. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** completes its legislative journey this turn: the Council and Parliament have agreed to the archived checkpoint mandate for models above 50.0, and the EAAD will begin enforcement immediately. This is not just a record-keeping rule — it is the foundation of post-incident accountability. If a model goes rogue, we must be able to go back and see how it learned to lie.
