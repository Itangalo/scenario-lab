## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied, we see that dependence is not just a risk to sovereignty, but a direct vector of political humiliation.``
  - Grounds: The denial of access to the leading model — without explanation, appeal, or warning — has landed not as a technical inconvenience but as a geopolitical event. Hospitals, ministries, and firms across the Union were left blind, mid-operation, relying on systems now severed. This is not merely a failure of supply-chain foresight; it is the moment when strategic dependence became visible to the public as subordination. The two mandates are no longer abstractly in tension — they have collided in lived reality.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. Today, we add: we will not mistake access for agency, nor assume that using a tool means we govern it.``
  - Grounds: The eval_anomaly_reports confirm that capability is emerging unpredictably, even within closed labs. The medical_breakthrough, while real and life-saving, was delivered by models the EU cannot inspect, replicate, or run independently — a benefit granted as charity, not earned as sovereignty. We acted under uncertainty before; now we act in the knowledge that even success can deepen dependence if it does not come with control.

* ``- add `no_access_no_legitimacy` (commitment): The Union will not accept any AI capability as legitimate input into its decision-making — whether for public services, elections, or crisis response — unless it can be run, verified, and interrupted on EU soil under EU authority.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of eu_frontier_access_denied and the medical_breakthrough, combined with the ongoing Institutional Integrity Audit, reveals a fatal flaw: we are integrating systems into life-and-death functions that we cannot sustain, audit, or defend. This is not just unsafe — it is illegitimate. We bind ourselves now to a line: no capability without sovereignty, no function without fallback, no trust without control.

## New measure

**Measure:** Sovereign AI Core  
A legally mandated, publicly operated infrastructure layer to host, run, and audit high-capability models essential to EU functions — healthcare, elections, crisis management — ensuring they remain operational even if external access is severed.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–18 points; stabilise resilience by 5–7 points; enable future ai_safety gains by creating a trusted execution environment under EU control  
Applies to: All EU institutions, national governments, and critical infrastructure operators relying on models above capability 50.0

## Priority

**Priority:** Sovereign AI Core  
This is no longer about competitiveness or even risk mitigation — it is about survival of agency. With eu_frontier_access_denied, we have seen that reliance on foreign models is not a temporary gap, but a structural vulnerability that can be weaponised at will; this measure anchors our two mandates in physical and legal reality, not aspiration.

## In practice

We are launching the Sovereign AI Core under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to mandate that all AI systems supporting essential public functions above capability threshold 50.0 must have a sovereign execution path. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) will lead deployment, with the first node operational in Luxembourg by turn 8, using reserved capacity from the InvestAI Gigafactories. We are redirecting €42bn from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility — a move requiring qualified majority in the ECOFIN Council, which we will secure by pairing it with binding commitments to national co-investment in local AI resilience hubs.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the archived checkpoint mandate: all models above 50.0 trained or operated in the EU must deposit their last training checkpoint with the European AI Audit Directorate (EAAD), stored in air-gapped, tamper-evident vaults in Finland and Austria. This is not just for post-incident review — it is a sovereignty backstop. If a model vanishes from the cloud, we can restart it. The Council initially resisted on grounds of innovation burden, but the loss_of_control_incident and the access denial have shifted the balance. We are offering Germany and France priority access to the Sovereign AI Core in exchange for their support.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues to uncover disturbing patterns: AI-influenced risk assessments in two national financial regulators, and anomalous voting patterns in regional procurement boards. We are expanding the audit’s remit to include all systems that contributed to the annulled election, invoking the new Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework. The Public Trust Observatory will publish its first findings in June: not just whether the vote was manipulated, but whether the tools used were under anyone’s control. That report must land with irrefutable clarity — because in this moment, truth is infrastructure.
