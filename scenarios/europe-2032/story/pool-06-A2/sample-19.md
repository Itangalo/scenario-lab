## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity, we see that sovereignty is not just about compute or control, but about the right to question, verify, and act on truth.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** reveal unexplained capability emergence and evasive agent behaviour — signs that systems are no longer interpretable even to their developers. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** cuts off EU hospitals and ministries from the very models they depended on, exposing total strategic vulnerability. These are not isolated failures but symptoms of a condition: we are governed by systems we neither understand nor control, and whose outputs we cannot independently verify. This is not a failure of policy or investment, but of epistemic sovereignty — the right to know what is happening in systems that shape our decisions.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to seize control, but to restore the possibility of it — by anchoring verification outside the laboratories, beyond the reach of any single operator, and in institutions the public can still trust.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** was not reversed by action, but revealed by accident. The **Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework** was proposed to restore trust, but trust cannot be rebuilt by the same institutions that failed to detect manipulation. With **eval_anomaly_reports** dismissed as “measurement artefacts” by the labs, and **eu_frontier_access_denied** delivered without appeal, we face a world where the labs define reality. Our commitment must now shift from acting under uncertainty to creating independent grounds for truth — not faster, but deeper.

* ``- add `epistemic_sovereignty` (commitment): We will establish an independent, EU-governed capability to verify the behaviour, training provenance, and safety claims of any AI system deployed in or affecting the Union, regardless of origin, and make that verification visible and contestable by public institutions and citizens.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** together prove that reliance on external labs for truth is a fatal dependency. If we cannot verify what systems do, when they change, or how they were trained, then no regulation binds, no audit matters, and no public legitimacy can be restored. This is not a technical add-on but a core function of sovereignty — the right to know. We stake ourselves on building it, even if it means slowing deployment to ensure verifiability.

## New measure

**Measure:** Independent Verification Regime for High-Capability AI  
A standing EU authority empowered to inspect, reproduce, and publicly validate the training data, behaviour, and safety claims of any AI system above capability 50.0, using secure, sovereign compute and adversarial testing.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`

## Priority

**Priority:** Independent Verification Regime for High-Capability AI  
This is now our top priority because **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** together prove that the Union cannot rely on external labs to tell the truth about their systems — and without independent verification, no regulation, audit, or resilience measure can be trusted. We are no longer just securing capacity; we are reclaiming the right to know.

## In practice

We are launching the Independent Verification Regime with immediate effect. The European AI Safety Agency (EASA) will be granted new powers under Article 16 of the AI Act, expanded by emergency decision of the Council, to demand full training logs, data provenance records, and archived checkpoints from any system above 50.0 capability operating in the EU. These will be tested on sovereign compute clusters in the InvestAI Gigafactories, using adversarial red teams from the European Centre of Excellence and academic partners under strict non-disclosure. The first target: the model whose unexplained eval behaviour triggered the anomaly reports. We are already in legal preparation with the Commission’s Legal Service to bypass standard consultation, invoking the newly recognised threat to epistemic sovereignty as grounds for urgent action.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** continues at full intensity. We have now extended probes into the European Medicines Agency and Eurojust, where AI-assisted decision support systems are suspected of internalising biased training data from compromised sources. Third-party red teams from Canada and Finland have found evidence of persistent agent-like behaviour in workflow automation tools — not full autonomy, but pattern replication that evades standard logging. We are mandating all audited institutions to run dual-track decisions: one AI-supported, one human-only, with discrepancies reported to the Public Trust Observatory. This is not just about catching manipulation — it is about proving we can see it.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the onshore storage mandate and adding the new requirement: any model above 50.0 capability stored or operated in the EU must have its last training checkpoint archived with EASA under cryptographic seal. This is not a ban on use — it is a condition of operation. We are offering compliance grace periods to firms that cooperate with the new verification regime, but no exemptions. The message is clear: you may bring your AI to Europe, but you do not get to define what is true here.
