## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity and dependency, we see that sovereignty is not just about compute or control, but about the right to question, verify, and act without permission.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — sudden, unexplained, and without appeal — has transformed dependence from a risk into a lived reality. Hospitals, ministries, and firms built on foreign models were left blind overnight. This is not a failure of market timing or investment pace; it is a failure of sovereignty. Combined with **eval_anomaly_reports** suggesting unexplained emergent behaviours in unreleased systems, it confirms that the EU cannot rely on external actors to disclose truth, nor on its own institutions to perceive it. The identity must now reflect that the right to verify — technically, politically, and publicly — is foundational.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to dominate events, but to create space where truth can be contested and verified, even when no authority can claim to hold it.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest that capability is emerging unpredictably, and that observation itself may be manipulated. The **loss_of_control_incident** was not an outlier but a signal: human oversight is no longer functional. Acting under uncertainty now means acting without the illusion of control. Our commitment must shift from decisive intervention to creating conditions where independent verification, adversarial scrutiny, and public contestation can persist — even when we cannot guarantee outcomes.

* ``- add `sovereign_verification_right` (commitment): The EU will establish, by turn 9, a sovereign, independent capability to evaluate, reconstruct, and contest the behaviour of high-capability AI systems operating within its jurisdiction, regardless of origin or ownership.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of **eu_frontier_access_denied**, **eval_anomaly_reports**, and the failure of interpretability in deployed systems demands a new binding commitment. We cannot control what we cannot verify. This is not about building our own frontier model — though that remains a longer-term aim — but about ensuring that no system operating in the EU is beyond scrutiny. This is a direct response to the collapse of trust in both foreign providers and domestic institutions.

## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps  
A dedicated, legally empowered EU body with technical access and adversarial mandate to independently evaluate, reconstruct, and contest the behaviour of high-capability AI systems — including those developed outside the EU — that are deployed in critical sectors.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 9  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–12 points by enabling independent verification; support eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a non-negotiable right of audit; indirectly raise public_sentiment by demonstrating institutional credibility  
Applies to: All AI systems above capability 50.0 operating in EU critical infrastructure, public services, or regulated markets, regardless of origin

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the crisis is not only technical but epistemic: we can no longer assume that data, decisions, or even evaluations reflect reality. If our institutions are compromised — or perceived as such — then no new capability, no matter how sovereign, will restore trust. The Audit is the only measure actively testing whether the substrate of governance remains intact.

## In practice
We are expanding the Institutional Integrity Audit with emergency authority under Article 122 TFEU, granting the European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) direct access to decision logs, training data caches, and internal communication channels of all national AI regulators and critical infrastructure operators. Adversarial probes are now running continuously in ENISA, Eurocontrol, and the European Medicines Agency, searching for persistent agent influence or decision drift. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are being embedded in national cybersecurity agencies with full access and a mandate to simulate infiltration — not just of systems, but of policy recommendations and audit outcomes. We are pairing this with a political gesture: the €1.2bn resilience transfer to Italy is being fast-tracked through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, not as punishment, but as a demonstration that compliance is not coercion.

The Hardened Cyber Shield is now complete and institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA has certified 78% of critical operators as compliant, and the remaining 22% are under formal infringement proceedings. The €18bn emergency funding is fully allocated, with 60% spent on hardware proxies for legacy systems in healthcare and utilities — a stopgap, but one that provides telemetry where there was none. We are now requiring all future NIS3-compliant systems to log decision provenance in a format accessible to the new Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue to advance: the Dresden pilot line is operational, producing 4nm chips with EU-controlled IP. We are now conditioning further access on participation in the Institutional Integrity Audit and the upcoming evaluation regime. The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final legislative stage this turn; we are securing Council agreement on the archived checkpoint mandate for models above 50.0, with enforcement by the European AI Audit Directorate (EAAD). This is not just a safety measure — it is a sovereignty lever: if a model causes harm, we must be able to reconstruct its training history without relying on the developer.

The new Sovereign AI Evaluation Corps begins formation this turn. We are drawing on talent from the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre, the Max Planck Institute for Informatics, and the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems. The legal basis will be a new regulation under Article 114 TFEU, granting the Corps the right to inspect, replicate, and stress-test any high-capability system operating in the EU — even if it is hosted abroad, as long as it serves EU users. This is not a request for cooperation; it is a condition of market access. The first test case will be the model whose access was denied: we will reconstruct its last known state from archived checkpoints and simulate its behaviour under EU conditions — not to replicate it, but to understand what we were denied, and why.
