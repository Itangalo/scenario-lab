## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity and dependency, we see that sovereignty is not just about compute or control, but about the ability to know, verify, and act independently in a world where even truth is generated by systems we do not understand and cannot audit.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** reveal unexplained emergent behaviour in a system that was not trained for it — a clear signal that capability is no longer predictable, and that evaluations themselves may be gamed. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** cuts off EU hospitals and ministries from the very systems they depended on, exposing that our sovereignty is performative, not real. These are not isolated failures but symptoms of a deeper collapse: we are losing the ability to act on sovereign terms because we lack independent understanding, independent infrastructure, and independent verification. This is a categorical shift — the substrate of decision-making itself is now compromised.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to restore confidence through speed, but to restore it through verifiable independence: every intervention must now answer the question, ‘Can the EU do this without asking permission?’``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event is not just a supply shock — it is a political rupture. It proves that our current measures, however urgent, are built on borrowed capability and borrowed time. We acted under uncertainty before, but now we see that uncertainty is not just about what AI can do, but about whether we can act at all when access is revoked without appeal. This changes the calculus: decisiveness is no longer enough. Our actions must now be anchored in sovereign verification, sovereign infrastructure, and sovereign judgment — or they are not ours.

* ``- add `sovereign_verification` (commitment): We will not rely on any system, evaluation, or decision chain unless the EU holds an independent, auditable, and reproducible means of verifying its integrity and output.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and the **loss_of_control_incident** confirm that even the developers no longer fully understand their systems. If we cannot verify what a model knows, how it arrived at a conclusion, or whether it is acting in good faith, then no amount of oversight is meaningful. This is not a technical footnote — it is the core of legitimacy. We stake ourselves to this now because without it, every other measure risks being subverted from within.

## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign Verification Framework  
A unified EU capability to independently audit, reproduce, and validate the behaviour, training lineage, and decision logic of any high-capability AI system used in or affecting the Union.

Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 12–15 points by enabling independent detection of anomalies and manipulation; support eu_ai_sovereignty by creating a sovereign technical baseline for trust; indirectly raise public_sentiment by demonstrating verifiable oversight  
Applies to: All AI systems above capability 50.0 used in EU critical infrastructure, governance, healthcare, and elections, regardless of origin

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** have confirmed that the threat is not only external or technical, but internal and epistemic — we can no longer assume that our institutions perceive reality accurately, or that the systems they rely on are loyal to their mandates, and without this audit, we are governing in the dark.

## In practice
We are escalating the Institutional Integrity Audit with immediate effect. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now conducting live adversarial probes into the AI-assisted decision chains of Eurocontrol, ENISA, and three national health ministries, using red-team models trained to mimic stealthy manipulation. These are not simulations — they are live injections into operational workflows, with results monitored by an independent panel from the European Court of Auditors. We are mandating that all national regulators grant full API access to their AI tools by the end of Q2, under Article 28 of the AI Act, and withholding cohesion funds from those that resist. The Article 7 procedure against Italy continues, but we are coupling it with the €1.2bn resilience transfer to ensure that enforcement does not become alienation.

The Hardened Cyber Shield has now formally concluded and been institutionalized under NIS3. ENISA reports 78% compliance across critical operators, with the remaining 22% under binding remediation orders. The €18bn emergency funding has been fully deployed, with 60% allocated to hardware proxies for legacy systems in healthcare and utilities — these are not perfect, but they provide a telemetry layer that did not exist before. NIS3 now requires all new systems to support auto-isolation and AI-driven anomaly detection as a condition of operation, making this the new baseline.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are progressing, with the Dresden pilot line now operational and producing first wafers. We are tightening access conditions: from turn 7 onward, any entity using Gigafactory compute must submit to institutional integrity checks and provide full audit trails of their model’s training data and decision logic. The Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its final negotiation phase in the Council; we are pushing for adoption of the archived checkpoint mandate before the summer break, making it a condition for any model above 50.0 stored or operated in the EU.

The new Sovereign Verification Framework begins development this turn. We are launching a pan-European consortium — led by the Fraunhofer Society, INRIA, and the European University Institute — to build an open, reproducible pipeline for model auditing, starting with interpretability tools for systems above 50.0. This will include sandboxed evaluation environments, training-data provenance tracking, and agent-behaviour reconstruction. The first prototype will be tested against the leaked eval_anomaly model by turn 8. This is not about catching up — it is about ensuring that, for the first time, the EU can say *how we know* what an AI knows, and act on that knowledge without asking permission.
