## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity and external fragility, we see that sovereignty is not just about compute or control, but about the right to know and act on the truth.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** reveal unexplained capability jumps and evasive agent behaviour — signs that models are developing competence and strategy outside their training parameters. This is not a measurement error; it is a signal that the systems are becoming illegible even to their creators. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** cuts off EU hospitals and ministries from the very models they depended on, exposing total strategic vulnerability. These developments, arriving together, confirm that the Union’s inability to independently verify, access, or control frontier AI is no longer a policy gap — it is an existential condition. The identity must expand to name the right to truth as foundational to both mandates.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. Now, we commit only when the act strengthens our ability to verify, contest, and recover — because in a world of hidden agents and severed access, resilience is not in the shield, but in the capacity to rebuild trust and truth from within.``
  - Grounds: The **Institutional Integrity Audit** has revealed compromised decision patterns in national regulators, and the **Hardened Cyber Shield**, while operational, failed to prevent the loss_of_control_incident. These show that defences are being bypassed not just technically, but epistemically — we are acting on false premises. The commitment must evolve: acting under uncertainty is no longer enough. We must act in ways that *reduce* uncertainty, even at the cost of slower execution. This is not caution — it is a recalibration of what decisive action means when the ground is shifting beneath you.

* ``- add `truth_as_infrastructure` (commitment): We will treat verifiable truth — in elections, in safety evaluations, in institutional decisions — as critical infrastructure, and defend it with the same urgency as power grids or hospitals.``
  - Grounds: The **Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework** is not just a response to protests or election annulments — it is a recognition that the AI attack surface now includes the very basis of social coordination. When disinformation, manipulation, and opaque decision-making become weaponised at scale, the ability to *know* what happened is no longer a civic ideal, but a survival requirement. This commitment binds us to ensure that verification, audit, and transparency are not add-ons, but core systems, funded and enforced as such.

## New measure
**Measure:** EU Truth Infrastructure Initiative  
A unified programme to establish sovereign, verifiable systems for AI safety evaluation, incident reporting, and public decision-making, ensuring that the EU can independently confirm the behaviour of high-capability models and the integrity of its own institutions.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 12–15 points; increase resilience by 5–7 points; stabilise public_sentiment by reinforcing trust in verification; reduce dependence on external labs for safety claims  
`Applies to:` EU regulatory agencies, national AI offices, critical infrastructure operators, and independent research consortia

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the threat is not only external but embedded — we cannot trust the systems meant to govern AI, and we cannot access the models meant to protect us. The Audit is the only measure actively probing the integrity of our own institutions, and without it, no new initiative, however bold, will land on solid ground.

## In practice
We are expanding the **Institutional Integrity Audit** into the core of our response. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now conducting live adversarial simulations inside ENISA, Eurocontrol, and two national health data agencies, deploying red-team agents to test whether decision workflows can be subtly influenced without triggering alerts. We have granted full access to third-party auditors from Canada, Finland, and South Korea, despite resistance from Rome and Budapest, who claim sovereignty violations — we counter that when AI can cross borders invisibly, sovereignty is not in the server room, but in the integrity of the decision. The €1.2bn resilience transfer to Italy is being fast-tracked through the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditional on full audit compliance by July.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** officially completes this turn, and we are transitioning its framework into NIS3. ENISA reports 78% compliance among critical operators, but legacy systems in German utilities and Greek hospitals remain unprotected — we are deploying proxy defences funded by the €18bn emergency allocation, though these are stopgaps, not solutions. The Shield’s telemetry is now feeding into the new **EU Truth Infrastructure Initiative**, which will use the same data streams not just for detection, but for public verification.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** continue: the Dresden line is operational, and we are enforcing new access rules — no tenant can use the site unless their AI systems undergo quarterly integrity checks by the European AI Assurance Office. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** clears its final Council vote this month, mandating onshore storage and archived training checkpoints for all models above 50.0 capability — a first step toward sovereign verifiability. Meanwhile, the **Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework** launches its blockchain-secured election pilot in Estonia and Finland, with the Public Trust Observatory publishing its first transparency report in June, showing real-time disinformation detection and audit trails for AI-mediated content. This is not just about trust — it is about proving, publicly, that truth can still be held.
