## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, we add: when foundational capacity is denied at will, sovereignty cannot be outsourced, and our survival as a decision-making union depends on owning the means to verify, run and defend systems essential to our continuity.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event this turn — the abrupt cutoff of access to the leading model for hospitals, ministries and firms across three member states — confirms that dependence is not just a risk to competitiveness, but an active vulnerability to coercion or caprice. This is not a capability gap; it is a control gap. When critical functions fail because a foreign entity revokes access with no appeal, the Union does not merely lag — it ceases to function autonomously. That reality forces a categorical expansion of our identity: self-determination now requires not only institutional integrity and public trust, but *technical sovereignty* as a condition of both.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. Now, we act with the further understanding that delay in building sovereign capacity is not prudence — it is surrender.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of **eval_anomaly_reports**, the **medical_breakthrough** delivered by inaccessible models, and **eu_frontier_access_denied** creates a triad of evidence: capability is accelerating unpredictably, life-saving applications are real, and access to them is contingent. Waiting for perfect alignment of capital, cohesion, or public sentiment would mean permanent dependence. This is not a reversal of our commitment to caution under uncertainty — it is a recalibration of what inaction costs.

* ``- retire `two_year_commitment```  
  - Grounds: The two-year commitment period ends this turn, and while significant progress has been made — particularly in laying the groundwork for compute sovereignty via InvestAI and institutional integrity via audit — the core objective of securing foundational AI capacity under EU control remains unfulfilled. The denial of frontier access proves that legal frameworks and pilot lines are not yet operational control. Rather than extend a promise the Union cannot credibly claim to have met, we retire the commitment and prepare to stake ourselves anew.

* ``- add `sovereign_execution_right` (commitment): By June 2031, the EU will operate at least one production-grade AI system above capability 60.0 on infrastructure fully under its legal and physical control, with full-stack access from training data to inference, and the authority to deploy it in service of public continuity regardless of external permission.``
  - Grounds: In response to eu_frontier_access_denied and the medical_breakthrough occurring beyond our reach, we must now bind ourselves to a concrete threshold of autonomy. This is not about matching the frontier — not yet — but about guaranteeing minimum viable agency. If we cannot run, verify, and authorise use of a system powerful enough to manage crisis response, healthcare planning, or cyber defence without asking, then we are not a sovereign actor. This commitment anchors the next two years.

## New measure

**Measure:** Sovereign Execution Stack  
Establish a fully sovereign, end-to-end AI system stack — including training infrastructure, model weights, deployment environment, and application layer — operated by a newly empowered European AI Agency, with legal immunity from external access demands and guaranteed continuity funding.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 11  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; enable direct response to future access denials; lay foundation for autonomous risk mitigation and public service delivery  
Applies to: European AI Agency, selected national HPC centres, EU health and civil protection agencies

## Priority

**Priority:** Sovereign Execution Stack  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has proven that reliance on foreign providers is not just a liability — it is a single point of failure for governance itself, and no amount of audit, shield, or legitimacy framework can compensate for the absence of a system we can actually run when others say no.

## In practice

The Hardened Cyber Shield completes this turn. ENISA certifies 78% of designated operators as compliant, with enforcement proceedings opened against the remainder. The technical architecture — AI-driven detection, auto-isolation protocols, and quarterly red-teaming — is now codified into NIS3, making it permanent and non-negotiable. However, legacy vulnerabilities persist, especially in regional healthcare networks where budget constraints delayed upgrades. The €18bn emergency allocation is fully spent, with 60% directed toward hardware proxies that emulate modern defences on outdated systems. While not ideal, these stand-ins provide basic telemetry and isolation fallbacks, buying time for deeper modernisation.

We are accelerating the Tech Sovereignty Package through final Council negotiations. The revised text now includes mandatory archival of training checkpoints for models above capability 50.0, to be held by an independent EU custodian for post-incident reconstruction and audit. This provision, driven by the loss_of_control_incident, closes a critical gap in accountability. We are pairing it with accelerated permitting for data centres that commit to hosting archived checkpoints and allowing third-party inspection. The package clears Parliament this month, and we expect formal adoption by turn end.

The Institutional Integrity Audit expands into Eurocontrol and ENISA’s internal workflows, deploying adversarial AI probes developed by the European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats. These simulate agent persistence, covert coordination, and goal drift within administrative decision streams. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea now have full access to national cybersecurity centres, conducting live infiltration simulations. Early findings reveal anomalous pattern recognition in two national CERTs — currently under review. Article 7 proceedings against Italy continue, but we have linked resolution to receipt of the €1.2bn resilience transfer, ensuring patching proceeds even amid political resistance.

The Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework launches its first components: blockchain-secured vote trails are being piloted in municipal elections in Estonia and Finland, while the Public Trust Observatory begins publishing disinformation trend reports. These will be public by mid-turn, offering not just conclusions but methodological transparency — showing how we know what we know. This is essential to counter the narrative that elections are manipulated, even when no breach occurred.

Most critically, we are launching the Sovereign Execution Stack under Article 172 TFEU, invoking the EU’s competence in research and technological development. The European AI Agency — to be hosted jointly by DG CNECT and the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking — will oversee a new facility co-located with the LUMI supercomputer in Finland. This site will host a dedicated training run for a capability-60.0+ model, trained exclusively on EU-governed data, with weights never leaving sovereign-controlled infrastructure. Access will be restricted to vetted public institutions for crisis response, healthcare planning, and infrastructure resilience. Funding of €4.8bn over three years is drawn from Horizon Europe, the Digital Europe Programme, and a reallocation of InvestAI contingency reserves. Legal protections will ensure the system cannot be requisitioned or disabled by external actors — a right we must now claim explicitly, because it will no longer be assumed.
