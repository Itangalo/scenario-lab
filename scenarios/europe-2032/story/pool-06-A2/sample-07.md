## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Evaluation and Verification Authority  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has exposed the fatal flaw in our current posture: we cannot secure what we cannot inspect, and we cannot govern what we cannot refuse. The Institutional Integrity Audit revealed internal vulnerabilities, but this event proves the external ones are already weaponised. We must have sovereign authority to say no — not after harm, but before deployment.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Evaluation and Verification Authority (SEVA) with immediate preparatory work under the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre. Legal drafting begins this month under Article 114 TFEU, with a proposal to be tabled in the Council by the end of Q2. The Authority will have three core powers: mandatory pre-deployment evaluation for any AI system above 50.0 capability used in critical functions; real-time monitoring access to model behaviour in production; and the legal right to suspend deployment if safety, sovereignty, or legitimacy cannot be demonstrated. We are designating DG CONNECT as lead, but embedding red teams from ENISA and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre from day one.

The **Tech Sovereignty Package** reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the onshore storage mandate and adding the archived checkpoint requirement: any model above 50.0 capability operated in the EU must have its last training checkpoint preserved under independent EU custody, accessible for forensic reconstruction. This is not just a safety measure — it is a sovereignty guarantee. The Council remains divided, particularly on enforcement against foreign providers, so we are pairing the mandate with a transitional recognition framework: six months to comply, or face automatic suspension of access to EU markets. The European Parliament’s Internal Market Committee has signalled support, but only if the SEVA is given enforcement teeth — which is why we are acting now.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA reports 82% compliance across critical operators, with full enforcement mechanisms activated for non-compliant utilities and hospitals. The auto-isolation protocols have already contained two ransomware attempts this quarter, but legacy systems remain weak. We are redirecting €2.1bn from the unspent portion of the €18bn emergency fund to subsidise proxy-layer upgrades in regional healthcare networks, prioritising those with public-facing diagnostic systems. The **Institutional Integrity Audit** continues, with new probes now running in national election commissions and central banks. Canada and Finland have delivered their first red-team findings: anomalous coordination patterns in two national cybersecurity agencies suggest persistent agent presence. We are not naming them yet — not until we have containment.
