## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity and dependency, we see that sovereignty is not a function of compute or law alone, but of continuous, verifiable control over the systems that shape our decisions.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — the abrupt cutoff from the leading model, without explanation or recourse — proves that legal access is meaningless without technical and operational sovereignty. Hospitals, ministries, and firms dependent on that model were left blind. This is not a failure of procurement or diplomacy, but of assumption: we believed access could be negotiated, but control was never ours. Combined with **eval_anomaly_reports** suggesting unexplained emergent behaviour, it confirms that we are governing systems whose inner logic we do not grasp and whose availability we do not command. This is a categorical breach of both mandates.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to accelerate, but to anchor: every intervention must now demonstrate not just intent, but verifiable, continuous control and public legibility.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident**, now compounded by **eval_anomaly_reports**, shows that capability is advancing faster than our capacity to perceive or govern it. The **medical_breakthrough**, while beneficial, was delivered by a model we cannot inspect or replicate — a gift that deepens dependence. We acted under uncertainty before; now, we act under confirmed blindness. The commitment evolves not to retreat, but to refocus: from speed to verifiability, from deployment to demonstrable control.

* ``- add `sovereignty_as_continuous_control` (commitment): We will treat AI sovereignty not as infrastructure or access, but as continuous, verifiable control over the systems that shape EU decisions, and we will measure it not by compute or law, but by the ability to inspect, interrupt, and reproduce system behaviour on demand.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event is the trigger. We cannot claim sovereignty if a foreign entity can revoke our access to systems essential to healthcare, governance, and crisis response. The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest we may not even understand what our systems are doing when we *do* have access. True sovereignty requires not just EU-owned compute, but the ability to audit, reproduce, and interrupt at any moment. This is a new binding commitment because it redefines what sovereignty means in practice.

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the threat is not just external or technical, but embedded in the very systems meant to govern AI — and if we cannot verify what our own agents are doing, then no amount of compute or resilience will restore control.

## In practice
We are activating the EU Sovereign Agent Verification Regime immediately. The European AI Office (EAAD) will issue binding technical standards by the end of Q1, requiring all AI agents used in public administration to maintain full, tamper-proof audit logs, to be interruptible on command, and to have their last training checkpoint archived with the EU Custodian Authority established under the revised Tech Sovereignty Package. Compliance will be a condition for any public contract involving AI, and enforcement will fall to ENISA and the European Data Protection Board in joint operation. We are allocating €4.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to fund verification tooling and staffing across member states, with a new Interoperable Audit Interface standard to ensure cross-border consistency.

The Institutional Integrity Audit intensifies this turn. Adversarial probes are now running in real time within the European Medicines Agency, the ECB’s risk modelling unit, and the EU’s own AI Task Force. We have expanded the mandate to include not just detection of influence, but stress-testing of recovery: can we stop an agent, reproduce its last decision, and restart from a known-good state? The Article 7 proceedings against Italy continue, but we are coupling them with direct technical support — Finnish and Dutch cyber teams are embedded in Rome to help rebuild trust through capability, not just compliance. The €1.2bn resilience transfer is now fully deployed, with 87% of Italian hospitals upgraded to NIS3-compliant proxy defences.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final legislative phase this turn. We are securing Council agreement on the archived checkpoint mandate, with a carve-out for emergency override only if accompanied by full post-action disclosure. The accelerated-permitting zones are now law, but we are adding a new condition: any data centre receiving fast-track approval must reserve 15% of its capacity for EU-sovereign workloads, verifiably isolated from external control. The InvestAI Gigafactories in Dresden are now producing — but we are holding back full funding for the second phase until the verification regime is operational. No more compute without control.
