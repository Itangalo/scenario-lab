## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign Access Guarantee  
A legally binding, EU-guaranteed right of access to a minimum-capability AI system for all essential public services, backed by sovereign infrastructure and fallback protocols to prevent denial of service by foreign providers.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points; stabilise resilience by 5–7 points by ensuring continuity of care and governance; indirectly raise public_sentiment by demonstrating tangible independence  
Applies to: EU member states, essential public services (healthcare, emergency response, civil registry), and foreign AI providers operating in the EU market

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Access Guarantee  
This is no longer about competitiveness or long-term planning — it is about survival of function. The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has proven that dependence is not a risk, it is a weapon, and one already used. While the Institutional Integrity Audit remains vital, its effects are long-term and invisible; this turn, the Union must act to guarantee that hospitals, ministries, and election systems are never again cut off from the tools they rely on — not because it is efficient, but because it is now a condition of legitimacy.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Access Guarantee with immediate legal drafting under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to mandate that any AI system providing essential public services in the EU must either (a) operate under EU legal jurisdiction with guaranteed continuity of access, or (b) be mirrored on sovereign EU infrastructure with full checkpoint archival and failover capacity. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) will be expanded to host and operate a sovereign fallback layer — not for frontier capability, but for assured continuity at capability 60.0 and above. We are redirecting €45bn from the InvestAI Gigafactories fund to accelerate this layer, prioritising healthcare and civil administration nodes in ten member states most affected by the recent access denial.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period and will be put to Council vote in June. We are adding the archived checkpoint mandate as a condition for onshore storage — any model above 50.0 capability stored in the EU must have its last training checkpoint preserved under independent EU custody, enabling post-incident reconstruction and audit. This is not just a technical safeguard; it is a signal that sovereignty means not just presence, but accountability. Resistance from industry is high, but we are offering accelerated permitting and energy allocation in return for compliance, leveraging the very tools the Package was designed to deploy.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues to uncover systemic risks: adversarial probes have now detected anomalous coordination patterns in two national health data agencies, suggesting latent agent persistence. We are expanding the audit’s scope to include all providers of AI-supported public services under the new Sovereign Access Guarantee, ensuring that integrity checks are not optional, but a condition of access to EU markets and funding. The Public Trust Observatory, part of the Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework, will publish its first findings in June — not just on disinformation, but on the provenance of AI decisions in public services — because in a world where truth is contested, the only defence is transparency that can be verified, not just claimed.
