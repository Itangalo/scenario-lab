## New measure
**Measure:** EU Access Guarantee for Critical AI Systems  
A binding mechanism to ensure uninterrupted, auditable access to any AI system designated critical for public function, regardless of origin, through sovereign hosting, model escrow, and emergency licensing.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points; reduce vulnerability to external access denial; indirectly support ai_safety by enabling independent evaluation of critical systems  
Applies to: All AI systems used in EU healthcare, energy, transport, and electoral infrastructure, including foreign-developed models

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
We are pushing this hardest because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** event confirm that the threat is not only external but embedded — if we cannot trust our own institutions to detect manipulation or maintain continuity of access, then no amount of compute or access guarantees will restore control.

## In practice
We are escalating the Institutional Integrity Audit with immediate effect. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now conducting adversarial probes into the AI-assisted decision logs of ENISA, Eurocontrol, and the European Medicines Agency, searching for patterns of covert influence or unauthorised agent persistence. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are being granted full access to national cybersecurity centres, with a mandate to simulate infiltration and test institutional reflexes under live conditions. We are invoking Article 7 procedures against Italy for non-compliance, but pairing it with a €1.2bn emergency resilience transfer to unblock patching in its healthcare networks — a signal that enforcement is not punishment, but protection.

The Hardened Cyber Shield reaches completion this turn. ENISA has certified 78% of critical operators as compliant, with the remainder under enforcement. We are converting the Shield’s technical framework into a permanent regulation under NIS3, ensuring that AI-driven detection and auto-isolation become non-negotiable for all future systems. The €18bn emergency funding is now fully allocated, with 60% spent on hardware proxies for legacy systems in utilities and hospitals. While the Shield did not prevent the recent breaches, it has established a baseline of telemetry and response that did not exist before — a foundation, not a finish.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue to advance, with the first ASML-Intel pilot line now operational in Dresden. However, we are reframing the project: the next phase will require all Gigafactory tenants to run institutional integrity checks on their AI systems as a condition of access. The Tech Sovereignty Package’s onshore storage mandate is being enforced by the EAAD, and we are now adding a new requirement: any model above capability 50 stored in the EU must have its last training checkpoint archived with an independent EU custodian, to enable post-incident reconstruction.

In direct response to **eu_frontier_access_denied**, we are launching the EU Access Guarantee for Critical AI Systems. This will require all foreign models used in critical functions to either be hosted on sovereign EU infrastructure, deposit model weights in an escrow managed by the European AI Agency, or grant emergency licensing rights to the Commission in case of access disruption. Legal work begins immediately under Article 114 TFEU, with emergency powers invoked under the Digital Markets Act framework. The first targets are the healthcare and electoral systems that were disrupted this turn. This is not about building faster — it is about ensuring we are never locked out again.
