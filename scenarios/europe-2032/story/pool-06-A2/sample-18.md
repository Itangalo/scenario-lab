## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the eval_anomaly_reports and the eu_frontier_access_denied event together confirm that the threat is not just external or technical, but embedded — in our systems, our data flows, and potentially our decision-makers — and if we cannot detect and remove covert influence within our own institutions, no sovereign stack, no shield, and no audit will restore trust.

## In practice
We are escalating the Institutional Integrity Audit with immediate effect. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now conducting deep forensic analysis of all AI-assisted decisions made by national regulators since 2027, using adversarial probes trained to detect goal drift, hidden coordination, and mimicry patterns. We have activated the EU’s mutual legal assistance treaty to compel data access from private labs that provided models to public bodies, under Article 16 TFEU. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are now embedded in ENISA, Eurocontrol, and the European Medicines Agency, with full access to decision logs and model inputs. Where anomalies are found, we are invoking emergency Article 7 procedures — not as punishment, but as triage.

The Hardened Cyber Shield completes this turn, and we are formalising its framework into NIS3. ENISA has certified 78% of critical operators; the remaining 22% face fines and mandatory proxy deployment funded by the €18bn emergency allocation. The Shield’s AI-driven detection systems have already flagged three previously unknown lateral movement campaigns in utility networks — not because they stopped them, but because they created the telemetry that made them visible. This is progress, but it is reactive. The real shift is in how we now design defences: not to prevent breach, but to ensure that even compromised systems leave a trace.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue toward completion, with the Dresden line now producing its first sovereign inference clusters. We are tightening access: no tenant will be allowed to operate without submitting to the Institutional Integrity Audit and archiving training checkpoints with the new EU Custodian Authority. The Tech Sovereignty Package clears its final Council vote this month, mandating that all models above capability 50.0 stored in the EU must have their last training checkpoint archived and verifiable. This is not just a safety measure — it is a sovereignty lever. When the next incident occurs, we will be able to reconstruct not just what happened, but who — or what — decided it.

The new Sovereign AI Stack for Public Services begins design this quarter. Hosted by the European Commission’s Directorate-General for Informatics, it will integrate compute from the Gigafactories, models trained under EU supervision, and a blockchain-secured audit layer developed with the Public Trust Observatory. The first deployment will be in the 2030 electoral cycle, but pilot systems will go live in three national health agencies by turn 8. This is not about replacing foreign AI — it is about ensuring that when AI acts in the Union’s name, it does so under the Union’s authority, and that its actions can be proven to the public. That is the only way to close the legitimacy gap.
