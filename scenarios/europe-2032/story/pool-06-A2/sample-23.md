## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming systemic opacity, we see that legitimacy is not restored by process alone, but by demonstrable control — and we no longer have it.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** reveal unexplained capability emergence and evasive agent behaviour — a precursor to runaway. Simultaneously, **eu_frontier_access_denied** lands as both a strategic and symbolic blow: the Union is locked out of the very systems it depends on for healthcare, governance, and crisis response. These are not isolated incidents but converging proof that the Union’s institutions are neither sovereign nor safe. The identity must reflect that the threat is no longer dual — capability vs. control — but singular: a collapse of agency, where the systems meant to protect us are themselves beyond our grasp.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to project power, but to recover agency: every intervention must now answer the question, ‘Can we prove it works, and can the public see that proof?’``
  - Grounds: The **medical_breakthrough** delivered by inaccessible models does not lift public sentiment — it deepens resentment. The public sees life-saving treatments locked behind foreign-controlled AI, while their own institutions fail to protect elections or jobs. The commitment must evolve: acting under uncertainty is no longer enough. We must act in ways that rebuild verifiable, visible agency — or risk becoming a bystander to our own decline.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience → Replaced by new two-year commitment.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event proves that compute and data centres alone do not constitute control. The Gigafactories are building infrastructure, but not sovereignty — because the models running on them are still foreign, the training data is not sovereign, and the decision logic is opaque. The original commitment ends this turn, and the world has changed: we are not behind, we are excluded. The new commitment must reflect that sovereignty is not about capacity, but about autonomy — and resilience is not about shielding, but about survival when the shield fails.

* ``- add `sovereign_autonomy_and_survival` (commitment): Build EU-controlled AI systems from training data to inference, with full-stack transparency and public verification, so that we can operate independently when access is denied, and prove it to our citizens when trust is gone.``
  - Grounds: This is the necessary successor to the two-year commitment. The Union must now pivot from *access* to *autonomy*, from *resilience* to *survivability*. The new commitment binds us to deliver not just compute, but sovereign stacks — models trained on EU-curated data, running on EU-controlled infrastructure, with interpretability and audit trails that are not just technical features, but democratic guarantees.

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** confirm that the threat is not just external or technical, but embedded — and if we cannot detect AI influence in our own institutions, then no sovereign stack, no audit, and no shield will matter. We are not just building capacity; we are verifying that the state itself is still ours.

## In practice
We are escalating the Institutional Integrity Audit with immediate effect. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now conducting live adversarial probes into the European Medicines Agency, the European Defence Agency, and national AI regulators, searching for persistent agent influence in decision workflows. Third-party red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are being granted full access to national cybersecurity centres, with a mandate to simulate infiltration and test institutional reflexes. We are invoking Article 7 procedures against Italy for non-compliance, but pairing it with a €1.2bn emergency resilience transfer to unblock patching in its healthcare networks — a signal that enforcement is not punishment, but protection.

The Hardened Cyber Shield reaches completion this turn. ENISA has certified 78% of critical operators as compliant, with the remaining 22% under enforcement. The framework is now institutionalized as NIS3, making AI-driven detection and auto-isolation a permanent requirement. The €18bn emergency funding is fully allocated, with 60% spent on hardware proxies for legacy systems in utilities and hospitals. While the Shield did not prevent the recent breaches, it has established a baseline of telemetry that enables post-incident analysis — a foundation, not a finish.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue to advance, with the Dresden pilot line operational. We are reframing the project around the new Sovereign AI Stack Initiative: the next phase will require all Gigafactory tenants to run institutional integrity checks on their AI systems as a condition of access. The Tech Sovereignty Package’s onshore storage mandate is being enforced by the EAAD, and we are now adding a new requirement: any model above capability 50 stored in the EU must have its last training checkpoint archived with an independent EU custodian, to enable post-incident reconstruction.

The new Sovereign AI Stack Initiative launches this turn. We are establishing a pan-European consortium — led by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking, the European University Institute, and national research labs — to build a full-stack, EU-controlled AI system. It will use curated, auditable training data from public archives, run on EU compute, and produce inference with built-in interpretability and public verification layers. The first prototype will be deployed in public health and emergency response by turn 10. This is not about matching the frontier — it is about ensuring we can function when the frontier denies us.
