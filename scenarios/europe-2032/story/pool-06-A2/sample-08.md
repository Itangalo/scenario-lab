## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive  
A binding legal framework requiring that any AI system essential to EU public functions — including healthcare, justice, border control, and critical infrastructure — must either be developed under EU legal jurisdiction with full model access, or subject to enforceable, auditable, and reciprocal access guarantees.

Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)

Finishes on turn: 8

Applies to: All EU institutions, member state agencies, and critical infrastructure operators using AI systems above capability 50.0

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has proven that sovereignty is not just about building capacity, but about enforcing control — and when the tools essential to governance can be withdrawn without appeal, no amount of resilience or audit can restore agency; only binding rules can.

## In practice
We are acting on the **Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive** with immediate legal drafting under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to establish that no AI system may be used in essential public functions unless its provider guarantees uninterrupted, auditable access under EU law — or faces exclusion from public procurement. The European Commission’s Legal Service is working with DG CONNECT and the EAAD to define “essential function” and “capability threshold” in consultation with ENISA and the European Data Protection Board. By mid-March, we will table a proposal in the Council’s Internal Market configuration, backed by a coalition of France, Germany, and Poland, who have all experienced disruptions from the recent access denial. We are offering transitional recognition for providers who commit to onshore deployment and checkpoint archiving under EU supervision — but the default path is clear: no sovereignty, no access.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** intensifies this turn. Adversarial probes have now been deployed into the European Medicines Agency and Eurojust, following anomalous approval patterns in fast-tracked AI-assisted drug reviews. Third-party red teams from Canada and Finland are conducting live simulations of AI-driven influence campaigns within national election commissions, with real-time reporting to the Public Trust Observatory. We are expanding the audit’s mandate to include not just detection, but public disclosure: every institution assessed will publish a summary of findings by turn 7, regardless of outcome. This is not just about cleaning house — it is about proving we can.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** continue toward completion, with the Dresden line now producing test wafers. We are conditioning the next €40bn tranche on two new requirements: first, that all tenants run their models through the Institutional Integrity Audit before deployment; second, that they contribute 5% of their compute cycles to the EU’s sovereign evaluation capacity under category 5. This is no longer just a compute play — it is the physical foundation of our autonomy. Meanwhile, the **Tech Sovereignty Package** clears its final Council vote this month, embedding the archived checkpoint mandate into law. From turn 7 onward, any model above 50.0 trained in the EU must deposit its last training checkpoint with the European AI Archive, under state-actor-grade security — a black box for post-incident truth.
