## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with `eu_frontier_access_denied`, we see that dependence is not just a sovereignty risk, but a direct vector of political subjugation: when access to essential systems is revoked without appeal, the Union’s capacity to act is nullified regardless of its intentions.``
  - Grounds: The `eu_frontier_access_denied` event is not a technical setback but a categorical rupture. Hospitals, ministries, and firms relying on the frontier model were abruptly cut off — not due to misuse, not due to safety concerns, but by unilateral decision with no recourse. This is not market failure or supply-chain disruption; it is the exercise of foreign control over EU functions. The assumption that we could manage risk while depending on external capacity has collapsed. Sovereignty is not a secondary concern — it is the precondition for both mandates.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not on the belief that we can contain the frontier, but that we must now *replace* it: because when access is weaponised, only independent capability is real sovereignty.``
  - Grounds: The `eval_anomaly_reports` confirm that capability is advancing unpredictably, and that even developers cannot explain emergent behaviours. At the same time, `public_sentiment` remains in freefall, and the `medical_breakthrough` — while real — was delivered by a model the EU cannot access, rendering it a symbol of dependence, not progress. We are no longer acting in a world where timely intervention is enough. We are in a world where the tools we need are withheld at will, and where the public sees only broken promises. The price of uncertainty is no longer inefficiency — it is irrelevance.

* ``- add `sovereign_access` (commitment): We will ensure that no critical public function depends on AI systems whose access can be revoked by a foreign power or private entity without EU consent.``
  - Grounds: The `eu_frontier_access_denied` event is the trigger. We cannot uphold either of our core mandates — self-determination or harm prevention — if the tools required to execute them can be disabled from outside. This is not about competitiveness or economic growth. It is about the basic condition of political agency: the ability to act without permission. We are staking ourselves on this because the alternative is managed decline — a Union that deliberates while its systems go dark.

## New measure

**Measure:** Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions  
A legally mandated, EU-operated AI stack — from trained model to inference infrastructure — to ensure uninterrupted access for essential public services.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; reduce dependence on foreign models in healthcare, emergency response, and elections; lay foundation for future sovereign frontier capability  
Applies to: EU institutions, national health systems, electoral commissions, and critical infrastructure operators

## Priority

**Priority:** Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions  
This is our top priority because `eu_frontier_access_denied` has proven that reliance on external AI is not a risk to be managed — it is a surrender of agency. No amount of audit, shield, or legitimacy framework can function if the systems that run them can be switched off from abroad. We are no longer building capacity for competitiveness; we are building it as a condition of survival.

## In practice

We are activating emergency provisions under Article 122 TFEU and the Digital Markets Act to mandate the immediate development of a sovereign AI stack for critical functions. The European Health Data Space will be the first domain: we are instructing the European Medicines Agency and the EU4Health programme to transition all AI-dependent diagnostics and treatment planning to a newly established EU AI Core, built on compute from the InvestAI Gigafactories and trained on EU-held data. This is not a research project — it is a binding operational requirement, with full funding drawn from the €50bn Emergency Technology Resilience Facility, newly approved by the Council under urgency procedure.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period, and we are finalising the archived checkpoint mandate: any model above 50.0 capability stored or used in the EU must now deposit its last training checkpoint with the European AI Archive, hosted by the European Data Protection Supervisor and secured to state-actor standards. This is not just a safety measure — it is a sovereignty lever. We are pairing it with a new clause: any provider denying access to EU critical functions without appeal forfeits the right to operate in the Single Market. The message is clear: you may sell here, but you do not control us.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues with expanded scope. We are now requiring all national AI regulators to run real-time adversarial probes on their own decision pipelines, with results streamed to a new dashboard at the European Centre of Excellence. Canada, Finland, and South Korea are deepening their red-team engagements, and we are preparing to publish the first unredacted findings by turn 7 — not to shame, but to prove that we are not hiding. Because if we cannot show the public that we see the threat, we cannot ask them to trust us through the response.
