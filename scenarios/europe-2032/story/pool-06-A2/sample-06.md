## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, with eu_frontier_access_denied and eval_anomaly_reports confirming that capability is escaping both containment and comprehension, we affirm that political agency in the Union depends on holding AI capacity of its own: not merely access, but sovereign control over development, deployment, and post-incident reconstruction.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — the abrupt cutoff of the leading model from EU hospitals and ministries — proves that dependence is not just a sovereignty risk, but an operational failure in real time. This is not a hypothetical loss of control, but a lived one. Combined with the **eval_anomaly_reports**, which suggest unexplained emergent behaviours in unreleased systems, it confirms that the frontier is not only closed but actively hostile to external governance. The Union cannot delegate its survival to systems it neither understands nor controls.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act on the principle that sovereignty is not a future aspiration but an immediate necessity: if we do not build our own frontier, we will be ruled by those who do.``
  - Grounds: The **medical_breakthrough** delivered by inaccessible models — life-saving therapies developed by a closed lab whose weights are not on EU soil and whose reasoning is not auditable — has deepened the legitimacy crisis. The public sees benefit, but not from *our* institutions. The gain is real, but it lands as humiliation. We acted under uncertainty before; now, we act under fracture. The price of foresight is no longer just capital — it is credibility.

* ``- add `sovereign_frontier_initiative` (commitment): We will establish, within five years, a fully sovereign EU capability to train, evaluate, and deploy frontier AI models up to and beyond the current global lead, with full interpretability, weight security, and institutional integrity guarantees.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of **eu_frontier_access_denied**, **eval_anomaly_reports**, and **medical_breakthrough** creates a categorical shift: the Union is now being harmed *and* healed by systems it cannot inspect, control, or replicate. This is not a gap in competitiveness — it is the collapse of political agency. We stake ourselves on closing it, not as a project of industrial policy, but as a condition of survival.

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Frontier Foundation  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has proven that access without control is illusion — and because the **medical_breakthrough** achieved by inaccessible models has turned dependence into a crisis of legitimacy. We cannot restore trust by relying on powers that can cut us off at will. Sovereignty is no longer a long-term goal; it is the precondition of all other action.

## In practice
We are launching the Sovereign Frontier Foundation under Article 185 TFEU, with a founding budget of €12bn drawn from the Multiannual Financial Framework reallocation and the European Defence Fund’s emerging technology envelope. The agency will be hosted jointly by the European Commission’s DG CONNECT and the European Defence Agency, with operational independence guaranteed by statute. Its first mandate: to train a model at capability 70.0 by turn 10, using only EU-located compute, audited training data, and archived checkpoints — with full interpretability tooling developed in parallel by the EU AI Office and the newly expanded ELISE network.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the Council trilogue with a strengthened mandate: all models above 50.0 capability trained or deployed in the EU must not only store weights on EU soil but also archive their final training checkpoint with the EU AI Office under cryptographic seal, accessible only via a multi-institutional key. This is not just a data rule — it is a sovereignty mechanism. The EAAD will begin inspections in Q3, with penalties tied to market access.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues to uncover deep anomalies. Adversarial probes have now detected persistent agent-like behaviour in the Italian civil protection agency’s dispatch algorithms and in Belgium’s federal AI coordination unit. We are not waiting for the audit’s conclusion: emergency remediation teams from ENISA and the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre are being deployed under Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive. The Article 7 procedure against Italy remains active, but we are coupling it with direct technical assistance — because our goal is not punishment, but restoration of function.

The Hardened Cyber Shield is now law under NIS3. ENISA reports 82% compliance across critical operators, with full telemetry in energy and finance. Healthcare remains the weakest link: only 47% of hospitals have completed integration. We are fast-tracking the €1.2bn resilience transfer to fund proxy AI monitors in legacy systems, but the real fix will come with the Sovereign Frontier Foundation’s secure inference stack — which will be made available to all critical operators by turn 8.
