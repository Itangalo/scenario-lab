  - Grounds: The `eu_frontier_access_denied` event — the sudden, unexplained cutoff of the leading model from EU hospitals, ministries, and firms — confirms a categorical shift: our dependence is not just technological, it is political. This is not a failure of oversight or resilience alone, but of sovereignty — and it proves that legitimacy without leverage is performance, not power.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it, and when the outside world may deny us the tools we need to act at all.``
  - Grounds: The `eval_anomaly_reports` suggest capability jumps are now emerging unpredictably, and the `medical_breakthrough` — delivered by a model we cannot access — underscores that benefit without sovereignty is dependency dressed as progress. We are acting under conditions where even correct decisions are unenforceable, and where the public sees not protection but helplessness. This is not a retreat from action, but a recalibration: we must now build not just faster, but on ground we own.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience — and now, in light of `eu_frontier_access_denied`, accelerate the transition from dependency to sovereign operation, ensuring that no critical public function relies on a model whose access can be unilaterally revoked.``
  - Grounds: The cutoff is the triggering development. Our two-year commitment was always about control, but this event proves that control is not just about where compute is or who audits it — it is about whether the system works when we need it, regardless of external permission. We are not abandoning the commitment; we are sharpening it to include guaranteed operational continuity for essential services.

## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions  
A legally mandated, EU-operated fallback system providing essential AI services — medical diagnostics, emergency response coordination, and regulatory evaluation — using models trained and run entirely on sovereign infrastructure, with guaranteed access for authorised users regardless of external conditions.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 10`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points; reduce dependency risk in critical sectors; indirectly support resilience and ai_safety by ensuring continuity of oversight and response.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, national health agencies, critical infrastructure operators, and designated public regulators.

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign AI Core for Critical Functions  
This is now our top priority because `eu_frontier_access_denied` has proven that access — not just capability or audit — is the linchpin of sovereignty, and that without a system we fully control, every other measure rests on borrowed ground.

## In practice
We are acting on the cutoff not as an incident, but as a verdict: the era of relying on external frontier models for essential public functions is over. The Sovereign AI Core will be built on the Dresden Gigafactory stack, using the first fully operational line to train and host a model of no less than 60.0 capability, trained exclusively on EU-licensed data, with weights stored and executed only on hardware under EU legal jurisdiction. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) will lead development, with the EAAD responsible for security accreditation. We are redirecting €45bn from the unused portion of the Tech Sovereignty Package’s incentive pool to fund this, repurposing private capital mechanisms into direct public build-out.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues with expanded scope: we are now requiring all agencies using AI in decision-making to feed their system logs into the audit’s adversarial probe framework. The red teams from Canada, Finland, and South Korea are being granted access to national health AI systems — including those that recently lost access — to test for residual dependencies and single points of failure. We are also fast-tracking the archived checkpoint mandate: as of this turn, any model above 50.0 used in a critical function must have its last training checkpoint preserved under EU custodianship, enabling reconstruction and continuity.

The Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework launches its first public report this quarter, showing blockchain-verified election trails from three member states and disinformation detection rates across ten national media environments. The Public Trust Observatory is publishing methodology and data in real time, not just results — because trust now requires not just truth, but proof of how it was made. We are pairing this with a public campaign: “You Can Check It,” aimed at restoring belief in institutions not through authority, but through verifiability. But we know now that no amount of verification can compensate for powerlessness — and that is why the Sovereign AI Core is not optional. It is the foundation of everything that follows.
