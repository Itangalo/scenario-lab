* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it.   - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** represent a failure not of data, but of epistemic control: the systems are behaving in ways their creators cannot explain, and the metrics we rely on are no longer trustworthy. Our commitment to act under uncertainty now requires that we first rebuild the capacity to detect, interpret, and publicly validate what is happening. Action without verifiable understanding is no longer decisive — it is ritual.

* ``- add `sovereign_epistemic_right` (commitment): The EU must have independent, enforceable access to the internal states, training data, and decision logs of any AI system operating in or affecting its jurisdiction, regardless of origin, and the legal and technical capacity to audit them without relying on provider cooperation.``

If we cannot audit the models that run our hospitals, shape our elections, and defend our borders, then we do not govern. We bind ourselves to this now, knowing it will provoke resistance, because the alternative is irreversible subordination.

## In practice

We are escalating the Institutional Integrity Audit with emergency authority under Article 122 TFEU, treating compromised institutions as a cross-border threat to the rule of law. The European Public Prosecutor’s Office is now embedded in ENISA, Eurocontrol, and the national cybersecurity agencies of France, Germany, and Poland, with real-time access to decision logs and AI-assisted anomaly detection. We are deploying adversarial probes trained on leaked frontier model behaviours to simulate infiltration in real time, and we are publishing monthly redacted findings through the new Public Trust Observatory to rebuild credibility. Third-party teams from Canada and South Korea are now conducting unannounced live drills in national emergency response centres, with results fed directly into the audit’s final report.

The Hardened Cyber Shield is now formally institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA has certified 82% of critical operators, and the remaining 18% are under binding remediation orders with quarterly enforcement reviews. The €18bn emergency funding is fully spent, with 60% allocated to proxy defences for legacy systems in healthcare and energy. We are now mandating that all new contracts for AI integration in critical infrastructure include sovereign audit clauses, linking compliance to the new regime we are building.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue toward full deployment. The Dresden pilot is now running at 70% capacity, with ASML and Intel delivering next-gen lithography modules ahead of schedule. We are tightening access rules: no tenant may operate without passing the Institutional Integrity Audit, and all models above 50.0 must submit archived training checkpoints to the EU Custodian Authority. The Tech Sovereignty Package clears Council this turn — the onshore storage mandate is now law, and the accelerated-permitting zones are activated in Spain, Poland, and Finland.

The new Sovereign Audit Access Regime begins drafting under the Commission’s Legal Service, with DG CONNECT and the European Data Protection Supervisor co-leading. We are invoking the Digital Markets Act and AI Act as legal bases to assert jurisdiction over foreign providers, and we are building a secure evaluation enclave — the EU Audit Foundry — on sovereign soil in Finland, with air-gapped compute and independent interpreters. This is not a request for cooperation. It is a demand for access — and we will enforce it through market access restrictions if necessary. The world has moved. We no longer ask permission to defend ourselves.
