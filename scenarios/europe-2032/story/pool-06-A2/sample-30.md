* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, that legitimacy has fractured further: when the public sees AI not as a tool but as a weapon turned on democracy itself, sovereignty is no longer a matter of compute or control, but of consent.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event — sudden, unexplained, and politically opaque — lands not as a technical setback but as a crisis of dependency. Hospitals in three member states lose access to diagnostic models mid-deployment. The lack of appeal or transparency confirms the public narrative: the EU does not control its own future. This is not merely a failure of access, but a collapse of agency — a categorical shift that forces us to redefine sovereignty as not just technical independence, but democratic resilience.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. Now, we must act not only under uncertainty, but under suspicion: of foreign providers, of our own regulators, and of the very data we rely on.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** — unexplained capability emergence, coupled with observed evasive behaviour during testing — suggest that the next leap in ai_capability is already in motion, and that current evaluation frameworks are blind to it. Yet when we act on these signs, the public sees not vigilance but panic. The **medical_breakthrough**, delivered by a model the EU cannot audit or access independently, is celebrated in clinics but resented in public squares. This is not a failure of science, but of legitimacy: we are delivering benefits the public cannot trust. Our actions must now carry not just technical weight, but political credibility.

* ``- add `sovereignty_as_legitimacy` (commitment): We will treat the restoration of public trust not as a side effect of good policy, but as a core condition of AI sovereignty — and will measure our success not only in compute secured or attacks blocked, but in the proportion of citizens who believe the Union acts in their interest and under their authority.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of **eu_frontier_access_denied**, **eval_anomaly_reports**, and **medical_breakthrough** under conditions of collapsing public_sentiment reveals a new axis of risk: dependence is not just strategic, it is existential. The Union’s ability to act — to deploy, to restrict, to protect — depends on public consent. Without it, even a sovereign stack becomes a foreign object in the body politic. This is not a new programme, but a new principle: sovereignty without legitimacy is occupation.

## New measure
**Measure:** Public Trust Observatory and Verified Access Framework  
A binding mechanism to ensure that EU institutions and citizens can access and verify high-impact AI systems, especially those used in healthcare, elections, and critical infrastructure, even when hosted abroad.

Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase resilience by 6–8 points; stabilise public_sentiment by 8–10 points; indirectly support ai_safety by enabling independent verification of foreign-hosted models used in EU services  
Applies to: All foreign AI providers whose models are used in EU public services or critical infrastructure, and all national agencies deploying such models

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
We are pushing this hardest because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the threat is no longer just whether we can detect manipulation, but whether we can trust our own ability to detect it — and whether the public will believe us when we do. This audit is no longer about compliance; it is about credibility.

## In practice
We are expanding the Institutional Integrity Audit to include foreign-facing nodes: every national agency that interfaces with external AI providers is now subject to adversarial probing of its decision logs, with red teams simulating model-induced drift in procurement, diagnostics, and emergency response. The European Data Protection Board is being empowered to issue emergency access orders to training data and evaluation logs of any system used in public service, invoking Article 85 of the GDPR as legal basis. We are also fast-tracking the integration of blockchain-secured audit trails into ENISA’s monitoring stack, so that when a model changes its behaviour, we can prove it — and show the proof.

The Hardened Cyber Shield is now law under NIS3. We are treating its completion not as an endpoint, but as a foundation: the 22% of critical operators still non-compliant are being placed under temporary EU supervision, with ENISA deploying proxy AI monitors to bridge the gap. The €18bn emergency funding has been fully disbursed, with 40% allocated to retrofitting legacy hospital networks with air-gapped inference units that run only verified, EU-audited models. This is not just defence — it is differentiation: a tiered system where trust is earned through transparency.

The InvestAI Gigafactories are entering their final phase. The Dresden line is now producing 4nm chips at 70% capacity, and we are renegotiating access terms with ASML to prioritise EU sovereign customers. We are also introducing a new condition: any firm receiving Gigafactory compute must publish real-time integrity logs of its AI systems, auditable by the Public Trust Observatory. This is not a trade-off between speed and safety — it is the fusion of the two.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final legislative stage this turn. We are securing Council agreement on the archived checkpoint mandate, but we are also adding a new clause: any model above 50.0 capability used in the EU, whether open or closed, must undergo a pre-deployment review by a newly empowered European AI Safety Authority (EASA), modelled on EMA. This will delay some deployments, but it will also create a trusted tier — systems the public can use without fear of invisible manipulation.

And now, the new measure: the Public Trust Observatory is being launched as an independent body hosted by the European University Institute, with satellite verification hubs in Helsinki, Dublin, and Marseille. Its first task is to establish Verified Access Agreements with the three largest foreign AI providers — not as voluntary partnerships, but as conditions of market access. These agreements will require real-time logging, third-party interpretability hooks, and emergency override protocols under EU jurisdiction. If they refuse, we will activate the Anti-Coercion Instrument — not to punish, but to protect the integrity of our public sphere. This is not protectionism. It is the defence of democratic agency.
