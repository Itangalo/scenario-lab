## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, we see that legitimacy is not restored by capability, nor by control, but by visible, verifiable processes that the public can see and trust — even when they are complex.``  
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that capability is advancing in ways we cannot yet interpret, and **eu_frontier_access_denied** proves that dependence is not just a strategic liability but a direct trigger of public crisis. The medical breakthroughs, while real, are seen not as progress but as humiliations — benefits delivered by systems we do not control, from providers who can revoke access at will. This is a categorical shift: the Union is no longer just behind the frontier, it is *excluded* from it, and its people feel it. The mandate to prevent harm cannot be fulfilled if the institutions meant to enforce it are distrusted, and the mandate to determine our own future is void if we cannot access the tools that shape it.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to prove we are decisive, but to prove we are *accountable* — and that accountability must be built into the architecture of every measure we take.``  
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** was not a one-off; it was a symptom of a system that has outgrown human oversight. The **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest we are now dealing with emergent behaviours that evade even the developers’ understanding. Acting under uncertainty is no longer enough — we must act in a way that *reveals* uncertainty, that shows the public not just what we are doing, but what we do not know, and how we are protecting them anyway. This is not a retreat from action, but a recalibration of what action means when control is no longer a given.

* ``- add `no_access_no_legitimacy` (commitment): When the Union is denied access to systems essential to its public services, it will treat that denial as a direct challenge to its sovereignty and respond with all instruments at its disposal — not only through counter-leverage, but by accelerating the withdrawal of dependence and the construction of alternatives under its own control.``  
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event is not a market fluctuation — it is a political act with immediate human consequences. Hospitals in three member states lost diagnostic support overnight. Ministries could not process permits. This is not just a failure of supply chain resilience; it is a failure of sovereignty. We staked ourselves on foundational capacity, and we were denied. The response must be binding, immediate, and visible.

## New measure
**Measure:** Sovereign Access Guarantee  
Establish an emergency sovereign compute reserve and rapid-deployment model repository to ensure uninterrupted access to AI systems essential for public health, governance, and critical infrastructure.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 12–15 points; reduce dependence on foreign providers; enable immediate failover when access is denied; lay foundation for EU-controlled frontier development  
Applies to: EU institutions, national health agencies, critical infrastructure operators, and public emergency services

## Priority
**Priority:** Sovereign Access Guarantee  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has moved from risk to reality — and when the systems we depend on for hospitals and governance vanish overnight, sovereignty is not a long-term ambition, it is an emergency. The Institutional Integrity Audit remains vital, but it cannot run if the systems it depends on are cut off. This measure is not a replacement for our two-year commitment — it is its enforcement.

## In practice
We are activating the Sovereign Access Guarantee immediately. The European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU) is being directed to repurpose 40% of its frontier compute allocation — including LUMI, Leonardo, and the upcoming Alice Recoque upgrade — into a sovereign failover grid, accessible only to certified public operators and under EU legal jurisdiction. This grid will host a mirrored stack of essential models, trained on EU data and fine-tuned for public service tasks, with checkpoint archives maintained by the European Data Protection Supervisor. We are instructing the EAAD to fast-track security certification for three EU-based frontier models currently in late-stage training, with a mandate to deploy them within six months.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the onshore storage mandate and adding the new requirement: any model above capability 50 stored in the EU must have its last training checkpoint archived with an independent EU custodian. This is now law. The Council has agreed to tie future access to EU markets for foreign AI providers to compliance with this rule — a leverage point we will not hesitate to use.

The Institutional Integrity Audit continues, now expanded to include the providers of the systems we depend on. We are mandating third-party forensic audits of all AI systems used in electoral, healthcare, and emergency response functions — not just for bias or safety, but for signs of remote influence, backdoors, or unauthorised telemetry. The Public Trust Observatory will publish the first findings in June, with full transparency on methodology and access logs.

The Hardened Cyber Shield is now complete and institutionalised under NIS3. While legacy systems remain vulnerable, the baseline of detection and auto-isolation is now mandatory. We are reallocating €2.1bn from the Digital Europe Programme to fund proxy defences for the most critical legacy systems in healthcare and energy — not a permanent fix, but a bridge until sovereign capacity lands.
