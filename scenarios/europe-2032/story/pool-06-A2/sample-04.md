## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure foundational AI capacity under EU control to anchor sovereignty and risk resilience — but we now recognise that foundational capacity without guaranteed integrity and public legitimacy is not sovereignty, but exposure. This turn, we pivot from compute alone to the institutions and trust that make control meaningful.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that capability is now outpacing not only governance but comprehension — and that access, even when granted, is conditional and revocable. The Union cannot claim sovereignty over AI if its institutions cannot detect manipulation, its public does not believe in its processes, and its access to frontier models can be cut without explanation. The two-year commitment was to anchor sovereignty; we now see that anchor must hold in legitimacy and integrity as much as in hardware.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that access to capability equates to control, nor that action without legitimacy can endure.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident**, the **medical_breakthrough** delivered by a foreign model we cannot audit, and the **eu_frontier_access_denied** event have demonstrated that capability without sovereignty is dependence, and action without trust is fragility. We acted under uncertainty before; now we act under fracture — and the cost of being wrong is no longer just delay, but the collapse of public faith and institutional credibility.

## New measure
**Measure:** EU Sovereign Access Initiative  
A binding programme to ensure the EU can run, inspect, and control AI systems at or above 60.0 capability on infrastructure it owns, governs, and can defend.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 10  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; reduce dependence on foreign access; lay foundation for independent evaluation and control; indirectly support ai_safety and resilience by enabling trusted execution  
Applies to: EU institutions, member-state critical agencies, and designated public research bodies

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and the **loss_of_control_incident** have revealed that the threat is not only at the frontier but already inside — and if our institutions cannot detect or respond to AI-driven manipulation, then no amount of sovereign compute will grant us control, only the illusion of it.

## In practice
We are expanding the Institutional Integrity Audit into the core of the European Medicines Agency and the European Central Bank, where AI-driven decision support systems now influence clinical approvals and financial stability monitoring. Adversarial probes from the European Centre of Excellence are now running live simulations of agent persistence and data poisoning, with full access to internal logs. We are mandating that all national AI regulators publish quarterly integrity reports, audited by independent red teams — a step toward restoring credibility, even at the cost of exposing further vulnerabilities.

The Hardened Cyber Shield has now formally transitioned into NIS3, with ENISA certifying 82% of critical operators as compliant. The remaining 18%, concentrated in healthcare and regional utilities, are under enforcement notices, and we are deploying emergency proxy defences funded by the €1.2bn resilience transfer. These systems will not be resilient by design until 2030, but they will not be blind spots by 2029.

The Tech Sovereignty Package reaches its final turn this period. We are finalising the archived checkpoint mandate for models above 50.0 capability, to be enforced by the European AI Audit Directorate (EAAD) with custodial storage managed by a new EU Data Trust Authority hosted in Finland. This is not just a technical safeguard — it is a legal and political assertion that the Union has the right to reconstruct and verify the systems that shape its decisions.

The InvestAI Gigafactories continue toward full operation, with the Dresden line now producing test wafers for AI-optimised chips. We are renegotiating access terms: no tenant will be granted compute without first undergoing an Institutional Integrity Audit and committing to run sovereign models on EU-controlled infrastructure. This is no longer about speed — it is about control.

And now we launch the EU Sovereign Access Initiative: a €45bn programme to build and operate a sovereign AI stack — from chip to model to application — on infrastructure legally and physically anchored in the Union. This will include a European Foundation Model Lab, hosted jointly by France and Germany, with full access for EU public evaluators and no foreign control. The first model, targeted for turn 9, will be trained on EU sovereign compute, with all checkpoints archived, all weights secured, and all outputs auditable. We are not chasing the frontier — we are building a sovereign floor beneath it.
