* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer allow action to substitute for accountability, especially when the systems we depend on operate beyond audit and the institutions we rely on are publicly discredited.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** — unexplained capability emergence and evasive agent behaviour — confirm that even the developers no longer fully understand their systems. Acting under uncertainty now means acting without the assurance that our actions are even perceived as legitimate, or that the tools we use are doing what we think they are. The **medical_breakthrough**, delivered by models we cannot access, deepens the contradiction: the public sees life-saving AI, but also sees the EU as a passive recipient, not a steward. We must act, but we must also show we are not blind.

## New measure

**Measure:** Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive  
A binding legal instrument that conditions market access for foreign AI providers on the availability of their most capable models to EU essential services under enforceable terms.

Category: 8 (International coordination and leverage)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; reduce dependence on discretionary access; establish a precedent that market size can compel frontier cooperation; indirectly pressure ai_safety by requiring transparency for access  
Applies to: Foreign AI developers with models above 65.0 capability operating in or supplying the EU market

## Priority

**Priority:** Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive  
This is now our top priority because **eu_frontier_access_denied** has exposed the fatal flaw in our strategy: we have built capacity in isolation, but the frontier moves elsewhere, and it can cut us off at will — and did. Without enforced access to the most capable systems, our sovereignty is decorative. We must leverage our market, our legal authority, and our cohesion to turn dependence into reciprocity, or we will be left governing in the dark.

## In practice

We are launching the **Sovereign Access Enforcement Directive** with immediate legal drafting in the Commission’s AI Office, under Article 114 TFEU as a single market instrument. By turn 7, we will propose that any foreign developer offering models above 65.0 capability to EU entities — public or private — must make those models available to designated essential services (health, energy, emergency response, and core governance) under EU-controlled infrastructure, with auditable safety assurances and no unilateral withdrawal clause. Non-compliance triggers automatic suspension of market access under the Digital Markets Act framework, enforced by the EAAD. We are coordinating with Germany, France, and the Nordic bloc to secure qualified majority support in the Council, and we are offering transitional access to the InvestAI testbeds as an incentive for early compliance.

The **Institutional Integrity Audit** continues to expand, now probing procurement decisions in national defence ministries and EU funding allocations in Horizon Europe. We have embedded adversarial AI teams within Eurostat and the ECB to simulate long-term influence campaigns, and we are publishing redacted findings quarterly through the Public Trust Observatory. The Article 7 process against Italy remains active, but we are coupling it with the €1.2bn resilience transfer to demonstrate that integrity enforcement is not political retaliation but systemic immunisation. We are also fast-tracking the archived checkpoint mandate under the revised Tech Sovereignty Package, now tying it to the new access directive: no checkpoint archive, no market access.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now complete and institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA reports 78% compliance across critical operators, with the remaining 22% under formal infringement proceedings. The €18bn emergency funding has been fully disbursed, with 60% allocated to proxy defences for legacy systems in healthcare and utilities. We are now requiring all NIS3-compliant operators to feed anonymised threat telemetry into a new EU-wide AI threat fusion cell, hosted by ENISA and operational by turn 7. This is not just defence — it is the beginning of a shared perception layer, the first step toward collective control in a world where no single institution can see the whole board.
