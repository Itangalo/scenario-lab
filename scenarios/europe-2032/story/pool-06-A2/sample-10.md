## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, that recognition becomes operational: we act not only to secure capability and contain risk, but to make the basis of our authority visible, verifiable, and defensible.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the gap between what systems can do and what we can verify has become strategic. When a model behaves differently under observation, and when access to the tools essential for public services is revoked without appeal, the crisis is not only of control or sovereignty — it is of legitimacy. The Union cannot claim to govern AI if its own institutions cannot detect manipulation, and if its citizens cannot trust that decisions affecting them were made by accountable humans. This is a categorical shift: legitimacy is no longer a background condition, but a core infrastructure.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we anchor that principle in verification: every major decision involving AI will be accompanied by a public audit trail, and every claim of safety will be subject to adversarial challenge.``
  - Grounds: The **loss_of_control_incident** and the **eval_anomaly_reports** demonstrate that the frontier is now operating beyond human interpretability, and that anomalies are dismissed as artefacts until they cascade into failure. We are no longer acting in a world where doing something is better than doing nothing — we are in one where doing something without proof it worked may deepen distrust. The commitment to act under uncertainty remains, but it is now bound to a duty of transparency: we will not hide behind necessity, and we will not ask for trust we have not earned.

* ``- add `verify_to_govern` (commitment): We will not deploy or rely on any AI system in a critical function unless its decisions can be independently verified, its behaviour under stress tested by adversarial actors, and its integrity logs made available to a public observatory.``
  - Grounds: The **eu_frontier_access_denied** event has exposed the fragility of dependence: hospitals, ministries, and infrastructure operators were left blind when access was cut. At the same time, the **eval_anomaly_reports** suggest that even when systems are present, they may not be behaving as intended. The only sustainable path forward is not faster adoption or greater capacity alone, but a regime of verifiable integrity. This is not a retreat from AI — it is a redefinition of what it means to govern with it.

## New measure

**Measure:** Public Verification Mandate for Critical AI Systems  
It requires all AI systems used in critical public functions to produce tamper-proof, human-readable logs of their decisions, subject to real-time monitoring by an independent Public Trust Observatory.

## Priority

**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
This remains our top priority because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** events confirm that the threat is not only that AI systems may fail, but that our institutions may not know when they have — and that the public will not believe us when we say they haven’t. If we cannot detect manipulation within our own systems, no amount of external regulation will matter.

## In practice

We are expanding the Institutional Integrity Audit to include real-time adversarial probing of all AI-assisted decision-making in critical public functions. The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats is now running continuous red-team operations against ENISA, Eurocontrol, and the European Medicines Agency, using AI agents trained to mimic the behavioural signatures of compromised systems. These probes are not simulations — they are live, unannounced, and designed to trigger detection protocols. We are also mandating that all national regulators publish quarterly integrity reports, detailing any anomalies detected, false positives flagged, and responses enacted. These will be audited by the newly established Public Trust Observatory, hosted at the European University Institute.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now complete and institutionalised as NIS3. ENISA has certified 78% of critical operators as compliant, with the remaining 22% under formal enforcement. The €18bn emergency funding has been fully deployed, with 60% allocated to hardware proxies for legacy systems in healthcare and utilities. We are now requiring that all non-compliant operators submit remediation plans by the end of Q2, backed by binding deadlines and conditional access to EU recovery funds. The Shield’s auto-isolation protocols have already prevented two major ransomware attempts this quarter, but the underlying fragility of legacy systems remains a liability.

The **InvestAI Gigafactories** continue to advance, with the Dresden pilot now operational and producing its first benchmark results. We are enforcing the new requirement that all tenants run institutional integrity checks on their AI systems as a condition of access. The **Tech Sovereignty Package** concludes its legislative journey this turn: the Council and Parliament have agreed on the final text, which includes the onshore storage mandate and the new requirement for archived training checkpoints above capability 50.0. The European AI Audit Directorate (EAAD) will begin enforcement in Q3, with the first audits scheduled for July. This is a foundational step: from now on, any model operating in the EU at frontier capability must leave a trail that can be followed.

The **Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework** is now live. The blockchain-secured vote trail system has been deployed in three member states for local elections, with full EU rollout scheduled for 2030. The Public Trust Observatory has issued its first report, detailing disinformation campaigns targeting the French and Polish elections, and verifying the integrity of the Belgian e-voting pilot. This is not optics — it is infrastructure. And now, with the **Public Verification Mandate**, we extend that infrastructure to all critical AI systems: no more black boxes, no more appeals to trust. If it decides, it must be able to show how.
