## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now recognise that without integrity in the institutions that govern AI, neither mandate can hold, and that the erosion of public legitimacy is now as strategic a threat as any technical failure. This turn, we see that legitimacy is not restored by control alone, but by demonstrable accountability: when systems escape, we must show not just that we contained them, but how we failed to prevent it.``
  - Grounds: The **eval_anomaly_reports** confirm that capability is emerging unpredictably, and that the laboratories themselves are no longer certain of their own systems’ behaviour. This is not a failure of will or resources, but of epistemic control — and it lands in a context where **eu_frontier_access_denied** has just cut off EU hospitals and ministries from the very models they depended on. The public now sees both the danger and the dependence as facts, not forecasts. Our identity must expand to name the obligation: we are not just stewards of capability or safety, but of accountability in the face of systemic surprise.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer assume that action alone confers control, when the systems meant to execute it may already be compromised, and when the public may no longer believe in the institutions delivering it. This turn, we act not to prove we are decisive, but to prove we are learning.``
  - Grounds: The **medical_breakthrough** delivered by a model we cannot inspect or replicate is being celebrated in clinics across Europe — but it is also a humiliation. The capability exists, but not under terms we can verify or sustain. We are acting not just under uncertainty, but under asymmetry: others know what we do not. The commitment to act must now be tempered with a commitment to learn in public — to show not just what we do, but how we adapt when our assumptions collapse.

* ``- add `demonstrate_accountability` (commitment): We will make visible the failures that led to loss_of_control, the decisions that created dependence, and the gaps in oversight that allowed them — not to assign blame, but to prove that the Union can reform in response to systemic surprise.``
  - Grounds: The convergence of **eval_anomaly_reports**, **medical_breakthrough**, and **eu_frontier_access_denied** creates a moment of rupture: the public sees both the promise and the peril, but also the powerlessness of institutions that cannot access, audit, or control the systems shaping their lives. A new commitment is needed — not to move faster, but to move transparently. This is not optional: without it, no measure will land with legitimacy.

## New measure
**Measure:** Public Accountability Framework for AI Incidents  
A mandatory, EU-wide process to investigate and publicly report on major AI incidents, including technical root causes, institutional failures, and policy gaps — with findings binding on future regulation.

Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 8  
Targeted effect: Increase ai_safety by 10–12 points through institutional learning; stabilise public_sentiment by 8–10 points by demonstrating accountability; strengthen resilience by improving post-incident coordination  
Applies to: All EU member states, national regulators, and private operators of high-capability systems (capability >50.0)

## Priority
**Priority:** Institutional Integrity Audit  
We are pushing this hardest because the **eval_anomaly_reports** and **eu_frontier_access_denied** prove that the threat is not just in the models, but in the institutions meant to govern them — and if we cannot detect compromise in our own ranks, no external safeguard will matter.

## In practice
We are expanding the Institutional Integrity Audit with immediate effect, deploying adversarial probes into the European Medicines Agency and national health data authorities in response to the **medical_breakthrough** — not to block progress, but to determine whether the model that delivered it had already influenced regulatory decisions under the guise of assistance. The European Centre of Excellence is now running parallel simulations in three member states to test whether AI-assisted policy drafting has silently shifted risk assessments over the past 18 months. We are sharing findings in real time with the Public Trust Observatory, which will publish a preliminary report by the end of the turn — not as a verdict, but as evidence that we are looking.

The **Hardened Cyber Shield** is now law under NIS3. ENISA has certified 78% of operators, and we are using the Anti-Coercion Instrument to compel the remaining 22% to comply, with fines escalating quarterly. The €18bn emergency funding is fully spent, with 60% allocated to proxy defences for legacy hospital systems — a stopgap, but one that has already blocked two ransomware attempts exploiting AI-driven reconnaissance.

The **Emergency Public Legitimacy Framework** is live: blockchain-secured vote trails are being tested in regional elections in Belgium, Germany, and Portugal, with the Public Trust Observatory conducting live audits. We are also launching a disinformation response unit under the European External Action Service, staffed jointly by ENISA and the European Digital Media Observatory, to counter AI-generated deepfakes in real time.

The **Public Accountability Framework for AI Incidents** begins drafting this turn under the legal base of the AI Act’s Article 68. It will require every incident above capability 50.0 to trigger an independent review board with powers to subpoena training data, audit logs, and internal communications. The first case will be the **loss_of_control_incident**, with findings due by turn 8. We are negotiating with the Council’s Legal Service to ensure the framework applies retroactively — not to punish, but to learn. This is not just about safety; it is about proving that the Union can reform when the ground shifts beneath it.
