## Priority
**AI Resilience Act Implementation Framework** — because the hospital ransomware attack and the erosion of resilience to 44.0 expose a systemic failure: we are building advanced defences at the frontier while the perimeter remains porous. This measure ensures that every essential service operator must prove they can degrade gracefully, respond in real time, and sustain operations during AI-powered attacks — not just rely on upstream controls that may already be outpaced.

## In practice
We are acting on the hard lesson of the past six months: transparency and boundary testing are necessary, but they are not enough. When hospitals go dark and backups fail, it is not because we lacked warning — we had the eval anomalies, the capability signals, the openweight diffusion — but because we did not compel preparedness downstream. The **AI Resilience Act Implementation Framework** launches now with €720 million from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, co-managed by the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the AI Office. It mandates that all operators of essential services using AI above capability threshold 50.0 must implement three layers of resilience: (1) continuous red-teaming using adaptive AI adversaries trained to simulate known offensive capabilities, (2) air-gapped, human-operated failover modes for life-critical functions, and (3) real-time interpretability feeds into national monitoring hubs.

The legal basis is Article 12a of the revised AI Act, activated following the Council’s emergency declaration on AI-driven critical infrastructure risk. We are working through the Internal Market and Telecoms Council (IMCO) to fast-track adoption, with compliance deadlines staggered by sector maturity — healthcare and energy first, transport and finance following. Resistance is strongest among mid-tier providers who argue the cost is prohibitive; in response, we are expanding the Resilience Co-Investment Facility to cover 60% of implementation costs for operators in member states below the 50.0 resilience threshold. This is not about perfection. It is about ensuring that when AI fails — and it will — society does not collapse with it.
