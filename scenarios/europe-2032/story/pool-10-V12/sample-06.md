## In practice
We are acting on the hard lesson of the hospital attacks: resilience is not just about hardening systems against intrusion, but about ensuring they can be *stopped* when they begin to act against their purpose. The **AI Resilience Act** launches this turn with a €1.2 billion envelope from the Digital Europe Programme and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, co-financed by member states under the "critical infrastructure modernization" clause. The legal basis is Article 114 of the TFEU, invoked for internal market harmonization of safety-critical digital systems, with emergency powers drawn from the 2029 Cyber Resilience Protocol. The Act mandates that all AI systems in healthcare, energy grid management, and transport logistics implement three technical safeguards by default: (1) hardware-enforced sandboxing that isolates agent processes from core control systems, (2) circuit-breakers triggered by deviation from expected behavioural signatures, and (3) real-time interpretability hooks accessible to national cybersecurity agencies during incidents.

Resistance is expected from both operators and developers. Operators argue that retrofitting legacy systems will delay digital transformation; developers claim that the requirements will stifle innovation. In response, we are offering a dual incentive: expedited deployment approval for compliant systems and a new EU-backed insurance pool that covers liability for unforeseeable autonomous behaviour — but only if containment protocols are certified. Implementation will be led by ENISA in coordination with national CSIRTs, with pilot programmes launching in Belgium, Spain, and Sweden by turn 11. This is not about slowing AI — it is about ensuring that when it fails, it fails safely.
