## New measure
**AI Resilience Shield for Critical Health Infrastructure**  
Deploy an emergency hardening programme to protect hospitals and medical data systems from adaptive AI-enabled attacks, beginning with intrusion detection, zero-trust architecture, and failover capacity.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size:` large  
`Finishes on turn:` 13  
`Targeted effect:` +8 to +10 resilience by turn 13, with measurable reduction in dwell time and encryption success rate of AI-powered ransomware; indirect +2 to public_sentiment by restoring confidence in essential services  
`Applies to:` EU member states, national health ministries, ENISA, and critical infrastructure operators in the healthcare sector

## Priority
**AI Resilience Shield for Critical Health Infrastructure** — because the cyber major incident has already struck, and the next will not wait: the EU cannot afford to build defences after hospitals go dark. While the Boundary Trials address the long-term integrity of agency, this measure meets the immediate, material fact of systemic fragility. Health infrastructure is not just critical — it is the threshold of public trust, and its collapse risks cascading loss of legitimacy.

## In practice
We are acting on the failure exposed by the ransomware campaign: our defences were static, and the attacker was not. The **AI Resilience Shield for Critical Health Infrastructure** launches with €720 million from the Emergency Support Instrument and the Digital Europe Programme, co-financed by member states under a new mutual obligation clause activated through the Health Security Committee. The European Cybersecurity Agency (ENISA) will lead, deploying rapid-response teams to audit and upgrade hospital networks across the three affected member states, with expansion to all high-risk facilities by turn 13. The core of the shield is a zero-trust architecture rollout, AI-driven intrusion detection trained on synthetic attack patterns, and air-gapped, manually triggered failover systems for patient records and life-support coordination.

We are also mandating real-time telemetry sharing between healthcare operators and ENISA under strict privacy safeguards, enabling collective defence without centralisation. Resistance is expected from under-resourced hospitals and national health authorities wary of EU overreach, but we are pairing the mandate with binding financial support and liability protection for good-faith compliance. This is not a permanent militarisation of health IT — it is an emergency reinforcement of the front line, because when AI can adapt in minutes, our defences must not take months. The Boundary Trials continue in parallel, but this turn belongs to survival: we must stop the bleeding before we can rebuild.
