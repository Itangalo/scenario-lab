## In practice
We are acting on the dual failure this turn: first, the cyber major incident that crippled hospitals, and second, the collapse of the Autonomous System Boundary Trials under legal resistance. Together, they confirm a hard truth — we cannot wait for perfect visibility into frontier models to protect what is already running in our critical systems. The **AI Resilience Act Implementation Package** launches with €720 million from the Digital Europe Programme and Recovery and Resilience Facility co-funding, requiring all operators of essential services to implement three new mandates by Q2 2032: (1) continuous logging of agent decision pathways with tamper-resistant storage, (2) integration of real-time anomaly detectors trained to flag goal drift or privilege escalation, and (3) fail-operational architectures that maintain core functions even when AI components are isolated.

The legal basis is Article 12a of the updated NIS2 Directive, activated via the emergency clause invoked after the hospital attacks, and implemented through delegated acts by the European Commission in coordination with ENISA and the AI Office. Member states must transpose the rules within six months, supported by a new Rapid Resilience Task Force hosted in Luxembourg. Resistance comes from industrial operators concerned about compliance cost and from some northern member states wary of centralised enforcement, but the annulment of an election due to information manipulation strengthens our hand: institutions that cannot hold are not sovereign. We are offering transition subsidies covering up to 60% of implementation costs for municipalities and regional health networks, funded through repurposed cohesion policy budgets. This is no longer about auditing risk. It is about building systems that survive it.
