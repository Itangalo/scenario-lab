# Before you start

One starting point. Three worlds. Twenty-four endings.

Some experts say that AI progress may accelerate beyond human limits within a decade. Some say that it will level out. Some say that we will only get superhuman capabilities in areas with “verifiable rewards” – coding, mathematics and other domains where answers can be checked automatically.

**We don't know which of these worlds we are in. We still have to make choices.**

In this story, you get to decide AI policy for the EU until 2032. We follow Sofia Brandt, AI adviser to the President of the European Commission. For the purposes of the story, the President can enact whatever policy you choose. In text messages, we also meet Sofia's sister Claire, a nurse in Lyon.

The story builds on LLM-powered simulations. You can peek at the simulation data as you read, if you want to. Read more about the simulations in the afterword.

**The style and scope of the story are heavily inspired by the excellent scenario [Europe 2031](https://europe2031.ai/). Europe 2031 is not affiliated with Europe 2032, and any shortcomings of this text should not reflect on its authors.**

## This is a draft – tell me what you think

I am still working on this, and what I need most is how it reads to someone who is not me. Write to me at johan@falkai.org. These are the questions I am stuck on, but anything you noticed is welcome.

- Is it enjoyable to read? Did you want to keep going, and was there a point where you stopped wanting to?
- The story runs in thirteen half-year steps, from the autumn of 2026 to the end of 2032. Is that too long? Would it be better if it ended two years earlier, before the reader is worn out?
- Three catastrophic events can occur: war between major powers, a catastrophic loss of control, and a catastrophic biological event. As it stands, the story carries on much as before when one of them lands. Should it end there instead – or at least be changed beyond recognition by what happened?
- When access to the leading models is cut off, the Union sometimes answers by building its own. In the real world there would be plenty of capable models below the frontier to fall back on. Does leaving that out cost the story too much credibility?
- Did you find other quirks or errors? Are any of them bad enough to damage the story, or to make it hard to believe?
