## In practice
This turn, we act within the narrow corridor between collapse and continuity. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally concludes, but its legacy is mixed: no new compute capacity is operational, and the legal instruments remain ungrounded in physical reality. Yet the proposals to Berlin and Dublin—offering co-location with military cyber units and sovereign green corridors with guaranteed off-take—have not been rejected outright. They remain open, with the European Investment Bank holding disbursement authority and the European Defence Fund ready to co-finance. We treat this not as failure, but as pause: the instruments stand, the offers stand, and we will not chase consensus with more concessions than mutual interest justifies.

The fracture in the European Evaluation Shield is now irreparable this turn. Two member states have formally rejected its authority, and without a legal basis to compel remediation, the redacted assessment we authorised has no teeth. We do not withdraw the market access conditions—principles must outlast power—but we acknowledge they cannot be enforced alone. Instead, we pivot to coordination: DG HOME and ENISA are instructed to integrate the Shield’s technical findings into the Municipal Resilience Backchannel’s audit framework, turning what was meant to be top-down oversight into a distributed verification network. If national governments will not accept mandates, we build systems that make compliance their interest—by showing that certified nodes gain faster access to crisis funding, mutual aid, and Digital Europe grants.

On the ground, the interoperability pilots in Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki advance. DG Energy finalises draft standards for model-driven grid response, and ENISA begins its first audit cycle, focused on agent persistence in local emergency systems. This is not the control we sought, but it is control that functions: it does not assume unity, but builds from what still works. When capital is near zero, we do not spend it on spectacle—we spend it on scaffolding.
