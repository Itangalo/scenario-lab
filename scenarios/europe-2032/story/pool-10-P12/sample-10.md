## In practice
This turn, we close the Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation, not with fanfare, but with salvage. The directive finishes in name, but its implementation fractures along national lines: Berlin remains silent, Dublin rejects, and no new capacity is secured. Yet the legal instrument itself still holds value — it has established a precedent for binding state-level commitments on compute allocation, and it has exposed the fault lines between military utility, energy sovereignty, and civilian control. We now shift from directive to negotiation: the European Defence Fund offer to Germany stands until turn 11, and the sovereign green corridor proposal to Ireland remains open with grid absorption guarantees. These are not dead ends — they are conditional trades, and we will not abandon them until the conditions are met or formally withdrawn.

Meanwhile, the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration continues on minimal funding, sustained by municipal initiative and ENISA’s pilot audit. DG Energy and DG HOME have delivered interoperability standards on schedule, and Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki are now stress-testing cross-border response protocols. This is not resilience by fiat, but resilience by function — and in a moment of collapsing political capital, it is the only kind that holds. We are not scaling it yet, but we are certifying it: nodes that pass the audit will be first in line for Digital Europe funds, creating a pull mechanism without new spending.

The redacted Evaluation Shield assessment was released under Article 16 TFEU, confirming systemic risks without exposing sensitive data. It was meant to restore credibility, but instead deepened the rift: two member states formally reject its authority, arguing that safety enforcement cannot be centralised without democratic legitimacy. We are not withdrawing the mandate — but we are pausing further enforcement actions until the legal basis is clarified. That conversation must happen in the Council, not in public. For now, we hold the line with what still works — and prepare to rebuild from the ground up.
