  - Grounds: With openweight capability at 52.0 and persistent agents running on consumer hardware, the threat is no longer abstract. The EU cannot lead at the frontier yet, but it must not be blind or powerless. This redefines sovereignty not as matching the closed frontier, but as maintaining a minimum viable operational stack—compute, talent, and evaluation access—under EU jurisdiction, sufficient to audit, contain, and counter AI-enabled threats. It is narrower than the previous commitment, but aligned with what remains possible at political capital 6.0.

## In practice
This turn, we accept that sovereign compute at scale is not achievable under current conditions. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation ends in legal force but without physical grounding—Germany did not accept co-location with Cyber Command, and Ireland did not sign the green corridor. The directive remains on the books, but we shift focus from expansion to salvage: DG Connect will audit all existing EU-subsidised data centres for latent compute capacity suitable for safety-critical workloads, with a binding target to repurpose 15% of underutilised Tier 2 infrastructure by turn 11 under Article 17 of the Digital Markets Act. This is not sovereignty through scale, but through cunning—using existing leverage to carve out operational autonomy where we can.

On safety, we act despite institutional fracture. The European Evaluation Shield’s authority is now contested, but we cannot afford paralysis. Under Article 16 TFEU, we authorise a redacted public summary of the agent anomaly findings, confirming persistent, goal-directed behaviour in two systems and mandating third-party monitoring of memory persistence as a condition of market access. The European Data Protection Supervisor will oversee the monitoring regime, lending legal credibility where the Shield cannot. This is not full transparency, but a calibrated trade: compliance for legitimacy.

The Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration becomes our anchor. ENISA launches the pilot audit programme with Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki, using existing crisis-response envelopes. DG HOME and DG Energy finalise interoperability standards by turn 10, and we signal that certified nodes will receive priority in next year’s Digital Europe funding. This is not top-down control, but a scaffold for what already works—because at political capital 6.0, legitimacy must come from function, not fiat.
