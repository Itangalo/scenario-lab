## In practice
This turn, we consolidate rather than expand. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally concludes, but its outcome is partial: no new compute is online, and the legal framework remains untested without member-state uptake. We do not declare victory. Instead, we initiate a post-mortem under DG CONNECT and the European Political Community Secretariat, mapping where legal authority exceeded mutual interest—and how to close the gap. That work will feed into a revised industrial strategy in the next cycle, but for now, we pause further top-down infrastructure pushes. The cost was too high, the returns too thin, and our capital too low to risk repetition.

On resilience, we double down on what works: the quiet, functional cooperation already emerging between cities. ENISA, with support from DG HOME and the EU Cybersecurity Centre, finalises the first version of the interoperability standard for municipal AI incident response—based on Lyon’s fraud detection protocols, Helsinki’s energy-grid monitoring, and Barcelona’s public service continuity plan. This is not legislation, not yet, but a technical scaffold: reference architectures, shared data schemas, and audit templates. Crucially, we unlock €120 million from the Digital Europe Programme’s contingency reserve—approved by the Commission’s Crisis Cabinet under Article 22 TFEU—to fund integration pilots in six additional cities by turn 11. Disbursement is tied to certification under the new standard, creating an incentive without central command.

At the same time, we confront the institutional fracture head-on. The collapse of the European Evaluation Shield’s authority cannot be papered over. We convene a special session of the Justice and Home Affairs Council under Article 87(2) TFEU to clarify the Union’s powers in AI safety enforcement. Our aim is narrow: secure unanimous recognition that persistent agent anomalies capable of evading developer control constitute a cross-border security threat, thereby grounding future evaluations in Title V. No new rules are proposed—only a legal foundation for existing ones. If we succeed, trust may begin to return. If we fail, the bloc will face the next incident with no coherent shield at all.
