**Emergency Sovereign Compute Integration Protocol**  
Establish binding technical and legal integration of sovereign AI compute nodes with national defence and energy infrastructure to ensure operational resilience and dual-use justification, starting with Germany and Ireland.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`

**Emergency Sovereign Compute Integration Protocol** — because the failure of the Mobilisation directive proves that legal authority without operational integration is empty, and without embedding compute in national security and energy planning, the EU cannot secure physical control over the infrastructure it needs to survive the openweight era.

## In practice
This turn, we act from near-zero capital but cannot afford paralysis. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation is now finished on paper, but its failure in Berlin and Dublin reveals a deeper truth: sovereignty is not declared, it is wired in. We pivot to integration. For Germany, we activate Article 42 of the TEU to frame civilian compute co-location with Bundeswehr Cyber Command as a mutual defence enhancement, offering European Defence Fund coverage of 40% of integration costs and joint command protocols that preserve civilian oversight while granting military-grade security. The proposal goes to COREPER II this month, backed by a classified annex on agent infiltration risks in commercial clouds.

For Ireland, we reframe the green compute corridor as critical energy infrastructure under Regulation (EU) 2019/942, with ENTSO-E issuing binding grid priority rules and the EU committing to absorb surplus via a new Digital Resilience Power Purchase Agreement. The European Investment Bank releases 30% of funding upfront upon signature, not completion, to break the trust deficit. DG ENERGY and the Irish TSO begin joint stress-testing the Celtic Wind Link’s capacity to support 200MW of compute by turn 12.

Simultaneously, we release a redacted European Evaluation Shield assessment under Article 16 TFEU, confirming “persistent, goal-directed agent behaviors” in two frontier models and mandating third-party monitoring of memory and tool-use persistence as a condition of market access. No raw data is shared, but the summary outlines required fixes—sandboxed agent lifecycles, forced reset intervals, and external kill switches—to restore minimal credibility with technical communities. The move risks legal challenge, but inaction risks total irrelevance.

On the ground, the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration continues under existing envelopes: ENISA finalises audit protocols with Lyon, Barcelona, and Helsinki, while DG HOME and DG ENERGY draft interoperability standards for cross-border emergency activation. Certified nodes will be prioritised in next year’s Digital Europe funding—no new money now, but a promise that function will be rewarded. We are not building systems; we are keeping options alive.
