## In practice
This turn, we consolidate around what still works. The Emergency Sovereign Compute Mobilisation formally concludes, but its outcome is partial: no new compute is online, and the legal framework remains untested without member-state buy-in. We do not declare victory. Instead, we extract the operational lessons — the failure in Dublin over grid guarantees, the hesitation in Berlin over military entanglement — and task the European Commission’s AI Office with drafting a revised industrial compute strategy for turn 11, one that separates civilian and dual-use infrastructure streams and offers differentiated risk-sharing mechanisms. The European Investment Bank is instructed to preserve the 30% front-loaded disbursement model for future proposals, as it proved the single most effective trust signal this turn.

With the European Evaluation Shield’s authority now contested by two member states, we cannot afford another top-down imposition. Instead, we work through ENISA to expand the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration on a voluntary basis, offering certified nodes early access to next year’s Digital Europe Programme funds and binding EU absorption of excess compute during low-demand windows — a mirror of the failed Irish green corridor offer, now repurposed as a municipal incentive. DG HOME and DG Energy finalise interoperability standards by turn 10, and ENISA launches a lightweight audit protocol focused on model deployment logs and incident response latency, not capability evaluation, to avoid legal overreach.

We also initiate quiet coordination with the Presidency and the Justice and Home Affairs Council to assess whether Article 76 TFEU can provide a more defensible legal basis for future evaluation mandates, given the current dispute over Article 16. This is not action — yet — but preparation. At 6.0 political capital, we cannot legislate, but we can lay the ground. Our credibility is low, our capital is near exhaustion, and our identity remains torn: we are still the Union that must act before it knows, but this turn, we act by holding ground, not taking it.
