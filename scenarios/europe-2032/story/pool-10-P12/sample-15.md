In Berlin, we advance the co-location offer with Bundeswehr Cyber Command independently through the European Defence Fund, treating it as a dual-use security investment rather than a sovereign compute rollout, with a side letter on data access for civilian resilience planning. In Dublin, the green compute corridor is restructured as a pilot under the Celtic Wind Link’s phase two feasibility study, with EIB disbursement now tied to grid-stability benchmarks rather than political signatures — a slower path, but one that bypasses immediate legal resistance.

Meanwhile, the Municipal Resilience Backchannel Integration becomes our de facto priority. DG HOME and ENISA finalise interoperability standards this turn, based on Lyon’s emergency-routing model, Barcelona’s public-service AI dashboard, and Helsinki’s automated incident escalation tree. These are published as a non-binding EU recommendation to avoid Council deadlock, but with a clear signal: certification under this framework unlocks priority access to Digital Europe Programme funds in turn 12. No new money is spent, but DG REGIO redirects unused cohesion funds to support municipal audits, and the European Cyber Resilience Act is interpreted to allow certified cities to count their AI monitoring as part of critical infrastructure compliance. This is not centralisation — it is scaffolding — and it is the only thing still moving at `eu_political_capital` of 6.0.
