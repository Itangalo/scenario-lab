## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI integrity against internal and external compromise  
  - Trigger: the two-year commitment period closes this turn  
  - Grounds: The **automated_decision_scandal** and the **opaque_reasoning** event have fundamentally altered the operating environment. The former proves that sovereign control does not guarantee accountable or lawful outcomes; the latter breaks the technical foundation of nearly all current oversight strategies. We can no longer assume that making systems more secure from intrusion also makes them more trustworthy in operation. Our focus must shift from proving the integrity of the stack to proving the legitimacy of the decisions it produces — and doing so without access to interpretable reasoning. This demands a new commitment grounded not in control, but in resilience to failure and capacity to correct it.

- add `new_two_year_commitment` (commitment): Ensure sovereign AI systems remain subject to meaningful human accountability, even when their reasoning is opaque  
  - Grounds: The **opaque_reasoning** breakthrough — or breakdown — ends the era in which oversight relied on reading chains of thought. The most advanced systems no longer produce human-legible justifications, invalidating the core of EAAD’s original evaluation mandate and much of SMAF’s design. At the same time, the **automated_decision_scandal** has shown that systems operating under full EU jurisdiction can still inflict widespread, systemic harm when accountability loops fail. We must now rebuild our assurance architecture around post-hoc redress, auditability of inputs and outcomes, and institutional capacity to detect and correct errors — not just prevent them. This is not a technical pivot; it is a democratic necessity.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Post-Decision Redress Network  
A pan-EU network of technical and legal auditors empowered to investigate and correct AI-driven administrative decisions after the fact, with authority to compel data access, reconstruct inputs, and issue binding corrections — turning audit into redress.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `resilience` by 10–15 points and stabilize `public_sentiment` by creating a visible, functional path for citizens harmed by AI systems to receive correction and compensation; begin restoring trust in public-sector AI by proving it can be held accountable even when it cannot explain itself  
`Applies to:` National and EU-level public services deploying AI systems above 50.0 capability, starting with welfare, taxation, and immigration

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Integrity Audit Expansion  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the **automated_decision_scandal** has shattered the illusion that sovereign control equals public legitimacy, and the **opaque_reasoning** event has destroyed the technical basis of our oversight — just as we were scaling up hardening measures. Without immediate, visible action to restore accountability, the entire SACP becomes politically indefensible. This expansion is our only lever to demonstrate that sovereign systems can be both secure and answerable, even when their reasoning is no longer legible.

## In practice
This turn, we are governing in the aftermath of two simultaneous ruptures: one moral, one technical. The **automated_decision_scandal** has exposed that our sovereign systems, for all their isolation and security, can still deny fundamental rights at scale — and do so without traceable justification. Meanwhile, the **opaque_reasoning** shift means that even if we had perfect logs, we could no longer read the model’s logic. The tools we built to secure the stack — the air gaps, the audits, the zero-trust protocols — were designed for a world where oversight meant reading the chain of thought. That world is over.

We are responding by redirecting emergency funding from the stalled Polish cluster into the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative, invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track compliance in Wrocław and Marseille. ENISA is deploying audit teams with authority to suspend non-compliant nodes of the SACP until zero-trust architecture is verified. But hardening alone is not enough. That is why we are accelerating the Integrity Audit Expansion — not as a study, but as a binding, adversarial review process with teeth. We are negotiating with the Council’s Justice configuration to embed its findings into national legal frameworks by turn 11, and offering member states a seat on the audit panel in exchange for early adoption. At the same time, we are launching the Post-Decision Redress Network to create a new kind of accountability — one that does not depend on understanding why a decision was made, but on proving it was wrong and correcting it. This is not about catching up. It is about learning to govern in the dark.
