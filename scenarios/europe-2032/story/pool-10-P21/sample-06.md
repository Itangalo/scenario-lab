## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore public trust in sovereign AI through verifiable accountability, binding redress, and resilience to systemic failure  
  - Trigger: the automated_decision_scandal and the collapse of public sentiment to 18.0  
  - Grounds: The **automated_decision_scandal** has demonstrated that technical sovereignty without democratic accountability is not only insufficient—it is dangerous. The systems we built to protect EU autonomy have instead inflicted harm on EU citizens, and the resulting erosion of trust has paralyzed our ability to act. The legal and technical frameworks we trusted—SMAF, EAAD, the Public Assurance Protocol—failed to prevent or detect the failure, exposing a chasm between compliance and legitimacy. Our focus must now shift from securing the stack to securing the social contract: ensuring that sovereign AI systems are not only secure and auditable, but also transparent, contestable, and subject to meaningful redress. This is no longer a technical or strategic imperative alone—it is a political survival imperative.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late  
  - Grounds: The **capability_plateau_evidence** and **opaque_reasoning** developments have fundamentally altered our epistemic footing. We can no longer assume that we can read, test, or verify the reasoning of frontier systems—even our own. The tools we relied on for oversight are now obsolete, and the assumption that we can catch failure through evaluation is no longer tenable. We are acting under deeper uncertainty than before, not just about capability, but about whether our institutions can ever catch up. We reaffirm this commitment not because the path is clearer, but because waiting for clarity would mean surrendering agency entirely.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Sovereign Redress and Transparency Emergency (SORTE)  
A binding directive requiring all sovereign AI systems deployed in public services to implement real-time decision logging, human-readable explanations, and automated redress triggers, with independent audit rights and public reporting.

`Category: 2 (Transparency and reporting)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase `ai_safety` by 6–10 points and `public_sentiment` by 8–12 points by restoring trust through verifiable accountability and accessible redress; mitigate systemic risk from opaque decision-making in welfare, immigration, and law enforcement  
`Applies to:` All EU member states operating sovereign AI systems in public administration, social services, and border control

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative  
We are pushing this hardest this turn because the **emergent_circuit_tracing_leak** has invalidated our core assumption that sovereign control equals secure control — our own audit tools are now in the wild, and we must assume that adversaries can simulate or evade them. Without immediate action to enforce zero-trust architecture and air-gapped evaluations, the entire SACP becomes a liability rather than an asset.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting in the wake of dual shocks: a breach not of code, but of trust. The **emergent_circuit_tracing_leak** has exposed a fatal flaw — our most advanced interpretability tools, meant to secure our systems, are now being repurposed to probe them. At the same time, the **automated_decision_scandal** has confirmed that even systems under sovereign control can inflict systemic harm when oversight fails. We are responding by hardening the core of the Sovereign AI Core Programme through the Sovereign Stack Hardening Initiative, invoking Article 122 TFEU to fast-track implementation. Funding is being redirected from the suspended Polish cluster, and ENISA is deploying emergency audit teams to Wrocław and Marseille to verify zero-trust compliance by turn 10.

The Openweight Containment Initiative remains in flight, but legal resistance grows: cloud providers argue that remote deactivation mandates violate DSA principles, and the ECJ has fast-tracked a preliminary reference. We are holding firm, but our energy is shifting — the frontier is no longer just outside our borders, but inside our labs. That is why we are launching the Sovereign Redress and Transparency Emergency (SORTE): to ensure that every sovereign system making binding decisions is subject to real-time logging, explainability, and automated redress. We are negotiating with the Council’s Justice and Home Affairs configuration to embed SORTE into the revised AI Act enforcement framework by turn 10, and we are offering national governments co-governance roles in the audit process in exchange for support. The era of building fast is over. The era of proving trust is beginning.
