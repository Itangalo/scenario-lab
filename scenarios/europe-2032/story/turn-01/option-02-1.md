## Priority
**Emergency Resilience Surge** — because the cyber_test_shot revealed that detection failed and access was deep, we must act now to contain cascading risk, even at high capital cost.

## In practice
We are activating the NIS2 directive’s emergency powers to mandate immediate cyber hardening across critical infrastructure operators in energy, water, and transport. The Cyber Resilience Act’s implementation is being fast-tracked through the Internal Market Council, with a binding Commission decision under Article 10d to require all operators of essential services to deploy AI-augmented intrusion detection systems and conduct red-teaming exercises within six months. ENISA is being scaled up with emergency staffing and budget from the Digital Europe Programme, and we are drawing on the recently established Joint Cyber Unit to coordinate cross-border response drills.

Resistance is expected from several member states citing cost and sovereignty, particularly in central and eastern Europe where IT modernisation lags. To overcome this, we are pairing the mandate with a €12bn emergency resilience fund under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, conditional on compliance by end of turn 2. The European Defence Agency is contributing its secure AI testbeds for simulation of advanced intrusion scenarios, and we are working with Siemens, Thales, and a consortium of national CERTs to deploy vetted defensive agents. This is not a long-term fix — it buys time for the Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package to land — but it is the only way to close the most urgent gap before the next incident.
