## Priority  
Priority: Accelerate EU sovereign evaluation regime for frontier models  
This measure is the cornerstone of reclaiming agency — without independent verification, the EU cannot know what it faces, cannot regulate what it does not see, and cannot act as a sovereign power in AI. While compute builds slowly, credibility in oversight can shift faster and changes how the world treats us now.

## In practice  
We are moving immediately to draft a Regulation for Independent AI Evaluation (RIAE), to be proposed under Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, with a delegated act establishing the European AI Evaluation Authority (EAA). The EAA will be hosted under the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre, with satellite labs in Seville, Graz and Aarhus, drawing on existing expertise in cybersecurity, metrology and algorithmic auditing. Its first mandate: any model trained with more than 10^25 FLOPs or scoring above 45.0 on the EU’s open benchmark suite must undergo third-party evaluation before deployment in the Union. We will fund it with €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme and Horizon Europe, repurposed with urgency.

Resistance is expected from both foreign hyperscalers and domestic industry, who see this as duplication or delay. We are offering a fast-track pathway: developers who submit early and fully will receive a “trusted partner” designation, streamlining future approvals and granting preferential access to EU compute under the InvestAI programme. Simultaneously, we are negotiating with member states in the Internal Market and Industry Council to accept mutual recognition of evaluations from Japan and Canada, building a bloc of aligned democracies. This is not just about safety — it is about making the EU a pole of gravity in the global AI order, not a rule-taker.

The Tech sovereignty package and InvestAI Gigafactories remain on track. We have convened a task force under the European Investment Bank to finalise site selection for the first two gigafactories by Q1 2027, prioritising locations with grid headroom and cooling access — southern Spain and northern Finland. The permitting fast-track will be piloted in three designated zones, with environmental assessments compressed from five years to twelve months through pre-approved templates and digital twin modelling. This is not a sprint — but it is the beginning of a pace we can sustain.
