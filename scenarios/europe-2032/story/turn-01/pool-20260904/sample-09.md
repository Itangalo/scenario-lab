## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the foundation of our two-year commitment: without compute on EU soil under EU legal control, every other measure depends on permission from abroad, and we are already six months behind schedule in standing up the first site.

## In practice  
We are moving now on three fronts. First, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is being operationalised through a new joint undertaking under Article 187 TFEU, hosted by the European High-Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), with binding site selection by December and front-loaded grants to secure land and grid access. The resistance is not political but physical — local planning regimes, grid saturation, and skilled labour shortages — so we are pairing the fund with a directive on strategic infrastructure permitting, to be proposed in early 2027, that will override delays in member states that fail to act. The money is committed, but the pace depends on breaking bottlenecks, not writing cheques.

Second, the Tech sovereignty package is being leveraged to lock in private capital through conditional access: hyperscalers will get fast-track permitting only if they co-locate at least 40% of new EU capacity with sovereign-controlled workloads and agree to audit rights on energy use and access controls. We are negotiating with three major providers this quarter, and the Commission will publish a list of designated sovereignty zones by Q1 2027. This is not a subsidy play — it is a conditionality play, using our market size and supply-chain leverage (especially via ASML) to anchor capability.

Third, we are launching the sovereign evaluation lab under DG CONNECT, with staffing drawn from national cybersecurity agencies, academic AI safety groups, and seconded engineers from non-frontier EU AI firms. The legal basis will be an amendment to the AI Act’s general-purpose AI provisions, to be proposed in early 2027, requiring weight-sharing with this lab for any model above 45.0 capability index. This is not about replicating US or UK efforts — it is about being able to say “we saw it” when something goes wrong, and to act without waiting for a foreign government’s assessment. The cost is high, but the alternative — dependence on external claims about internal code — is higher.
