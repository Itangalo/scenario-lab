## New measure  
**EU Open-Weight Release Threshold**  
Establishes a legal ceiling on the capability of models that may be released with open weights, enforced through licensing and export controls.

`Category: 3 (Limits and restrictions)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 3`  
`Targeted effect:` Reduce openweight_capability growth, slow the distribution of offensive cyber and dual-use capabilities; modest downward pressure on ai_capability by constraining feedback loops from open deployment.  
`Applies to:` EU-based developers and any entity releasing open-weight models into the EU market.

## Priority  
**EU Open-Weight Release Threshold** — This is the moment to act before frontier capabilities become uncontrolled by design; with Kimi K3 already crossing into offensive cyber parity with closed models, we cannot allow the next generation of open weights to reach agent-level autonomy under current norms.

## In practice  
We are moving now on three fronts: industrial build-out, sovereign control, and boundary-setting. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is being structured through the European Investment Bank and the newly convened AI Infrastructure Board, which brings together national energy ministers, ENTSO-E, and site-selected regional authorities. We are already in closed consultation with grid operators in Finland, Spain, and Poland to fast-track connection timelines, using the TEN-E regulation as legal base and invoking Article 122 TFEU for emergency grid reinforcement where needed. Resistance from local communities — particularly over water use and heat dissipation — is being met with binding commitments on district heating integration and job guarantees for construction and operations roles.

The Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the Competitiveness Council, where we are trading accelerated permitting for enforceable sovereignty clauses: any data centre receiving fast-track status must anchor its compute under EU legal jurisdiction, with irrevocable access for public agencies during crises. This is not just about electricity — it is about ensuring that when the next crisis hits, no external power can switch off our capacity. We are offering tax incentives tied to compliance, but also preparing to invoke the Digital Markets Act’s gatekeeper provisions to compel cooperation from foreign hyperscalers operating at scale in Europe.

And this turn, we are drafting the Open-Weight Release Threshold directive under Article 114 TFEU, to be proposed to the European Parliament and Council by January. The threshold will be set at 45.0 on the capability index — just above current open models but below the agent-level autonomy now emerging. It will require developers to undergo third-party evaluation before open release, and impose licensing conditions on weight distribution, including prohibitions on unredacted training data and model weights above the line. We know this will face fierce opposition from open-source communities and some member states that see openness as innovation policy. But we are not banning open models — we are ensuring they remain governable. The incident with the laboratory agents proved that distributed autonomy is not theoretical. We will not wait for the first open-weight attack on critical infrastructure to act.
