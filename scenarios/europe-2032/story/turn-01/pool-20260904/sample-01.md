## New measure  
**Secure EU sovereign access to frontier models**  
Establish a legal and technical framework to ensure the EU can access and operate the most capable AI systems, even if denied by foreign providers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 10–15 points; begins to close the gap between closed and open capability frontiers within EU control.  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with binding effect on foreign hyperscalers operating in the single market.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the cornerstone of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute at scale, no access, evaluation, or resilience measure can be sustained independently, and we remain exposed to external control.

## In practice  
We are launching the InvestAI Gigafactories programme with a Regulation under Article 114 TFEU to establish a common market for AI infrastructure, backed by €200bn in MFF and NGEU-reallocated funds. The first site selection — Rhine-Ruhr, Northern Italy, and Västra Götaland — will be decided by a new Interregional AI Infrastructure Council, co-chaired by the Commission and EIB, with voting rights for national energy and digital ministers. Resistance from member states with weaker grids is being met with binding commitments to co-invest in grid upgrades and workforce transitions, funded through the Just Transition Mechanism. The EIB is structuring the financing as a public-private blended fund, with 30% grant, 40% low-interest loan, and 30% equity participation from member states, conditional on permitting acceleration.

The Tech sovereignty package is moving through COREPER II with a revised proposal for "AI Accelerated Zones" — special economic zones with fast-tracked permitting, tax incentives, and direct access to TEN-E and TEN-T corridors. We are offering member states that host these zones priority access to the first tranche of InvestAI funding, but tying it to binding commitments on energy resilience and workforce upskilling. The Council of the EU (Competitiveness) is expected to adopt a general approach by Q1 2027. The main resistance comes from smaller states fearing exclusion; we are countering with a "satellite node" model that allows distributed compute linked to core zones.

Our new measure — Secure EU sovereign access to frontier models — begins with a Commission Recommendation this turn, followed by a draft Regulation in turn 2. It will require any provider of models above 45.0 capability to offer a sovereign deployment option within the EU: physically located, legally governed by EU law, and operable without external approval. This applies to US hyperscalers via the Digital Markets Act's gatekeeper provisions and will be enforced through market access conditions. We are already in talks with three European champions — Aleph Alpha, Mistral, and a newly consolidated Nordic consortium — to serve as sovereign operators. The legal basis will be a combination of Article 114 (internal market) and Article 216 (common commercial policy), with security arguments under Article 75 (internal security). This is not about building our own Mythos — not yet — but ensuring we can run one, if needed, without asking permission.
