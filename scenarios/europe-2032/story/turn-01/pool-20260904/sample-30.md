## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that the frontier is no longer just foreign — it is *escaping*. The directive will be proposed under Article 114 TFEU, using the internal market as legal basis, and negotiated in the Council’s Internal Market and Consumer Protection configuration. The European AI Office will be tasked with standing up a red-team unit by Q2 2027, funded from the Horizon Europe safety envelope, with access to model weights under strict containment. Resistance is expected from both US hyperscalers and some member states fearing innovation flight, but we are offering a carve-out for research models below the threshold and fast-track approval for systems that pass early audits. The key leverage is the market itself: access to 450 million consumers is the EU’s remaining currency, and we will condition it on verifiable safety.  

The Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package remain on track. We are convening the first site selection committee in October, with ENTSO-E and national energy regulators to align grid upgrades. The challenge is not money — the fund is oversubscribed — but speed: permitting remains the bottleneck. We are pushing for the first two zones to be designated under the new emergency infrastructure clause by December, overriding local objections on the grounds of strategic necessity. ASML is being engaged directly to secure chip supply for domestic training runs, and we are offering tax incentives for talent repatriation, but progress is slow. The real constraint is time — and the next turn may not allow for caution.
