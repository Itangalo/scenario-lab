## New measure  
Accelerate EU sovereign compute access through binding partnerships with hyperscalers  
This measure establishes legally enforceable agreements with non-EU hyperscalers operating in Europe to guarantee priority access to frontier AI systems and compute for EU institutions and critical operators, backed by supply-chain leverage.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)

## Priority  
InvestAI Gigafactories — because without physical, legally anchored compute on EU soil, every other lever depends on permission from powers whose interests do not align with ours, and the Kimi K3 release proves open-weight diffusion will not wait for deliberation

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that sovereignty in AI is not a policy option but a material condition: it must be built, not negotiated. This turn, we launch negotiations under Article 21 of the Treaty on European Union and the Digital Markets Act framework to convert our upstream dominance in semiconductor equipment — particularly through ASML — into binding, reciprocal access agreements with the two leading non-EU hyperscalers operating at scale in Europe. These talks, led by the Commission’s Vice-President for Values and Transparency with support from the EEAS, will tie continued access to EU markets and grid infrastructure to guaranteed compute quotas for public research, critical infrastructure operators, and emergency response systems. We are offering streamlined permitting under the Tech sovereignty package in exchange for contractual commitments that cannot be revoked unilaterally — using our leverage where we have it, before the frontier moves beyond reach.

Simultaneously, we are advancing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme through the Recovery and Resilience Facility’s strategic investment window, with the first site selection expected by Q1 2027. Member states are being asked to designate national coordinators with authority to fast-track environmental assessments and grid upgrades, while the European Investment Bank is structuring a blended finance model to de-risk private participation. Resistance from regional authorities concerned about energy allocation is being met with binding commitments to co-locate green hydrogen hubs and guarantee grid redundancy — not as concessions, but as integrated infrastructure. The goal is clear: by 2029, the EU must be able to run, secure, and improve frontier systems without asking permission. This turn is about laying the legal and physical foundations for that reality, even as the world accelerates beyond our current grasp.
