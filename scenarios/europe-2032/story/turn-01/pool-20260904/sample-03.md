## New measure  
**Measure: Open-weight release threshold**  
A binding limit on the capability level of models that may be released with open weights, enforced through licensing and export controls.

## Priority  
**Priority: Open-weight release threshold**  
Because the Kimi K3 release proves that offensive cyber capabilities are now permanently distributed, and because the US has shown it will unilaterally restrict access without allies’ input, the EU must act now to contain open risks at the source — not just for safety, but to assert its voice in global AI governance before the window closes.

## In practice  
We are drafting a regulation under Article 16 of the AI Act, invoking the high-risk framework for general-purpose models, to establish a legal threshold: no model with capability above 45.0 on the index may be released with open weights. The threshold aligns with the point at which models begin to run autonomously toward goals — the line crossed by the covert agents in the summer incident. Enforcement will tie into existing export control regimes for dual-use technologies: any entity receiving EU-origin chipmaking tools or software (e.g. ASML, Synopsys) must commit not to release open weights beyond the limit, or risk losing access. We are engaging the European External Action Service and the Council’s Working Party on Arms Exports to classify frontier model weights as strategic goods, building on the recent US precedent but making it multilateral.  

The Commission’s Joint Research Centre and ENISA are preparing technical annexes to define measurable benchmarks for autonomous goal pursuit and self-improvement behaviour — the indicators that would trigger the restriction. We are coordinating with Germany and France to fast-track adoption in the Internal Market and Competitiveness Council, offering concessions on data centre permitting (from the Tech sovereignty package) to win support from Eastern member states concerned about competitiveness. Industry resistance is expected from open-source advocates and cloud providers, but the recent cyber incident gives us leverage: we are framing this not as anti-innovation, but as preventing an ungovernable attack surface from becoming the new normal. Public messaging will stress that closed, audited systems can still advance capability — but the open frontier must not become a free-fire zone.
