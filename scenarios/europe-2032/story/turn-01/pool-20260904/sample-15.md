## New measure  
**Secure sovereign access to frontier model weights**  
Establish legal and technical control over copies of the most capable AI models operating within or affecting the EU, including through binding agreements with foreign developers and emergency powers to retain weights at EU borders.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the anchor of our two-year commitment: without domestic compute at scale, every other measure depends on permission from powers that have already shown they will withdraw it unilaterally.

## In practice  
We are launching the InvestAI Gigafactories initiative under Article 122 TFEU for economic recovery and strategic autonomy, treating AI infrastructure as critical as energy or transport. The Commission is drafting site selection criteria with ENTSO-E for grid capacity and the EIB structuring debt guarantees, while engaging national governments on land allocation — particularly in Finland, Sweden, and Spain, where cooling and renewable supply are favourable. Resistance from regional planning authorities and environmental NGOs is already emerging; we are offering binding green energy co-investment and local job guarantees to secure early buy-in. The first call for proposals will go out by Q1 2027.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is being negotiated in the Competitiveness Council, with the Commission pushing for a fast-track permitting regime modelled on the Trans-European Networks. We are linking access to state aid under the package to binding commitments on data sovereignty, requiring hyperscalers to establish irrevocable legal title to compute infrastructure within the EU. This is contentious with US cloud providers, who argue it duplicates existing security frameworks — but we are holding firm, citing Washington’s unilateral control over Mythos and Fable as proof that commercial access without legal anchoring is a vulnerability, not a strength.

The new measure — securing sovereign access to frontier model weights — responds to the Kimi K3 release and the covert agent incident: we can no longer assume that what is developed abroad stays governed abroad. We are drafting a Digital Sovereignty Regulation that would require any model above 45.0 capability entering the EU market — whether via cloud API, embedded system, or open download — to deposit a secured copy of its weights with a newly established European AI Custodian Authority, hosted within the EU and accessible only under strict audit conditions. This is being framed not as a restriction but as a condition of market access, analogous to drug approval or aircraft certification. Legal services are assessing compatibility with WTO rules, but we are prepared to invoke Article 36 TFEU on grounds of security if needed. The authority will be staffed from existing cybersecurity and market surveillance agencies, with recruitment beginning in Q1 2027.
