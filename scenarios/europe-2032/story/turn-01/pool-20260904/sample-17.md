## New measure  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive**  
A binding legal instrument to reserve 30% of all frontier-class compute capacity in the EU for state-critical and public-interest use, effective on deployment.

Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)  
Size: large  
Finishes on turn: 3  
Targeted effect: +10 to +15 to eu_ai_sovereignty by turn 4, begins closing the gap between openweight and sovereign capability; signals EU as a non-dependent actor  
Applies to: EU jurisdiction, with enforcement on all data centres above 100 petaFLOPS-scaled capacity located in member states

## Priority  
**Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive** — because without binding control over compute deployed on our soil, all other measures depend on permission from others, and the Kimi K3 release proves that openweight capability is already weaponising at scale beyond governance reach.

## In practice  
We are acting on the conviction that compute is power, and that power cannot be outsourced if sovereignty is to mean anything. This turn, the Commission will draft the Emergency Sovereign Compute Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market legal base to override national concessions to foreign hyperscalers. The text will mandate that any operator of a data centre exceeding 100 petaFLOPS of AI-specific throughput must reserve 30% of capacity for uses defined by the EU: critical infrastructure resilience, public health modelling, verified safety research, and state-authorised defence applications. This is not nationalisation — it is a use condition for operating at scale in our market, akin to grid or spectrum licensing.

The pushback will be fierce. The Council’s Competitiveness formation is already signalling resistance, fearing capital flight and retaliation from US cloud providers. We will counter with a tripartite offer: first, a fast-track certification process for compliant operators to access the InvestAI Gigafactories fund; second, a liability shield for operators acting in good faith under the directive; and third, a binding commitment that the reserved capacity will be scheduled through a transparent, auction-based public utility model, not political favour. The European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) will be empowered as the allocator, reporting directly to the Commission’s AI Office.

At the same time, we are advancing the Tech sovereignty package by negotiating with seven member states on designated “AI Acceleration Zones” — geographic areas where permitting for data centres is capped at six months, grid connection guaranteed within 18, and local opposition addressed through binding community benefit agreements. These zones will be the physical anchors for both the Gigafactories fund and the new directive. We are not waiting for consensus; we are building the infrastructure of autonomy while the world fractures.
