## New measure
**Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Directive**  
Establish real-time monitoring of open-weight model releases and their downstream capabilities to detect dangerous thresholds.

`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 3`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase ai_safety by 5–8 points; slow openweight_capability growth through early intervention; strengthen EU’s standing in global evaluations  
`Applies to:` EU jurisdiction, with reporting obligations for any entity distributing open-weight models above 40.0 capability index

## Priority
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the foundational lever: without sovereign compute, every other measure depends on permission from others, and our political capital will erode under constant negotiation. The Kimi K3 release proves open-weight capability is now a vector of strategic risk, and only domestic infrastructure can close that loop.

## In practice
We are moving immediately on three fronts. First, under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission has launched the legislative proposal for the Open-Source Frontier Monitoring Directive, to be negotiated in the Internal Market Council and the European Parliament’s IMCO committee. The text will require any distributor of open-weight models above 40.0 capability — assessed via EU-certified benchmarks — to submit to pre-release evaluation and ongoing telemetry. We are offering compliance safe harbours for open research communities, but the obligation is universal. National cybersecurity agencies will be tasked with enforcement, coordinated through ENISA, and we are allocating €1.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme to build the evaluation nodes in France, Finland, and Poland by turn 3.

Second, InvestAI Gigafactories is entering its site selection phase. We are convening the Energy Council and the Competitiveness Council jointly to resolve grid access conflicts, and offering regional incentives under the Cohesion Fund to accelerate local approvals. The bottleneck is water and power in northern Bavaria and central Wallonia — we are fast-tracking interregional transmission projects and offering temporary tax rebates to utilities that prioritise AI loads. Our goal is to have two sites under construction by turn 2, with ASML and Intel already signaling interest in co-location for chip assembly.

Third, the Tech sovereignty package is being repositioned: we are no longer treating it as a passive incentive framework, but as a conditionality tool. Member states seeking access to InvestAI funds must now adopt the accelerated-permitting zones within 12 months or lose eligibility. This is contentious — especially in Germany and Austria — but we are pairing it with a new clause in the State Aid Guidelines allowing temporary derogations for AI-critical infrastructure, provided environmental offsets are binding and transparent. The Council will resist, but we have enough support in the Nordics and Eastern Europe to hold a blocking minority.
