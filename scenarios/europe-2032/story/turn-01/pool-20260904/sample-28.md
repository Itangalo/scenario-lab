## In practice  
We are acting on the fact that compute is power, and that power today resides in the hands of a few private laboratories and one foreign state. The InvestAI Gigafactories and Tech Sovereignty Package are our long-term bets: building domestic compute at scale, with legal anchoring and energy infrastructure to match. But those take years. In the meantime, we cannot wait. This turn, we are drafting emergency clauses under Article 22 of the Digital Markets Act to designate certain AI infrastructure as gatekeeper-critical for public function, allowing us to impose binding access obligations on any provider operating at frontier scale in the EU.  

The Commission’s Digital Services and Competition Directorates are leading, with legal input from the Legal Service and strategic coordination from the Secretary-General’s office. We are targeting three hyperscalers — two US-based and one with dual jurisdiction — whose data centres in Ireland, Finland, and Greece now host models capable of running autonomous agents. Our demand is simple: commit to reserving 15% of your EU-based training and inference capacity for EU-controlled workloads — including for civil protection, scientific research, and regulatory evaluation — or face suspension of new model deployment rights under the AI Act. We are offering in return fast-track compliance status, liability shielding for EU-mandated uses, and access to our own grid-priority lanes under the Tech Sovereignty Package.  

Resistance is expected, particularly from legal teams arguing extraterritorial overreach, and from member states dependent on foreign investment in data centres. We will counter with a Council of the EU meeting under Article 16 TFEU on security of supply, framing this not as market intervention but as infrastructure sovereignty — akin to energy in 2022. The European Parliament’s Internal Market and Civil Liberties committees will be briefed jointly, to anchor legitimacy. If we succeed, we gain operational independence within 18 months. If we fail, we confirm that the EU cannot enforce its will on the very infrastructure that will decide its future.
