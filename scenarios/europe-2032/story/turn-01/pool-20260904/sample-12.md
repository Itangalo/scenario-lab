## New measure  
**Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Directive**  
A binding directive to fast-track grid access, land use, and security-clearance processing for AI infrastructure deemed critical to EU strategic autonomy.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 4`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 8–12 points; accelerates compute deployment by compressing permitting from 24 to 6 months for designated projects.  
`Applies to:` EU member states, European Investment Bank, national energy regulators, and designated AI infrastructure operators.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the anchor of our two-year commitment: without physical compute on EU soil under EU legal control, no evaluation, no resilience, no sovereignty. While the new directive sets the stage, Gigafactories are where metal meets earth — and where delays now will cost us capability irreversibly.

## In practice  
We are treating AI compute as strategic infrastructure, equivalent to energy or defence. This turn, the Commission is drafting the Emergency Sovereignty Acceleration Directive under Article 114 TFEU, leveraging the internal market clause to override national permitting bottlenecks. We are working with the EIB to create a fast-lane financing window, and with ENTSO-E to integrate AI load projections into grid expansion plans by Q1 2027. The legal basis is contested — some member states see this as an overreach — so we are pairing it with binding commitments to regional co-benefits: local job guarantees, heat-reuse mandates for data centres, and a 15% allocation of capacity for public research.

At the same time, we are pushing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme into site selection and land acquisition. The first two sites — one in Finland, one in Spain — are entering final negotiation, with co-investment offers from ASML and Bosch to anchor demand. We are offering national governments a trade: accelerated EU-level permitting in exchange for binding commitments to security-of-supply and non-extraterritorial access clauses. Resistance is strongest in capitals with close US ties, but the Washington blackout on Mythos 5 has made the risk of dependence real and immediate. We are not waiting for consensus — we are building leverage through momentum.
