## In practice  
We are moving now on three fronts. First, the InvestAI Gigafactories programme is being operationalised through the European Investment Bank and a new Joint Undertaking under the Digital Europe Programme. We are negotiating with France, Germany, and Poland on host sites, offering grid priority and fast-tracked environmental assessments in exchange for co-investment and legal guarantees that these facilities cannot be subject to extraterritorial control. Resistance from smaller member states centres on fairness of distribution — we are countering with a binding commitment that 30% of capacity will be reserved for SMEs and public research across all sites.

Second, the Tech Sovereignty Package is entering its legislative phase in the Council’s Competitiveness formation. The core tension is between speed and oversight: industry demands automatic permitting in designated zones, but several member states insist on national veto rights over energy allocation. We are offering a compromise — independent technical review panels with EU-level authority over grid access, but national seats on those panels and a sunset clause at 2030. This keeps momentum while preserving cohesion.

Third, we are launching the Sovereign AI Corps through a new Article 114 TFEU legal base, framed as a market integrity and security of supply issue. Recruitment begins this autumn via a fast-track scheme for top AI graduates and experienced engineers, with salaries benchmarked to the private sector and security clearances expedited through Europol’s CBRN unit. The hardest sell is access to model weights: we are offering labs regulatory goodwill and early consultation on future rules in exchange for audited, on-premise access — not remote, not filtered. Some resistance from US-based developers is expected, but we will make clear that market access in the EU post-2028 depends on compliance. This is not optional.
