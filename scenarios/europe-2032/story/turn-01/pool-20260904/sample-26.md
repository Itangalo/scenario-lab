## In practice  
We are treating the Gigafactories as strategic infrastructure, not industrial policy. The Commission is drafting a legal base under Article 114 TFEU for a temporary Crisis Compute Regulation, to fast-track grid access, environmental waivers, and state aid approvals for the four designated sites. The Council’s Competitiveness formation will be asked to approve qualified majority voting on siting disputes by December, breaking national vetoes that could delay deployment. Resistance is strongest in Austria and Luxembourg, who fear energy diversion and overheating; we are offering binding guarantees on grid resilience and a 15% allocation of non-strategic compute for academic and SME use as compensation.

The Tech Sovereignty Package is being re-framed as a public-private covenant: the Commission will match private investment in data centres up to €100bn, but only if operators sign long-term capacity reservation agreements with EU institutions and agree to sovereignty clauses — no remote shutdowns, no foreign jurisdiction override. We are negotiating with Equinix, OVHcloud, and a consortium led by Nokia and Siemens to anchor the first zones in Finland, Spain, and Poland by Q1 2027. The permit acceleration mechanism will piggyback on the EU’s existing Important Project of Common European Interest (IPCEI) framework, but with a new digital infrastructure designation to bypass local zoning inertia.

On evaluation, we are launching a pilot under the Joint Research Centre to build sovereign red-teaming capacity, starting with access to Mistral’s latest model under a special research licence. The goal is to demonstrate public-sector capability by turn 3, when we will propose the full evaluation authority under the revised AI Act. This is not about catching up with the US or China — it is about ensuring that when a model is deployed in Europe, *we* decide whether it is safe, not a foreign regulator or a corporate policy shift.
