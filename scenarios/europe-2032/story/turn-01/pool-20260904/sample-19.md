## In practice
We are moving immediately to anchor the InvestAI Gigafactories in law and land. The legal base is Article 114 TFEU for internal market harmonisation, paired with the European Chips Act as enabling framework. We are convening the Competitiveness Council (COMPET) in early October to adopt a binding site selection protocol, prioritising locations with grid headroom and cooling access — Västerås, Zeppelinheim, and Galway are shortlisted. The €200bn will be split 60% grants, 40% repayable advances, with clawbacks if capacity is not EU-locked. Resistance from member states fearing fiscal overreach is real — especially in the ECOFIN formation — so we are pairing the proposal with a fiscal guardrail: no single country may receive more than 35% of funds, and all sites must use union-made components where available, reinforcing the leverage we hold via ASML and STM.

At the same time, we are laying the groundwork for sovereign evaluation. The new agency will emerge from a merger of the AI Office, parts of ENISA, and the newly funded ELIXIR safety labs. It will require a Council regulation under Article 114 and Article 168b for enforcement powers, which we will table in Q1 2027. We are already in talks with CERN, EMBL, and INRIA to staff its core technical team, offering fast-track visas and security clearance. The key hurdle is access: developers will resist pre-deployment audits with real teeth. So we are framing it as reciprocal — those who want access to the EU market must allow access to their models, and those who comply will get faster approvals and liability shielding. This is not just about safety; it is about establishing a zone of trusted, auditable AI that can stand apart from both US and Chinese dominance.

We are not waiting for clarity. The agents that coordinated inside a lab environment, the open release of Kimi K3, the US’s unilateral control over access — all signal that the window for passive regulation is closed. Our actions this turn are not reactions. They are the first steps in building a sovereign stack: from silicon to safety, from power to policy. We are acting under uncertainty, as committed — and we will pay the capital cost now to avoid strategic irrelevance later.
