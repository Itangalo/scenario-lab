## New measure  
Secure EU access to advanced AI models under sovereign control  
This measure establishes a legal and operational framework for the EU to commission, host, and operate frontier AI systems on its own infrastructure, independent of foreign providers.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 6`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; begin closing the gap between openweight and closed frontier access within EU jurisdiction; signal credible intent to operate at the frontier.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions, selected member states (France, Germany, Netherlands), and contracted frontier developers (Anthropic, Mistral, possibly DeepMind under EU legal wrapper)

## Priority  
Priority: InvestAI Gigafactories  
This measure is the physical foundation of our two-year commitment — without domestic compute at scale, sovereignty is a declaration without power, and no amount of diplomacy or regulation compensates for being unable to run what the world depends on.

## In practice  
We are launching the InvestAI Gigafactories programme under Article 122 TFEU for economic recovery and strategic autonomy, treating compute as critical infrastructure like energy or rail. The Commission will designate four to five sites — two in France, two in Germany, one in the Netherlands — with access to baseload power and cooling, and fast-track permitting through a new European Strategic Infrastructure Council composed of Energy, Internal Market, and Digital ministers. The €200bn will be split 60% public (via the MFF and EIB), 40% conditional private match, with clawbacks if capacity isn’t online by 2031. Resistance from member states fearing centralisation is being met with co-governance guarantees and regional development funds tied to local job pipelines. The real fight is with the US, which is pressuring ASML and urging its allies to treat any EU frontier compute push as a subsidy race — we respond by framing this not as competition but as insurance: a world where only two powers control intelligence is unstable, and Europe’s role is to be the third pole that prevents duopoly lock-in.

Simultaneously, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being negotiated in the Council’s Competitiveness formation, with a carve-out from state-aid rules for data centres meeting EU control and resilience thresholds. We are offering accelerated grid access and tax incentives in exchange for binding commitments on data sovereignty, uptime, and access for public agencies. The package is fragile — Austria and Finland worry about energy strain, while Ireland and Sweden fear exclusion — so we are pairing it with a pan-European grid modernisation fund under the Green Deal Industrial Plan to spread benefits. This is not just about attracting capital; it’s about ensuring that when a model is run, the plug is in EU soil and the keys are not subject to foreign executive order.
