This measure is the cornerstone of our two-year commitment—without domestic compute anchored in EU law, no evaluation, no restriction, no resilience can be trusted as truly under our control.

## In practice  
We are moving now on three fronts. First, under Article 114 TFEU, the Commission is drafting delegated acts to operationalise the InvestAI Gigafactories fund, releasing €50bn in initial capital to be matched by member states and private investors. The Council’s Competitiveness formation will debate the first site allocations in December, with Spain, Poland and Finland already positioning for host status. Resistance from smaller states fearing exclusion is being met with a guarantee of distributed infrastructure—each site will feed into a federated EU AI grid, not serve national champions. The real friction is energy: 800 megawatts per site cannot be sourced without overriding national grid priorities, so we are preparing a regulation under the Security of Energy Supply framework to designate AI compute as critical infrastructure.

Second, the Tech sovereignty package is being advanced through the European Parliament’s IMCO and ITRE committees, where amendments are attempting to dilute the accelerated-permitting clauses. We are offering a compromise: faster approvals tied to binding commitments on local hiring, energy efficiency above 1.2 PUE, and open-weight prohibitions for models above 45.0 capability. This trade—speed for safeguards—has quieted some opposition, particularly in Germany and the Nordic states.

Third, we are launching the legal and institutional groundwork for the new measure: real-time EU access to frontier models. This will be built through a new mandate for the European AI Office, backed by a network of vetted research institutes under the EMAI (European Mechanism for AI Integrity). The model is inspired by the Euratom framework: developers gain market access in return for continuous transparency. The first target is access to any model above 52.0 capability undergoing deployment in the EU, with audit rights extending into training runs and agent behaviour logs. Pushback from US labs is expected, but we are prepared to make market access conditional—this is not a request, but a requirement for doing business here. The cost in political capital is high, but lower than the cost of blind trust.
