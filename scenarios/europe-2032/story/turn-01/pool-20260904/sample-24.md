## In practice
We are moving immediately to convert the InvestAI Gigafactories from a funding promise into an operational pipeline. This means activating Article 122 TFEU for emergency energy allocation, so that the first two sites—selected in Finland and France for their grid stability and cooling capacity—receive priority access to power infrastructure. We are convening the Competitiveness Council in early September to lock in member-state commitments, backed by a revised legal base under the European Chips Act, which allows us to designate these facilities as “strategic AI infrastructure” and fast-track permitting under the Tech sovereignty package.  

Resistance is strongest in Germany and Austria, where concerns over energy diversion from households and industry are politically acute. To overcome this, we are offering a side-car guarantee: any megawatt used for Gigafactories will be matched by an investment in national smart-grid resilience, funded from the same envelope. We are also engaging ENTSO-E to model load-balancing scenarios that show aggregate grid risk can be contained. The Commission’s Joint Research Centre will publish a technical annex by November confirming that these sites meet NATO-equivalent cyber-physical security standards, a prerequisite for hosting models above 50.0 capability.  

The goal is clear: by the end of this turn, construction must be underway, not just permitted. This is not about catching up—it is about ensuring that when the next leap in capability occurs, the EU has a sovereign platform capable of running, evaluating, and if necessary containing it. Without that, every other measure—safety, resilience, oversight—becomes a request rather than a right.
