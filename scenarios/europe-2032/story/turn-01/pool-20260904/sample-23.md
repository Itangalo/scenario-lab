## New measure  
Accelerate EU sovereign compute access for crisis response  
A dedicated, legally ring-fenced allocation of frontier AI capacity on EU soil, reserved for emergency use in cyber, bio, and infrastructure crises.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the foundational lever of our two-year commitment: without sovereign compute, we cannot act independently when the world’s most capable systems are withheld or weaponised. While other measures support diffusion or oversight, only this one changes the balance of power.

## In practice  
We are executing the InvestAI Gigafactories programme through the European Chips Act legal base, with the Commission’s DG CONNECT and the European Investment Bank co-ordinating site selection and funding disbursement. The first two sites — in Finland and Spain — are moving toward grid connection, but permitting delays in Bavaria and Wallonia threaten the timeline. We are offering member states co-financing for local infrastructure upgrades and fast-tracking environmental assessments under the new emergency clause in the Energy Infrastructure Regulation. Resistance from regional authorities, concerned about energy allocation and water use, is being met with binding commitments on local job creation and grid rebalancing.

The Tech sovereignty package is being advanced through the Competitiveness Council, with a draft directive on accelerated permitting for AI infrastructure to be tabled in Q1 2027. We are negotiating with hyperscalers — particularly those dependent on ASML tools — to tie expanded EU operations to long-term compute access agreements, using export leverage as a quiet bargaining chip. The goal is not just more data centres, but ones that are legally and physically anchored to EU jurisdiction, with enforceable rights of access for public agencies.

The new measure — sovereign crisis compute — will be implemented through a new protocol under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism. A minimum of 5% of total operational capacity at each InvestAI site will be reserved and pre-configured for emergency use, with access controlled by a joint committee of the Commission, ENISA, and the European Medicines Agency. This is not a new build, but a binding allocation within existing infrastructure, which allows it to stand up faster. It signals that sovereignty is not just about competitiveness, but about survival when the outside world locks us out.
