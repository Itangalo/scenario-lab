## New measure
``Strengthen EU evaluation authority with pre-deployment review rights (category 1, costs 3 per turn, started turn 1, finishes on turn 3): Grant independent evaluators legal access to model architectures and training data before release``
`Category: 1 (Evaluation and oversight)`
`Size: large`
`Finishes on turn: 3`
`Targeted effect:` Increases `ai_safety` by at least +10 points; raises `eu_political_capital` by demonstrating credible risk governance; signals to developers that the EU is not a passive market but a standards-setter.
`Applies to:` The Union's own jurisdiction, specifically all general-purpose AI systems above a defined capability threshold placed on the EU market.

## Priority
``Strengthen EU evaluation authority with pre-deployment review rights`` — because without independent verification of safety claims, every other measure rests on trust in actors who have already demonstrated opacity and self-interest, and because this turn offers a narrow window before the next wave of agent-capable models locks in new norms.

## In practice
This turn, we begin work through the AI Office in Brussels to draft a binding implementing act under Article 12 of the AI Act, expanding the mandate of EU-certified third-party assessors to include mandatory pre-release evaluation of model capabilities, training data provenance, and weight security. Legal services are reviewing whether the current Act allows this extension unilaterally, or whether a formal amendment will be required — if the latter, we prepare a proposal for urgent revision under comitology, to be fast-tracked through COREPER and the Council's Internal Market configuration. The European Parliament’s EDP group is already signalling support, but resistance is expected from member states with large digital economy dependencies on US cloud providers, particularly Ireland and the Netherlands. To offset this, we pair the proposal with an offer of preferential access to InvestAI sites for any hyperscaler that submits voluntarily to early review, tying compliance to tangible benefits in compute sovereignty. We also allocate €1.2bn from Horizon Europe to expand the European Laboratory for Learning and Security (ELLS) in Paris and Helsinki, doubling its audit capacity by mid-2027 to deliver on the review burden. This is not just oversight — it is the first step in making the EU a pole of judgment, not just consumption.
