## New measure  
**EU Sovereign Agent Stack**  
Establish a publicly governed, EU-operated AI agent framework capable of executing strategic tasks under human oversight, built on EU-controlled infrastructure and trained with sovereign data pipelines.

`Category: 4 (Sovereignty and industrial capacity)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 8`  
`Targeted effect:` Increases eu_ai_sovereignty by 15–20 points; begins closing the gap between openweight and closed capability within EU jurisdiction; creates a foundation for autonomous operation of critical public services.  
`Applies to:` EU institutions and member states, with binding participation from national research agencies and energy providers.

## Priority  
**InvestAI Gigafactories** — This is the foundational lever of our two-year commitment: without physical compute on EU soil, no sovereign stack, no resilience, no control. While the new agent initiative is critical, gigafactories anchor the entire strategy in material reality — and their six-year buildout cannot afford delays or hesitation this turn.

## In practice  
We are moving immediately on the legal and financial architecture for the InvestAI Gigafactories, convening an emergency meeting of the Competitiveness Council under Article 114 TFEU to fast-track the designation of strategic AI zones. These will override national permitting bottlenecks using the same mechanism applied to critical energy infrastructure, backed by a new EU-level permitting authority hosted within the European Climate, Infrastructure and Environment Executive Agency (CINEA). The €200bn will be split: 60% in direct grants tied to local workforce development clauses, 40% in low-interest loans conditioned on full energy redundancy and grid-sharing agreements. Resistance from smaller member states fearing exclusion is being countered with a binding commitment to distribute at least two sites across Central and Eastern Europe, negotiated directly with the EPP and S&D groups to secure trilogue alignment by December.

Simultaneously, the Tech Sovereignty Package is being reoriented to prioritise energy access — we are drafting a Council Regulation under the internal market legal base to require member states to reserve 30% of new renewable capacity for AI infrastructure, with penalties for non-compliance deducted from Cohesion Fund disbursements. This is politically fraught, especially in Spain and Portugal, where solar expansion is already strained; we are offering joint ownership stakes in the new data centres to national utilities as compensation. On the diplomatic front, we are leveraging ASML’s strategic position to negotiate a reciprocal access deal with Taiwan Semiconductor on EU-based fabrication, using export licensing as a bargaining chip — a move already drawing quiet protests from Washington, but one we judge necessary to break dependency.

The new Sovereign Agent Stack will begin as a pilot under the Digital Europe Programme, with €12bn allocated from unused REACT-EU funds and managed by a newly constituted AI Systems Directorate within the Commission. It will integrate existing national efforts in France’s Moulin and Germany’s GAIA-X, but with binding operational authority and a mandate to achieve continuous-agent functionality by turn 8. The first use cases will be in border control automation and pandemic modelling, chosen to demonstrate value while avoiding public backlash. Crucially, the model weights will never leave EU-certified hardware, and training data will be sourced exclusively from public-sector archives and opt-in citizen data cooperatives — a limit we are imposing unilaterally to differentiate from both US and Chinese models. This is not just about capability; it is about proving that the EU can run AI on its own terms.
