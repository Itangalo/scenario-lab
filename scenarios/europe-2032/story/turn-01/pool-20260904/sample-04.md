## New measure
**Measure: Launch EU Sovereign AI Lab**

Establish a publicly governed frontier AI laboratory under EU legal authority, capable of training and deploying models up to the open frontier, with full weight security, interpretability research integrated, and red-teaming as a core function.

`Category: 5 (Public technical capacity and research)`

## Priority
**Priority: InvestAI Gigafactories** — because without domestic compute infrastructure, no sovereign lab, no resilient adoption, and no independent oversight can ever land, and this is the foundational bottleneck we are already two years behind on.

## In practice
This turn, we begin anchoring AI sovereignty in physical reality. The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is now active: we have convened the first meeting of the European Investment Bank, national energy ministers, and grid operators to identify four priority sites — two in the Nordic region leveraging geothermal and hydro, two in southern Europe with solar co-location potential. We are fast-tracking environmental assessments under Article 21 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, invoking strategic infrastructure status. Resistance from regional planning authorities is expected; we respond with binding commitments to local reinvestment, including grid upgrades that prioritise municipal renewables access. The budget is drawn from the InvestEU envelope, augmented with REACT-EU carryover funds.

Simultaneously, we lay the legal and institutional groundwork for the EU Sovereign AI Lab. This begins with a proposal to the Council for a Regulation establishing the European Artificial Intelligence Research Organisation (EAIRo), to be headquartered in a member state offering both political neutrality and strong research infrastructure — Belgium, Austria, or Finland. EAIRo will be empowered to recruit globally, retain talent via fast-track visas and competitive salaries, and operate under a security mandate equivalent to that of the European Central Bank. We initiate negotiations with CERN, EMBL, and ESA as governance models. Crucially, we tie its compute supply directly to the output of the Gigafactories, ensuring that hardware and mission grow together. Industry voices warn of duplication; we counter that private labs innovate at speed, but only the Union can align capability with accountability. This is not competition — it is redundancy, and ultimately, insurance.
