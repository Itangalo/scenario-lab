Establish legal and technical guarantees that EU institutions and critical agencies can run and access frontier AI systems independent of foreign jurisdiction or commercial terms.

## In practice
We are launching the InvestAI Gigafactories initiative in earnest this turn, anchoring it in the European Chips Act’s legal framework and routing funding through the European Investment Bank with co-guarantees from Germany, France, and the Netherlands. The first site selection will be decided by December, with Spain, Poland, and Finland in final contention—each offering grid headroom and political willingness to fast-track environmental assessments. The real friction is not geographic but industrial: US-based operators are reluctant to commit without ironclad assurances on export continuity of advanced chips, while domestic utilities fear being locked into 20-year power contracts that crowd out residential demand. We are offering a tripartite guarantee: ASML export stability via a new Council regulation under Article 346 TFEU, priority grid access under revised TEN-E guidelines, and a €30bn liquidity backstop for host-state energy providers.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is entering its first legislative trilogue, with the Parliament pushing to expand the accelerated-permitting zones to include legacy industrial sites and the Commission resisting over environmental compliance risks. We are holding firm on a narrow definition to preserve credibility, but offering a parallel fast-track for brownfield data centres under the Just Transition Fund to appease the cohesion argument. Behind the scenes, we are conditioning access to the InvestAI fund on binding commitments to EU jurisdiction—no remote kill switches, no foreign legal override, no data sovereignty carve-outs. This is not just about capacity; it is about control that cannot be revoked by a server call from Mountain View or Redmond.

The new EU Sovereign Access Directive is being drafted in DG CONNECT with input from ENISA and the Joint Research Centre, aiming for a proposal to the College by Q1 2027. It will mandate that at least two sovereign AI stacks—hardware, software, and operational control—be fully operational by mid-2028, with priority access for defence, energy, and emergency response. The model is inspired by the Gaia-X precedent, but with teeth: participation in EU procurement above a certain threshold will require compliance. This is not about building a European GPT, but about ensuring that when the next Kimi K3-level capability emerges, we are not left begging for access or trusting in goodwill.
