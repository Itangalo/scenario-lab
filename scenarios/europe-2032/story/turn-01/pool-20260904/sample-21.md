## In practice  
We are activating the InvestAI Gigafactories fund through the European Sovereignty Fund, with the first allocation decisions to be made by the Competitiveness Council in December. The legal base is Article 122 TFEU, invoked for strategic infrastructure, and we are pairing it with a Council Recommendation to align national energy ministries on priority grid access. The resistance is not ideological but practical: Germany and Poland stress grid stability, while Spain and Greece demand regional equity in site selection. We are offering binding commitments on workforce development and local reinvestment to break the deadlock, using the Just Transition Fund as a co-financing lever.  

The Tech sovereignty package is moving through the European Parliament’s ITRE and ECON committees, where concerns about "gold-plating" permitting exceptions are being met with a strict sunset clause: accelerated zones expire in 2036 unless renewed under new safety and energy efficiency benchmarks. We are also negotiating with three major hyperscalers — one American, one Asian, one European — to anchor their next-gen deployments in these zones, with access conditioned on data residency, audit rights, and non-discrimination clauses that ensure EU institutions retain priority compute access during crises.  

The new measure — emergency grid and permitting access — is being framed as a security imperative, not an industrial favour. We are drafting a Regulation under the existing framework of the Net-Zero Industry Act, designating AI compute as a strategic infrastructure class alongside clean hydrogen and battery production. This allows us to bypass normal environmental impact assessment timelines, but only for sites that meet strict energy-recycling thresholds (e.g., district heating integration). The pushback from environmental NGOs is expected, so we are pre-emptively publishing heat reuse and water sourcing requirements, and committing 15% of each site’s energy output to public research and SME access. This is not just about speed — it is about proving that sovereignty does not mean surrendering sustainability.
