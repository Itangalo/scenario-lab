## New measure  
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure**  
Launch immediate, targeted hardening of energy, water and port operators against AI-enabled intrusions, using existing EU civil protection mechanisms and emergency funding.  
Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)  
Capital cost: medium  
Lead time: 1 turn  
Targeted effect: resilience +10–15, ai_safety indirect support via reduced incident surface  
Applies to: particular member states (those with affected operators and others at high risk)

## Priority  
Priority: Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure  
This measure is urgent because the cyber_test_shot incident proves that undetectable, persistent intrusions are already possible with current AI capabilities, and our resilience metric of 35.0 leaves essential services critically exposed — especially in light of AI systems that can operate continuously and autonomously. Acting now, under the authority of existing civil protection frameworks, allows a rapid response without overrunning public or member-state consent, aligning with our identities of legitimacy_by_consent and evidence_before_assertion.

## Actions  
Initiate emergency coordination under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism to assess the full scope of the cyber_test_shot intrusion across affected EU member states, deploying ENISA and Joint Research Centre teams to support national authorities in forensic analysis and containment, with preliminary findings to be delivered within six weeks.  
Direct the Commission to activate pre-existing emergency funding instruments, including the Crisis and Investment Facility, to finance immediate defensive upgrades — such as air-gapped monitoring systems, enhanced anomaly detection using AI-augmented SOC tools, and manual override protocols — for electricity transmission operators, major water utilities, and strategic port authorities identified as vulnerable.  
Convene a special meeting of the European Council on AI and Critical Infrastructure, presenting evidence from the cyber_test_shot incident and proposing a binding but limited-duration directive requiring minimum hardening standards across all member states’ essential services, to be implemented within 12 months, with technical support coordinated by ENISA and the newly empowered Scientific Panel.  
Begin structuring a European AI Rapid Assessment Unit under the Scientific Panel, to be staffed by cross-institutional experts and equipped with access to frontier-model proxies and red-teaming capabilities, enabling the EU to independently evaluate AI-enabled threats rather than relying solely on developer disclosures or foreign intelligence.
