## New measure  
**Secure Access for EU Evaluators**  
Establish a legally mandated, technically sovereign EU evaluation capability for advanced AI models, with direct access to weights and real-time monitoring during pre-deployment testing.  

- **Category:** 1 (Evaluation and oversight)  
- **Capital cost:** medium  
- **Lead time:** 2 turns  
- **Targeted effect:** +10–15 ai_safety, +5 eu_ai_sovereignty (via independent verification capacity), +5 eu_political_capital (if successful and visible)  
- **Applies to:** frontier developers operating in the EU (including US-based), under AI Act systemic-risk provisions

## Priority  
**Secure Access for EU Evaluators** — because without independent visibility into what models can do, we cannot credibly assess risk, enforce our rules, or build public and member-state trust in our capacity to act — and the Hugging Face and cyber_test_shot incidents prove that self-reporting is already obsolete.

## Actions  
Initiate emergency rulemaking under Article 46 of the AI Act to require all providers of general-purpose models above a defined capability threshold to grant secure, auditable access to model weights and training data for EU-appointed evaluators, hosted within sovereign EU infrastructure. This access will be time-limited, non-recallable, and subject to strict containment protocols, with penalties for non-compliance including market suspension.  

Convene a task force of ENISA, the AI Office, and selected national security agencies to design the technical architecture for a sovereign evaluation environment by Q1 2027, including air-gapped facilities, red-team orchestration, and automated anomaly detection — prioritising resilience against model escape or exfiltration attempts.  

Launch negotiations with Mistral and other EU-aligned developers to establish the first pilot access channel, using them as a test case for compliance mechanics and building domestic goodwill, while simultaneously signalling to US providers that equivalence will require reciprocity in access — not just paperwork.  

Commission a fast-track study from the Scientific Panel on the minimum technical requirements for detecting autonomous agent behaviour and emergent cyber capabilities, to inform the evaluation protocol’s design and ensure it targets real risks, not theoretical ones — grounding our actions in `evidence_before_assertion`.  

Begin preparatory work on a €6 billion allocation from the InvestAI Fund to finance the physical and human infrastructure of the evaluation regime, including secure data centres and recruitment of frontier-aware technical staff, while ensuring this does not trigger fiscal overreach that would fracture `legitimacy_by_consent`.
