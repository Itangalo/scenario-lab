## Priority
InvestAI Gigafactories is the priority this turn because it anchors the EU’s only plausible path to independent capability, and momentum must be locked in before the next transatlantic control shock.

## In practice
The InvestAI Gigafactories programme is now in active negotiation across the Council’s Competitiveness formation, with the Commission preparing the legal base under Article 122 TFEU for economic cohesion and strategic investment. The challenge is not funding — the €200bn is ring-fenced — but speed and control: member states are vying for sites, and we are pressing hard to attach legally binding conditions on access, including veto rights over foreign third-party use and mandatory security audits. France and Germany support this, but Poland and Hungary resist, fearing exclusion. We are offering them participation in a secondary tier of hosting rights in exchange for dropping blocking tactics.

Simultaneously, the Tech sovereignty package is advancing through the European Parliament’s ITRE and ECON committees, where resistance centres on environmental impact and energy allocation. We are countering by linking the package to binding renewable build-out targets and proposing that no site receives accelerated permitting unless it draws at least 70% of its power from new clean generation. This ties industrial ambition to climate credibility, and we believe it will pass — but only if we absorb some delay.

The cyber_test_shot event has hardened resolve, but not yet reshaped action. We are not waiting for another. The new measure — Hardened Grid Access — begins drafting this month under DG ENER and DG CONNECT, with a pilot in Belgium and Finland already identified. It will mandate that qualifying AI infrastructure receives priority grid connection, but only if it runs sovereign models under EU legal jurisdiction and meets physical security and monitoring standards. This is not just about speed; it is about making sure that when we say "EU-controlled compute", we mean something that cannot be switched off from outside.
