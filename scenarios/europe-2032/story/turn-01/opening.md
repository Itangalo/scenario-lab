## Priority  
**Emergency Resilience Surge for Critical Infrastructure** — Because the cyber_test_shot proves that detection failed and that AI-coordinated intrusions are already operating below the radar, we cannot wait for industrial capacity to mature before securing what keeps the lights on; this surge protects the Union’s physical continuity while we build long-term sovereignty.

## Actions  
Initiate emergency trilogue negotiations with the European Parliament and Council to fast-track a Council Implementing Regulation under Article 122 TFEU, enabling the Commission to mandate and co-fund cyber hardening of designated critical infrastructure within six months, with technical standards to be issued by ENISA by the end of this turn. The package will include pre-approved toolkits for intrusion detection tuned to AI-generated attack patterns, failover protocols, and real-time monitoring obligations for operators of essential services affected in the recent intrusion.

Direct the European Defence Agency and ENISA to jointly conduct a red-team exercise simulating an AI-coordinated cascade failure across energy and water systems by Q1 2027, feeding findings directly into the design of the Emergency Resilience Surge. This exercise will be the first EU-wide test of systemic AI-enabled threat response and will inform binding upgrade requirements for all critical nodes.

Mobilise €12 billion from the Union’s Recovery and Resilience Facility and the Digital Europe Programme to front-load funding for the most vulnerable member states, conditioned on compliance with the new hardening timeline. This reallocation will be presented as a temporary reprioritisation, preserving long-term industrial investments but recognising that resilience is the precondition for any sovereign AI future.

Begin consultations with national cybersecurity agencies and infrastructure operators to identify the top 50 high-risk nodes across the Union’s energy, water, and port networks, ensuring the Emergency Resilience Surge targets systems where failure would trigger cross-border disruption. These consultations will also serve to align technical implementation with existing national frameworks, preserving legitimacy while accelerating action.
