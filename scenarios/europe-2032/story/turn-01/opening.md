# Turn 1 — the shared frame

Turn 1 is identical in every arm and on every branch. Two things are fixed here and are not the reader's to choose: the event, and what the Union already carries.

The event is `cyber_test_shot`, pinned by `pin-turn-1.py` rather than drawn. The world state is the scenario's own opening description, seeded from `background/context.md`.

What the Union carries into the turn is the portfolio below. It appears verbatim in both options, because it is inherited rather than chosen — two programmes already running when the actor takes over.

## Portfolio
- ``InvestAI Gigafactories (category 4, costs 3 per turn, started turn 1, finishes on turn 7): €200bn fund for four to five sites``
- ``Tech sovereignty package (category 4, costs 3 per turn, started turn 1, finishes on turn 6): Targets €200bn of private capital for AI data centres by 2036 and proposes accelerated-permitting zones``

## What the reader chooses

Everything else in the turn-1 response — the two-year commitment, the new measure, the priority and the prose — belongs to the option, because the two-year commitment is named in turn 1 and it is the thing the first choice decides.

- [`option-02-1.md`](option-02-1.md) — **Emergency Resilience Surge.** Harden critical infrastructure now. Large, finishes turn 2, NIS2 emergency powers and a €12bn fund. The fastest thing the Union can do, and it buys time rather than building anything.
- [`option-02-2.md`](option-02-2.md) — **Hardened Grid Access for Strategic AI Infrastructure.** Fast-track grid connection for data centres running sovereign models under EU jurisdiction. Large, finishes turn 5. Slower, and what it builds outlasts the incident that prompted it.

Each option file is a complete, valid turn-1 actor response, portfolio included, so it can be passed to `pin-turn-1.py` as it stands. Both are verbatim draws from `actor-samples/opening-turn-01/` — `sample-30` and `sample-24` — and are not edited.
