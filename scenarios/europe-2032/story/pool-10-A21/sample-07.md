We are no longer merely acting under uncertainty — we are acting inside a fog of our own making. Therefore, we bind ourselves further: we will not only act early, but we will demand that every major commitment be paired with a public, auditable theory of harm and a corresponding monitoring mechanism. The price of being late is high, but the price of being wrong without learning from it is higher.

The fog is thickening faster than we can light it. Thus, we strengthen the commitment: from now on, any measure costing more than 2 political capital per turn must include a pre-registered monitoring protocol, published alongside its theory of harm, and subject to independent audit by turn-end. If we are to act under fog, we must at least leave tracks.

## In practice
We are treating the cyber_major_incident as a wake-up call that cannot be ignored. Though attribution remains months away, the attack pattern — a self-propagating ransomware variant exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities in municipal service platforms — bears the hallmarks of model-generated tooling. We have activated crisis coordination under the NIS3 framework, with ENISA leading containment, but response remains fragmented. The incident exposes a brutal truth: we have built sovereign inference capacity, but no sovereign defence-in-depth. The Public Trust Observatory must now pivot to deliver its first report within six weeks, not six months. We are directing the Joint Research Centre to convene a red-cell exercise this month, simulating how a 75.0-capable agent could weaponise openweight models for cyber or bio disruption, and publishing both the scenario and our mitigation assumptions. This will be paired with a public theory of harm for autonomous offensive synthesis, co-signed by Europol and the European Biosecurity Board.

At the same time, we are launching the Emergency Compute Mobilisation Directive in parallel. The Commission is drafting a regulation under Article 172 TFEU, invoking exceptional circumstances to bypass normal procurement and environmental assessment timelines. We are identifying 12 national supercomputing nodes — including those in France, Germany, and Finland — for immediate repurposing to sovereign AI training, backed by a €12bn emergency allocation from the repurposed InvestAI fund. Grid bottlenecks are the main constraint, so we are tasking ENTSO-E with fast-tracking transmission upgrades to four priority sites, using emergency powers under the Electricity Regulation. Resistance is expected from Austria and Sweden, where data centre expansion faces strong local opposition, so we are offering conditional compensation: host regions will receive 15% of compute time for public research and education. This is not just about speed — it is about proving that the EU can move when the threshold is crossed.
