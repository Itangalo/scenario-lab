We are treating the completion of the Accelerated Sovereign Core Deployment as a technical milestone, not a victory. The node in Paris runs, but it is isolated — the Baltics remain bound to US clouds under contracts the Commission cannot unilaterally void. We are now pushing ECOFIN to approve transitional co-financing: 70% reimbursement for migration costs, funded from repurposed InvestAI reserves. Simultaneously, we are drafting a joint liability shield — the Union assumes 90% of disruption risk during transition — to break legal deadlock. But the real leverage lies in Article 172 TFEU: we are fast-tracking the Public Trust Observatory’s founding regulation through the Research Programme, bypassing unanimity requirements. The Joint Research Centre is assembling the first team, drawing from the disbanded Institutional Integrity Audit, and negotiating access to Europol’s classified threat feeds. National CERTs are being integrated into a unified EU watch, with shared AI-driven detection models — developed from the Sovereign Core stack — deployed within six weeks. This is not about perfect security — it is about creating recoverable failure, so that when (not if) the next attack comes, services degrade gracefully, not catastrophically. Public communications will stress visible progress: dashboards showing hardened nodes, not just warnings.
