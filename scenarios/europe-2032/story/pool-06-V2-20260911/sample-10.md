## Priority
M5 Middle-Power Supply-Chain Compact, because the member-state side-deal threatens to unravel joint bargaining with Washington just as the automated cyber incident proves dependence is now a security liability, not just an industrial one.

## In practice
We run the cyber response off what the finished Grid Shield left behind: ENISA-led incident coordination under NIS2 Article 11, CER mutual-aid teams redeployed to hit municipalities and clinics, and the spare-parts and legacy-controller lists handed to national budgets in December now used to triage restoration. No new fund is opened; DG HOME reallocates CEF-Digital bridging to keep hospitals and ports degrading rather than stopping.

On defection we hold the line politically, not financially. In the Trade and Competitiveness Councils we name the side-deal as falling inside the Anti-Coercion file kept live last turn, offer the defecting capital a carve-in to the common Washington compute ask via the Netherlands/Japan/Korea table, and warn that separate hyperscaler terms void solidarity on tiered-access relief. DG CNECT and the EIB meanwhile do only preservation on M1: permits, reservations and grid connections on the five sites kept warm, no cash promised.
