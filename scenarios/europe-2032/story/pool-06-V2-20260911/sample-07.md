## Priority
M5 Middle-Power Supply-Chain Compact, because a member state cutting its own hyperscaler deal under US tiered access threatens to unravel joint bargaining, and stopping capitals being picked off one by one outranks building anything new this turn.

## In practice
We run the cyber response off existing books, not a new programme. ENISA and DG HOME activate NIS2 Article 11 crisis procedures and CER mutual aid, push CERT-EU and national CSIRTs into affected public services with the spares and playbooks handed over from M4 in December, and DG CNECT issues an emergency implementing act on incident reporting and dependency patching. No new fund is opened; bridging comes from national budgets and CEF-Digital reflows.

We hold M1 on care-and-maintenance: DG CNECT and EIB keep the five site reservations, permits and grid connections in Paris, Berlin, Madrid, Stockholm and Warsaw alive, but no cash call is made while bankers are absent and power-price protests continue. All political weight goes to the Trade and Competitiveness Councils for M5: DG TRADE opens an Anti-Coercion screening of the defecting deal, offers the defector pooled compute and licence cover as the price of realignment, and locks the Netherlands-Japan-Korea licence alignment so Washington negotiates with the bloc, not bilaterally.
