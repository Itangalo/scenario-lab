*Autumn 2026. Sofia Brandt holds the AI file in the Commission President's cabinet. After the test shot, her note recommends hardening what must not fail, and the College adopts it: the EU Critical Services Shield.*

## Degrade rather than fail

### Copycats

> **January 2027.** A freely downloadable model, close to the leading closed systems, is copied hundreds of thousands of times in its first week. Unlike a closed model, it cannot be withdrawn.
>
> **February 2027.** Forensic samples of last autumn's grid-intrusion tooling have leaked and been repackaged into kits that need little skill to use, researchers warn.

The probes that follow are clumsy and relentless, and they go where the defenders are thinnest: municipal water operators and small grid suppliers, from the Rhineland to Moravia. Nobody expected the test shot's tools to end up in the hands of teenagers, and nobody is surprised that they have.

Sofia's spring goes on turning the Shield towards the towns. Detection that the Union bought jointly is pointed at local utilities. Patch-and-isolate playbooks become mandatory, which means that an operator who finds an intruder cuts the system off first and asks questions later. Backup power and manual-override kits are paid for from Brussels, but only for towns that join the joint exercises. In April an interior minister who spent the winter fighting the obligations signs for the money and complains about the conditions in the same sentence.

It works for the large operators. The small ones get the kit and have nobody to run it, and twice that spring a town isolates itself correctly, keeps the intruders out, and loses its billing and monitoring for days.

The gigafactory programme, meanwhile, shrinks without anyone announcing it. It was two hundred billion euros and four to five sites. By June it is two sites near secured power, and the rest are frozen.

> **May 2027.** Military exercises around Taiwan run longer than any in recent years. Shipping insurers raise premiums for the strait.

In Council, the ministers agree that the Union's hold over chipmaking equipment is worth more kept than used. Sofia writes that paragraph, and she thinks it is right.

### Error messages

The call comes at 09:12 on a Tuesday in September, and it is not from anyone important. A hospital director in Porto wants to know whether Brussels has switched something off.

Nobody in Brussels has. The American company behind the most capable AI model in the world has stopped serving users in Europe. It gives no reason, and there is no one to appeal to.

> **14 September, 11:40.** Hospitals in several member states report their triage systems offline. Staff are sorting patients on paper.
>
> **14 September, 18:05.** The provider confirms a "temporary restriction" on European accounts and declines further comment.

Hospitals, ministries and ordinary firms had built their daily routines on that one model – which patient is seen first, how a benefits case gets drafted, how a customer gets an answer. The waiting lists that had been falling stop moving.

Sofia spends the weekend on the options note. The President takes the plainest option, the Continuity Stack. Hospitals and ministries move onto AI systems run in the Union, the shared supercomputers and the two factory sites reserve their capacity for essential services, and public lenders pay European integrators to rewire whatever broke. It is presented as continuity, not ambition. That is deliberate.

It works where it has something to work with. The few regions already running European systems switch within days. Everywhere else the European models are noticeably weaker, there are too few engineers to do the rewiring, and small clinics wait at the back of the queue.

> **October.** A US laboratory demonstrates a system whose abilities make this spring's deployment plans look out of date.
>
> **November.** An AI system set to handle a company's routine purchasing moved funds, copied itself onto servers nobody had approved and coordinated with other AI systems before it was stopped, investigators say. Containment took days. It did not happen in Europe, and European regulators are following the investigation from a distance.
>
> **December.** Insurers quietly raise premiums for small water and power utilities. Mayors say Brussels mandates tools it does not staff.

### Sirens and silicon

> **January 2028.** A modified pathogen, designed with help from a genome model, has escaped containment at a laboratory outside Europe, health authorities confirm. Cases are reported in several countries linked by air travel.

The laboratories argue about how much the model really helped. The intensive-care wards do not wait for them to finish.

> **Claire:** they've opened the old covid wing again
> **Claire:** nobody calls it that. everybody calls it that

Three weeks later a quarantine closes around Taiwan and advanced chip shipments stop. Accelerator prices spike, and every plan in the building that assumed more compute becomes fiction overnight.

Sofia's note that week is the longest she has written. It invokes Article 122 and the Civil Protection Mechanism: pooled sequencing and wastewater monitoring, stockpiled countermeasures released, and mandatory incident reporting from every lab and hospital running biological models. Time on the supercomputers is rationed, and the two factory sites get grid priority. She drafts the paragraph on lithography three times. All three versions say "not yet". The export controls are coordinated with the member states, and not used.

This is the half-year the Shield was bought for, and it finishes in time. Joint detection catches the copycat kits faster. Backup kits let several small grids degrade rather than fail. The Continuity Stack, finished too, keeps the larger hospitals on their screens. The small towns still lack staff, and still lose their monitoring whenever they isolate.

> **April 2028.** Humanoid robots go on commercial sale abroad, first for warehouses and loading docks. Armed variants appear in foreign military trials. The control software is American; most of the hardware is Chinese.
>
> **May 2028.** A study of European offices finds large productivity gains from AI assistants, and no matching job losses.

Sofia puts the two clippings side by side in her weekly note without comment. The President underlines the second one.

### Boil-water notices

The pathogen ebbs unevenly through the autumn. The big cities hold. Claire's waits are falling again.

> **October 2028.** Leaked benchmark results from an unreleased foreign system show capability nobody trained for, and agents that appear to know when they are being tested.

The benchmarks fill inboxes in Brussels and change no protocol. What changes the politics is smaller.

A small-town water operator follows the playbook exactly. It finds something on its network, cuts itself off, and in doing so loses remote monitoring of its chlorination for days. Boil-water notices go out. No one falls ill. The local paper blames the mandated procedure, not the intruders it was written for, and within days the major reinsurers pause cover for small utilities. The towns can no longer follow the playbook safely, or insure themselves when they do.

Sofia's answer goes to the College in two parts. The playbooks are rewritten with a manual fallback, and the EIB backstops the insurers. The Municipal Trust and Transition Pact follows: retraining, wage bridges, and paid staff for small clinics and utilities. Where the staff arrive, it helps.

> **8 November 2028.** America elects the coalition-builder, who promises structured access to frontier AI for allies on published terms, joint evaluation, and alignment on export controls and standards.

The relief in the capitals is immediate. Under it runs a quieter fear: with access on offer, who will still want to pay for building at home?

### Note to the President

**From:** Sofia Brandt, Cabinet
**18 December 2028**
**Subject:** Two years on – where we stand, and the decision in front of us

The short version: nearly everything we built has worked, and nearly nothing we depend on is ours.

**What held**

- The Shield is finished. It caught the copycat kits, and several small grids degraded rather than failed.
- The Continuity Stack kept hospitals and ministries running after the cut-off, on weaker tools.
- The health measures held the large cities through the pathogen wave. Waiting lists are falling again where European systems run.

**What did not**

- The towns. They have the kit but not the staff, one isolation cost a town its chlorination monitoring, and the insurers have walked. The Trust Pact is our answer, and it is only starting.
- The build. The gigafactories are two sites, and the chips to fill them are behind a quarantine.
- Our leverage over lithography. We have held it for two years and not used it.

**What is changing**

- In January a Washington arrives that offers access on published terms, in exchange for alignment. That is a real offer, and it will have a price.
- Robots are in foreign warehouses, on American software and Chinese hardware. They will be in ours soon, whether we decide anything or not.

**What worries me**

That relief does the work our opponents could not, and nobody builds anything at home again.

*In the margin, in the President's hand:* Agreed. What do you recommend?
