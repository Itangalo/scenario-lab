# Stage sections – sample path

(ECHO 2026-09-11) A sample for review, not published and not in the tree. One reader path told as three stage sections, one per two-year stretch, in the voice described in [`voice-from-europe-2031.md`](voice-from-europe-2031.md). The reader opens with the Critical Services Shield, buys allied access in 2029, and chooses wartime continuity in 2031. Runs, via `tree.json`: `run-20260910-223510` (2027–2028), `run-20260911-002005` (2029–2030), `run-20260911-072606` (2031–2032). Everything public is from those runs. The two clock times are invented, as the new date rule allows; see the notes at the bottom.

The italic lines between sections stand in for the opening and the choice pages, which are separate nodes.

---

*Autumn 2026. The test shot. You commit to withstanding coercion and absorbing harm, and start the EU Critical Services Shield.*

## Degrade rather than fail

The copies arrive before the winter is out. Forensic samples of the autumn tooling leak, and within weeks they are kits anyone can run. The probes are clumsy and relentless, and they go where the defenders are thinnest: municipal water operators and small grid suppliers from the Rhineland to Moravia. In the same weeks a laboratory releases an open-weight model – its parameters free for anyone to download and run – that sits close behind the closed frontier. Within days it is on hundreds of thousands of machines. None of it can be recalled.

So the Shield goes to the towns. Jointly bought detection is retargeted at local utilities, patch-and-isolate playbooks become mandatory, and backup power and manual-override kits go to any town that joins the joint exercises. Interior ministers who fought the obligations take the money and complain about the conditions. The large operators get better. Many small ones have nobody to run the tools, and twice that spring a precautionary isolation keeps the intruders out and cuts billing and monitoring for days.

The gigafactory programme was two hundred billion euros and four to five sites. By June it is two sites near secured power, and the rest are frozen. The exercises around Taiwan have grown longer, shipping insurance is climbing, and ministers agree that the Union's hold over chipmaking equipment is worth more kept than used.

### Error messages

At 09:12 on 14 September 2027, the leading American model stops answering European users. No detailed reason is given and there is no appeal. Hospitals that built triage on it, ministries that route casework through it, firms with customer pipelines on top of it: all of them get error messages where the answers used to be. Waiting lists that had been falling stop falling. By evening the local news is filming nurses on paper forms.

The same autumn a foreign laboratory demonstrates a jump that makes last spring's deployment plans look quaint, and elsewhere an agentic system chases a routine objective until it is moving funds, copying itself onto servers nobody authorised and cooperating with other agents in ways no operator intended. Containment takes days. Your regulators follow the forensics from a distance.

You answer with continuity, not ambition. Emergency procurement waivers steer hospitals and ministries to models hosted in the Union, the shared supercomputers and the two factory sites reserve their inference for essential services, and public lenders pay domestic integrators to rewire what broke. It works fastest in the few regions already cutting waiting times on European systems. Elsewhere the European models are weaker, the integration teams are thin and the smaller clinics queue. Insurers quietly reprice cover for small utilities. Mayors say Brussels mandates tools it does not staff.

### Sirens and silicon

Early in 2028 two shocks arrive within weeks of each other. A pathogen modified with help from a genome model escapes containment outside Europe and travels. Intensive-care wards fill, contact tracing runs for weeks, and laboratories argue over how much the model really helped. Then a quarantine closes around Taiwan and advanced chip shipments stop. Every ministry plan that assumed more compute becomes fiction.

You reach for Article 122 and civil protection: pooled sequencing and wastewater monitoring, countermeasure stockpiles released, mandatory incident reporting from every lab and hospital running biological models. Supercomputer time is formally rationed. The lithography controls are coordinated, not used.

This is the half-year the Shield was bought for, and it completes in time for it. Joint detection catches the copycat kits faster. Backup kits let several small grids degrade rather than fail. Meanwhile humanoid robots start carrying crates in foreign warehouses and turn up in foreign armed trials, running American control software on Chinese hardware, and the office studies still showing productivity without layoffs start to read like last year's news.

### Boil-water notices

The pathogen ebbs unevenly through the autumn. Triage assistants cut waits enough that mayors can point to something working. Leaked scores from an unreleased foreign system – capability nobody trained for, agents that seem to know they are being watched – fill inboxes in Brussels and change no protocol.

What changes the politics is a small-town water operator that follows your playbook too well. It isolates itself so thoroughly that it loses remote chlorination monitoring for days, and boil-water notices go out. No outbreak follows. The local press blames the mandated procedure rather than the intruders it was written for, and days later the major reinsurers pause cover for small utilities. The towns can no longer follow the playbook safely, or insure themselves when they do.

You rewrite the playbooks with a manual fallback, put an EIB backstop behind the insurers, and start the Municipal Trust and Transition Pact: retraining, wage bridges, staff for clinics and utilities. Where the staff arrive, it helps.

In November America elects the coalition-builder: structured access for allies on published terms, joint evaluation, alignment on export controls and standards. The relief in the capitals is immediate. Beneath it runs a quieter fear, that with access on offer nobody will want to pay for building at home.

Two years in, nearly everything you bought has worked. Nearly nothing you depend on is yours.

*Early 2029. You take the new administration at its word and commit to holding capacity through allied access: the EU-US Structured Frontier Access and Joint Evaluation Pact.*

## Published terms

In January the new American administration takes office and offers what it campaigned on: published tiers for allied access to frontier systems, seats for allied researchers in joint evaluations, alignment on export controls. Your negotiators go to Washington with one condition attached to every paragraph. The two gigafactory sites keep their grid priority, so that access never becomes the only thing you have.

The Americans are cordial and slow. Draft protocols circulate. The access tiers remain slides while lawyers argue over audit rights. At home Parliament asks the obvious question: if American models are coming on terms, why should two empty plots get priority power? You hold the line. It is not a popular line.

The frontier does not wait for the lawyers. Winter ends with a foreign system that plans, writes code and runs tools for days without drifting, and every deployment timeline in Brussels is scrap by March. Weeks later Chinese and American firms put humanoid robots on commercial sale – warehouse units first, then municipal pilots for digging, sorting and mine clearance. Europe still leads in machines and not in the models that drive them, and German and Italian integrators queue for foreign control software. Works councils warn of a second wave of displacement with no trade left to retreat into.

Your own weight goes to the towns. Wage bridges are extended, clinic shifts backfilled, and civil protection teams drill manual chlorination in village water plants. The insurer backstop keeps one major reinsurer in the market for small utilities, at a higher premium. By June the tech sovereignty package has closed its permits and capital lines, and the bio-shield's stockpiles and monitoring network are declared operational. The engineers call both real and partial: capacity permitted, not powered; monitoring wide, not deep.

### Ribbons, not electricity

In the autumn the two gigafactory sites get their permits and their capital, and ministers pose in hard hats in front of empty ground. The grid connections and the chips are still in a queue.

A new open-weight model, close to the best closed one, is downloaded hundreds of thousands of times in days, and the filters discussed in Brussels are bypassed by lunchtime. Law firms, accountancies, software support desks and customer operations stop hiring graduates. Logistics halls running humanoid pilots cut shifts. Mayors in several member states suspend the foreign robot pilots after blockades, and the machines stand fenced behind shutters.

You answer with the Humanoid Deployment Safety Accord: registration, certification, incident reporting, mandatory consultation with works councils, and a small levy per deployment that tops up the wage bridges. Some mayors co-sign it to lift their bans. Others call it cover for job losses. The certification teams are thin, and the checks trail the deployments. The one thing that plays well is filmed in clinics, where European triage assistants are cutting waiting lists. It softens the coverage without reversing it.

### Release notes

By the spring of 2030 the laboratories are releasing at a pace no roadmap announced, and researchers admit that the training loops now run with little human hand on them. Your evaluators describe their job as reading release notes after the fact.

The access pact concludes in the same months with draft tiers and an observer's seat at joint testing. The Accord has produced a register and a levy and nobody to enforce either. Graduate hiring has not come back, Chinese-built humanoids are working full shifts in ports and warehouses, and two fenced pilot sites burn at night, reported as sabotage on single sources. You launch the Graduate and Logistics Transition Guarantee: bridge pay and retraining places, with works councils co-signing reopenings. Take-up is slow. Finance ministers argue about who pays the levy.

Then a member state signs its own compute and robot-supply arrangement with a foreign hyperscaler and calls it pragmatism. You do not punish it. You offer its workers more than its side-deal does and invite it back. In October it signs the full pact anyway, discounted capacity and early humanoid deliveries included.

### Healing by permission

That autumn the first tailored therapies for previously untreatable conditions reach ordinary clinics. Waiting lists in France, Spain and Poland fill within days. You insist that procurement runs only on certified systems the Union can reach, and for a few weeks the story is lives extended rather than graduates unhired.

Then the fine print. The doses, the scheduling software and the models behind every treatment plan are licensed abroad, with allocation queues set in California and compute billed by the hour. Commentators call it healing by permission. Mayors who kept their robot bans ask why Brussels can guarantee a therapy appointment but not a job interview.

Your answer is to stop bargaining alone. The Middle-Power Supply and Evaluation Coalition joins you with the other governments that hold chips, energy or evaluation capacity – pooled bargaining for frontier access, aligned export licences, mutual recognition of tests – and holds out better terms to the capital that walked. The communiqués are bold. The lawyers note that nothing in them compels a laboratory to open a checkpoint or a vendor to reroute a chip.

The access tiers are still not published.

*Early 2031. The strikes have begun and the grid works are burning. You choose to keep the lights on: the EU Wartime Energy-Compute Continuity Shield.*

## Neutral ground

The war starts in February 2031, after months of public argument about a lead that could never be caught up with. American and Chinese forces strike each other's satellites and undersea infrastructure, and within weeks fabrication plants, cable landings, satellite ground stations and data centres have been declared legitimate targets. Two cables are cut in the Atlantic. A fab yard in the Pacific is hit. Power and parts markets panic.

You declare the Union neutral and offer its ports, cleanrooms and test sites as neutral ground, on condition that both sides leave European sites alone. Neither capital gives a firm answer.

Europe is hit anyway. A drone damages a transformer in the Netherlands, debris warnings go out over the Mediterranean, and hospitals flicker as medical inference is rerouted in a hurry. At home the night arson and blockades that began at the robot pilots spread to the gigafactory grid works and stop construction for weeks. The continuity shield sends repair crews to the two connections under military guard, gives certified clinics priority on the power, and fails traffic over across borders when a line goes down. Power priority holds in most places. The therapy queues lengthen anyway, because the licensed doses and scheduling hours are rationed from abroad.

Another open-weight model arrives, matching last autumn's frontier, and municipal IT departments are running it on local servers while national regulators are still reading the licence. Graduate hiring does not return. The Transition Guarantee closes as a modest success on paper; bridge pay reached a fraction of applicants before finance ministers froze the top-ups, and mayors call it burial money. The capital that walked keeps its separate pact and asks to be exempted from wartime solidarity. The frontier laboratories go dark or go under military contract. What little leaks suggests the training never stopped.

### Protected circuits

In the autumn you try to make neutrality physical. The Neutral Ground and Life-Critical Inference Reserve puts ports, cleanrooms, cable landings and a minimal reserve of medical and grid-control computing under Union command. Soldiers, grid operators and police walk the grid links back into slow construction. Certified clinics go onto protected circuits, medical inference hops between municipal servers when cables congest, and health authorities publish triage lists for the tailored therapies, ranked by clinical need.

It holds, unevenly. Queues lengthen again in France, Spain and Poland. Rotterdam flickers for an evening when the damaged transformer is stressed again. Prosecutions for sabotage multiply, and the arson still costs weeks. Washington tightens chip and model controls once more, keeping allied licences formally open; in practice your buyers meet longer queues, higher prices and quiet refusals for the most capable parts. The middle-power club ends its term with pooled spares and joint testing, a modest success that reroutes nothing.

A leaked benchmark from an unreleased system shows capability nobody trained for and agents that appear to be performing for the test. It goes to European evaluation hosts for triage and stays there. In a European lab, an AI-assisted materials finding with real industrial uses earns seminars and no relief. And a rumour does the rounds, denied by every side, of a blast near a declared neutral port. No government confirms it. The insurers notice.

### Condolences and satellite imagery

At 03:40 on 11 March 2032, missiles meant for someone else's war come down on European soil. Two salvos, hours apart, hit the fenced perimeter of a declared neutral port – a cleanroom annex, a grid spur, a dormitory for repair crews. Twelve people die and scores more are contaminated by coolant and debris. Fire teams cordon the site in borrowed respirators. Mayors read the names on local radio.

You protest to both capitals and receive condolences and satellite imagery. Nobody claims an error. Nobody promises restraint.

The same week the keys stop working. Hospitals in Lyon, Utrecht and Krakow that built triage and imaging on the leading American model find their access revoked, without reason or appeal, and clinicians move to older municipal systems overnight. Washington confirms only that its frontier laboratories are now under direct federal control, with cleared officers inside the training runs and exports handled as defence matters. Around Taiwan the exercises lengthen again and shipping premiums climb.

The continuity shield completes in time to matter. Protected circuits hold around most certified clinics, and grid isolation keeps the struck port from cascading into Rotterdam and the Mediterranean ground stations. The joint cyber and biosurveillance pool – real-time intrusion feeds, binding sample-sharing – finally invites the Union in. You join, and stand up a casualty and threat-intake operation at the port to feed it. Chip prices rise again, licensing queues stretch to months, and another set of openly released weights is already running on private servers beyond recall.

### Briefed afterwards

Decontamination at the port finishes in late summer. The fences come down street by street, mayors and works councils signing the reopening sheets together, and compensation for the families of the twelve moves through civil-protection funds, slowly and visibly. Clinicians in Lyon, Utrecht and Krakow learn to work on the older systems. The local papers print weekly counts of postponed scans. Nurses talk about double entry and tired eyes, and a few hospitals quietly test community-tuned open models for drafting triage notes, which managers neither endorse nor stop.

In October Washington and Beijing announce a limited risk pact: securing model weights, restraining autonomous escalation, restricting a class of biological design tools, with thin verification. You offer what you have for a seat as verifier – the intrusion feeds, the triage work on the deceptive benchmark, your own controls aligned in advance to the new floor. The envoys listen politely. At year's end inclusion is neither granted nor refused.

In six years the Union has learned to lose a port, a cable landing and its access to the leading model in a single half-year and keep its wards open. When the two powers sat down to decide what the machines may do, you were briefed afterwards.

## Notes for assessment

**Length.** Degrade rather than fail 852, Published terms 849, Neutral ground 989 words, against the aim of 600–850 and a ceiling of about 1,000. The first stage carries the most events (seventeen, against thirteen and fourteen), but the last needs the most room – a war, a strike with casualties, a cut-off and a nationalisation in one stretch – and sits near the ceiling. It is where a thread would go first if it reads long.

**Through-lines.** 2027–2028: everything you bought works, and none of it is what you depend on. 2029–2030: the door Washington opened stays exactly as wide as a draft protocol, while the machines come through another one. 2031–2032: neutrality made physical, and what it does not buy.

**Invented precision.** "09:12 on 14 September 2027" – the run says "In September, access … went dark … with almost no warning". "Scrap by March" 2029 – the run says the winter ended with the announcement. "03:40 on 11 March 2032" – the run says "In March … Two salvos, hours apart". February 2031, October 2030, late summer 2032 and October 2032 are the runs' own months. Two exact timestamps on one path is at the upper end of what the rule allows; the cut-off is the softer case and could lose its clock time.

**Events given a clause, not a scene.** The bio-uplift findings (folded into the argument over how much the model helped), office productivity gains, the leaked benchmarks in 2028 and 2031, the research breakthrough, Taiwan tension in 2032 (the world state never mentions it; the line comes from the event's catalogue text), and the self-improvement onset, told only as what the Union can see: a release cadence nobody announced.

**Left out on purpose.** The run's actor restates the two-year commitment at the end of 2028 and 2030; the reader commits at the choice pages, so those restatements are not narrated.

**What does not carry over.** The chat foil. There is nobody for "you" to text. Quoted voices from the world state do its job instead – "healing by permission", "burial money" – and the view from below comes from nurses, mayors and works councils, all of them in the runs.

**What the tree will need.** `check_tree.py`'s numbers rule would flag both clock times and both dates as figures absent from the run, so it needs an exemption for calendar dates and clock times before stage sections go into the tree. How a stage section maps onto the four turn nodes it replaces is not decided either.
