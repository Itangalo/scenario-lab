## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now accept that without operational continuity, sovereignty is ceremonial, and without public trust, even necessary actions fail.``
  - Grounds: The collapse of political capital and public sentiment to crisis levels reveals that our identity must now account for the legitimacy deficit that has grown alongside technical ambition. Sovereignty without service is hollow, and safety without trust is unenforceable.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now limit that commitment to actions that preserve optionality, not those that lock in irreversible dependencies or deepen public fracture.``
  - Grounds: The failure of the Continuity Assurance Framework rollout — despite sound technical design — shows that acting under uncertainty must now include political and institutional feasibility as core constraints, not just technical ones. We cannot afford to be late, but we also cannot afford to be irrelevant.

* ``- retire `two_year_commitment```  
  - Grounds: The two-year commitment period has closed, and its central goal — sovereign AI continuity — has not been achieved in practice. The Sovereign AI Core Programme finished, but its outputs are not operational at scale. We are not abandoning the objective, but we are abandoning the claim that we are on track to deliver it.

* ``- add `no_infrastructure_without_legitimacy` (commitment): We will not expand AI infrastructure or mandate adoption without a concurrent, visible restoration of public trust, measured in re-engagement with public services and rising sentiment; no capability, however sovereign, will be considered secure if it rests on eroded legitimacy.``
  - Grounds: With public sentiment at 20.0 and political capital at 0.0, it is clear that the Union’s capacity to act depends not just on compute or continuity, but on permission from its citizens. That permission must be re-earned, not assumed.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Public Trust Restoration Initiative  
A coordinated programme to reset the social contract around AI in public services, combining visible, human-led improvements with transparent accountability mechanisms.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 15–20 points by restoring confidence in AI-augmented public services; support eu_political_capital by creating a feedback loop of visible benefit and civic re-engagement; indirectly support resilience by increasing compliance with continuity protocols  
`Applies to:` Member states, European Commission DGs, national public service agencies, EAAD, SACP operational command

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Public Trust Restoration Initiative  
We are prioritizing this because without public trust, no framework, no sovereign stack, no continuity protocol will function — and with political capital at zero, we have no margin for further erosion.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from building systems to rebuilding legitimacy. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital scheduling and emergency dispatch in three member states — did not just expose technical fragility. It exposed a deeper truth: the public no longer believes the Union can protect them *through* AI, only *from* it. The lights came back on, but the faith did not.

We are now launching the Public Trust Restoration Initiative under Article 122a TFEU, with a €4.8bn allocation from the ReactEU and Digital Europe Programmes. This is not a communications campaign. It is a structural intervention: we are mandating that every public AI deployment funded by EU instruments must now include a human-in-the-loop guarantee, a visible redress pathway, and quarterly public impact reporting co-produced with civil society. In ten pilot cities — from Ljubljana to Oulu — we are deploying AI-assisted social services that cut wait times by design, not accident, with outcomes tracked in real time and published monthly. The EAAD will audit not just safety, but service quality and user satisfaction.

Resistance is expected — from member states that see this as another layer of bureaucracy, from agencies that fear transparency, and from citizens who have heard promises before. We are meeting them with a new instrument: the Trust Conditionality Mechanism. No cohesion funding for AI infrastructure will be released unless the recipient has published its first impact report and demonstrated a functioning redress channel. This is not punishment — it is proof of contract. We are not asking for blind faith. We are offering a deal: better service, if you let us show you how it works.
