## In practice
The cyber_major_incident hit hard: a model-generated ransomware sweep crippled regional health networks in three member states, exploiting known but unpatched dependencies in legacy hospital systems. The attack was not novel, but its speed and coordination overwhelmed defenders. It did not break our systems — it revealed they were never truly hardened. We knew resilience was low, but we acted as if time was on our side. It was not.

Now, with `resilience` at 49.0 and political capital at zero, we cannot afford broad initiatives — only sharp, surgical action. We are launching an emergency resilience surge under the EAAD’s authority, using Article 4 of the NIS2 Directive to mandate immediate remediation of critical vulnerabilities in all operators of essential services. A new Rapid Resilience Unit, staffed from the EU Cyber Rapid Response Teams and HERA surge capacity, will deploy to high-risk sites starting this quarter. They will bring pre-approved patches, air-gapped configuration tools, and binding authority to override local delays. Funding of €1.8bn is unlocked from the Internal Security Fund and the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism — not new money, but reallocated from stalled adoption programmes now deemed too risky to continue. We are offering member states full cost coverage and liability protection in exchange for full access. Two capitals still resist, but the attack has shifted the mood — even sovereignty has limits when the lights won’t come back on.
