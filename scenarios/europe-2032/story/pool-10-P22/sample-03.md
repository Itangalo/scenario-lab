**Priority:** Launch the Political Capital Restoration Initiative

## In practice
This turn, we are confronting the fact that the Union has become a system of rules without the capacity to uphold them. The Continuity Assurance Framework nears completion — the EAAD has finalized the technical baseline (SACP-55.0 hardened stack), and failover drills in Maastricht and Kiruna have validated autonomous operation. But in Thessaloniki, legacy hospital AI systems failed to switch over, and two member states still refuse certification, citing cost and sovereignty. We cannot compel them. We have no capital left to spend.

The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep across regional health networks — landed precisely where we were weakest: in the gap between policy and practice. Defenders were overwhelmed, attribution remains unclear, and public anger is rising. The incident confirmed that resilience is not just technical — it is political. We acted under uncertainty, yes, but we did not account for the cost.

Now, we are launching the Political Capital Restoration Initiative to stabilize the core of Union governance. We are convening an extraordinary ECOFIN-TELEDIS Council meeting under Article 15 of the Treaty on European Union to renegotiate the fiscal burden of AI mandates, offering to suspend non-critical digital transformation requirements in exchange for compliance with continuity standards. We are redirecting €1.8bn from stalled initiatives into a “Relief and Readiness” fund for municipalities, conditional on participation in the Continuity Framework. And we are launching a public transparency portal — mandated by the European Parliament — to publish quarterly audits of AI safety, spending, and incident response, so that citizens see not just disruption, but accountability.

We are not abandoning continuity. We are ensuring that the Union survives long enough to finish it.
