## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now understand that without operational continuity, sovereignty is ceremonial, not strategic.``
  - Grounds: The failure of the Continuity Assurance Framework to achieve real-time readiness despite legal enactment reveals that control without continuity is symbolic. Our identity must reflect that sovereignty now means endurance under duress, not just technical possession.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we will no longer treat deployment as validation. If a measure cannot be exercised under real stress within six months of completion, we will assume it does not work.``
  - Grounds: The failover drills in Thessaloniki exposed a fatal lag between policy and performance. Committing under uncertainty is necessary, but we can no longer tolerate the illusion of readiness. This turn, we anchor action to demonstrable operational resilience.

* ``- retire `two_year_commitment```  
  - Grounds: The two-year commitment period has ended, and its objective — sovereign AI continuity — has been institutionalized in law and partially in practice. However, the gap between framework and function is now the central challenge. We are moving from a time-bound commitment to a structural redefinition of sovereignty.

* ``- add `no_service_without_continuity` (commitment): From now on, no public AI service classified as critical may go live without a certified, air-gapped, autonomously operable fallback node, tested under unannounced conditions, and no upgrade to such a service may proceed without revalidation of continuity.``
  - Grounds: The cyber_major_incident proved that continuity is not a feature — it is the baseline condition of service. We are binding ourselves to this standard because the cost of failure is no longer theoretical.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Operational Continuity Audit Regime  
A mandatory, real-time audit programme requiring all certified fallback nodes under the Continuity Assurance Framework to undergo unannounced activation drills every quarter, with results published and non-compliant systems automatically downgraded in criticality classification.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 6–8 points by forcing real-world validation of continuity systems; reduce the gap between policy and performance; indirectly pressure eu_ai_sovereignty by exposing where sovereign systems fail to function  
`Applies to:` Member states, EAAD, national digital agencies, critical service operators

## Priority
**Priority:** Establish the Continuity Assurance Framework  
We are prioritizing this because the cyber_major_incident revealed that having a fallback is not enough — it must work when called upon, without warning, and without external support. This is no longer about capability or control — it is about whether the state can function when cut off.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from building continuity to proving it. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled hospital scheduling systems in three member states — did not break our defences because they were weak, but because our continuity systems were offline, misconfigured, or dependent on the very networks they were meant to replace. The lights stayed off for 36 hours in Miskolc. That is unacceptable.

We are now enforcing the Continuity Assurance Framework not as a checklist, but as a live requirement. The EAAD is launching the Operational Continuity Audit Regime under Article 21 of the AI Act and NIS3, mandating unannounced drills every quarter for all certified fallback nodes. These are not simulations — they are full isolation events, with external comms cut and local AI stacks required to maintain core functions for 72 hours. Results will be published, and any system failing two consecutive drills will be downgraded from “critical” status, losing access to central funding and emergency override privileges. We are allocating €480 million from the Digital Europe Programme to cover audit logistics and remediation grants for lagging systems.

Resistance persists. Two capitals still refuse central certification, and some national CIOs argue that quarterly drills risk destabilizing fragile legacy systems. We are offering a compromise: phased audit intensity, starting with biannual drills for systems in migration, but with full quarterly enforcement by turn 13. We are also fast-tracking grid upgrades for data centres hosting fallback nodes, using emergency permitting under the Net-Zero Industry Act. This is not about control — it is about ensuring that when the next shock comes, the state does not go dark.
