## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but when they collide, we now answer first to survival: if continuity of critical function is at stake, we act to preserve it, even if that means deferring sovereignty or accelerating adoption under imperfect conditions.
- Grounds: With political capital at zero, public sentiment collapsing, and a major cyber incident exposing systemic fragility, the Union can no longer afford symmetrical attention to both mandates. The failure of enforcement mechanisms and the inability to compel compliance mean that *preventing lasting harm* must now be operationalised through *ensuring continuity of service*, not abstract control. Sovereignty without function is not sovereignty.

- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks — but we now narrow the definition of success: it is not that we *have* fallback systems, but that they *activate without delay or failure* when needed, under real duress, and with no external dependencies.
- Grounds: The Continuity Assurance Framework was meant to institutionalize resilience, but failover drills revealed that certification does not equal readiness. The cyber incident showed that systems failed to switch over autonomously, and human operators were untrained or unavailable. The commitment stands, but its standard must rise: continuity is not paperwork, it is performance under fire.

- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement is now suspended until eu_political_capital reaches 20.0, because we cannot uphold the regime without the capacity to enforce it fairly and support compliance.
- Trigger: eu_political_capital has reached 0.0, and two member states have openly defied central certification while warning of legal action. Attempting enforcement now would fracture cohesion without achieving compliance.
- Grounds: The Union cannot credibly threaten sanctions when it cannot deliver the alternative. The Sovereign AI Core Programme is finished, but its deployment is patchy. Until we can offer a working, secure alternative at scale, and until we have the political room to act, enforcement is suspended. The rule remains, but its teeth are sheathed.

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Emergency Continuity Readiness Surge  
We are prioritizing this because the Continuity Assurance Framework proved that we can mandate readiness — but the cyber incident proved we cannot assume it; if our fallbacks don’t activate when the lights go out, sovereignty is a fiction.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from compliance to competence. The Continuity Assurance Framework is law, its nodes are certified, and the money has been spent — but in Thessaloniki, when the attack hit, the switch-over failed. Not because the hardware was missing, but because the logic was brittle, the triggers unclear, and the operators untrained. We built generators — but no one taught them to start automatically.

Now, we are launching the Emergency Continuity Readiness Surge: a focused, six-month retrofit of all certified fallback nodes with AI-driven stress testing and autonomous activation protocols. The EAAD will issue binding technical updates by end-March, requiring all nodes to pass three simulated failure modes — network denial, supply-chain spoofing, and insider compromise — with zero human input. We are pulling in SACP engineers to harden the stack, and directing €800 million from unspent Digital Europe funds to cover urgent upgrades, especially in municipalities that passed certification on paper but lack operational depth.

We are meeting resistance not with penalties, but with support. Two capitals still refuse full certification — we are not cutting them off, but embedding joint task forces to help them meet the standard. This is not leniency; it is realism. We cannot enforce unity from a position of exhaustion. The cyber incident showed what happens when systems fail silently. This surge ensures that when the next shock comes, the lights don’t just come back on — they stay on.
