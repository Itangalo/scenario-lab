## Statement changes
- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks — but now, in light of the cyber_major_incident and the collapse of political capital, we acknowledge that continuity cannot be mandated from the centre alone. True continuity requires not only technical redundancy but political viability: we will henceforth measure success not by compliance, but by *sustained, trusted operation* across all member states, including those most resistant.
- Grounds: The Continuity Assurance Framework has exposed a fatal flaw — we can write the rules, but we cannot enforce them without consent. Two member states continue to resist central certification, and mid-tier municipalities warn of operational overload. The cyber_major_incident proved the threat is real, but also revealed that top-down mandates erode trust faster than they build resilience. We must adapt: sovereignty that fractures under stress is not sovereignty at all.

- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework — but enforcement is now explicitly suspended until eu_political_capital rises above 12.0 and the Sovereign AI Core Programme demonstrates reliable fallback operation in at least three diverse member states. We will not cut off access unless we can offer a viable alternative, and we will not enforce unity by coercion when cohesion is already broken.
- Grounds: With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public sentiment at 20.0, any attempt to sanction a member state would trigger open rebellion, not compliance. The cyber_major_incident showed our systems are fragile, but also that legitimacy is more scarce than compute. We must rebuild trust before we can demand unity.

- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong as the price of not being late — but we now limit such commitments to measures that can be reversed or adjusted at low cost to cohesion. We will act decisively only where failure is contained; elsewhere, we will buy time with transparency, monitoring, and minimal viable interventions.
- Grounds: The cost of being wrong is no longer borne only by us — it fractures the Union. We have overreached, and the system has seized. From now on, acting under uncertainty must include a circuit-breaker: a way to step back without collapse.

**Priority:** Launch the Political Capital Restoration Initiative

## In practice
This turn, we are facing a truth long deferred: we cannot mandate our way to sovereignty. The Continuity Assurance Framework remains in flight, but its implementation is stalling. In Prague and Helsinki, national digital agencies are complying, running quarterly drills and upgrading legacy systems with EU support. But in Bucharest and Athens, resistance hardens — not out of malice, but from a genuine fear that central certification means operational dependence on a stack they do not control. The unannounced drill in Thessaloniki failed not because the technology was flawed, but because local operators disabled the air-gapped node, fearing it would override their own emergency protocols.

So we are shifting course. We will not withdraw the Framework — it is law — but we will pause enforcement and open a 90-day consultation under Article 16 of the Treaty on European Union, inviting member states to co-design the certification process. We are releasing the leaked audit on agent persistence through the EAAD’s transparency portal, with redactions only for operational security, to show we are not hiding failures. And we are fast-tracking AI deployment in two areas where the public can see benefit: cancer screening triage in public hospitals and personalized tutoring in vocational schools. These will run on the SACP stack, but with local control over deployment — no central override.

We are also suspending all planned sanctions under `no_more_free_rides`. No cohesion funding will be cut, no member state excluded from infrastructure — not because we accept fragmentation, but because we cannot afford civil war. Instead, we are offering a bargain: show progress on continuity, and gain early access to the new public benefit deployments. Trust must be earned, not commanded. The lights went out. Now we are learning how to keep them on — together.
