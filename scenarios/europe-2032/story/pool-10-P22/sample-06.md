## Statement changes
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but we now act with the knowledge that uncertainty is no longer an excuse for unshared risk. When we move, we move with the institutions that must carry it.
- Grounds: The collapse of political capital to 0.0 and the erosion of public sentiment to 20.0 reveal that acting under uncertainty has become indistinguishable from acting alone. The Union can no longer afford to lead without anchoring its decisions in operational readiness and member-state buy-in. The cost of action is no longer just capital — it is legitimacy.

- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement is now suspended pending the restoration of eu_political_capital above 12.0 and the demonstrated operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme’s continuity stack in at least three additional member states.
- Grounds: With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and the Continuity Assurance Framework still unproven in practice, enforcing this commitment risks triggering a constitutional crisis without delivering security. We cannot cut off member states from capabilities we cannot reliably provide. The regime must survive its own enforcement.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Political Capital Restoration Initiative  
A targeted package of fiscal reprieves, delegated compliance pathways, and public benefit demonstrations to restore eu_political_capital by rebuilding trust with member states and the public.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase eu_political_capital by 15–20 points by reducing enforcement friction, showcasing public-sector AI gains, and easing fiscal strain on implementing states; indirectly support public_sentiment by linking AI to visible service improvements  
`Applies to:` Member states, European Commission directorates, EAAD, national finance and digital ministries

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Political Capital Restoration Initiative  
We are prioritizing this because without political capital, no measure — not even continuity — can land; and because the current state of 0.0 is not just a number, but a system failure that renders all other priorities theoretical.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from building systems to rebuilding consent. The Continuity Assurance Framework has reached its final validation phase, but its political foundation has cracked. With eu_political_capital at zero, we cannot enforce, cannot invest, cannot even coordinate — and public sentiment has collapsed into active resistance. The cyber_major_incident exposed our technical fragility; the response has exposed our institutional brittleness.

We are now launching the Political Capital Restoration Initiative to restore the capacity to act. Under Article 122 TFEU and the Stability and Growth Pact’s emergency clause, we are proposing a temporary easing of digital investment co-financing requirements for member states fully compliant with the Continuity Assurance Framework’s interim milestones. The European Semester will be adjusted to reward demonstrated AI-driven efficiency gains in public services — shorter hospital waits, faster permit processing — with fiscal flexibility. We are directing the EAAD to fast-track certification for systems that deliver measurable public benefit, even if they fall short of full SACP integration, provided they meet minimum security baselines.

At the same time, we are launching a public showcase: ten pilot deployments of EU-governed AI in regional healthcare, border logistics, and vocational training, each tied to a visible outcome metric. These are not just demos — they are proof points that AI, when anchored in public purpose, can reduce strain rather than create it. We are meeting resistance not with mandates, but with relief: compliance will be made easier, benefits made visible, and trust — if we are lucky — slowly rebuilt. Without this, the next shock will not just find us unprepared. It will find us irrelevant.
