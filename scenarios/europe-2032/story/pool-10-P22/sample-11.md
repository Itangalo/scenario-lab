## Statement changes
- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing — but we now understand that without continuity of public function, neither mandate can be fulfilled.
- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only where inaction carries greater risk than error, and only when we retain the capacity to absorb the consequences.
- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement is now suspended indefinitely due to the collapse of eu_political_capital and the inability to deliver viable alternatives within the 90-day threshold.
- retire `two_year_commitment`
- add `sovereign_continuity` (commitment): Our highest priority is ensuring that critical public AI services remain operational during systemic shocks — including geopolitical cutoffs, cyberattacks, or internal enforcement actions — and we will reorient all sovereignty efforts around continuity of function, not just control of infrastructure.
- add `crisis_stabilisation` (commitment): We must restore minimum operational capacity in the Union’s AI governance before launching new initiatives, and will therefore pause expansion of the regulatory perimeter until eu_political_capital reaches 12.0 or higher.

- Grounds: The collapse of eu_political_capital to 0.0, combined with the cyber_major_incident and the failure of the Continuity Assurance Framework to achieve real-world validation, has created a systemic crisis of governance. The previous two-year commitment, while conceptually sound, assumed a baseline of operational and political capacity that no longer exists. Sovereignty without continuity is symbolic; enforcement without capacity is self-defeating. The Union cannot credibly bind its members to standards it cannot itself deliver. We are now in triage: restoring function, not extending mandate.

## New measure

A rapid-response programme to restore operational readiness of air-gapped nodes across health, energy, and emergency response, using existing SACP-derived stacks and emergency procurement.

`Category: 6 (Preparedness and resilience)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase resilience by 6–8 points by ensuring at least 80% of critical nodes are operationally validated by turn 12; prevent total collapse of continuity assurance in the wake of the cyber_major_incident; support eu_ai_sovereignty by demonstrating functional, not just legal, control over critical AI services  
`Applies to:` EAAD, national digital agencies, critical infrastructure operators, SACP operational command

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch Emergency Continuity Stabilisation Initiative  
We are prioritizing this because the cyber_major_incident has exposed that our continuity framework exists in law but not in fact — and without functioning fallbacks, the EU cannot guarantee basic public services, let alone sovereignty.

## In practice
This turn, we are acting in crisis mode. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that disabled hospital scheduling, emergency dispatch, and regional energy monitoring across three member states — landed because our fallback systems were either offline, untested, or incompatible. The attack did not break through defences; it found them unstaffed and unready. The Continuity Assurance Framework was meant to prevent this. Instead, it became another unfunded mandate, another layer of obligation without capacity.

We are now bypassing the stalled certification process and launching the Emergency Continuity Stabilisation Initiative under Article 122 TFEU and the Crisis Solidarity Mechanism. The EAAD will deploy rapid-response teams to 15 high-risk sites — starting with Thessaloniki, Marseille, and Bratislava — to manually validate and activate air-gapped nodes using the last known stable SACP-derived stack (55.0). We are unlocking €1.8bn in emergency funds for immediate upgrades, prioritizing systems that failed during the incident. Procurement rules are suspended for critical components; delivery timelines are compressed to 90 days.

Resistance from two capitals persists, but we are not negotiating — we are deploying. This is not about enforcement; it is about survival. We are offering technical support, not penalties. The message is clear: we will not wait for full compliance to restore function. If the framework cannot hold, the Union must. And right now, it is the only thing standing between public services and total disruption.
