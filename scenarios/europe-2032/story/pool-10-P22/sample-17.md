## Statement changes

* ``- modify `two_mandates` (identity): We exist both to keep the EU capable of determining its own future and to prevent lasting harm from AI, and we do not pretend these are always the same thing, but we now acknowledge that without political capital, neither mandate can be fulfilled — and preserving the Union’s ability to act is itself a core part of our duty.``
- Grounds: With `eu_political_capital` at 0.0 and agenda control lost, the Union cannot execute either mandate effectively. The identity must now reflect that agency — the capacity to decide and act — is not a means but a precondition. The two mandates remain, but their fulfillment depends on restoring the ability to govern at all.

* ``- modify `act_under_uncertainty` (commitment): We will commit before the picture is clear, and accept being wrong sometimes as the price of not being late — but only when such action does not risk total institutional paralysis. We now pause initiative to restore capacity to act.``
- Trigger: The cumulative cost of ongoing measures has drained political capital to zero, rendering the Union unable to enforce or sustain its own priorities.
- Grounds: Acting under uncertainty remains necessary, but not when it extinguishes the ability to respond to what comes next. The commitment stands in principle, but its application must now be tempered by the imperative to avoid self-neutralization.

* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Restore the EU’s capacity to act by stabilizing political capital and re-establishing credible, enforceable authority over sovereign AI systems — beginning with ensuring that no critical public function depends on AI access that cannot be guaranteed during a shock.``
- Trigger: The two-year commitment period closes this turn, and the original goal of securing sovereign control has not been operationally realized due to implementation gaps and political overextension.
- Grounds: The goal of sovereign continuity remains valid, but the path must shift: from expansion of control regimes to consolidation of what exists. The focus is no longer on building new systems, but on making current ones functionally reliable and politically sustainable.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement is now suspended pending restoration of operational readiness in the Sovereign AI Core Programme and sufficient political capital to apply consequences without triggering systemic breakdown.``
- Trigger: The Sovereign AI Core Programme is finished but not yet operational at scale, and with `eu_political_capital` at 0.0, enforcement would provoke resistance the Union cannot contain.
- Grounds: The rule remains valid, but enforcing it now would deepen fragmentation without increasing control. The commitment is preserved, but its enforcement mechanism is conditionally paused to prevent collapse of the very framework it is meant to defend.

## New measure

**Measure:** Stabilization Pause and Political Capital Recovery Programme  
A temporary freeze on new initiatives and a negotiated downsizing of enforcement burdens to allow political capital to rebuild, beginning with mutual recognition of national continuity plans and deferral of non-critical compliance deadlines.

`Category: 10 (Other)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 12`  
`Targeted effect:` Halt the decline of `eu_political_capital`, enabling recovery to at least 15.0 by turn 12; reduce institutional strain; preserve cohesion by replacing rigid mandates with phased, verified compliance pathways  
`Applies to:` Member states, European Commission, Council, EAAD, national digital agencies

## Priority

**Priority:** Stabilization Pause and Political Capital Recovery Programme  
We are prioritizing this because without political capital, no measure — not continuity, not sovereignty, not safety — can be enforced, and the Union risks becoming a legislator of paper realities.

## In practice

This turn, we are halting forward motion to prevent collapse. The Continuity Assurance Framework remains in flight, but we are no longer demanding full certification on an unyielding timeline. Instead, we are negotiating mutual recognition of national continuity plans that meet core technical baselines, even if not yet fully integrated with the EAAD’s central verification system. In return, we are deferring non-critical compliance deadlines by six months and suspending penalties for delayed reporting. The goal is not to weaken standards, but to stop driving member states into open resistance.

We are also pausing all new initiatives beyond what is already in flight. The Frontier Access Guarantee continues, but we are renegotiating its implementation pace with partner hyperscalers to reduce upfront political overhead. No new audits, no new enforcement actions, no new funding conditionality will be launched this turn. The €3.2bn from the Digital Europe Programme remains allocated, but disbursement is now tied to verified progress, not calendar deadlines.

Internally, we are convening an emergency session of the Digital Agenda Council under Article 138 TFEU to formalize the stabilization pause, framing it not as retreat but as recalibration. The message is clear: we are not abandoning sovereignty or continuity — we are ensuring they survive. The lights went out. Now we are learning how to keep them on without burning down the house.
