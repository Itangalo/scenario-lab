  - Grounds: The collapse of political capital and public sentiment to crisis levels reveals that technical sovereignty and risk prevention are insufficient without institutional endurance and social license.   - Grounds: Acting under uncertainty remains necessary, but the cost of failed execution — now visible in eroded legitimacy, member state resistance, and institutional strain — demands a recalibration. The commitment must now bind us to operational realism, not just strategic urgency.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Public AI Trust Restoration Initiative  
A cross-institutional campaign to re-establish credibility with citizens and member states by demonstrating functional, transparent, and beneficial AI in public services, starting with visible improvements in healthcare wait times and administrative responsiveness.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: small`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 15–20 points by showcasing AI that works for people, not just systems; support resilience by anchoring trust in performance, not mandates; indirectly aid eu_political_capital by creating visible wins that rebuild legitimacy  
`Applies to:` Member states, EAAD, national health and administrative agencies, European Digital Innovation Hubs

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Public AI Trust Restoration Initiative  
We are prioritizing this because without public trust and political capital, no framework, no sovereignty, no continuity can hold — and the cyber_major_incident has shattered both; we must demonstrate that AI serves citizens, not just infrastructure, or we will have no authority to govern it.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from enforcement to demonstration. The Continuity Assurance Framework has formally completed, but its promise remains unfulfilled: in Thessaloniki, Kiruna, and Maastricht, the air-gapped nodes activated — but in two other capitals, they did not, and the EAAD could not compel compliance. The law is on our side, but the legitimacy is not. With eu_political_capital at 0.0 and public_sentiment at 20.0, we cannot afford another top-down mandate. Instead, we must show what sovereign AI can do when it works.

We are launching the Public AI Trust Restoration Initiative under the Digital Europe Programme and the AI Act’s public-sector adoption provisions, directing €1.8bn to fast-track AI deployment in three domains: hospital triage and scheduling in six member states, automated permit processing in mid-sized cities, and AI-assisted language instruction in under-resourced schools. Each use case will run on the SACP stack, auditable and EU-controlled, with real-time dashboards showing performance gains — wait times falling, decisions accelerating, errors dropping. The European Digital Innovation Hubs will coordinate deployment, and the EAAD will publish monthly assurance reports.

We are not waiting for perfection. These systems will not reach 70.0 capability — they will run at 55.0 to 60.0, prioritising reliability over ambition. But they will work visibly, they will be explainable, and they will be ours. In the Council, we are framing this not as a technology rollout but as a political repair mission: a chance to prove that the EU can deliver. In Brussels, Berlin, and Bucharest, we are meeting resistance not with sanctions but with pilots — offering mayors and ministers not compliance deadlines, but working tools. The lights went out. Now we are lighting small fires, one room at a time, until the building warms again.
