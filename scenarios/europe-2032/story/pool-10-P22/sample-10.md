## Statement changes
* ``- modify `two_year_commitment` (commitment): Secure sovereign AI continuity to ensure critical public functions remain operational during systemic shocks, and now extend this continuity to include the resilience of public trust — for a system that fails in the eyes of its people is as discontinuous as one severed by blockade or attack.``
- Grounds: The cyber_major_incident and the collapse of public sentiment to 20.0 reveal that operational continuity is not enough: if citizens no longer believe the system serves them, or fear it as a source of disruption rather than stability, the mandate to govern through AI erodes. This turn, we recognise that continuity must include legitimacy.

* ``- modify `no_more_free_rides` (commitment): No member state may access frontier AI outside the EU’s sovereign control framework, and any attempt to do so will trigger immediate suspension of cohesion funding and exclusion from sovereign AI infrastructure — but enforcement will now be conditional on the operational readiness of the Sovereign AI Core Programme *and* the demonstrated public benefit of its services, so that compliance is not only compelled but invited.``
- Grounds: With public sentiment collapsing and political capital exhausted, enforcement without demonstrated value risks deepening resistance. We maintain the line against fragmentation, but now tie its enforcement to proof that our alternative works — not just that it exists.

## New measure
**Measure:** Launch the Public Trust Reinforcement Initiative  
A coordinated programme to demonstrate and communicate clear, tangible public benefits from EU-governed AI, starting with visible improvements in healthcare access, administrative responsiveness, and education support.

`Category: 9 (Diffusion, adoption and public trust)`  
`Size: large`  
`Finishes on turn: 13`  
`Targeted effect:` Increase public_sentiment by 15–20 points by delivering and amplifying measurable improvements in public services powered by sovereign AI; strengthen eu_political_capital indirectly by rebuilding legitimacy; support resilience by anchoring trust in performance, not mandate  
`Applies to:` Member states, EAAD, national health and education ministries, Eurostat, public broadcasters

## Priority
**Priority:** Launch the Public Trust Reinforcement Initiative  
We are prioritizing this because the Continuity Assurance Framework is now law — but without public belief in the system, no amount of technical redundancy will preserve continuity when the next shock hits; this turn, legitimacy is the most critical infrastructure we must build.

## In practice
This turn, we are shifting from continuity as survival to continuity as credibility. The cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled hospital scheduling in three member states — exposed not only technical fragility but a deeper rot: the public no longer trusts the AI systems meant to serve them. The Continuity Assurance Framework finishes its implementation cycle this turn, and while air-gapped nodes in Kiruna and Maastricht held during the incident, Thessaloniki’s failed activation was widely reported. The lights came back on — but not in time for the patients who waited. We cannot harden the network if we lose the people.

So we are launching the Public Trust Reinforcement Initiative under the legal base of the Digital Services Act and the AI Act’s public-sector adoption clauses, funded by a €4.1bn reallocation from the stalled Gigafactories initiative and supported by the European Social Fund. We will target three visible wins by turn 13: reduce specialist referral wait times by 40% using sovereign diagnostic agents in public hospitals; cut permit processing in mid-tier municipalities from 45 to 10 days using auditable AI workflows; and deploy AI tutors in under-resourced schools, with results tracked and published by Eurostat. The European Public AI Observatory will release quarterly impact reports, and public broadcasters will run a dedicated series — not on risks, but on results.

Resistance remains, but it is no longer only from capitals fearing sovereignty loss — it is from citizens fearing the system itself. We are meeting this with transparency: every AI decision in these pilots will carry a digital provenance tag, verifiable by citizens. We are not asking for faith. We are delivering proof. And this time, we are measuring not just uptime — but trust.
