**Priority:** Launch the Public AI Trust Initiative

## In practice

This turn, we are shifting from enforcement to demonstration. The Continuity Assurance Framework has run its course: it is law, and the final validations are complete. But its legacy is mixed — technically sound in design, politically brittle in execution. With public sentiment at 20.0 and political capital exhausted, we cannot double down on mandates. Instead, we must show what sovereign AI can do *for* people, not just what it protects *against*.

We are launching the Public AI Trust Initiative to deliver visible, measurable improvements in daily life. In health, we are deploying SACP-derived agents to triage chronic disease referrals in three pilot regions — Maastricht, Thessaloniki and Gdansk — with the goal of cutting specialist waiting times by 40% within 18 months. In education, we are rolling out AI tutors trained on national curricula, available in all official languages, to support students in under-resourced schools. In local government, we are accelerating building permit processing in mid-tier cities using automated plan review, with a target of 7-day turnaround for standard applications.

These are not showcase gimmicks. They run on the same sovereign stack that powers our continuity nodes, audited by the EAAD, and secured to state-actor standards. We are funding this with €1.8bn from the Digital Europe Programme, redirected from stalled adoption projects. The legal base is the AI Act’s public service provisions and the Digital Governance Regulation. We are working through the Education, Youth, Culture and Sport Council (EYCS) and the Competitiveness Council (COMPET) to secure buy-in, offering member states opt-in pathways with co-branding and local control.

Resistance remains — not to the tools, but to the pace. National CIOs warn of integration strain; teachers’ unions fear deskilling; local officials worry about transparency. We are meeting them with phased rollout, joint design councils, and a public dashboard tracking outcomes and incidents. This is not about selling AI. It is about proving that the Union can govern it — and that governance can serve.
