## In practice
This turn, we are crossing a threshold: from managing AI as infrastructure to defending it as territory. The Continuity Assurance Framework nears completion, and while two member states still delay full certification, both have now activated interim nodes under EAAD supervision. We’ve released the final €1.1bn tranche of Digital Europe funding tied to compliance, with an off-ramp for those who demonstrate credible migration plans by Q3. The drills in Thessaloniki were painful but instructive — configuration drift in legacy hospital systems nearly caused cascade failure, but the air-gapped stack held. That counts as progress.

But the cyber_major_incident — a model-generated ransomware sweep that crippled municipal services in three countries — revealed what the metrics already whispered: we are operating at 71.0 globally, yet our sovereign stack remains capped at 55.0, and no pathway exists to refresh it under external pressure. The Frontier Access Guarantee failed silently: one lab complied, two redirected, and the third invoked jurisdictional override. We cannot mandate continuity if we cannot guarantee capability renewal.

So we act. Under Article 173 TFEU and leveraging emergency procurement clauses from the Semiconductor Act, we are launching the Emergency Compute Resilience Initiative. EuroHPC JU will stand up two micro-foundries — one in Finland, one in southern France — each capable of assembling and running small-scale, high-efficiency training clusters using non-restricted chiplets and repurposed supercomputing hardware. Simultaneously, we are brokering capacity-swaps with Swiss and Singaporean hyperscalers under mutual data sovereignty agreements — not ownership, but assured access when borders close. This is not about matching the frontier head-on; it is about ensuring that even in isolation, the EU can iterate. The cost is high, and our political capital is zero — but that is precisely why we must move now, before the next shock forces a choice between surrender and chaos.
