## In practice
This turn, we are closing the Continuity Assurance Framework — law is now fact in 22 of 27 member states. The EAAD certified 87 critical nodes across energy, health and emergency response, and unannounced drills in Lisbon, Riga and Bratislava confirmed autonomous failover within 90 seconds. But Thessaloniki and two capitals remain out of compliance, citing sovereignty and cost. The framework works — but only where it was already wanted.

Now, we act on what the cyber_major_incident revealed: continuity is not enough if access is gated by peacetime bureaucracy. The attack on public health databases in January exposed a fatal hesitation — a member state, under sustained AI-driven intrusion, delayed activating its fallback node for 11 hours, waiting for central certification that should have been pre-approved. That delay cost lives. We cannot afford another.

So we are launching the Emergency Access Protocol: any member state under verified systemic disruption — certified in real time by the EAAD using AI-verified telemetry — will gain immediate, audited access to the SACP’s 55.0-stack deployment, bypassing compliance checks that were never meant to apply under fire. This is not a backdoor — it is a fire exit. We are allocating €450 million from the unused portion of the Digital Europe Programme to pre-position hardened nodes in five high-risk regions, ready to activate under protocol. We are meeting resistance with a simple offer: trust the system before the storm, or we will open the gates when the storm hits — but you will not be allowed to suffer in silence.
