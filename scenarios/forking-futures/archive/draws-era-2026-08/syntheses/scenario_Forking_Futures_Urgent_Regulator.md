## Summary

The ensemble of eight runs in the "Forking Futures" scenario reveals a world where AI capabilities advance steadily across all regimes, with US and Chinese systems converging near 63–66 on the capability index by 2035. Incident pressure remains high throughout, peaking in mid-simulation due to recurring cyber_mass_campaigns (which occurred in all runs) and bio_incidents (in half). Regulatory capacity is fragile, fluctuating narrowly around 45–50, eroded by overreach, legal challenges, and implementation burdens. The openweight gap narrows significantly—from 30 to ~22 on average—indicating that dangerous capabilities are diffusing widely, undermining containment strategies. Early interventions show mixed results: while they can delay or mitigate some harms, they often fail to prevent major incidents due to enforcement gaps, geopolitical friction, and systemic overextension. The most consequential precursors are eval_anomaly_reports and cyber_recon_wave, which reliably precede capability jumps and cyber crises. No no-regret policy package emerges across all regimes, though measures targeting evaluation, transparency, and coalition-building show relative resilience.

## Research Questions

### rq_early_vs_reactive

Early, broad interventions do not consistently outperform reactive ones across regimes; in 5 of 8 runs, early measures failed to prevent major incidents due to implementation delays, legal challenges, or evasion, while reactive responses after crises (e.g., post-cyber_mass_campaign or post-bio_incident) often gained political traction and led to more effective enforcement. In run-20260825-145404, early alliance formation (Turn 5) enabled later enforcement, but in run-20260826-003715-03, early emergency directives were struck down in court. Reactive measures succeeded when they followed high-impact events that raised incident_pressure above 60 (e.g., Turn 10 in run-20260826-003715-01), creating a political window for coercive tools like kill switches. However, in runs where early measures were narrowly focused—such as pre-release evaluations in run-20260825-145416—they reduced the likelihood of capability_jump by one-third, per event statistics. The key condition for early success is not timing alone, but whether the measure is institutionally resilient (e.g., backed by coalition or cryptographic verification) and whether it targets a narrow, verifiable domain (e.g., cyber or code). In contrast, reactive measures succeed when they exploit post-crisis political capital but often come too late to prevent harm.

### rq_early_signals

The most reliable precursors to large divergences are eval_anomaly_reports and cyber_recon_wave: eval_anomaly_reports preceded 6 of 6 capability_jump events (100% true positive rate), and cyber_recon_wave preceded 6 of 8 cyber_mass_campaigns (75% true positive rate), with only one false positive (in run-20260826-003715-03, where recon occurred but no mass campaign followed). bio_uplift_findings preceded 4 of 4 bio_incident events (100% true positive), but also fired 5 times without incident (80% false positive rate), making it a noisy signal. taiwan_tension_rise occurred in 6 runs and preceded 3 of 3 taiwan_blockade events (100% true positive), but in 3 runs it did not escalate. funding_round_pulled occurred in 3 runs and preceded both ai_market_crash events (100% true positive), with one false positive. agent_misconduct_disclosure preceded 3 of 4 agent_supply_chain_compromise events (75% true positive), with one false positive. The ensemble shows that gate-based precursors (those that open a window for a follow-up event) are more reliable than standalone indicators, but their value depends on the regulator’s ability to act within the window—e.g., negotiation_window opened in 6 runs but was only leveraged in 2 (run-20260825-145404, run-20260826-013515), due to lack of prepared proposals or political capital.

### rq_no_regret

There is no no-regret package of measures that is universally beneficial across all three regimes; every measure category shows trade-offs. Limits-and-restrictions (category 3) reduced the probability of capability_jump by one-third when fully implemented, but in 3 runs (e.g., run-20260826-003715-02) they triggered covert_defection or regulatory_backlash, reducing regulatory_capacity. Preparedness-and-response (category 5) measures halved the probability of cyber_mass_campaign when covering cyber, but in 2 runs (run-20260825-145428, run-20260826-003715-01) they were overwhelmed by incident_pressure, leading to capacity erosion. Evaluation and transparency (category 1) measures improved regulatory credibility (e.g., in run-20260825-145416, the Foundational Attribution Report restored capacity post-crash), but in 4 runs they led to emergent_court_challenge or staffing crises. Adoption and integration (category 8) measures reduced algorithmic_bias_scandal by half when auditing was implemented, but in 2 runs (run-20260826-003715, run-20260826-013515) they were undermined by labour_displacement_wave. The only partial no-regret pattern is in coalition-building: alliance_bloc_forms (a category 7 measure) occurred in 3 runs and was associated with a 4.7-point higher us_capability and 3.2-point higher cn_capability on average, suggesting it supports stability without clear downside, but it only formed in 3 of 8 runs and did not prevent major incidents.

## Outcome Patterns

- **High-pressure equilibrium (4 runs)**: Incident pressure stabilizes between 60–80, with recurring cyber and bio incidents, public sentiment below 30, and regulatory capacity eroding to 42–48. The openweight gap narrows to 14–20, indicating widespread diffusion. Examples: run-20260826-003715, run-20260826-003715-02, run-20260826-003715-03, run-20260825-145404. In these runs, the regulator shifts from multilateralism to unilateral enforcement but remains reactive.
- **Crisis and correction (2 runs)**: A major shock—ai_market_crash (run-20260825-145416, run-20260826-003715-01)—collapses economic_context to 35–38, slowing capability growth and reducing political resistance to regulation. Regulatory capacity rebounds to 56 after the crash, and incident pressure declines. This pattern shows that exogenous economic collapse can create space for regulatory consolidation.
- **Geopolitical rupture (1 run)**: taiwan_blockade occurs (run-20260825-145428), halting semiconductor exports, collapsing economic_context to 35, and triggering emergency containment. Capability growth slows, but the regulator gains crisis authority. This outcome is distinct in its shift from regulatory to national security framing.
- **Emergent governance (1 run)**: A multilateral governance initiative forms (run-20260825-145428), creating a third path outside US-China duopoly. This is a one-off outcome where non-state actors and Global South states drive norm-setting, reducing reliance on the regulator’s authority.

## Recurring Turning Points

- **Cyber_mass_campaign (Turns 2–17)**: Occurred in all 8 runs, typically following cyber_recon_wave within 1–3 turns. It consistently spiked incident_pressure by 20–30 points and triggered emergency cyber measures (e.g., ECRD, UCAF). In 5 runs, it marked the shift from anticipatory to reactive governance.
- **Capability_jump (Turns 1–16)**: Occurred in 6 runs, always preceded by eval_anomaly_reports. It accelerated us_capability by +3 to +7 and justified pre-release evaluation mandates. In run-20260826-013515, it coincided with taiwan_blockade, creating a paradox of scarcity and leapfrogging.
- **Bio_incident (Turns 6, 7, 11, 16)**: Occurred in 4 runs, each time after bio_uplift_findings, spiking incident_pressure to 78–90 and collapsing public sentiment. It validated the regulator’s warnings but also triggered court challenges (e.g., emergent_court_challenge in run-20260825-145404), weakening enforcement.
- **AI market crash (Turns 13, 15)**: Occurred in 2 runs, following funding_round_pulled, collapsing economic_context to 35–38. It slowed capability growth organically and made restrictions politically cheaper, allowing regulatory capacity to rebound.
- **Alliance_bloc_forms (Turns 4–6)**: Occurred in 3 runs, enabling coalition-based enforcement (e.g., FEC, JCDA). It was the only event consistently associated with a recovery in regulatory capacity, but it did not prevent later crises.

## Actor Dynamics

The regulator is the sole actor, but its effectiveness is shaped by interactions with modeled systems. It begins with a multilateral, anticipatory stance but shifts to unilateral, crisis-driven action as cooperation fails. In 6 of 8 runs, it violates the one-new-measure-per-turn constraint, managing 10–15 concurrent measures, which strains regulatory_capacity. Its credibility is preserved not by legal authority but by technical competence—e.g., in run-20260825-145416, the Foundational Attribution Report restored influence after a court ruling. The regulator’s statements evolve: the “international_first” position is modified in 5 runs, and “act_before_proof” is tempered by demands for corroboration. US and Chinese actors are not directly controlled but respond to chip_export_escalation (7 runs) and covert_defection (2 runs), reflecting a pattern of competitive escalation. Labs respond to whistleblower_disclosure (8 runs) and eval_anomaly_reports (7 runs), but often suppress findings, leading to scandals. The public reacts to labour_displacement_wave (7 runs) and companion_harm_scandal (3 runs), driving public_sentiment_to_ai below 20 in 4 runs. The regulator’s leverage is strongest when it builds coalitions (alliance_bloc_forms) or produces verifiable evidence, weakest when it relies on binding international agreement.

## Surprises and Outliers

- **Orbital_datacenter_success occurred only once (run-20260825-145416)**: Despite a 10% base probability in FAST regime, it fired in only 1 run, where it highlighted jurisdictional fragility but had minimal impact on governance.
- **Emergent events dominated in run-20260826-003715-03**: 12 emergent events (e.g., emergent_regulator_crisis_of_legitimacy, emergent_security_breach_among_allies) reshaped governance, showing how non-state actors and legal interventions can override regulator intent.
- **Regulator capacity rebounded post-crash**: In both ai_market_crash runs, regulatory_capacity increased by 6–8 points despite economic collapse, contradicting the expectation that busts weaken institutions. This suggests that slower growth reduces political resistance to regulation.
- **Open_weight_frontier_release did not trigger rsi_onset**: In 2 runs where the release occurred, it narrowed the openweight_gap but did not accelerate capability growth, indicating that diffusion alone does not cause runaway progress.
- **One run avoided all major catastrophes**: run-20260825-145404 had no bio_incident, taiwan_blockade, or rsi_onset, yet incident_pressure reached 83, showing that sustained pressure can arise from cumulative incidents without singular disasters.

## Simulation Caveats

- Only 8 runs were completed, below the recommended 10+ for robust ensemble analysis; outcome frequencies (e.g., 75% for negotiation_window) may not be stable.
- Runs used different model configurations (per ensemble statistics), so variation may reflect model differences rather than scenario stochasticity.
- The regulator frequently violated constitutional constraints: capability metrics were incorrectly reduced in 5 runs, the one-new-measure-per-turn rule was breached in 6 runs, and phase progression was accelerated without justification in 4 runs.
- Narrative telegraphing occurred: internal notes in run-20260826-003715 implied RLVR-LIMITED, and run-20260826-013515 suggested FAST regime, violating the hidden trajectory rule.
- Metric movements approached the 25-point cap (e.g., incident_pressure in run-20260826-003715-01), risking implausibility.
- Per-run analyses often attributed outcomes to regulator actions without sufficient grounding in event probabilities or metric mechanics, risking over-interpretation.

## Confidence Assessment

Confidence in the ensemble findings is moderate. The small run count (8) limits statistical power, especially for low-probability events (e.g., orbital_datacenter_success at 12%). The use of different model configurations introduces uncontrolled variation, making it difficult to distinguish scenario effects from model artifacts. However, the high occurrence rates of key events (e.g., cyber_mass_campaign in 100% of runs, eval_anomaly_reports in 88%) and consistent metric trends (e.g., steady capability growth, narrowing openweight_gap) suggest robust patterns. Per-run analyses provide rich causal narratives but are inconsistent in attributing outcomes, with some overemphasizing regulator agency despite systemic constraints. The declared research questions can be answered with moderate confidence for rq_early_signals (due to clear precursor-event chains) and rq_early_vs_reactive (due to observable intervention timing), but rq_no_regret is less certain due to the absence of any universally beneficial measure package. More runs with consistent configurations are needed to increase confidence, particularly for regime-specific dynamics.
