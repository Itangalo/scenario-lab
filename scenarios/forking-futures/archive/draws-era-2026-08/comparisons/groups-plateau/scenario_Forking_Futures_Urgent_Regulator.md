## Summary

The ensemble of eight runs in the "Forking Futures" scenario reveals a world where AI capabilities advance steadily under a PLATEAU-like regime, with US and Chinese systems converging by the end of the simulation. The openweight gap collapses dramatically in most runs, rendering frontier capabilities widely accessible and undermining lab-centric regulation. Incident pressure rises to crisis levels due to repeated cyber incidents, bio precursors, and social harms, while economic context fluctuates but ultimately declines due to multiple AI market crashes. Regulatory capacity remains relatively stable, reflecting institutional resilience, but is strained by legal challenges, diplomatic fragmentation, and the sheer pace of change. The regulator consistently shifts from multilateral diplomacy to unilateral enforcement as cooperation fails, yet struggles to close the enforcement gap before dangerous capabilities become uncontainable. Public sentiment deteriorates steadily, driven by cyber incidents, information integrity crises, and social disruption, though it shows modest rebounds in some runs after market crashes. The most consequential events are the open-weight frontier release, which collapses the openweight gap; repeated cyber mass campaigns, which drive incident pressure to crisis levels; and AI market crashes, which reset the economic landscape but fail to halt capability growth.

## Research Questions

### rq_early_vs_reactive

Early, broad interventions do not consistently outperform reactive ones across regimes; outcomes depend more on the timing of capability diffusion and incident escalation than on the regulator's initial posture. In 6 of 8 runs, the openweight gap collapsed below 10 by turn 12, making early containment measures obsolete regardless of their quality. Reactive measures—particularly unilateral enforcement, compute-level intervention, and post-deployment detection—were more effective in runs where the open release occurred early (e.g., run-20260825-145424, run-20260825-223258-02). In runs where the regulator committed capital early to pre-deployment audits (e.g., run-20260825-145400), those measures were later abandoned as irrelevant. However, early investment in technical evaluation capacity (e.g., Independent Evaluation Constellation) paid off in runs that avoided catastrophic incidents, as it enabled faster response when crises hit. The key determinant was not early action per se, but whether the regulator adapted its strategy to the collapsing openweight gap before a major incident (e.g., cyber_mass_campaign or bio_incident) overwhelmed its capacity. Runs that failed to pivot—such as run-20260825-223258—ended with regulatory capacity eroded and incident pressure at crisis levels.

### rq_early_signals

The most reliable precursors to large divergences are cyber_recon_wave, bio_uplift_findings, and funding_round_pulled, but their predictive value depends on context. cyber_recon_wave occurred in 7 of 8 runs and preceded cyber_mass_campaign in all cases, making it a high-sensitivity but moderate-specificity signal (false positive in run-20260825-145412, where no cyber_mass_campaign followed). bio_uplift_findings occurred in 6 of 8 runs and preceded bio_incident in 3 of 3 cases, making it a strong predictor of biological risk escalation. funding_round_pulled occurred in 2 of 8 runs and preceded ai_market_crash in both, but ai_market_crash also occurred without it in 4 runs, indicating it is sufficient but not necessary. taiwan_tension_rise occurred in all 8 runs but only led to taiwan_blockade in 3, giving it a high false-positive rate. eval_anomaly_reports occurred in 3 of 8 runs and preceded capability_jump in 2 of 5 cases, but capability_jump also occurred without it, reducing its reliability. The most valuable signal set combines cyber_recon_wave (for cyber risk), bio_uplift_findings (for bio risk), and funding_round_pulled (for market risk), with taiwan_tension_rise as a contextual amplifier. Runs like run-20260825-145400 and run-20260825-223258-02 show that monitoring these precursors allows the regulator to anticipate and prepare for major shocks, but only if it has the flexibility to pivot strategy.

### rq_no_regret

There are no no-regret packages of measures that are universally beneficial across all regimes; however, a combination of technical evaluation capacity and post-deployment detection shows consistent value. Measures in category 1 (evaluation and audit) and category 4 (agent monitoring and weight security) were never worse and often better across runs. For example, Independent Evaluation Constellation and Open-Source Threat Intelligence Commons improved regulatory capacity in 5 of 8 runs and helped contain agent_supply_chain_compromise in run-20260825-233458. In contrast, category 3 (limits and restrictions) measures like Binding International Agreement failed in 6 of 8 runs due to diplomatic resistance, and category 6 (labour and social) measures had mixed effects on public sentiment. The only package that approached no-regret status was a combination of category 1 and category 4 measures, which provided value even in runs with early open releases (e.g., run-20260825-145424) and market crashes (e.g., run-20260825-223258-01). However, this package did not prevent capability diffusion or incident escalation, so it is not a complete solution. The evidence suggests that no-regret strategies must focus on adaptive, technical capabilities rather than binding agreements or broad restrictions.

## Outcome Patterns

- **Managed Crisis (3 runs):** High incident pressure (75–85), low openweight gap (1–5), and stable regulatory capacity (50–58). The regulator shifts to unilateral enforcement and crisis triage, containing harm but failing to prevent diffusion. Example runs: run-20260825-145400, run-20260825-223258-01, run-20260825-233458.
- **Runaway Diffusion (2 runs):** Near-zero openweight gap (1–2), very high incident pressure (83–85), and low regulatory capacity (38–44). The regulator loses containment, and dangerous capabilities become widespread and uncontrolled. Example runs: run-20260825-223258, run-20260825-223258-03.
- **Stabilized Fragmentation (2 runs):** Moderate incident pressure (50–60), moderate openweight gap (10–15), and stable regulatory capacity (48–52). The regulator accepts a multipolar AI order, with China exporting standards and the regulator focusing on coalition-based technical integration. Example runs: run-20260825-145412, run-20260825-223258-02.
- **Contained Escalation (1 run):** High incident pressure (68), low openweight gap (2), and moderate regulatory capacity (50). A bio_incident and cyber_mass_campaign drive pressure up, but the regulator adapts to distributed defense and avoids total collapse. Example run: run-20260825-145424.

## Recurring Turning Points

- **Turn 4: Open-Weight Release and Market Shock (4 runs):** The open_weight_frontier_release occurs in 7 of 8 runs, but in 4 runs (run-20260825-145412, run-20260825-223258-02, run-20260825-223258-03, run-20260825-233458), it coincides with ai_market_crash or funding_round_pulled, forcing a strategic pivot from containment to detection. This turn marks the collapse of lab-centric control models and the beginning of distributed defense.
- **Turn 7–8: Cyber Mass Campaign and Capability Jump (3 runs):** A cyber_mass_campaign in turn 7 or 8 (run-20260825-145412, run-20260825-223258, run-20260825-233458) drives incident pressure to crisis levels, while a capability_jump in turn 7 (run-20260825-145424) or turn 14 (run-20260825-233458) invalidates assumptions of gradual progress. These events force the regulator to abandon multilateralism and focus on emergency enforcement.
- **Turn 11–12: Taiwan Blockade and Market Crash (2 runs):** The taiwan_blockade in turn 11 (run-20260825-223258-03) or turn 8 (run-20260825-223258-02) disrupts compute supply, while ai_market_crash in turn 12 (run-20260825-223258-01) collapses the investment climate. These shocks fracture global cooperation and force a shift to sovereign self-reliance.
- **Turn 15–17: Agent Supply Chain Compromise and Final Release (2 runs):** The agent_supply_chain_compromise in turn 17 (run-20260825-223258-03) or turn 9 (run-20260825-223258) reveals autonomous threats, while the open_weight_frontier_release in turn 17 (run-20260825-223258-01) or turn 18 (run-20260825-145400) makes frontier capabilities unrecallable. These events mark the point of no return in capability diffusion.

## Actor Dynamics

The regulator begins with a multilateral, coalition-building strategy in all runs, prioritizing binding international agreements and technical credibility. However, in 7 of 8 runs, diplomatic fragmentation, legal challenges, and technical failures force a pivot to unilateral enforcement and sovereign self-reliance. The regulator consistently shifts from pre-deployment audits to post-deployment detection as the openweight gap collapses, but this adaptation is often too slow to prevent major incidents. In runs where the regulator maintains focus on technical evaluation capacity (e.g., run-20260825-145424), it achieves modest success in containment. In runs where it overcommits to diplomatic solutions (e.g., run-20260825-223258), it loses credibility and capacity. The regulator's identity as an independent rule-maker is preserved in all runs, but its legitimacy is strained by enforcement gaps and political backlash. The absence of direct interaction with US and Chinese actors means that the regulator's influence is mediated through market access, standards, and reputation, which are insufficient to secure compliance in high-stakes scenarios.

## Surprises and Outliers

- **run-20260825-145424** is an outlier in that it avoids ai_market_crash and rsi_onset, focusing instead on systemic risk accumulation. It is the only run where bio_incident occurs without a prior market crash, and it shows a clear trend of capability diffusion without economic collapse.
- **run-20260825-223258** is an outlier in its early agent_supply_chain_compromise (turn 2), which forces a reactive posture from the start. It is the only run where regulatory capacity drops to 38.0, and it ends with the highest incident pressure (85.0) and lowest openweight gap (1.0).
- **run-20260825-233458** is an outlier in its late sovereign_ai_fund formation (turn 16), which provides a floor under valuations after ai_market_crash. It is the only run where emergent_multilateral_coordination_group and emergent_water_use_conflict occur, indicating a more complex web of secondary crises.
- **run-20260825-145400** is an outlier in its high narrative diversity and multiple constitutional violations, suggesting simulation instability. It is the only run where orbital_datacenter_success occurs, introducing off-world compute as a new domain.

## Simulation Caveats

- Only 8 completed runs were analyzed, which is below the recommended 10+ for representative results. This limits confidence in the generalizability of findings.
- Runs used different model configurations (llm blocks in config.json differ), meaning outcome variation may reflect model differences rather than scenario stochasticity. A dedicated model-sensitivity analysis is recommended.
- Several runs exhibit metric movements near or at the 25-point constitutional cap, particularly for incident_pressure and economic_context, requiring multiple correction cycles. This suggests tension between event-driven shocks and bounded metric dynamics.
- The regulator's capacity metric shows surprising stability in some runs despite major setbacks, which may understate the organizational toll of managing multiple high-cost initiatives and legal battles.
- The narrative descriptions in some runs risk telegraphing the underlying trajectory (e.g., PLATEAU regime) despite constitutional prohibitions on naming regimes.

## Confidence Assessment

Confidence in the ensemble findings is moderate. The small number of runs (8) and use of different model configurations limit statistical robustness and raise concerns about model sensitivity. However, the high occurrence rates of key events (e.g., ai_market_crash in 6 of 8 runs, open_weight_frontier_release in 7 of 8) and consistent patterns in regulator behavior (pivot from multilateral to unilateral) suggest that the core dynamics are robust. The ensemble statistics provide strong ground truth for metric trajectories and event frequencies, but the per-run analyses show variability in narrative interpretation, particularly around turning points and actor behavior. Confidence is highest in the findings on the collapse of the openweight gap and the ineffectiveness of early containment measures, and lowest in the identification of no-regret packages, where the evidence is more mixed. Additional runs with consistent model configurations would improve confidence.
