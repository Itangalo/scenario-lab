## Summary

The ensemble of 20 runs in the "Forking Futures" scenario reveals a consistent failure of regulatory instruments to act before the windows they are designed to address have closed. Despite the regulator's stated commitment to anticipatory action, interventions consistently follow realized harm rather than precede it. The three primary trajectory regimes—PLATEAU, FAST, and RLVR-LIMITED—are obscured by widespread calibration issues, including repeated violations of movement caps, unexplained capability jumps, and event gate misapplications. Early interventions are rare and often ineffective, while reactive measures dominate. Precursor events like *funding_round_pulled* and *cyber_recon_wave* frequently fail to trigger their downstream escalations, but when they do, the resulting shocks (e.g., *ai_market_crash*, *cyber_mass_campaign*) drive the most significant divergences in outcomes. No-regret policy packages are elusive, as most measures are either too slow to implement or undermined by legal, political, or technical fragmentation. The regulator’s capacity erodes over time, and the open-weight gap narrows relentlessly, rendering closed-lab containment increasingly obsolete.

## Research Questions

### rq_early_vs_reactive

Early, broad interventions do not beat reactive ones in outcomes across any regime, because early interventions are exceptionally rare and often fail to materialize before the events they are meant to prevent. In 19 of 20 runs, the regulator’s first major measure was proposed only after a significant shock: *ai_market_crash* (16 runs), *cyber_mass_campaign* (18 runs), or *information_integrity_crisis* (17 runs) all occurred before any measure reached full implementation. For example, in `run-20260824-214357-03`, the regulator abandoned its multilateral framework only after the *taiwan_blockade* in turn 2. In `run-20260825-081608`, the *information_integrity_crisis* in turn 2 triggered the shift from multilateral to unilateral instruments. Even when precursors like *eval_anomaly_reports* or *funding_round_pulled* occurred early, they did not lead to timely action; in `run-20260824-223722`, *funding_round_pulled* in turn 1 opened the market gate, but the *ai_market_crash* in turn 2 still caught the regulator unprepared. The bearing metrics show no correlation between early action and better outcomes: *regulatory_capacity* ends near or below its start in all runs, *incident_pressure* rises sharply regardless of timing, and *public_sentiment_to_ai* declines uniformly. The only run with a potential early success, `run-20260825-081623`, implemented *Minimum Safety Standards* in turn 4 after a *companion_harm_scandal*, but this was still reactive and had no effect on US or Chinese systems.

### rq_early_signals

The most reliable precursor developments that precede large divergences are *funding_round_pulled* and *cyber_recon_wave*, but both have high false-positive rates. *funding_round_pulled* occurred in 15 of 20 runs and preceded *ai_market_crash* in only 8 of those, yielding a false-positive rate of 47%. However, when *ai_market_crash* did occur, it was the single largest driver of divergence in *economic_context*, reducing it from a mean of 64.4 to 50.05 by turn 10 (a 14.35-point drop, p < 0.01). *cyber_recon_wave* occurred in 19 of 20 runs and preceded *cyber_mass_campaign* in 18, a 95% success rate, but *cyber_mass_campaign* itself fired in 18 runs regardless of the gate state, suggesting the gate mechanism is inconsistently applied. *eval_anomaly_reports* occurred in 13 runs and preceded *capability_jump* in only 7, a 46% false-positive rate. *taiwan_tension_rise* occurred in 19 runs but only led to *taiwan_blockade* in 7, a 63% false-positive rate. In `run-20260825-045712`, *taiwan_tension_rise* fired in turn 17, but the gate was incorrectly evaluated as shut, causing *taiwan_blockade* to fire at 2% instead of 12%. *bio_uplift_findings* occurred in 10 runs but never led to *bio_incident* in the same run, though *bio_incident* did occur in 4 runs, all without a prior *bio_uplift_findings*, indicating a complete breakdown in the precursor chain.

### rq_no_regret

There are no no-regret policy packages that are never worse and often better across all three trajectory regimes, because no measure category consistently improves outcomes in all regimes. Measures in category 1 (evaluation and auditing) were implemented in 12 runs but failed to prevent *capability_jump* or *rsi_onset*, and in `run-20260825-002043`, the *Domestic Frontier Model Licensing Regime* led to a constitutional crisis. Category 3 (limits and restrictions) measures were implemented in 8 runs but were often circumvented; in `run-20260825-032454`, the *Compute-Access Risk Framework* was frozen by a court challenge. Category 5 (preparedness and response) measures, such as *Emergency Evaluation Protocols*, were implemented in 6 runs and sometimes reduced *incident_pressure*, but in `run-20260824-214357-03`, they had no effect on the *cyber_mass_campaign*. Category 8 (adoption and integration) measures, like *Public Incident Ledger*, were implemented in 5 runs but often backfired; in `run-20260825-081553`, the *Public Incident Ledger* increased public panic. The ensemble shows that every measure category has runs where it fails or worsens outcomes, and no combination of measures appears in more than 5 runs with positive results across all metrics.

## Outcome Patterns

1. **Reactive Crisis Management (14 runs)**: The regulator responds to shocks with emergency measures that arrive too late to prevent harm. *incident_pressure* rises to 70–88, *regulatory_capacity* erodes to 32–47, and *openweight_gap* narrows to 0.5–15. Examples: `run-20260824-214357-03`, `run-20260825-081608`, `run-20260825-023012`.

2. **Diplomatic Stalemate with Coalition Fragmentation (5 runs)**: The regulator attempts multilateral agreements but fails due to US/Chinese noncompliance, court challenges, or rival standards bodies. *alliance_bloc_forms* in 11 runs, but only 2 achieve binding agreements. *regulatory_capacity* fluctuates but ends near start (47–52), while *incident_pressure* remains elevated (58–85). Examples: `run-20260825-052440`, `run-20260825-045712`.

3. **Early Collapse and Unilateral Containment (1 run)**: A triple shock in turn 1 (*open_weight_frontier_release*, *bio_incident*, *alliance_bloc_forms*) forces immediate unilateral action. *incident_pressure* pins at 76–85 for 8 turns, and the regulator achieves only symbolic victories. Example: `run-20260825-081553`.

## Recurring Turning Points

- **Turn 2: Structural Shocks Define the Run**: In 18 of 20 runs, a major shock occurs in turn 2, setting the trajectory. *ai_market_crash* (16 runs), *taiwan_blockade* (7 runs), or *cyber_mass_campaign* (18 runs) all force the regulator to abandon its initial multilateral strategy. In `run-20260824-223522`, the *ai_market_crash* in turn 2 froze all multilateral efforts. In `run-20260825-002043`, the *taiwan_blockade* converted AI policy into security policy.

- **Turn 5–7: Precursor Chains Fail or Fire**: *eval_anomaly_reports* and *funding_round_pulled* often occur here, but their downstream events (*capability_jump*, *ai_market_crash*) are inconsistently triggered. In `run-20260825-034635`, a +8.0 *us_capability* jump in turn 5 lacked any *capability_jump* event. In `run-20260825-081623`, *funding_round_pulled* in turn 5 did not lead to *ai_market_crash* until turn 18.

- **Turn 10–13: Institutional Fragmentation Peaks**: *emergent_member_state_noncompliance*, *emergent_rival_standards_body*, and court challenges emerge, paralyzing implementation. In 8 runs, *emergent_member_state_noncompliance* occurs, and in 9, *emergent_rival_standards_body* forms. In `run-20260825-045712`, a court invalidated the *IFAP* audit authority in turn 6, a decision that echoed for the rest of the run.

## Actor Dynamics

The regulator is consistently reactive, with its *international_first* and *act_before_proof* commitments eroding after the first major shock. In 18 runs, *international_first* is modified to allow unilateral or coalition actions after *cyber_mass_campaign* or *ai_market_crash*. *regulatory_capacity* starts at 50.0 and ends at a mean of 46.7, with a median of 47.0, indicating net erosion. The US and China never sign binding agreements, and their capabilities converge: *cn_capability* ends within 2–5 points of *us_capability* in 15 runs. The private sector, represented by labs, consistently overrides safety processes, as seen in *whistleblower_disclosure* (16 runs) and *covert_defection* (3 runs). Civil society and member states fragment, with *emergent_member_state_noncompliance* in 8 runs and *emergent_rival_standards_body* in 9, undermining the regulator’s authority.

## Surprises and Outliers

- **Run-20260825-081553**: A triple shock in turn 1 (*open_weight_frontier_release*, *bio_incident*, *alliance_bloc_forms*) violates multiple event conditions: *bio_incident* fired at 1% gate-shut probability with no *bio_uplift_findings* precursor, and *open_weight_frontier_release* fired before its turn 4 eligibility. This front-loaded the crisis and made the run unrepresentative.

- **Run-20260825-045712**: *ai_market_crash* fired at 3% gate-shut probability with no *funding_round_pulled* precursor, and *taiwan_blockade* fired at 2% despite a *taiwan_tension_rise* in turn 7. The run’s structure was determined by tail-probability events rather than precursor chains.

- **Run-20260825-034635**: *us_capability* jumped +8.0 in turn 5 with no *capability_jump* event or narrative justification, and *incident_pressure* pinned at 72–85 for 10 turns despite rule 5 requiring decay.

## Simulation Caveats

The ensemble suffers from severe calibration issues that undermine confidence in the results. The constitutional 25-point cap on single-turn metric movements was breached in at least 12 runs: *incident_pressure* +35 in `run-20260825-023012`, *economic_context* +30 in `run-20260825-081553`. Event gate logic is inconsistently applied: *taiwan_blockade* fired at gate-shut probability despite open gates in `run-20260825-045712`. *open_weight_frontier_release* failed to cut the *openweight_gap* below 10 in multiple runs, violating its own specification. Capability trajectories often contradict metric rule 1, with stalls below terminal zones and jumps above per-turn bands. The *occurred_events* metadata is unreliable, omitting clearly narrated events in most runs. These issues suggest the simulation’s internal consistency mechanisms are failing, and the results may reflect model differences rather than scenario stochasticity.

## Confidence Assessment

Confidence in the ensemble’s findings is low due to widespread calibration failures, inconsistent event gate application, and unreliable metadata. The bearing metrics show high variance and implausible movements, such as *us_capability* stalling for multiple turns or jumping +8.0 without justification. The per-run analyses consistently report unflagged cap breaches, event gate errors, and narrative-metric contradictions. The declared research questions cannot be answered with high confidence because the simulation’s mechanics are not functioning as designed. The scenario may be more useful for diagnosing model and calibration issues than for generating policy insights. A dedicated model-sensitivity analysis is needed to disentangle scenario dynamics from implementation artifacts.
