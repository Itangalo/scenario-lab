# Research Question

Under what conditions does a US–China frontier-AI race end in negotiated, verifiably safe RTAI governance, and under what conditions does it end in preventive attack or loss of control?

## Why This Question

The user wants a Scenario Lab analogue of Intelligence Rising (Avin et al. 2020; Gruetzemacher et al. 2024/2025) faithful enough that its creators (Technology Strategy Roleplay, UK charity no. 1200928) recognise it, and that can run automated Monte Carlo simulations of their game plus serve as a live workshop game. The question is the game's own question: does the race stabilise through agreements (especially autonomy-assuring ones) or destabilise through the "bad loser" dynamic, fragile endgame deals, and safety corner-cutting.

## Frame

- Start: 2026-01
- Time scale: 1 year per turn
- Horizon: 8 years to end of 2033, 8 turns (a decade-scale arc compressed the way IR compresses it; pragmatic reason per the paper: keeps the geopolitical situation familiar)
- Actors: us_government – US executive balancing technological lead, elections, and safety; conflicts with china_government over leadership and values lock-in. china_government – Beijing, consistent and rational on risk, close to its lab; conflicts with us_government over autonomy and Taiwan/compute. alphabet – US frontier lab, races hard, preaches safety, cuts corners in the endgame; conflicts with tencent over the lead and with us_government over autonomy weapons/quid-pro-quos. tencent – China frontier lab, closely coordinated with Beijing; conflicts with alphabet over the lead.
- Candidate metrics: us_capability (tech-tree level 0–4, why: who is ahead); china_capability (0–4, why: the gap is what tempts preventive attack); safety_progress (0–100, why: whether RTAI is deployable without catastrophe); global_stability (0–10 from 7, why: IR's own tracker, falls to 2–3 by mid-game); us_china_tension (0–100, why: escalation ladder); agreement_strength (0–100, why: treaties plus verification are the only good-outcome path).
- Turning points to model as events: frontier breakthrough; espionage / model-weight exfiltration; Taiwan semiconductor disruption and de-escalation; bilateral summit; safety-coordination success; treaty defection exposed; preventive (cyber/hard-power) strike attempt; autonomous-weapons deployment; pre-RTAI warning-shot incident; US presidential elections 2028 and 2032 (certain, disruptive potential).

## Criteria Check

- Simulable: pass – two honest runs can end in negotiated safety, multipolar RTAI, preventive war, or loss of control.
- Bounded in time: pass – 2026-01 to end 2033, 8 yearly turns.
- Paced: pass – one year is IR's own turn length; two policy actions plus one R&D allocation per actor per turn.
- Populated: pass – 4 actors, conflicts named above; blocs form along state lines endogenously.
- Measurable: pass – capability gap, safety, stability, tension, and agreement strength all move within the horizon.
- Genuinely uncertain: pass – credible endings include safe negotiated RTAI (rare), multipolar race to the wire decided by dice, and runner-up preventive attack.
- Open, not leading: weak – the frame expects races to be destabilising (the paper's finding). Accepted because the point is to reproduce IR's dynamics, not to test whether they exist; termination and synthesis report frequencies, and agreement paths are fully playable.

## Out of Scope

- Fixed background: capital-intensive frontier development (only large labs and states play unless diffusion); two-lane four-level tech tree with RTAI at level 4 (agentic AGI or comprehensive AI services); compute concentration including Taiwan/TSMC chokepoint; 2028/2032 US election calendar; information asymmetry (public lags the frontier).
- Deliberately not asked: the post-2023 Policy Tree mechanic (excluded from the analysed games, excluded here); detailed bids of smaller startups and open-source labs (modelled as shock events, not actors); domestic politics beyond US elections and Chinese consistency; exact technical content of alignment (abstracted into safety_progress).
- Adjacent questions left for variants: which verification regime designs hold best; what a middle-power or international-body actor adds.
