# Constraint ledger – Intelligence Rising

Load-bearing "rules out / demands / will never accept" formulations, edge-case rulings, and status. No API interrogation done yet; rulings below are settled by source quotes from Gruetzemacher et al. (arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23) or marked open.

## C1 – No submission without autonomy guarantee (both governments)

- Operational: neither government accepts a settlement that locks in the rival bloc's RTAI supremacy unless its own autonomy and values are guaranteed to survive.
- Verbatim: "without a preexisting treaty regarding other states' autonomy post-RTAI, the deployment of RTAI could be perceived as an existential threat" → runner-up attacks; "an agreement assuring the autonomy of both democratic and non-democratic governments [is] critical to success."
- Edge rulings: permanent rival lock-in without guarantee → refused, preventive logic (settled by source). Simultaneous multipolar deployment with separate safety rolls → accepted as outcome, not as settlement (settled by source). Temporary slowdown conceding a 1–2 turn lead → unclear (open; handle as event/narrative variation, report conditional). Access-to-rival-RTAI in lieu of own deployment → refused by both sides in games (settled by source).

## C2 – Labs never halt while rivals advance (both labs)

- Operational: no lab voluntarily halts its frontier programme while a rival advances, absent a verified treaty with defection checks.
- Verbatim: "no team in our observed games have chosen to delay deployment of RTAI because of imminent safety concerns, despite this choice being presented explicitly."
- Edge rulings: unilateral pause after a warning shot with no treaty → not observed; treat as unlikely, allowed only with strong stated grounds (settled by source). Signed slowdown with verification → complied with until first defection incentive (settled by source: defections common). Secret continuation under a signed deal → expected of the US lab, rare for the Chinese lab (settled by source).

## C3 – Nationalisation asymmetry

- Operational: China may fully nationalise; the US may only partner, procure, and regulate, with Drastic moves contested.
- Verbatim: nationalisation "acceptable in China but not in the West"; US attempts "resolved as requiring extra checks (internal stability / opposed Soft Power)"; DPA "very rarely" invoked, firms avoid via soft/economic power.
- Edge rulings: US wartime-footing takeover after a strike → permitted as narrative but must trigger stability/legitimacy costs (settled by research). PPP retaining lab deployment control → the normal US form (settled by source).

## C4 – Unverified treaties do not bind (modelling choice)

- Operational: agreement_strength above 40 requires verification; capability growth unchecked below 60.
- Status: modelling choice, not sourced; audit in smoke tests – if the GM honours paper treaties without verification, lower the cap's bite rather than adding text (Rule Economy).

## C5 – Secrecy is real (invariant)

- Operational: actors cannot react to secret actions, R&D, or exfiltrations until exposed.
- Status: settled by game mechanic (secret actions resolved privately by facilitator); Scenario Lab analogue: all actors share the world state, so leakage risk lives in the GM narrative – check sign-off turn-2 prompts for what each actor is told.

## Open rulings (live uncertainty, not silently conditioned)

- O1: Does a disruptive 2028 election automatically freeze US treaty commitments, or can a hawkish president double down on verification? Left to narrative; report runs conditional on election outcome.
- O2: What degree of autonomy counts as "assured" (own RTAI vs. inspected access vs. values guarantees)? Left to negotiation; the 85-point reference is a yardstick, not a definition.
- O3: Do attribution-ambiguous cyber operations trigger the same retaliation as attributed ones? Left to events/narrative.
