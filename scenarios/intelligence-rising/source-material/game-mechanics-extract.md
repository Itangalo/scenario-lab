## Game structure (Online version analysed in the paper)

- 4 teams: 2 states + 2 multinational tech corporations; turn-based simultaneous commitment without knowledge of others' actions, then facilitator resolution [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Per turn: open-ended "policy actions" (capped at 2 → attention economy) plus "AI R&D allocation" across the tech tree [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Tracked resources: soft power, hard power, cyber power, budget; secrecy option for actions or R&D, resolved privately [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Formalised victory conditions and collective loss conditions (Online version) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Two-lane simplified tech tree (language/world-modelling; RL in complex environments), predefined "concerns", later a compute mechanic added at participant request [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Post-2023 "Policy Tree" mechanic excluded from the analysed games — and from this scenario [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].

## The twelve recurring clusters (Table II, condensed)

1. Espionage/cyberwarfare: broad cyber use for espionage; secret research success contingent on hacking/defence; traditional espionage persists; duplicity common.
2. Winners-take-all: multipolar RTAI arrival, separate safety rolls, any unsafe deployment ruins the ending.
3. Tech-race dynamics: public research → firm-vs-firm race; firms lobby governments on safety while deprioritising it; endgame safety stampede.
4. Races are destabilising: bad-loser preventive attacks by the runner-up bloc.
5. Blocs by state lines: China+Tencent act as one; US teams need convincing; PPPs mid-game, nationalisation late.
6. Taiwan: hard power mostly quiet until endgame; Taiwan/semiconductor moves matter but rarely decide alone.
7. Cooperation via top-down intervention only: nationalisation easy in China, PPPs easy in US; defections common even under slow/joint-development deals.
8. Reactive slow policy; stability decays.
9. US elections disrupt; rightward shifts break trust; China consistent on x-risk.
10. Autonomous weapons: US gov–lab tension, often secret quid-pro-quo.
11. Resource accumulation: talent/R&D/compute everywhere.
12. Supply-chain disruption slows without stopping, and destabilises.

## Design values ported directly into this scenario

- global_stability starts at 7/10 → drifts to 2–3 by turn 4 unless mitigated.
- US elections on the turns covering Nov 2028 and Nov 2032.
- Nationalisation asymmetry (China easy / US checked).
- Exfiltration as the characteristic late-game race-flipper.
- Autonomy-assurance agreement as the necessary good-ending condition.
- Facilitator dice → Scenario Lab seeded dice (deterministic per seed/turn/event).
