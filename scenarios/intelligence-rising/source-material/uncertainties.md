## Recurring engines (high frequency, model as repeatable events)

- Espionage and cyberwarfare are widespread, especially state-led; model-weight/algorithmic-secret exfiltration is among the most common actions in all games [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Base rate: an operation somewhere most turns; major exfiltration rarer. Deception is the norm: public compliance plus private frontier work [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Talent/compute/data accumulation drives every turn: recruitment hubs, visas, semiconductor investment, poaching [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Treat as actor behaviour rather than events, except discrete breakthroughs and disruptions.
- Cyber-power disparities decide late games: when one bloc leads in cyber and the other in R&D, exfiltration can flip the race in the final resolution; pooled defence still fails on bad dice [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].

## Structural shifts (model as non-repeatable events)

- Frontier breakthrough (public or secret): unlocks the next lane level for the finder; typical mid-game accelerant [assumption].
- Taiwan semiconductor disruption: compute is the leading driver in ~1/3 of recent games; blockade sets Western labs back and may boost the PRC if fabs restart under its control [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Slows but never stops progress; always destabilises [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Bilateral summit → binding treaty with verification: the only reliable good-outcome path, and still usually needs a dice roll at the end (governance plus safety both hard) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Defection exposed: secret non-compliance revealed; usually at least one resource-rich defector; precipitates stability collapse and cyber/hard-power conflict [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Preventive strike attempt ("bad loser"): runner-up bloc (China+Tencent or USA+Alphabet) attacks to stop imminent rival RTAI, perceiving existential threat without an autonomy treaty; cyber or kinetic [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Trigger: leader at 3.5+ with gap ~1 and weak agreements. Military conflict never improves endgames: destroys datacentres, talent, and safety research, and forces rushed deployment in the fog of war [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Autonomous-weapons deployment: US-government demand vs. lab resistance, often resolved as secret quid-pro-quo; inherently destabilising [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Warning-shot incident: pre-RTAI systems cause dramatic destabilising harm; can re-engage complacent governments after early safety action [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Base rate: at least one per game once capability passes level 2 [assumption].
- Outside-startup shock: small labs produce the most innovative products (a known blind spot of the 4-team cast) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]; model as a diffusion/commercialisation shock event.

## Scheduled certainties

- US presidential elections covering Nov 2028 (turn 3) and Nov 2032 (turn 7): certain to occur; outcome uncertain. Rightward transitions are usually very disruptive to trust and agreements [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Model as certain events whose disruptive vs. continuous outcome the narrative resolves; the destabilising effect concentrates in the following turn.

## Endgame structure

- Winners-take-all rather than winner-takes-all: simultaneous arrival is common; each deployer rolls safety separately [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Scenario termination mirrors this: reaching 4 triggers deployment resolution against safety_progress and agreement_strength.
- Endgame negotiations under time pressure usually fail or produce hastily drafted agreements that defect into conflict in the closing minutes; outcomes often hinge on dice [source: ai-wargames-survey in-repo, 2026-09-23].
- Autonomy assurance (both democratic and non-democratic governments survive RTAI) is the critical good-outcome condition; facilitators grew more accepting of a China win under such an agreement [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
