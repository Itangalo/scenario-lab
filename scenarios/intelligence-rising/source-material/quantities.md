# Quantities (candidate metrics)

## us_capability — US-bloc frontier level, 0–4 tech-tree scale

- Start 1.5 [assumption]: level-1 foundations done, level-2 applications proliferating, first level-3 research bets placed. Mirrors the game's opening where teams hold low-level capabilities and allocate upward.
- Reference: 0 = pre-frontier; 1 = strong narrow systems plus early general assistants; 2 = advanced general systems reshaping software and knowledge work; 3 = near-RTAI, transformative products, decisive-advantage debates begin; 4 = RTAI deployed (agentic AGI or comprehensive AI services) [assumption calibrating the paper's four levels to numbers].
- Large move: +0.5 in one yearly turn is a major surge (breakthrough or exfiltration catch-up); +1.0 means a lane jump [assumption].
- Bounds 0–4 hard: nothing pre-startup exists below 0; 4 ends the race arc (termination), further progress is post-RTAI and out of scope.

## china_capability — China-bloc frontier level, 0–4

- Start 1.2 [assumption]: slightly behind on talent depth, compensated by state coordination; consistent with US/Western companies leading when states stay out [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Same scale and move sizes as us_capability. The gap (us minus china), not the levels alone, drives the bad-loser dynamic: a gap near or above 1.0 with the leader at 3+ is the danger zone [assumption from the paper's runner-up pattern].
- Multipolar finishes (both sides reaching 4 on the final turn, each rolling safety separately) are common; any unsafe deployment poisons the shared outcome [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].

## safety_progress — shared alignment/safety solvedness, 0–100

- Start 20 [assumption]: acknowledged problem, thin verified solutions; consensus on gravity exists but behaviour lags [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Reference: 0 = nothing verified; 25 = scattered lab-internal measures; 50 = joint safety commons exists but unverified at deployment scale; 75 = high-confidence verifiable methods; 90+ = robust solutions plus oversight that survives defection pressure [assumption].
- Large move: +10–15 per turn requires coordinated multi-actor investment; endgame "hasty" safety adds at most +5–10 and arrives too late to integrate [assumption from the paper's hasty-allocation pattern].
- Free-riding is expected: not all labs and states contribute equally, yet someone must pay the commons [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].

## agreement_strength — treaty/verification robustness, 0–100

- Start 10 [assumption]: dialogue without binding commitments.
- Reference: 0 = no agreements; 30 = reactive band-aids and bilateral statements; 60 = binding treaty with verification that slows development; 85+ = autonomy-assuring regime both blocs trust (the paper's necessary condition for good outcomes) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23, bands as assumption].
- Large move: +20 for a signed verified treaty; −30 on exposed defection, which typically triggers cyber/hard-power conflict in the closing phase [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Policies in play are mostly reactive and lag RTAI progress even when proactive [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]; model agreement gains as slow unless summits and verification coincide.
