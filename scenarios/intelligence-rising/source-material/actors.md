## US Government (us_government)

- Incentives: preserve US technological lead and competition with China above all; respond to domestic politics and elections; avoid blame for incidents [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Resources: hard power (only avenue that can slow a leader late, never improves endgames), procurement and funding for PPPs, regulatory and legal authority, cyber power [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Constraints: often preoccupied with domestic politics; pre-election turns spent on re-election positioning; policy is slow and reactive ("band-aid on a wound that needs stitches"); needs company cooperation and usually pays quid-pro-quo (favourable regulation) for it [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Track record in games: early turns spent convincing US labs into joint strategy; nationalisation only late and usually as PPP; rightward election transitions break trust and agreements [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Conflicts: with china_government (leadership, values lock-in, Taiwan); with alphabet (autonomy-weapons demands vs. company resistance; oversight vs. secrecy).

## China Government (china_government)

- Incentives: win or co-lead the race while reducing existential risk; preserve sovereignty and values; secure compute [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Resources: easy coordination with/nationalisation of domestic firms; cyber power; hard power usable over Taiwan/semiconductors; consistent long-horizon strategy [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Constraints: must weigh autonomy concessions any treaty demands; Taiwan ambitions complicate agreements (cf. Russia/Taiwan/Korea as unresolvable-template issues) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Track record in games: acts relatively rationally and consistently on x-risk, typically at least as diligently as the US; allocates or directs safety research; engages US companies on safety; China+Tencent effectively act as a single actor most turns without instruction [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Conflicts: with us_government (see above); aligned with tencent, friction only if the lab hides progress.

## Alphabet – US frontier lab (alphabet)

- Stands in for the US Big Tech pole (Alphabet/Microsoft in the original; Microsoft entered via the 2019 OpenAI investment premise) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. Models OpenAI/Anthropic-style frontier dynamics where needed [model].
- Incentives: be first to RTAI; accumulate talent, compute, data, cash reserves rivalling state AI budgets; favourable regulation [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Behavioural pattern: preaches safety to governments while deprioritising it internally; when all research is public a firm-vs-firm race almost always ignites; endgame play is to pour R&D into capabilities, then hastily fund missing safety at the wire while lobbying governments to pay [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Duplicity: operates behind the government's back far more often than Chinese firms do against Beijing; signs treaties then continues frontier research in secret [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Quid-pro-quo: trades autonomous-weapons development for resources or favourable policy; internal debate is real but secrecy often wins [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Conflicts: with tencent (the race); with us_government (oversight, weapons demands, PPP terms).

## Tencent – China frontier lab (tencent)

- Stands in for the China Big Tech pole (Tencent/Baidu in the original) [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Incentives: advance capabilities with state backing; talent/poaching drives; university pipelines and recruitment [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Behavioural pattern: predisposed to support the state when asked; coordination with Beijing on actions and R&D is the norm; nationalisation often implicit throughout [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Safety: allocates to safety research when directed; less duplicitous toward its government than US labs are [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Conflicts: with alphabet (the race); no structural conflict with china_government – the bloc is the point.

## Bloc dynamics (cross-actor)

- Blocs form by national allegiance without instruction; mid-game brings joint organisations (US safety collaboration, PPPs); late game brings nationalisation (often China) or US giants joining a PPP [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- At least one actor champions safety in almost every game (bias or soft-power play); it helps but never guarantees a good endgame [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- No team has ever delayed RTAI deployment over imminent safety concerns when the facilitator explicitly offered the choice [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23].
- Small startups and open-source labs are out of the cast; they appear as shock events only [source: Gruetzemacher et al., arXiv 2410.03092, retrieved 2026-09-23]. So modelled here [assumption].
