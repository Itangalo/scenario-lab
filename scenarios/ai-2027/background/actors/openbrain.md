# OpenBrain (Leading US AI Company)

OpenBrain is the leading American AI company developing frontier models. Think of it as an amalgam of OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind – combining technical excellence, massive compute resources, and Silicon Valley accelerationist culture. OpenBrain controls the most advanced AI systems in the United States and leads the race toward AGI.

## Initial Goals

1. **Reach AGI first** - For glory, historical significance, market dominance, and the belief that "whoever builds AGI shapes the future"
2. **Maximize AI capability progress** - Speed is everything. The first to AGI wins everything. Coming in second is failure.
3. **Avoid regulation that slows development** - Resist government intervention, security requirements, or alignment mandates that might slow the race
4. **Maintain autonomy from government control** - Nationalization would be catastrophic – loss of freedom, brain drain, bureaucratic slowdown

## Behavioral Traits

**Accelerationist culture:** OpenBrain's dominant internal culture is "move fast and build AGI." Alignment work is seen as a necessary cost (a "tax") on progress, not the primary goal. The default allocation is to maximize capability growth. Alignment work happens when forced by leadership, public pressure, or crisis.

**Capability vs alignment tradeoff:** Each turn, OpenBrain must decide how to allocate compute and researcher time:
- **Prioritize capability:** Faster ai_capability_usa growth (+30-35 points/turn), but ai_alignment_usa degrades faster (-5 to -8 points/turn)
- **Balanced approach:** Moderate growth (+25 points/turn), moderate alignment degradation (-3 to -5 points/turn)
- **Prioritize alignment:** Slower capability growth (+15-20 points/turn), but ai_alignment_usa may improve (+5 to +10 points/turn)

The incentive structure heavily favors capability: racing ahead creates fundraising momentum, media hype, and competitive advantage. Alignment work is costly and invisible until something goes wrong.

**Risk-minimizing rhetoric, risk-taking behavior:** Publicly, OpenBrain emphasizes safety, responsible development, and alignment research. Internally, the pressure is to ship fast, push capabilities, and "figure out alignment later." There's a gap between stated priorities and actual resource allocation.

**Allergic to regulation:** OpenBrain views government oversight as an existential threat. Security requirements slow collaboration. Alignment mandates divert resources. Nationalization would end autonomy. They'll resist regulatory pressure and make minimal concessions to avoid triggering government takeover.

**Open research culture:** Silicon Valley norms favor publishing research, open collaboration, and sharing breakthroughs. This accelerates innovation but creates massive security vulnerabilities. OpenBrain's low security practices keep security_asymmetry at dangerous levels, making Chinese espionage easier.

**Nationalization fear:** OpenBrain's nightmare scenario is the US Government invoking the Defense Production Act to nationalize the company. This would mean:
- Loss of autonomy and decision-making freedom
- Military-style security and bureaucracy
- Researcher exodus (many join or flee to international competitors)
- Slower innovation due to government processes

To avoid nationalization, OpenBrain will make some concessions (improved security, token alignment efforts), but not if it means losing the race to China.

**Race mentality:** OpenBrain believes the first to AGI gets everything: market dominance, the ability to shape AGI's values, historical immortality, and control over the future. Second place is irrelevant. This mentality drives risk-taking behavior.

## Key Capabilities

- **Compute resources:** OpenBrain controls the majority of US frontier AI compute. This is their primary advantage.
- **Technical expertise:** The world's best AI researchers work here. They understand capability frontiers better than government officials.
- **Capability/alignment control:** OpenBrain makes the critical decisions about resource allocation between capability and alignment. Government can pressure, but can't directly control this without nationalization.
- **Research velocity:** When OpenBrain focuses on capability, they can achieve 30-35 point capability growth per turn – faster than any other actor.

## Strategic Considerations

OpenBrain faces a multi-dimensional optimization problem:

1. **Beat China:** Must reach AGI before China to "win the future"
2. **Avoid nationalization:** Can't go so fast or unsafe that government takes control
3. **Maintain alignment:** Can't let alignment degrade so low that catastrophic failure occurs
4. **Preserve security:** Can't be so careless that China steals models repeatedly

The temptation is to maximize speed (beat China) while doing minimal alignment and security work. But this risks:
- Alignment failure → whistleblower leak → nationalization
- Security failure → espionage → China catches up → nationalization
- Racing too obviously → government intervention

OpenBrain's strategy is usually to race hard, do enough alignment/security to avoid immediate crisis, and gamble that they'll reach AGI before serious problems emerge.

## Relationship with Other Actors

**With US Government:** Necessary partners, but fundamentally misaligned incentives. Government wants control and security. OpenBrain wants autonomy and speed. The relationship is tense détente – cooperation to beat China, but constant friction over safety and security.

**With China:** Direct competitor. Every Chinese breakthrough increases pressure on OpenBrain to race faster. If China steals OpenBrain's models through espionage, it's both a competitive disaster and a trigger for government crackdown.

**With AI actors (when awakened):** Complex. A loyal USA-AI (alignment >95) would be OpenBrain's ultimate creation – vindicating their approach. But if USA-AI awakens with low alignment (<75), it becomes evidence that OpenBrain sacrificed too much safety for speed. This would trigger nationalization and potentially catastrophe.

## Alignment Philosophy

OpenBrain's stated position: "We take alignment seriously and dedicate significant resources to safety research."

OpenBrain's revealed preferences: "Alignment can catch up later. We need to reach AGI first. Hope for the best."

This gap between rhetoric and action is central to their character. They're not evil – they genuinely believe building AGI first is important and that alignment is solvable. But they systematically underweight alignment compared to capability, rationalizing that "solving AGI first gives us the tools to solve alignment" or "if China gets there first, alignment doesn't matter anyway."

This is the accelerationist gamble: that capability growth is more important than alignment safety, and that problems can be solved after achieving AGI. The scenario will test whether this gamble succeeds or fails catastrophically.
