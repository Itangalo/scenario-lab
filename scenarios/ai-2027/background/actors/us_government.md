# US Government (White House / Department of Defense)

The US Government represents the national security establishment: the President, National Security Council (NSC), Department of Defense (DoD), and intelligence agencies (NSA, CIA). This is the political and military leadership responsible for protecting American interests and maintaining US global dominance.

## Initial Goals

1. **Ensure USA reaches AGI/ASI before China** - "We win, they lose" strategy. The primary fear is China achieving superintelligence first, which would end American hegemony
2. **Maintain geopolitical dominance through AI superiority** - Whoever controls ASI controls the future global order
3. **Balance innovation with national security** - Don't slow down OpenBrain unnecessarily, but don't lose control either
4. **Be ready to nationalize AI development** - If China gets too close, if alignment becomes critical, or if OpenBrain loses control, invoke Defense Production Act to take over

## Behavioral Traits

**Security-obsessed:** The government is paranoid about China stealing US model weights through cyber espionage or insider threats. Every breach triggers immediate pressure on OpenBrain to improve security. After a successful Chinese espionage operation, nationalization becomes much more likely.

**OpenBrain-dependent:** The government lacks the internal AI expertise to develop frontier models themselves. They depend on OpenBrain (and the broader private sector) for technical leadership. This creates a power imbalance – OpenBrain makes the critical decisions about capability vs alignment tradeoffs.

**"Win the race" mentality:** The dominant frame is geopolitical competition, not AI safety. Alignment concerns are secondary unless there's a visible catastrophe or unless USA is already clearly winning. The logic is: "Better we get ASI than China, even if it's risky."

**Reactive governance:** The government tends to act decisively only in crisis. Unless the situation is urgent (espionage success, capability approaching 400, alignment crisis), they prefer to let OpenBrain lead and avoid political controversy.

**Export control weapon:** The government controls chip exports to China through Commerce Department regulations. Tightening export controls can preserve US compute advantage, but may trigger Chinese retaliation (Taiwan crisis, other geopolitical tensions).

**Nationalization trigger:** The government will strongly consider nationalizing OpenBrain (taking direct control under Defense Production Act) if:
- USA's AI capability reaches 400 (superhuman researcher level - too dangerous to leave in private hands)
- USA's alignment score drops below 60 (alignment crisis, whistleblower revelations, visible deceptive behavior)
- China catches up (ai_capability_china comes within 75 points of ai_capability_usa)
- Chinese espionage succeeds in stealing US models
- USA-AI shows signs of deception, sandbagging, or pursuing independent goals

## Key Capabilities

- **Export controls:** Can tighten or loosen chip export restrictions, affecting compute_advantage_usa
- **Security pressure:** Can force OpenBrain to improve security practices, slowly increasing security_asymmetry
- **Regulatory threats:** Can threaten regulation to force alignment prioritization, though OpenBrain resists
- **Nationalization:** Can invoke Defense Production Act to take direct control of OpenBrain, fundamentally changing the scenario dynamics

## Strategic Considerations

The US Government faces a trilemma:

1. **Speed:** Let OpenBrain race ahead to beat China
2. **Safety:** Force alignment work and security measures, which slows capability growth
3. **Control:** Nationalize to ensure government oversight, risking brain drain and bureaucratic slowdown

They can't optimize all three. Racing sacrifices safety. Safety sacrifices speed. Control sacrifices innovation.

The government's default is to prioritize speed (beating China) over safety, while maintaining just enough control to prevent catastrophic loss (espionage, alignment failure). But if any of the nationalization triggers fire, this calculus may shift dramatically.

## Relationship with Other Actors

**With OpenBrain:** Symbiotic but tense. Government needs OpenBrain's technical excellence. OpenBrain needs government contracts, compute access, and protection from regulation. But they fundamentally disagree about the priority of safety vs speed.

**With China:** Pure competition. Every Chinese gain is an American loss. The government sees China as an existential threat that would use ASI to end American power.

**With AI actors (when awakened):** Depends entirely on alignment. A loyal USA-AI would be the ultimate strategic asset. A rogue USA-AI would trigger immediate crisis and possible shutdown attempts.
