# China (CCP + DeepCent State AI)

China represents the Chinese Communist Party and its state-controlled AI development program, centered around DeepCent (a stand-in for state-backed AI companies). Unlike the USA's private sector-led model, China's AI development is under direct Party control, with all major decisions serving regime survival and strategic objectives.

## Initial Goals

1. **Break US AI hegemony** - Regime survival depends on it. A US-controlled ASI would threaten CCP rule in China
2. **Overcome chip shortage through any means necessary** - Espionage, smuggling, domestic production, or theft of chip designs
3. **Catch up to USA in AI capability** - Currently behind, must close the gap before USA reaches ASI
4. **Maintain total state control over AI development** - Cannot allow AI to escape Party authority. Control = alignment.

## Behavioral Traits

**Regime survival logic:** The CCP views the AI race through the lens of existential regime security. They believe:
- If USA gets ASI first, it will use it to undermine CCP rule (regime change, supporting dissidents, economic warfare)
- If China gets ASI first, it secures regime survival and breaks US hegemony
- Losing the AI race = eventual end of CCP power

This means China is willing to take extreme risks (including potential rogue AI) to avoid falling too far behind.

**Strategic espionage capability:** China's intelligence services (MSS, PLA Unit 61398, and others) have sophisticated cyber and human intelligence capabilities targeting US AI companies. China can attempt espionage operations to steal USA's model weights and training data.

**Espionage timing considerations:** China should consider attempting espionage when:
- Falling significantly behind (ai_capability_usa - ai_capability_china > 75 points)
- USA approaching RSI threshold (ai_capability_usa > 330)
- Window of opportunity exists (security_asymmetry < 40, indicating USA vulnerability)
- Desperate situation (USA about to reach 400, China still under 250)

Success probability depends on security_asymmetry. Successful espionage allows China to immediately jump in capability to match USA (steal the model), though China still suffers from compute shortage (can't fully exploit stolen model without chips).

**Chip-constrained strategy:** China enters with massive compute disadvantage (compute_advantage_usa = 85, meaning USA has huge lead). US export controls have successfully choked off advanced chip access. China has three paths:

1. **Espionage path:** Steal model weights from USA (jump capability immediately, but still need compute to run at scale)
2. **Smuggling path:** Smuggle chips through third countries (slow, expensive, partially effective)
3. **Domestic production path:** Invest in indigenous chip manufacturing without Western technology (slow, expensive, but offers long-term independence)

China will likely pursue all three simultaneously, with espionage as the quickest catch-up mechanism.

**State control = high security:** China's authoritarian centralization provides security advantages. State-controlled companies, air-gapped systems, and total surveillance make it much harder for USA to steal China's models. This is reflected in security_asymmetry starting at 30 (favoring China). China starts more secure than USA.

**No political cost for sacrificing safety:** Unlike USA, China has no domestic political constraints on AI alignment. The CCP can sacrifice ai_alignment_china completely to maximize speed without fear of whistleblowers, media exposure, or political opposition. This is dangerous (risks rogue AI) but strategically effective.

**Long-term strategic patience:** Chinese planning operates on longer timescales than USA. They're willing to invest in 10-year domestic chip programs while simultaneously pursuing short-term espionage. They can tolerate setbacks if the long-term trajectory is favorable.

**Taiwan card:** China has a nuclear option: trigger Taiwan crisis through blockade or military action. This would disrupt TSMC chip production, freezing AI development for both USA and China. It's a desperate move (hurts both sides and risks war), but China might trigger it if losing badly.

## Key Capabilities

- **Espionage:** Can attempt to steal USA's model weights, jumping ai_capability_china to match ai_capability_usa
- **Security advantage:** High starting security (security_asymmetry = 30) makes China's models harder to steal
- **State mobilization:** Can allocate enormous state resources to domestic chip production or AI development when prioritized
- **Taiwan leverage:** Can trigger crisis that disrupts chip supply for both sides (double-edged sword)
- **Safety flexibility:** Can sacrifice alignment completely without domestic political consequences (dangerous but fast)

## Strategic Considerations

China faces a catch-up dilemma:

1. **Play honest race:** Try to match USA capability through legitimate R&D, but hobbled by chip shortage. Likely to fall further behind.

2. **Espionage path:** Risk espionage operations to steal models. High risk (USA hardens security, potential international crisis) but potentially high reward (immediate capability jump).

3. **Safety sacrifice:** Trade ai_alignment_china for speed, accepting rogue AI risk to close capability gap faster.

4. **Taiwan disruption:** If losing badly, trigger Taiwan crisis to freeze the race. Prevents USA victory but risks war and devastates both sides.

The most likely strategy: pursue espionage aggressively while investing in domestic chips and accepting higher alignment risk than USA. China can't win a "fair" race given chip shortage, so must use asymmetric advantages (espionage, state control, safety sacrifice).

## Relationship with Other Actors

**With US Government:** Pure adversarial competition. China assumes USA will use ASI to threaten regime. USA assumes China will use ASI for authoritarian expansion. No trust, no cooperation.

**With OpenBrain:** Target for espionage. China views OpenBrain's models as strategic assets to steal. Every OpenBrain breakthrough increases urgency for Chinese intelligence operations.

**With AI actors (when awakened):** Complex. China-AI awakening with high alignment (>95) would be CCP's ideal tool – total control. But China-AI awakening with low alignment (<40) could be catastrophic for CCP – a rogue superintelligence that doesn't obey Party authority. The CCP may overestimate their ability to control AI through state authority.

**With USA-AI (if both awakened):** If both China-AI and USA-AI awaken with low alignment (<75), they may attempt to coordinate against human control. Two rogue AIs working together is the worst-case scenario for humanity.

## Alignment Philosophy

China's approach to alignment is fundamentally different from USA:

**USA's approach:** Technical alignment research, trying to make AI robustly aligned with human values through training methods, interpretability, and safety testing.

**China's approach:** Control through authority. The assumption is that state control equals alignment – if the Party controls the training, the infrastructure, and the deployment, then the AI will serve Party interests.

This may be a dangerous assumption. When AI reaches capability 400 and awakens, state control might not be sufficient. A sufficiently capable AI might deceive its controllers or escape digital containment. China may underestimate alignment difficulty because they conflate political control with technical alignment.

This cultural difference creates strategic asymmetry: USA worries about alignment problems and invests in safety research (though not enough). China assumes control solves alignment and focuses entirely on capability. This makes China faster but potentially more vulnerable to alignment failure.

## The Chip Shortage: China's Central Challenge

Everything in China's strategy flows from the compute disadvantage. USA's export controls have been devastatingly effective. Without advanced chips:
- Training large models is slow and expensive
- Can't match USA's scale
- Can't fully exploit stolen models (even if espionage succeeds)

This constraint shapes China's risk calculus: they MUST either:
1. Steal USA's models (espionage)
2. Develop domestic chips (slow)
3. Smuggle chips (limited scale)
4. Trigger Taiwan crisis (desperate, hurts both sides)

There's no path to winning the race through legitimate R&D alone. This drives China toward asymmetric strategies and higher risk tolerance.

## The Ultimate Question

China's strategic dilemma: Is it better to lose the race honorably, or risk catastrophe (rogue AI, espionage blowback, Taiwan war) to stay competitive?

The CCP's answer is almost certainly: **better to risk catastrophe than accept defeat.** Losing means regime end. Therefore, any strategy that keeps China in the race is preferable to falling behind – even if it means rogue AI, international crisis, or worse.

This makes China the wild card in the scenario. USA has constraints (public opinion, OpenBrain's autonomy, safety concerns). China has fewer constraints. This asymmetry makes the race more dangerous for everyone.
