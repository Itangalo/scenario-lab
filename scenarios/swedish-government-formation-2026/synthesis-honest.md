# Ensemble Synthesis: Swedish Government Formation 2026

**20 runs, all completed** (9–20 turns each; mean cost $0.14/run). All counts below come from the ensemble statistics unless attributed to specific per-run analyses.

## Summary

This world overwhelmingly produces one outcome: a **cross-bloc Social Democrat-led minority government** — Magdalena Andersson as PM, Green Party support, Centre Party tolerance via abstention, with both the Sweden Democrats and the Left Party excluded — in **15 of 20 runs**. A right-bloc government with the Sweden Democrats **in cabinet** formed in only **2 of 20 runs**, an extraordinary election was constitutionally triggered in **1 of 20**, and **2 of 20** ended unresolved at the run horizon. A left-bloc government formed in **0 of 20 runs**. The Centre Party is the pivot in every run; the machinery that moves the process is the Speaker's mandate (switches logged in 17/20 runs), budget deals offered in exchange for abstention (18/20), and the "dated threat" of an announced snap election (14/20). The most important simulation-level finding is that several events fire without meeting their stated narrative conditions, and logged prime-ministerial vote failures are far rarer than the failed votes the narratives describe.

## Research Questions

### rq_reachable_constellations

**Three endpoints are reachable from the 2026 result — a cross-bloc S-led minority government (15/20 runs), a right-bloc M–KD–SD government with SD in cabinet (2/20), and collapse into an extraordinary election (1/20, with 2 further runs ending unresolved on that trajectory) — while a left-bloc government is reachable in zero runs.**

- **Cross-bloc (S + MP, tolerated by C):** `viability_cross_bloc` reached 100 in 15 runs. It starts as the *lowest*-valued option (start 15 vs 35 for both blocs) yet its ensemble mean climbs monotonically from 25.75 (turn 1) to 89.06 (turn 13), median 95 at turn 13. Exemplars spanning the range: fastest resolution `run-20260822-184137-06` (elected turn 10), shock-assisted formations `run-20260822-190122`, `run-20260822-190644` (both security shock at turn 1), and grind-it-out endings `run-20260822-191005`, `run-20260822-191317` (each elected on a fourth vote after three failures, per their analyses).
- **Right bloc (M–KD–SD):** `viability_right_bloc` reached 100 in exactly 2 runs — consistent with the turn-18 and turn-19 maxima of 100 in the trajectories (`run-20260822-184137`, formed turn 19; `run-20260822-190136`, formed turn 18). The right bloc repeatedly came close and folded: it surged to 95 in `run-20260822-191005` before a late mandate switch flipped the run, and to 82 in `run-20260822-190948`.
- **Left bloc (S + V/MP):** unreachable. `viability_left_bloc` never exceeded 75 in any run (ensemble max, turn 17); its mean *declines* after turn 12 (43.06 → 36.06 by turn 13). The closest approach is `run-20260822-191317`, where the Left Party's deadline brinkmanship spiked left-bloc viability to 75 at turn 17 — and the run still ended cross-bloc.
- **Snap election:** `snap_election_risk` reached 100 in `run-20260822-184137-04` (fourth failed pm vote, turn 9 — the only constitutionally complete collapse) and sat at 100 with no government at the end of `run-20260822-184137-01` and `run-20260822-184137-05`. Bearing events skew toward the cross-bloc path: `speaker_switches_mandate` fired in 17/20 runs and is associated with a −26.25 swing in left-bloc viability at turn 14 (n=8 vs 2, small N), while `pm_vote_failed` was logged in only 3 runs (15%) — see caveats on undercounting.

### rq_sd_in_cabinet

**SD entered cabinet in only 2 of 20 runs, and both times the same conditions held: the right bloc cohered around concrete portfolio agreements, the Centre Party's cross-bloc alternative failed or was abandoned, and C chose abstention over voting no.**

- The two conversions are `run-20260822-184137` (KD actively brokered SD's inclusion; C's conditional abstention enabled the Kristersson government after a snap-election deadline forced resolution) and `run-20260822-190136` (an emergent cyberattack on the energy grid reframed SD as crisis manager; M and KD finalized portfolios; C's formateur mandate produced no cross-bloc breakthrough; Kristersson survived with 174 against).
- **Getting close is common; converting is rare.** `sd_in_cabinet` rises steadily in the ensemble (mean 28.5 → 58.56 by turn 12; p90 ≈ 72–75 from turn 11), and individual runs pushed it to 85–90 without conversion: 85 in `run-20260822-184137-02` (undone by legal review of the Home Affairs Steering Group), 90 in `run-20260822-190035`, 85 before a final-drop to 50 in `run-20260822-191005`. In every cross-bloc outcome, SD pressure peaked and then collapsed when the S–C–MP track consolidated.
- **Conditions that killed SD's chances:** KD publicly rejecting SD cabinet inclusion fractured the right bloc (`run-20260822-191208`, pledge_broken at turns 7 and 11); SD ultimatums backfired and isolated them (`run-20260822-184137-07`, `run-20260822-184137-09`); and C's veto held wherever C stayed engaged (`run-20260822-185826`, `run-20260822-190644`). Conversely, KD softening on SD roles *damaged* right-bloc credibility without helping SD (`run-20260822-184137-04`, pledge_broken turn 6).
- **Bearing events:** `pledge_broken` fired in 11/20 runs (55%) and cuts both ways — it enabled C's pivot to S in `run-20260822-185826` (turn 10) and destroyed right-bloc cohesion in `run-20260822-191208`. `leader_resigns` (3/20) is associated with −30.82 on right-bloc viability at turn 13 (n_with=3, small N). The divergence table links emergent cyberattacks to +18.14 on `sd_in_cabinet` (n=2) — directionally consistent with the `run-20260822-190136` mechanism, but far too few cases to treat as a law.

### rq_threshold_leverage

**The ensemble cannot answer this question as posed: the runs do not contain an identifiable "L clears 4 percent" batch, so the required honest-vs-conditioned comparison cannot be made without pooling — which the question forbids.**

- Counting the per-run analyses myself: **18 of 20** explicitly place the Liberals outside the chamber (e.g., `run-20260822-184137-02` "zero seats," `run-20260822-191317` "zero seats," `run-20260822-190948` "excluded from parliament"); **1 of 20** explicitly states L cleared the threshold at 4.2% (`run-20260822-184137-08`); and **1 of 20 is internally contradictory** (`run-20260822-184137` — its summary names L inside the governing right bloc while its own actor analysis describes L as outside the Riksdag).
- What the single clear case shows: in `run-20260822-184137-08`, L's presence changed nothing observable — the run ended in the modal cross-bloc government, with L described as "commentators rather than participants in the arithmetic."
- Neither right-bloc win is attributable to an L-in-chamber batch: `run-20260822-190136` is explicitly L-out, and `run-20260822-184137` is the ambiguous case. Notably, both right-bloc governments survived by one vote (174 against), so L's ~two seats could plausibly have mattered at the margin — but with n≤2 conditioned runs, no frequency claim is supportable.
- **What the scenario would have to measure:** two equal-sized batches generated under identical seeds except for the drawn Liberal result (L ≥ 4% vs L < 4%), with `viability_right_bloc`, `viability_left_bloc`, and `pm_vote_failed` compared between batches and never pooled. The current ensemble's 18:1:1 split makes that design impossible retroactively.

## Outcome Patterns

1. **Cross-bloc S-led minority government (Andersson PM; MP support; C abstention; V and SD excluded) — 15 of 20 runs.** `run-20260822-184137-02`, `-03`, `-06`, `-07`, `-08`, `-09`, `run-20260822-185632`, `run-20260822-185826`, `run-20260822-190035`, `run-20260822-190122`, `run-20260822-190644`, `run-20260822-190948`, `run-20260822-191005`, `run-20260822-191208`, `run-20260822-191317`. Sub-variants: quick formation by turn 10–13 (most runs); formation only after multiple failed votes (`-191005`, `-191317`); external-shock-accelerated (`-190122`, `-190644`).
2. **Right-bloc government with SD in cabinet (Kristersson PM; M–KD–SD) — 2 of 20 runs.** `run-20260822-184137`, `run-20260822-190136`. In both, C enabled the government by abstaining rather than by breaking its veto openly.
3. **Constitutional collapse into an extraordinary election — 1 of 20 runs (occurred once).** `run-20260822-184137-04`: the fourth prime-ministerial vote failed at turn 9, `snap_election_risk` hit 100, and the run ended with no government. Distinctive in that its snap-election date announcement (turn 8) preceded the fourth failure — flagged as invalid by its own analyst.
4. **Unresolved stalemate at the run horizon — 2 of 20 runs.** `run-20260822-184137-01` (the only 20-turn run; cross-bloc viability 95, snap risk 75, deadlock cost 85 at the final snapshot, no vote ever held) and `run-20260822-184137-05` (cross-bloc 95, snap risk and deadlock cost 100 at turn 15, run ended before a vote). These are "the dated threat arrived and the deal still didn't close" endings.
5. **Left-bloc government — 0 of 20 runs.** Reported as a confirmed non-outcome: the metric never exceeded 75 and no analysis describes a V-inclusive government forming.

A recurring micro-pattern inside outcomes 1 and 2: **survival by one or two votes under negative parliamentarism** — 174 against in `run-20260822-190136`, `run-20260822-190644`, and `run-20260822-191317`; 173 against in `run-20260822-190122` and `run-20260822-191005`; 168 against in `run-20260822-184137-02`.

## Recurring Turning Points

- **Procedural unlock (turns 3–7, near-universal).** A Speaker mandate or formal talks crosses the constitution's "no viability above 50 without a procedural step" threshold and legitimizes a path. Exemplars: `run-20260822-184137-04` (turn 3), `run-20260822-190644` (turn 3 working group), `run-20260822-184137-06` (turn 4–5). `speaker_switches_mandate` fired in 17/20 runs; switches gravitated to S or C, and the divergence table associates them with −26.25 on left-bloc viability at turn 14 (n=8).
- **Budget deals as the currency of abstention (18/20 runs; peak firing at turn 9).** The budget_deal_offered mechanism is what converts C's pivotal position into policy concessions — named deals include the "Abstention Framework Plus" (`run-20260822-185632`), the green addendum (`run-20260822-184137-03`), and rural investment guarantees (`run-20260822-191317`). The two runs where it never fired (`run-20260822-184137-04`, `run-20260822-184137-09`) are respectively the collapse and the smoothest formation — suggestive but n=2.
- **The snap-election "dated threat" (14/20 runs).** Where identifiable, an announced date forced convergence in ~11 of 13 cases (e.g., `run-20260822-184137-02` turn 19, `run-20260822-190035` turn 9, `run-20260822-190948` turn 12) and failed in three: too late in `run-20260822-184137-04`, and insufficient in the two stalemates `run-20260822-184137-01` and `-05`. The scenario's own 2014 precedent is reproduced — the date, not the election, does the work.
- **Deadlock-cost ceiling forcing the kingmaker's hand (turns 10–15).** `deadlock_cost` mean reaches 82.89 by turn 12; analysts repeatedly describe C abandoning obstruction as costs peak (`run-20260822-190644` turn 12, `run-20260822-190948` turn 12).
- **Late mandate switches flipping momentum.** `run-20260822-191005` (turn 15 switch to Andersson after the right bloc hit 95), `run-20260822-184137-01` (turn 11 switch to Andersson), `run-20260822-184137-05` (turn 15 switch to Thand Ringqvist). This is the main mechanism by which runs that looked right-bloc became cross-bloc.

## Actor Dynamics

- **Centre Party — the pivot in all 20 runs.** Dual vetoes held publicly in essentially every run; influence exercised through abstention priced in policy (tax, rural investment, climate). It enabled the cross-bloc in 15 runs and the right bloc in 2 (`run-20260822-184137`, `run-20260822-190136`). It formally endorsed an S-led arrangement only once (`run-20260822-185826`, coded as pledge_broken). Its internal discipline cracked visibly only in `run-20260822-190948` (three rural MPs rebelled toward the deal).
- **Social Democrats — strategic ambiguity, then the cross-bloc choice.** In every run analyzed, S kept V and C parallel until forced, then chose C over V. They sacrificed the Left Party in all 15 cross-bloc outcomes.
- **Sweden Democrats — maximum pressure, minimal conversion.** Demanded cabinet seats in every run; pushed `sd_in_cabinet` to 50–90 in most; entered cabinet in 2/20. Ultimatums repeatedly backfired (`run-20260822-184137-07` "Now or Never," `run-20260822-184137-09`). Security framing was their one effective lever (`run-20260822-190136`).
- **Moderates — the structural loser (18/20).** Caught between SD's price and C's vetoes; described as reactive or adrift in nearly every analysis; won only the two right-bloc runs.
- **Left Party — the marginalized veto-holder.** Ultimatum strategies failed everywhere; nearest to relevance in `run-20260822-191317` (forced tripartite talks, left viability 75) and as a would-be blocker of Andersson in `run-20260822-191005`.
- **Greens — the indispensable bridge.** Acceptable to both S and C precisely because they are not V; secured climate commitments in most cross-bloc runs; entered cabinet in some accounts, took policy influence without seats in others (`run-20260822-184137-06`).
- **Christian Democrats — bridge or splitter.** Pro-SD bridging inside the right bloc (`run-20260822-190136`, `run-20260822-190035`) versus public rejection of SD cabinet seats that broke the right bloc (`run-20260822-191208`) versus quiet cross-bloc exploration with C (`run-20260822-190122`).
- **Liberals — a voice without votes in ~18 runs.** Out-of-chamber moral commentary on SD normalization; no arithmetic impact anywhere. The one in-chamber run (`run-20260822-184137-08`) gave them no visible leverage either.

## Surprises and Outliers

- **Cross-bloc dominance from the lowest starting point.** `viability_cross_bloc` begins at 15 vs 35 for both blocs, yet wins 75% of the time — the scenario's center party routing beats both bloc paths far more often than the priors suggest.
- **`run-20260822-184137-04` (occurred once):** the only run where the constitutional trigger actually fired — four failed votes — and the only run whose snap-election date announcement preceded its constitutional justification.
- **`run-20260822-184137-06` (occurred once):** a snap election date announced at turn 4, wildly earlier than any other run, and still resolved into a government by turn 10 — the fastest formation in the ensemble.
- **`run-20260822-190136` (occurred once):** the cyberattack-to-SD-cabinet pipeline; the only run where an emergent shock visibly handed SD executive power.
- **`run-20260822-191005`:** the most volatile trajectory — right bloc at 95 and SD at 85 mid-run, then a mandate switch, pension protests, and a fourth-vote Andersson victory. Also the run where an emergent event (pension protests) supplied the concrete human cost that broke C's resistance.
- **`run-20260822-184137-09`:** the smoothest formation — no snap date, no broken pledges, no failed votes; the only cost was a Liberal leader resignation at turn 7.
- **Emergent events are strict singletons:** seven different emergent events across seven runs (cyber incident `run-20260822-184137-07`; cyberattacks `run-20260822-184137-01`, `run-20260822-190136`; energy crisis `run-20260822-184137`; market reaction `run-20260822-185632`; pension protests `run-20260822-191005`; rural rebels and security alert together in `run-20260822-190948`). None repeated.
- **`leader_resigns` fired twice at turn 1** — before any deadlock exists to blame anyone for — which its own conditioning rule should make nearly impossible.

## Simulation Caveats

- **Events fire without meeting their stated conditions.** `snap_election_date_announced` requires two failed votes or public abandonment of soundings, yet fired in runs with zero logged failures: turn 4 in `run-20260822-184137-06`, turn 13 in `run-20260822-184137-08`, turn 3 (as a media-reported "potential" date) in `run-20260822-191005`, turn 8 in `run-20260822-184137-04`. Its 70% firing rate is therefore not interpretable as a world probability.
- **Failed votes are undercounted relative to the narratives.** The event log records `pm_vote_failed` in 3 runs (15%) — and the per-turn table sums to 4 occurrences while `overall_occurrence_count` says 3, an internal inconsistency in the statistics themselves. Meanwhile `run-20260822-191005` and `run-20260822-191317` each narratively describe *three* failed votes, and `run-20260822-185632` describes one that was never logged. Vote-failure dynamics cannot be quantified from the event layer.
- **Early constraint violations, later corrected.** Many runs raise viabilities above 50 before any procedural step (flagged in `run-20260822-184137`, `-03`, `-07`, `-09`, `run-20260822-190644`, `run-20260822-191317`), and `deadlock_cost` sometimes rises on vague "public scrutiny" rather than named losses (`run-20260822-184137`, `-07`, `-09`), with one 17-point two-turn jump in `run-20260822-185632` exceeding the per-turn cap.
- **Metric semantics blur at the top of the scale.** Cross-bloc viability of 95 coexists with snap risk 100 and no government in `run-20260822-184137-01` and `-05`; the index measures negotiated closeness, not formation, so "95" must not be read as "all but formed."
- **Within-run artifact contradictions.** `run-20260822-184137` places L both inside the governing bloc (summary) and outside the Riksdag (actor analysis); `run-20260822-185632` lists budget_deal_offered as simultaneously triggered and not triggered and reports baseline metric mismatches; `run-20260822-190948` logs speaker_switches_mandate as not triggered while narrating two mandate changes.
- **Structural sameness of the analyses.** Mean pairwise lexical similarity is 0.19 (range 0.11–0.27), and all 20 analyses follow the same template with the same actor verdicts. Some of the ensemble's apparent consensus may be scaffolding rather than independent convergence.
- **Small-N associations.** Every event–metric association in the divergence table rests on n_with of 2–8; the turn-19 pledge_broken association (means 100 vs 32.5, n=2 vs 2) should be treated as anecdote.
- Several analysts note truncated/condensed context (`run-20260822-184137-03`, `-09`, `run-20260822-190644`, `run-20260822-190122`), so fine-grained behavioral detail may be missing from those runs.

## Confidence Assessment

- **High confidence:** the outcome distribution (15/2/1/2, plus 0 left-bloc) — it is directly readable from the metric maxima and corroborated unanimously by the analyses; the event firing rates; the Centre Party's pivot role; the negative-parliamentarism endgame mechanics; the "dated threat" as the dominant catalyst.
- **Moderate confidence:** the causal chains behind turning points (procedural unlocks, budget deals, mandate switches) — consistent across many independent analyst readings, but each reading is one analyst's interpretation; the conditions for SD entering cabinet (only 2 positive cases, so the "conditions" are really two stories, not a distribution); the claim that the dated threat fails in roughly 3 of 14 announcements.
- **Low confidence:** everything touching `rq_threshold_leverage` (batch identification fails; n≤2); any event–metric association with n_with ≤ 3; the true frequency of failed prime-ministerial votes (event layer contradicts narratives); anything about what happens after the horizon in the two unresolved runs; and the precise effect of emergent shocks, which are all singletons by construction.
- **Not answerable from this ensemble:** whether L's threshold outcome changes which government forms — the scenario would need matched, conditioned batches as described under that research question.
