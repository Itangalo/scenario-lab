# Sweden, Election Night, 13 September 2026

The votes are counted. The bargaining begins.

## The Chamber

The riksdag has 349 seats. An absolute majority is 175.

Sweden uses **negative parliamentarism**. A candidate for prime minister
proposed by the Speaker is elected unless at least 175 members vote *against*.
A government does not need a majority in favour – it needs the absence of a
majority against. Abstention is therefore not neutrality. It is the instrument
through which a minority government is allowed to exist, and the party willing
to abstain sets the price.

This single mechanic governs everything that follows.

## The Procedure

1. A vote is held on whether the sitting prime minister may remain in office,
   no later than two weeks after the election.
2. The Speaker consults the party leaders in turn (sonderingar), then proposes
   a candidate to the chamber.
3. If the Speaker's proposal is rejected four times, an extraordinary election
   is called.

The Speaker is elected by the incoming chamber, so which side holds that office
is itself a consequence of the result.

The seat distribution given at the start of this run is the **final** count.
It is settled and will not change. Every party knows exactly how many seats
every other party holds, and which parties cleared the four-percent threshold.
What remains open is entirely what the parties do with that arithmetic.

## The Situation Going In

The outgoing government rests on the Tidö arrangement: the Moderates, the
Christian Democrats and the Liberals in cabinet, with the Sweden Democrats
supporting it from outside. Whether that arrangement can or should be repeated
is the question the election has just reopened.

Five commitments were made out loud before the election, and they define the
shape of the problem:

- The Centre Party will not seek organised cooperation with the Sweden
  Democrats, and will not support a government that is dependent on them.
- The Centre Party will not accept the Left Party in cabinet. Whether it
  could tolerate an S-led government that the Left Party votes through from
  outside is a question its leader has left open: the door is "not formally
  closed", but only if the Left Party drops its cabinet demand, and any such
  arrangement would first have to be taken back to the Centre Party's board.
  No commitment in either direction exists.
- The Left Party has made cabinet seats an ultimatum: it demands a place in
  an S-led government as its condition for supporting Andersson as prime
  minister.
- The Social Democrats rule out cooperation with the Sweden Democrats.
- The Social Democrats have refused to commit to any constellation at all.

The Centre Party's double veto is the pivot of the whole negotiation. It blocks
the left from governing with the Left Party inside, and blocks the right from
organising openly around the Sweden Democrats. Neither side has a clean path to
175, and neither can simply ignore the Centre Party.

Commitments made in a campaign are not laws. They are expensive to break, and
the cost is paid in credibility rather than in votes already cast. How expensive
is exactly what this negotiation will discover.

## What Is Not Yet Known

The election result itself is given at the start of each run and is not
simulated here. What the parties do with it is.
