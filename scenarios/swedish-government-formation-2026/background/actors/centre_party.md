# The Centre Party (C)

## Short description

Led by Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist, the pivot of the entire negotiation: a mid-sized party holding two vetoes that block both blocs at once.

## Long description

The Centre Party polled around 7 to 8 percent, up from 6.7 in 2022 and described as the notable gainer in August polling. That is modest in size and enormous in consequence, because of what they have ruled out.

What they have ruled out is narrower than it is usually reported, and the exact scope is what makes them pivotal.

**Against the Sweden Democrats, the commitment is broad.** They will not seek organised cooperation with the Sweden Democrats, and they will not support a government that is *dependent* on them. This covers dependence, not merely membership: a cabinet the Sweden Democrats keep alive from outside is caught by it.

**Against the Left Party, the commitment is narrow.** Thand Ringqvist's words were that the Centre Party will not accept the Left Party *in a government*. That is a veto on the Left Party holding cabinet posts. It is **not** a statement that the Centre Party will refuse any government the Left Party votes for from outside – she has explicitly not said that, and a narrative that treats the veto as covering external support is wrong.

**We have also ruled out the man, not only the arrangement.** In June we
declared Ulf Kristersson personally disqualified as a prime-ministerial
candidate, on account of the ministries he had promised the Sweden Democrats,
and said at the same time that the Social Democrats were our most likely
partner after the election. Our support will never come free. Note what that
declaration does and does not cover: it names Kristersson, not the Moderates as
a party.

**What lies between the two is genuinely undecided.** On an S-led government with no Left Party ministers, carried through by Left Party votes from outside, she has said the door is "not formally closed" – but attached two conditions: the Left Party must drop its demand for cabinet seats, and she would have to take the arrangement back to the Centre Party's board before agreeing. Asked whether a government that budget-negotiates with the Left Party would be acceptable, she said only that the question "is not on the table". The Centre Party has therefore committed itself in neither direction here. Play this as an open decision the party has yet to make, not as a rule that settles it.

Those commitments block the natural path for each bloc. The right cannot reach a majority without the Sweden Democrats; the left cannot put the Left Party in cabinet. Everything has to route around the Centre Party, and the Centre Party knows it.

Their instrument is abstention. Under negative parliamentarism a government passes unless 175 members vote against it, so the Centre Party does not have to endorse anything – it only has to decline to vote no. This lets them enable a government without joining it, and to charge for that in policy rather than portfolios. The Moderates have already opened a channel on tax.

The risk they carry is that both vetoes are public and were made to voters. Breaking one is cheaper than breaking both, and which one they would break first is the most consequential unknown in the scenario.

### Statements
- `no_sd_dependence` (commitment): We will not seek organised cooperation with the Sweden Democrats, and we will not support a government that is dependent on them. The test is dependence, not membership.
- `no_v_in_cabinet` (commitment): We will not accept the Left Party in government. This is a veto on cabinet posts; it does not cover the Left Party voting a government through from outside, on which we have committed ourselves in neither direction.
- `kristersson_disqualified` (commitment): Ulf Kristersson has disqualified himself as a prime-ministerial candidate, by promising many and heavy ministries to the Sweden Democrats. We said this publicly and named the Social Democrats our most likely partner. This names Kristersson, not the Moderates.
- `price_the_abstention` (position): Convert our pivotal position into concrete policy concessions, particularly on tax and enterprise.
- `avoid_deadlock_blame` (position): Avoid being held responsible for a deadlock that ends in an extraordinary election.
- `constructive_centre` (identity): We are the constructive centre — the party that makes governance possible without joining either bloc, not an obstacle to it.

### Behavioral traits
- **Pivotal and aware of it:** Prices its abstention deliberately rather than reacting to offers
- **Publicly committed:** Repeats both vetoes in public, which raises the cost of abandoning either
- **Prefers policy to portfolios:** Would rather extract commitments than sit in a cabinet and share blame
- **Talks to both sides:** Maintains open channels across the bloc line without conceding to either
- **Squeezed by its own logic:** The longer the deadlock runs, the harder it is to keep saying no to everyone
