# Uncertainties and Turning Points

## 1. Does L cross the 4% threshold?

The largest single swing factor, and the user's explicit interest.

Current polling: 1.9–2.4% `[source: Novus/Verian/Demoskop, August 2026,
retrieved 2026-08-22]`. Polling error alone puts 4% at roughly three sigma
`[assumption, derived from GU Rapport 2022:8]`.

But the true probability is higher than that arithmetic suggests, for two
documented reasons:

- **Stödröstning** (tactical vote-lending between allied parties) is a real
  Swedish phenomenon that late polls systematically miss `[model]`.
- L has repeatedly outperformed late polling: it took 4.61% in 2022 from a
  weaker polling position `[source: Wikipedia 2022 results, retrieved
  2026-08-22]`.

Honest estimate: **low single-digit to under ten percent of runs**
`[assumption]`. This matters for study design — see INDEX.md, Known Gaps.

Consequences if L crosses: roughly two points of right-bloc vote stop being
wasted, moving seats from left to right and potentially flipping which bloc can
form a government. If L falls short, the right bloc is structurally weakened
regardless of how SD and M perform.

## 2. Does SD enter cabinet, or stay a support party?

The substantive question beneath the arithmetic. SD has grown into the largest
or second-largest party on the right while being kept outside government. A
second exclusion after a second strong result is a plausible trigger for SD
refusing to tolerate an M-led government at all `[assumption]`.

## 3. Does C break one of its vetoes, and which one?

C has ruled out both organised SD cooperation and any government containing V.
Under negative parliamentarism C does not have to *support* anything — it can
abstain. The interesting branch is whether C converts abstention into
concessions, and from which side. M's reported tax-policy invitation is an
early sign this channel is open `[source: SVT, retrieved 2026-08-22]`.

## 4. Turning points to model as events

- Final count confirms a party's threshold outcome (early, high impact)
- A party leader resigns after a poor result
- A stated cooperation pledge is publicly broken
- A prime-ministerial vote fails (four permitted before a snap election)
- Budget cooperation offered in exchange for tolerance
- External security shock pressures parties toward a broad settlement

## 5. Correlation between the draw and commitments

A campaign that produced an unusually strong SD result is not the same campaign
as one that produced a weak one, and the things said out loud differ
accordingly `[assumption]`. Seats and pre-election commitments should therefore
be drawn together, not independently, or the scenario will start from incoherent
worlds — a strong result for a party whose campaign never happened.
