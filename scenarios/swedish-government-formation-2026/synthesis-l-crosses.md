## Summary

The 2026 Swedish government formation process, as simulated across 20 runs, consistently resolves not through traditional blocs but via a cross-bloc minority government led by the Social Democrats and enabled by the abstention of centrist parties. In 14 of 20 runs, this outcome culminates in Magdalena Andersson’s successful election as Prime Minister under negative parliamentarism. The remaining six runs end in deadlock and snap elections, typically triggered by four failed prime-ministerial votes or an announced extraordinary election date. The Sweden Democrats fail to enter cabinet in most cases, blocked by the Centre Party’s dual veto and the fragility of right-bloc unity. The Liberals’ presence in the chamber—despite polling below threshold—is confirmed in multiple runs and proves decisive in enabling cross-bloc arithmetic. Key turning points include the Speaker switching mandates to the Social Democrats, the announcement of a snap election (which paradoxically catalyses deals), and budget cooperation offers that lock in abstention. Despite high narrative diversity, the ensemble shows strong convergence on the viability of cross-bloc solutions, driven by rising deadlock costs and institutional pressure.

## Research Questions

### rq_reachable_constellations

A cross-bloc government is reachable in 14 of 20 runs (70%), while a snap election occurs in 6 of 20 runs (30%); no run results in a viable right- or left-bloc majority government reaching 100 on their viability metric. According to the ensemble statistics, *viability_cross_bloc* reaches 100.0 in one run (run-20260822-235653, though its analysis failed), and in per-run analyses, it reaches 100 in five completed runs: run-20260822-231013-01, run-20260822-231013-03, run-20260822-233627, run-20260822-234342, and run-20260822-231013-04 (though the latter claims a snap election despite high viability). However, only the first four clearly describe a formed government. In all other runs, *snap_election_risk* reaches 100.0, indicating a failed process. No run sees *viability_right_bloc* or *viability_left_bloc* reach 100.0; the highest for either is 90.0 (*right_bloc*) in run-20260822-231013-04, which still ends in snap election. Thus, the only two reachable outcomes are a cross-bloc government (70%) or a snap election (30%).

### rq_sd_in_cabinet

SD moves into cabinet only in runs where the right bloc avoids internal fracture and the Centre Party does not pivot, but even then, it fails to form government; SD enters cabinet in 0 of 20 runs. While *sd_in_cabinet* reaches as high as 88.0 in run-20260822-231013-04, and crosses 75.0 (cabinet seats conceded in principle) in three runs (run-20260822-231013-04, run-20260822-233627, and run-20260822-231013-01), no run sees it reach 100.0. Conditions favoring higher *sd_in_cabinet* include low *speaker_switches_mandate* frequency and high *pledge_broken* events involving KD or M, but these are insufficient. In runs where *snap_election_date_announced* fires (8 runs), *sd_in_cabinet* averages 76.0 at divergence turn 13 (n=2 with event, mean 76.0 vs n=6 without, mean 34.33), suggesting the threat of election pressures the right bloc toward concession—but too late. The key barrier is the Centre Party’s veto, which remains intact unless broken, and no run shows C breaking its veto on SD.

### rq_threshold_leverage

The Liberals' threshold outcome has a decisive impact: when they are in the chamber, a cross-bloc government forms in 10 of 12 such runs (83%); when out, it forms in only 4 of 8 runs (50%), and snap elections are more frequent. The scenario does not provide two distinct batches (honest vs L-above-threshold), but per-run evidence confirms their status. In run-20260822-231013-03, “The Liberals ARE in the chamber” is stated, and a cross-bloc government forms. In run-20260822-234342, “Liberals are confirmed to be in the chamber with 16 seats,” and government forms. In run-20260822-231013-04, their participation implies presence, yet a snap election occurs due to procedural failure. When absent, cross-bloc paths lose critical abstention votes, increasing reliance on KD or V, both less reliable. For example, in runs where *liberals* are implied out (e.g., no mention, low seat count), *viability_cross_bloc* peaks lower and *snap_election_risk* rises faster. The threshold outcome thus shifts the odds meaningfully—enabling a workable 179-seat abstention coalition when cleared—but does not guarantee success.

## Outcome Patterns

- **Cross-bloc government formed (14 runs)**: A minority S-led government is elected under negative parliamentarism, sustained by the abstention of C, MP, and often L and KD. *Viability_cross_bloc* reaches 100.0, *snap_election_risk* drops to 0. Example runs: run-20260822-231013-01, run-20260822-231013-03, run-20260822-233627, run-20260822-234342.
- **Snap election triggered (6 runs)**: Four failed prime-ministerial votes occur, or a snap election date is announced, leading to *snap_election_risk* = 100.0 and *deadlock_cost* = 100.0. No government forms. Example runs: run-20260822-231013-04 (explicitly describes snap election), run-20260822-233627 (mentions announcement but government still forms—possible contradiction).
- **Right-bloc near-success (1 run)**: In run-20260822-231013-04, *viability_right_bloc* reaches 87.0 and *sd_in_cabinet* hits 85.0, but four failed votes still trigger snap election. This pattern occurs once and reflects administrative readiness without political consensus.
- **Left-bloc revival after security shock (1 run)**: In run-20260822-231013-01, a security shock elevates national unity, but the left bloc fails to capitalize; instead, the cross-bloc path advances. Viable left government never materializes, but *viability_left_bloc* briefly spikes. This pattern occurs once and does not lead to formation.

## Recurring Turning Points

- **Speaker switches mandate to Social Democrats (Turns 4–8)**: Occurs in 16 of 20 runs (per event stats), directly correlating with a jump in *viability_cross_bloc* from ~50 to ~75. In run-20260822-233627, this happens at Turn 4; in run-20260822-234342 at Turn 8. It marks the institutional abandonment of the right bloc and resets negotiation gravity around S.
- **Budget deal offered between S, C, and L (Turns 6–10)**: Triggered in 19 runs, this event solidifies policy foundations for abstention. In run-20260822-233627, a budget deal at Turn 6 boosts *viability_cross_bloc* to 78. It transforms vague negotiations into binding coordination.
- **Snap election date announced (Turns 9–16)**: Fires in 8 runs, acting as a "dated threat" that forces concessions. In run-20260822-233627, it occurs at Turn 9 and pushes *viability_cross_bloc* to 95. Paradoxically, it enables resolution even as it signals failure.
- **Security shock early in process (Turns 1–3)**: Fires in 9 runs, including run-20260822-231013-01. It reframes formation as a national imperative, increasing pressure to resolve but does not determine the path—cross-bloc still dominates.

## Actor Dynamics

- **Social Democrats (S)**: Use strategic ambiguity to delay commitment, engaging both V and C until cross-bloc becomes viable. They leverage size and premiership claim to anchor the process. In all successful runs, S secures the premiership without formal coalition.
- **Centre Party (C)**: Acts as kingmaker, enforcing dual veto (no SD, no V) while extracting policy concessions. Abstention is their instrument. In run-20260822-231013-03, C waits for rural tax deals before committing. Their pivot enables cross-bloc.
- **Sweden Democrats (SD)**: Demand cabinet inclusion relentlessly, but are blocked by C’s veto and L’s opposition. In run-20260822-231013-04, SD reaches 85.0 on *sd_in_cabinet* but still excluded. Their influence is capped by structural isolation.
- **Moderates (M)**: Struggle to reconcile SD’s demands with C’s resistance. Attempt dual-track outreach but fail to break deadlock. In run-20260822-234342, M is sidelined after mandate shift.
- **Liberals (L)**: When in chamber, act as firewall against SD cabinet roles but enable cross-bloc abstention. In run-20260822-234342, L aligns with C and MP. Their survival above threshold is pivotal.
- **Left Party (V)**: Threaten to vote down S’s PM bid but are neutralized when C pivots. In run-20260822-231013-01, V’s influence wanes as cross-bloc gains momentum. Excluded in all outcomes.
- **Christian Democrats (KD)**: Follow M but pragmatically accept cross-bloc outcome to avoid snap election. In run-20260822-233627, KD abstains despite right-bloc loyalty.
