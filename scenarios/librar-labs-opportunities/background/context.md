# World Context: Librar Labs in the US School Library Market (April 2026)

## The Problem Space

Student literacy in the United States is in crisis. Post-pandemic reading scores have not recovered, and roughly 20% of high school graduates are considered functionally illiterate. This costs the US economy an estimated $2.2 trillion annually in lost productivity, healthcare costs, and reduced civic participation. The problem is widely acknowledged but politically difficult to solve.

School libraries are one of the most evidence-supported levers for improving student literacy – schools with well-resourced, active libraries consistently outperform those without. Yet the majority of US school libraries are understaffed, underfunded, and running on outdated systems that make managing even basic physical collections a burden.

## The Incumbent Landscape

The US school library system market is dominated by a handful of aging vendors:

- **Follett** (Destiny ILS) controls a substantial share of large district contracts
- **Baker & Taylor** and **Mackin** are major physical book distributors that bundle basic ILS functionality
- **Alexandria** and **Koha** serve smaller districts with lower-cost options
- **OverDrive / Libby** has become the de facto digital lending platform for school and public libraries, but is not an ILS

These vendors serve their existing clients adequately but have been slow to adopt AI. Their strength is relationships, institutional trust, and multi-year contracts – not technology innovation.

## Digital Content: A Fragmented Ecosystem

Students increasingly expect digital-native content experiences, but school library systems have not kept up. The digital content landscape includes:

- **E-books:** OverDrive/Libby dominates institutional e-book lending; Kindle and Apple Books are consumer platforms rarely integrated into school systems
- **Audiobooks/talking books:** Audible (Amazon), Learning Ally (purpose-built for students with reading challenges), and Libby offer audiobooks; school access is inconsistent
- **Podcasts:** Educational podcast content is abundant (NPR, Radiolab, PBS) but almost never formally integrated into library systems
- **Music:** Streaming music (Spotify, Apple Music) is not typically considered a library resource, but school music programs and media literacy curricula increasingly reference it

None of these content ecosystems talks to each other or to the school ILS. A student researching a topic must navigate multiple disconnected apps. This fragmentation is a structural opportunity.

## Librar Labs' Position (April 2026)

Librar Labs is a well-funded startup headquartered in San Francisco with an office in Stockholm. They have built a modern, AI-powered library system that:

- can be set up and managed without a trained librarian
- uses smartphone cameras and AI for bulk cataloging (cutting setup time dramatically)
- provides AI-powered reading recommendations and smart acquisition suggestions
- reports a 67% increase in student reading activity and 100 hours of saved librarian time annually

Their existing user base is primarily international schools and networks. US penetration is minimal – they are essentially unknown to US district procurement officers. Their differentiated technology, literacy mission, and compliance posture (GDPR, ISO 27001, EU AI Act) give them strong credibility but require translation to a US regulatory and cultural context.

Critically, Librar Labs now has access to substantial Anthropic API capacity, opening up possibilities for deeper AI integration in their product and in how they analyze and pursue market opportunities.

## Key Strategic Tensions

- **Mission vs. growth:** Librar's mission centers on eliminating illiteracy, not maximizing revenue. This is a strength (authenticity, purpose-driven talent) and a risk (undisciplined market focus).
- **ILS vs. content platform:** Should Librar compete on the system layer (replace Follett) or the content layer (become the reading discovery engine)? Or both?
- **Freemium vs. premium:** Their freemium entry is a smart wedge into underfunded schools, but premium revenue needs to follow.
- **Geographic prioritization:** The US market is the logical focus, but UK, Nordics, and emerging markets may offer faster initial wins.
- **Build vs. partner:** As digital content integration becomes more important, Librar must decide whether to build native content deals or rely on third-party integrations.

## The Simulation Goal

This scenario simulates 12 quarters (April 2026 – March 2029) of market dynamics in the US school library space. The goal is not to predict the future but to surface strategic opportunities and risks that Librar Labs may not have fully considered – and to stress-test their current strategic assumptions against plausible market developments.
