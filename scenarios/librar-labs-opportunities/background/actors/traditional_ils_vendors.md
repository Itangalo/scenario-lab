# Traditional ILS Vendors and Book Distributors

## Short description
Established library system vendors and physical book distributors who dominate US school library procurement through deep institutional relationships, bundled services, and switching costs – not technology superiority.

## Long description
This actor represents the incumbent ecosystem in US school library management: companies like Follett (Destiny ILS), Mackin, Baker & Taylor, Alexandria, and Capstone. They collectively hold the majority of district contracts and have maintained that position for decades – not because their software is best, but because they offer something AI-native competitors do not: physical book distribution bundled with the software.

This bundling is their deepest competitive moat. When a school district signs with Follett, they get not just an ILS but access to Follett's book catalog, processing services, curriculum-aligned purchasing recommendations, and physical delivery. A new school can order thousands of books, have them shelf-ready upon arrival, and have the ILS pre-populated. No AI startup can offer this end-to-end physical service. Any competitor that threatens to win on software must either build or partner their way into this physical distribution layer.

Beyond books, their incumbent advantages include: multi-year contracts (often 3–5 years with auto-renewal), data migration complexity that makes switching feel risky, deep relationships with state-level procurement bodies and librarian associations, and local sales staff who are known and trusted by district administrators.

Technologically, they are behind. Software built on legacy architectures has received cosmetic UX updates but not fundamental AI integration. Most have announced "AI roadmaps" but shipped little. This creates a window of opportunity for competitors – but that window may close faster than it appears, as incumbents have the resources to acquire their way to AI credibility.

Internal dynamics matter: Follett (the largest) has the resources and motivation to fight aggressively. Smaller players like Alexandria are acquisition candidates. Some internal technology teams at these companies are actively pushing for modernization and may be more open to partnership than corporate positioning suggests.

### Statements
- `protect_contract_renewal` (position): Protect contract renewal rates across existing accounts; the primary threat is not new school acquisition but losing established customers at renewal
- `build_acquire_credible` (position): Build or acquire credible AI capabilities before the gap between their product and AI-native competitors becomes a procurement dealbreaker
- `leverage_physical_book` (position): Leverage physical book distribution as a bundling and cross-sell advantage that software-only competitors cannot match
- `engage_librar_labs` (position): Engage with Librar Labs primarily as a threat to monitor, not a competitor to fight – until Librar starts winning contracts in their district accounts
- `explore_white_label` (position): Explore white-label or acquisition options for AI modules (including potentially Librar Intelligence) to shortcut internal AI development

### Behavioral traits
- Defensive and relationship-focused; they compete on trust and continuity, not on feature velocity
- Slow to innovate internally but capable of rapid response through acquisition when they perceive a serious threat
- Highly connected to state procurement bodies, school administrator associations, and AASL; will activate these relationships if regulatory or procurement standards can be shaped in their favor
- Actively frame the narrative around data migration risk, FERPA compliance uncertainty with foreign vendors, and the value of local support presence – all legitimate concerns that disadvantage new entrants
- Currently underestimating Librar because Librar is small, foreign, and not yet winning their district accounts; this will change as Librar gains reference cases
- Not a monolith: some internal factions want to modernize aggressively, others want to defend the status quo; the outcome of this internal tension shapes how quickly they can respond
