# Librar Labs

## Short description
An AI-powered school library startup with a mission to eliminate illiteracy, attempting to break into the US K-12 market from a foundation of international school customers.

## Long description
Librar Labs is a San Francisco-based startup (with a Stockholm office) founded by serial entrepreneurs, AI researchers, and former librarians. Their core product insight is that most school libraries fail not because of lack of intent, but because they are understaffed, under-resourced, and running on software that requires professional training to operate effectively. Librar's answer is a system that any school employee can run competently from day one.

Their product suite includes Librar ILS (the core integrated library system), Librar Mobile (physical library management via smartphone camera and barcode/RFID), and Librar Intelligence (the AI module – smart cataloging, reading recommendations, and acquisition suggestions – sold separately for integration with other ILS platforms). Certifications include GDPR, ISO 27001, and EU AI Act alignment. Reported outcomes: 67% increase in student reading activity, 100 hours of saved staff time per year.

Their freemium model lets any school start for free. The conversion path to paid tiers relies on usage growth and district-level consolidation. This model works well in markets where Librar already has name recognition; in the US, the challenge is different: procurement committees have simply never heard of them, and trust in a foreign vendor must be earned through local champions and documented outcomes.

US market entry has been slower than planned. Initial outreach to larger districts has stalled in procurement bureaucracy. The more promising path has been individual school champions (a tech-savvy librarian or progressive IT director) who adopt Librar informally, generate visible results, and create internal pressure for district-wide adoption.

### Statements
- `find_repeatable_market` (position): Find a repeatable US go-to-market motion: identify the buyer persona most likely to champion Librar internally and build a pipeline around that profile
- `build_specific_evidence` (position): Build a US-specific evidence base; the existing 67% reading increase claim is not independently verified and will not pass scrutiny in US district procurement
- `decide_within_next` (position): Decide, within the next 6–12 months, whether to pursue a platform strategy (integrate digital content, compete with OverDrive) or stay focused on the ILS layer and partner with content players
- `explore_whether_librar` (position): Explore whether Librar Intelligence can be licensed as a B2B AI module to traditional ILS vendors who want AI capabilities without rebuilding their core product
- `adapt_built_compliance` (position): Adapt their EU-built compliance posture to US requirements: COPPA, FERPA, and state-level student privacy laws differ meaningfully from GDPR

### Behavioral traits
- Mission-first but commercially savvy; willing to deploy the freemium model aggressively to build reference cases even at short-term cost
- Overconfident on US timeline; tends to assume that product quality will overcome procurement inertia faster than it actually does
- Learns from customer feedback and iterates quickly on product; struggles more with top-down institutional sales than with bottoms-up user adoption
- Relies heavily on the librarian members of the founding team to build authentic credibility with school library communities
- Strategically ambitious but operationally lean; risks spreading across too many parallel bets (ILS expansion, AI module licensing, content integration) without enough focus
- Genuinely uncertain about whether their biggest opportunity is replacing legacy ILS vendors or partnering with them
