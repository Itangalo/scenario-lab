# Metrics for Cold War Endgame

## soviet_stability

**Description:** The internal cohesion and governing capacity of the Soviet state. Reflects the Communist Party's ability to enforce decisions, maintain institutional loyalty, and prevent fragmentation.

**ID:** soviet_stability

**Min:** 0

**Max:** 100

**Unit:** (dimensionless)

**Starting value:** 72

**Reference points:**

- **85:** The party-state apparatus functions as intended. Dissent is marginal and controlled. This was the norm in the Brezhnev era.
- **72:** Early Gorbachev era. The system functions but stagnation is evident. Minor cracks in discipline.
- **55:** Significant institutional disagreements. Republics begin asserting autonomy. Central directives are unevenly followed.
- **35:** The center struggles to govern. Multiple republics openly defy Moscow. Military and security forces are divided on loyalties.
- **15:** The state is collapsing. Institutions no longer respond to central authority. Dissolution is imminent or underway.

## east_bloc_cohesion

**Description:** How tightly the Eastern European satellite states follow Moscow's lead. Reflects political alignment, economic dependence, and the perceived credibility of Soviet military intervention.

**ID:** east_bloc_cohesion

**Min:** 0

**Max:** 100

**Unit:** (dimensionless)

**Starting value:** 65

**Reference points:**

- **90:** Total alignment. Satellite states follow Moscow's directives without question. The Brezhnev Doctrine is credible and enforced.
- **65:** Formal alignment holds but several states quietly pursue limited reforms. Moscow's willingness to intervene is uncertain.
- **45:** Open cracks. One or more states pursue reforms that Moscow disapproves of but tolerates. Others watch closely.
- **25:** The bloc is fracturing. Multiple states pursue independent paths. The threat of Soviet intervention is no longer credible.
- **10:** The bloc has effectively dissolved. Former satellite states are negotiating their own futures independently of Moscow.

## us_soviet_tension

**Description:** The overall level of military and diplomatic tension between the superpowers. High values mean confrontation and arms race; low values mean détente and cooperation.

**ID:** us_soviet_tension

**Min:** 0

**Max:** 100

**Unit:** (dimensionless)

**Starting value:** 70

**Reference points:**

- **90:** Active military standoff or proxy confrontation. Nuclear war is discussed as a realistic possibility. Communication channels are strained.
- **70:** High tension but both sides are talking. Arms race continues. Rhetoric is confrontational but diplomacy is active.
- **50:** Cautious engagement. Arms control negotiations are progressing. Both sides signal willingness to reduce hostilities.
- **30:** Genuine détente. Major arms treaties are signed or imminent. Diplomatic cooperation extends beyond arms control.
- **10:** Near-partnership. The Cold War framework is dissolving. Former adversaries cooperate on security and economic issues.

## soviet_economic_output

**Description:** Soviet economic performance relative to 1985 baseline levels. Reflects industrial production, consumer goods availability, and agricultural output. Decline means worsening shortages and public discontent.

**ID:** soviet_economic_output

**Min:** 0

**Max:** 100

**Unit:** (dimensionless, 100 = 1985 baseline)

**Starting value:** 100

**Reference points:**

- **100:** 1985 baseline. The economy stagnates but basic functions are maintained. Shortages are chronic but manageable.
- **85:** Noticeable decline. Consumer shortages worsen. Reform disruptions add to existing inefficiencies.
- **70:** Serious economic trouble. Rationing in some areas. Public frustration with economic conditions is widespread.
- **50:** Economic crisis. Industrial output falling sharply. Basic goods are scarce. The social contract between state and citizens is breaking.
- **30:** Near-collapse. The centrally planned economy has largely ceased to function. Barter and black markets dominate.

## reform_momentum

**Description:** The strength and spread of political reform movements across the Soviet bloc, including both official reform programs and grassroots opposition. High values mean reform is accelerating and spreading; low values mean stagnation or repression.

**ID:** reform_momentum

**Min:** 0

**Max:** 100

**Unit:** (dimensionless)

**Starting value:** 25

**Reference points:**

- **10:** Reform is suppressed or dormant. Opposition exists only underground. The status quo feels permanent.
- **25:** Early reform signals. Gorbachev has announced intentions but concrete changes are limited. Dissidents are cautiously hopeful.
- **45:** Reform is underway and visible. Press freedom is expanding, political prisoners are released, opposition movements are testing limits.
- **65:** Reform has its own momentum. Multiple countries in the bloc are pursuing change. Events are moving faster than leaders can control.
- **85:** Revolutionary change. The old order is collapsing. New institutions and movements are forming rapidly. The question is no longer whether the system changes, but what replaces it.
