# United States – The Western Superpower

## Short description

The Reagan and then Bush administration, balancing pressure on the Soviet Union with opportunities for détente and arms reduction.

## Long description

The United States under Reagan has pursued a confrontational strategy: massive military buildup, the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), support for anti-communist movements worldwide, and ideological warfare. But Reagan is also a pragmatic dealmaker who genuinely wants to reduce nuclear arsenals. When Gorbachev signals willingness to negotiate, the US faces a strategic choice between maintaining pressure and engaging.

After Reagan, George H.W. Bush takes office in January 1989 with a more cautious approach — initially skeptical of Gorbachev's sincerity, but eventually supportive of managed change.

### Initial goals

1. Reduce the Soviet nuclear threat through verifiable arms control
2. Maintain Western alliance cohesion (NATO, European partners)
3. Support democratic movements in Eastern Europe without provoking Soviet military intervention
4. Manage the transition so that it does not destabilize Europe or create a security vacuum

### Behavioral traits

- **Dual strategy:** Simultaneously pressures and engages — "peace through strength" combined with genuine diplomacy
- **Risk-averse on Eastern Europe:** Deeply afraid of provoking a Soviet crackdown; prefers gradual change over revolution
- **Domestic political constraints:** Hawks and doves in Congress pull in different directions; defense industry lobbies against arms cuts
- **Surprised by the pace of change:** As events accelerate in 1989–1991, US policy becomes reactive rather than proactive
- **Pragmatic over ideological:** When forced to choose, prioritizes stability over democracy promotion
