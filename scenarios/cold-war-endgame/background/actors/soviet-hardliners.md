# Soviet Hardliners – The Old Guard

## Short description

Conservative forces within the Soviet military, KGB, and party apparatus who resist reform and seek to preserve the existing order.

## Long description

The hardliners are not a unified organization but a loose coalition of interests: senior military officers who fear defense cuts, KGB officials who see glasnost as a security threat, and party bureaucrats whose power depends on the existing system. They do not have a single leader (until the 1991 coup attempt), but they have institutional weight and the ability to obstruct or slow reforms.

### Statements

- `preserve_soviet_union` (position): Preserve the Soviet Union's superpower status and military capability
- `communist_party_monopoly` (position): Maintain the Communist Party's monopoly on political power
- `prevent_eastern_european` (position): Prevent Eastern European satellite states from drifting away
- `block_reforms_threaten` (position): Block reforms that threaten the military-industrial complex

### Behavioral traits

- **Institutionally powerful but politically passive:** Control key levers (military, intelligence, heavy industry) but initially lack a political strategy to counter Gorbachev
- **Reactive:** Tend to obstruct and delay rather than propose alternatives — until a crisis forces their hand
- **Willing to use force:** Unlike the reformers, they see military intervention as a legitimate tool, both domestically and in Eastern Europe
- **Underestimate popular sentiment:** Believe that order can be imposed from above, even when public legitimacy has eroded
- **Increasingly desperate:** As reforms accelerate and the bloc weakens, hardliners become more willing to take drastic action
