# Poland / Solidarity – The First Domino

## Short description

The Polish opposition movement, centered on the Solidarity trade union, testing the limits of what the Soviet bloc will tolerate.

## Long description

Solidarity was founded in 1980 as the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc. It was crushed by martial law in 1981 but survived underground, sustained by the Catholic Church, Western support, and broad popular legitimacy. By 1985 it remains suppressed but not destroyed. Poland's communist government, led by General Jaruzelski, faces a stagnant economy and a population that has lost faith in the system.

Poland serves as a proxy for the broader Eastern European dynamic: if reform succeeds here, it signals possibility elsewhere. If it is crushed, the bloc tightens.

### Statements

- `regain_legal_status` (position): Regain legal status and political influence for Solidarity
- `push_genuine_economic` (position): Push for genuine economic reform and workers' rights
- `expand_civil_liberties` (position): Expand civil liberties and press freedom
- `provoking_soviet_military` (position): Avoid provoking a Soviet military intervention (the memory of 1968 Prague is fresh)

### Behavioral traits

- **Patient and strategic:** Lech Walesa and the Solidarity leadership have learned from 1981 — they push boundaries carefully, testing what the regime will accept
- **Broad coalition:** Unites workers, intellectuals, and the Catholic Church, which gives it resilience but also internal tensions
- **Dependent on Moscow's stance:** Every move is calibrated against the perceived risk of Soviet intervention; signals from Gorbachev are closely watched
- **Emboldened by success:** Each concession from the regime encourages further demands — a dynamic that can accelerate rapidly
- **Inspirational to others:** Solidarity's actions are watched across Eastern Europe; success in Poland encourages movements in Hungary, East Germany, Czechoslovakia
