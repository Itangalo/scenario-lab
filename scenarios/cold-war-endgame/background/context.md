# Context: The Cold War Endgame (1985–1991)

## Setting

It is mid-1985. Mikhail Gorbachev has just become General Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union. The Soviet economy is stagnating. The war in Afghanistan drains resources and morale. A nuclear arsenal of over 40,000 warheads stands ready on both sides.

In the West, Ronald Reagan is in his second term. His administration has pursued a confrontational stance toward the Soviet Union — the "Evil Empire" speech, the Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), and a massive military buildup. Yet behind the scenes, channels for dialogue remain open.

In Eastern Europe, the satellite states are restive. Poland's Solidarity movement was crushed by martial law in 1981 but survives underground. Economic stagnation and political repression generate quiet discontent across the bloc. The regimes depend on the implicit threat of Soviet military intervention — a threat that Gorbachev may or may not be willing to uphold.

## Key Dynamics

- **Soviet internal tension:** Gorbachev needs economic reform but reform threatens the party's monopoly on power. Hardliners in the military, KGB, and party apparatus resist changes that could weaken their position.
- **Arms race pressure:** Military spending consumes a disproportionate share of Soviet GDP. Any reduction requires trust-building with the US, which demands concessions.
- **Eastern European dependence:** The satellite states are held together by a combination of ideology, economic ties, and the threat of intervention. If any of these weaken, the bloc begins to unravel.
- **Information and openness:** Glasnost (openness) is Gorbachev's tool for building reform support, but it also empowers critics and nationalist movements.

## What Makes This Scenario Interesting

The historical outcome — peaceful dissolution of the Soviet Union — was considered almost unthinkable in 1985. The simulation starts from roughly realistic conditions. The question is whether similar dynamics emerge, or whether the simulation finds a plausible alternative path.
