# Metric Rules -- AI Competence in Swedish Schools

Hard caps and invariant constraints are defined in `constitution.md`. These rules describe the dynamics -- how and why metrics change.

## Rules

1. **ai_capability** grows every turn and never decreases. It grows by factor 1.7-2.0 per turn when below 20, factor 1.4-1.6 when 20-50, factor 1.2-1.3 when 50-100, and factor 1.1-1.2 when above 100. Breakthrough events add 20-30% on top of the base growth.

2. **Practical obsolescence hits productive use more than critical literacy.** When ai_capability grows by a factor above 1.3 in a single turn, subtract an obsolescence penalty from teacher_competence and student_productive_use before any growth is applied: 1 point at factor 1.3-1.5, 2 points at factor 1.5-2.0, and 3 points at factor above 2.0. If the factor is 1.3 or below, no obsolescence penalty is applied. student_critical_literacy does NOT get an obsolescence penalty: broad critical judgment, source criticism, and ethical reflection do not vanish just because models improved, though they may stagnate or drift slightly if schools fail to update examples. school_readiness does NOT get obsolescence penalties from frontier capability jumps. When obsolescence triggers, equity_gap usually rises by 1-2 points because better-organized schools adapt faster.

3. **school_readiness is the main bottleneck and is a slow stock variable.** Passive discussion alone only moves school_readiness by 0-0.5. Concrete work by school authorities on guidance, approved tools, privacy/procurement routines, assessment redesign, release time, and support for both productive AI use and critical AI literacy adds 0-1.5 next turn, rarely 2 in unusually well-supported cases. Government strategy, national guidance, and curriculum reform add 0.5-1.5 for 2-3 turns starting two turns later. Pioneers can add 0-0.5 immediately by sharing practical models. Edtech can add 0-0.5 only when schools are already capable of evaluating tools; otherwise it creates noise, dependency, or legal uncertainty instead. school_readiness should usually stay flat or rise slowly; it should only fall when there is explicit controversy, leadership failure, budget withdrawal, legal blockage, or workload backlash.

4. **Teacher competence grows through supported practice, not awareness alone.** Passive growth is only 0-1 per turn. Organized growth depends on school_readiness: when school_readiness < 15, organized teacher growth is near zero to 1; at 15-29 it is 1-2; at 30+ it can reach 2-4. Pioneers can add 0-1 immediate peer effects, school-authority PD adds 1-2 next turn, and delayed government action adds 1-2 after two turns. Growth is strongest when training covers both classroom practice and broader AI literacy topics such as deepfakes, source criticism, basic technical intuition, labor-market implications, and ethics. Teacher competence can temporarily run ahead of school_readiness, but if it exceeds school_readiness by 15+ the extra competence stays local and average growth slows.

5. **student_productive_use depends on adult support and adapted tasks.** Unguided experimentation, social-media diffusion, or direct-to-student AI tools add at most 0-1 to student_productive_use and often mostly create cheating pressure, hype, or confusion rather than real gains. Active growth depends on both teacher_competence and school_readiness: near zero when either is below 20, around 0.5-1.5 when both are 20-35, and 1.5-3 only when both are well established. Powerful learning tools add 0-1 only when schools have adult oversight, adapted tasks, and assessment changes. If student_productive_use rises more than 10 points above teacher_competence or 15 points above school_readiness, further growth slows sharply and misuse pressure rises.

6. **student_critical_literacy grows through structured teaching and repeated reflection.** Public debate, scandals, and deepfake incidents can create attention and small same-turn gains of 0-1 by making risks visible, but larger gains require teacher_competence, school_readiness, and deliberate teaching across subjects. When teacher_competence < 15 or school_readiness < 15, growth is usually 0-1. At moderate support levels it is 0.5-2, and only with strong support can it reach 2-3. New tools alone do not raise this metric much. Stronger growth comes from source criticism, deepfake work, civics, language teaching, media literacy, and repeated discussion of social and ethical implications.

7. **equity_gap grows whenever competence improvement is local, optional, or tool-driven without coordination.** In decentralized growth phases it usually rises by 0.5-2 points per turn. During any turn where teacher_competence, student_productive_use, or school_readiness grow and no delayed national coordination is in effect, equity_gap must increase by at least 0.5 points — stagnation at a fixed value for multiple consecutive turns is not realistic. It can only fall by 1-2 points per turn after delayed government strategy or curriculum support has taken effect and school_readiness has reached at least the mid-20s nationally. School-authorities actions, edtech, and pioneers can slow the increase, but they cannot by themselves reduce the national gap. student_productive_use, student_critical_literacy, and teacher_competence cannot exceed (100 - equity_gap).

8. **public_sentiment_ai_education** has a natural resting point around 1-2 (cautiously positive but skeptical). Positive examples, research, or national guidance usually add only +0.5 to +1. Stronger increases require repeated visible evidence that schools are handling assessment, privacy, and safety well. Major negative events such as self-harm, privacy controversies, or cheating storms cause -1.5 to -3. High equity_gap above 12 or clear school unpreparedness causes an additional -0.5 drift. Values above 5 are hard to sustain without continued proof.

9. **Effect delays matter.** Government actions take effect two turns after announcement. Curriculum reform affects classrooms after 2-3 turns. School-authorities PD and local implementation mainly take effect next turn. Edtech launches and pioneer sharing are immediate, but their effect is limited unless teachers and schools are ready.

10. **Workload and absorption limits matter.** When school_readiness < 20, school systems spend most of their energy on containment, local troubleshooting, and policy confusion rather than large competence gains. When public_sentiment_ai_education is negative, or when new AI tools arrive faster than schools can adapt, both teacher_competence and student_productive_use should be reduced by roughly 0.5-1.5 compared with optimistic plans. student_critical_literacy may still edge upward from public debate, but should not leap without structured teaching. If public sentiment is positive but school_readiness is still low, pressure to scale often increases equity_gap more than it increases real competence.

11. **Institutional inertia in early turns.** In the first 2 turns (2026), there is no national coordination. school_readiness growth should mostly come from local mapping, guidance drafts, and a few pilots, usually around 0-1.5 points. Teacher growth is mostly passive or pioneer-led. student_productive_use should move very little. student_critical_literacy can improve slightly through debate, source criticism work, and teacher initiative, but it is still patchy. Do not narrate nationwide implementation, mass certification, or broad assessment redesign before government strategy and delayed implementation actually exist.
