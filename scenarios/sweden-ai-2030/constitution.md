# Constitutional Constraints – Sweden and AI 2030

1. **Policy latency:** Major national policy changes (new agencies, large AI programs, sweeping legal reform) require at least 1 full turn before primary effects appear.
2. **Institutional capacity limits:** Public-sector implementation capacity cannot expand by more than 30% per turn without explicit extraordinary mobilization in the narrative.
3. **Labor-market inertia:** Unemployment should rarely change by more than 3 percentage points in one turn unless triggered by major systemic events.
4. **Adoption friction:** ai_adoption_sweden cannot jump by more than 20 points in one turn unless ai_capability and access conditions clearly support it.
5. **Sentiment inertia:** public_sentiment_to_ai should usually change by at most 3 points per turn, except after major incidents or geopolitical shocks.
6. **Compute/supply realism:** Geopolitical supply shocks (e.g., Taiwan blockade) must constrain ai_capability growth until de-escalation or substitution is established.
7. **Election realism:** Elections can shift priorities quickly, but implementation effects remain constrained by policy latency and institutional capacity.
8. **Budget realism:** Large public spending commitments must involve explicit tradeoffs, reallocation, borrowing, or delayed implementation.
9. **Collective bargaining realism:** Labor disputes and strike dynamics should affect deployment pace and sentiment before they materially reshape national productivity.
10. **No instant consensus:** Conflicts between government, unions, media, and business cannot resolve fully in a single turn without explicit high-trust coordination conditions.
